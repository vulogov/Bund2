# The logic engine's costs: RFC-0013 criterion 16

Measured 2026-10-30 on macOS (Darwin 27.0.0), arm64, an Apple M5 Max, with
rustc 1.95.0, in a release build (`cargo bench`). Bund2 is commit e6a0c7a
plus RFC-0013 M6, which adds the `kb` bench to `bund2-bench`: it printed
every figure below. Criterion 16 is **measured, not gated**: no
figure here is a bound, and none fails anything.

## How it was run

Three rounds, each of three runs, one after another on an otherwise idle
machine:

    cargo bench -p bund2-bench --bench kb -- --save-baseline on
    cargo bench -p bund2-bench --bench kb --features kb-unchecked -- nrev --baseline on
    cargo bench -p bund2-bench --bench kb --features kb-unpolled -- nrev --baseline on

Each round's second and third runs are compared with the baseline its first
run saved, so every A/B is paired. Each group takes 10 samples. A time is
criterion's estimate of the mean, with its 95% interval.

`kb-unchecked` and `kb-unpolled` forward to measurement-only `bund2-stdlib`
features that compile out the occurs check (`unify::CHECKED`) and the
per-inference guard poll (`solve::POLLED`). The bench prints which is
compiled out before it measures. A shipped build has both, and
`the_shipped_engine_checks_and_polls` asserts it.

## The figures

| Group | What it runs | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nrev30x10000` | naive reverse of 30 elements, ×10,000, as one query: 4,980,001 inferences | 2.184 s [2.170, 2.197] | 2.095 s [2.088, 2.102] | 2.198 s [2.179, 2.215] |
| `join10000` | every answer of `[ j X Z ]` ← `[ p X Y ] [ q Y Z ]`, 10,000 facts each: 10,000 answers, 20,001 inferences | 51.7 ms [51.1, 52.3] | 51.0 ms [50.9, 51.0] | 53.1 ms [52.4, 53.5] |
| `assert10000` | 10,000 `kb.assert`s, one by one, against `[ [ f -1 ] ] kb!` | 186.3 ms [184.3, 189.3] | 179.8 ms [179.1, 180.6] | 191.0 ms [189.6, 192.7] |
| `nat1000000` | `[ nat _ ]` to 1,000,000 answers through `query`, `lambda` `{ drop }`, as criterion 4 runs it | 335.0 ms [334.2, 335.7] | 339.5 ms [337.3, 342.0] | 347.8 ms [345.2, 350.5] |
| `loop20000000` | criterion 5's `[ loop ]`, to its cap of 20,000,000 (E30) | 1.138 s [1.129, 1.148] | 1.138 s [1.133, 1.144] | 1.178 s [1.174, 1.184] |

**Per unit**, from the three rounds' mean:

- naive reverse: 2.159 s for 4,980,001 inferences, **about 2.3 million
  inferences a second**, or 434 ns each. One `nrev` of 30 elements is 496
  inferences, the textbook count; the driver adds 2 per round (`in`, `fail`)
  and 1 for `bench`.
- the join: about 5.2 µs an answer, each answer a MAP.
- an assert: about 18.6 µs, at an average KB size of 5,000 facts. One size
  was measured, so this says nothing of how an assert's cost grows with the
  KB.
- `nat`: about 0.34 µs an answer, `query`'s `lambda` included.
- `loop`: about 58 ns an inference, a ground clause renamed without
  allocating.

## Criterion 5's arena

Printed before `loop20000000` ran, from `Solver::arena_cells` after the
query stopped at its cap, in every round:

    stopped by the cap true, 20000001 inferences; arena: 1 main cells, 2 template cells

The arena does not grow with the inferences: the clause is ground, so its
renaming shares the template, and each goal node is reclaimed with no
choicepoint above it. The unit test
(`the_cap_warns_once_then_stops_and_the_arena_stays_flat`) holds the same
at 1,000,000.

## The two shares of naive reverse

| Compiled out | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| the occurs check (`kb-unchecked`) | −31.4% [−32.5, −30.2] | −30.8% [−31.1, −30.5] | −31.3% [−32.2, −30.3] |
| the guard poll (`kb-unpolled`) | −1.8% [−2.9, −0.6] | +3.5% [+2.6, +4.4] | −1.5% [−2.6, −0.4] |

- **The occurs check is about 31% of naive reverse**, steady across the
  three rounds. L7 keeps it on. *Why* it costs that much was not measured.
  A likely cause is that a clause head such as `[ app [ H | T ] L [ H | R ] ]`
  binds a fresh `T` to the rest of a bound list, and the check walks that
  rest. If so, the cost grows with the list rather than staying constant per
  binding. This file does not show that.
- **The guard poll's share cannot be told from noise.** The three changes
  have both signs and are no larger than the spread between the baselines
  themselves: round 2's baseline was 4.0% faster than round 1's, and round
  3's 4.9% slower than round 2's. The poll is one relaxed atomic load an
  inference (`memguard::take`), and this says it costs less than these runs
  can resolve, a few per cent at most.

## Run-to-run noise

The baselines moved by up to ±6% between rounds (`assert10000`: −5.4%, then
+6.1%), and in step across groups, so the machine's speed drifted between
rounds. A difference smaller than that between two unpaired figures here
means nothing.
