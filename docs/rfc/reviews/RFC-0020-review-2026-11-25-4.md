## Blocking

### B1. R's rank test is a QR of E itself, so its 10⁻⁷ is a floor on d_j, not on √d_j (RFC:753-760, :1287-1289; D278 choice 6)

§S7 refuses when d_j = L_jj² is at most 10⁻¹⁴ of the scaled E's largest
diagonal entry. Its reason is that "10⁻¹⁴ is the square of R's 10⁻⁷,
because `dqrdc2` compares column norms and d_j is a squared length".

The squaring would be right if R took the QR of the residual matrix. It
does not. It takes `qr(D %*% ss[[nt]] %*% D, tol = tol)` (`manova.R:103`,
`tol = 1e-7` at `:41`), the QR of the scaled **SSCP** matrix E. §What R
does says so correctly (RFC:359). E's columns are already squared
quantities: E's spectrum is the residual matrix's singular values
squared. So `dqrdc2`'s norm ratio on E's columns is on the scale of
d_j, not of √d_j.

**Run on R 4.6.2.** The input is 20 rows in two groups of 10, with
`set.seed(3)`. a = rnorm(20) + group, and b = a + ε·rnorm(20). d is
diag(chol(D E D))², taken relative to the largest diagonal entry (1
after scaling).

| ε | d_min/max | `summary.manova(…, "Hotelling-Lawley")` |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁻³ | 9.93·10⁻⁷ | answers |
| 5.6·10⁻⁴ | 3.14·10⁻⁷ | answers |
| 4.2·10⁻⁴ | 1.76·10⁻⁷ | stops, "residuals have rank 1 < 2" |
| 10⁻⁴ | 9.92·10⁻⁹ | stops |
| 10⁻⁵ | 9.92·10⁻¹¹ | stops |

R's boundary lies between d ratios of 1.8·10⁻⁷ and 3.1·10⁻⁷, of order
10⁻⁷ on d_j. With the RFC's 10⁻¹⁴, every row from ε = 4.2·10⁻⁴ down to
about 10⁻⁷ answers in Bund2 where R stops:
- the F is computed from an E that R calls rank-deficient;
- under B12's spirit, that answer is mostly rounding.

**Why the criteria miss it.** Criterion 6's rank-deficient E is column
1 + column 2 exactly, with d near 10⁻¹⁶, so it is refused under either
floor. No criterion tests the boundary.

**History.** The first draft's choice 6 read "a Cholesky pivot at 10⁻⁷
of the scaled E", which is close to R. The first amendment restated it
as 10⁻¹⁴, to answer the first review's SF2. D278's ruling took the
restatement, and the second and third reviews checked its arithmetic
but not its premise.

**Fix:**
- Take choice 6 back to the owner, as a dated correction to D278.
- Recommended: d_j at most 10⁻⁷ of the scaled E's largest diagonal
  entry is E106. Keep the statement that this is not `dqrdc2`'s test,
  since R pivots and Cholesky does not.
- Add a boundary pair to criterion 6: the ε = 10⁻³ input answers R's
  values, and the ε = 10⁻⁴ input is E106. Both sit at least a factor of
  3 from R's boundary.
- §S7's new N1 bullet (RFC:759-762) then changes too: Hotelling's 10⁻⁸
  and MANOVA's 10⁻⁷ are close, and the stated reason for the gap no
  longer applies.

## Should fix

### SF1. `stat.ecdf.at` answering 0 or 1 at t = ±∞ contradicts RFC-0015 §S11 (RFC:869-871)

The amendment answers 0 or 1 for a `t` of −∞ or +∞, "as R does".
RFC-0015 §S11, accepted, says: "A NaN or ±∞ operand is E101", and
RFC-0016 §S1 applies it to the family (`operand.rs`, `finite_all`). The
RFC also cites B12 for the NaN case on the same line, so the line
applies the rule to NaN and drops it for ±∞.

The answer is well defined, so a departure from the family rule could
be argued. If so, it is a decision for the owner, not a sentence in an
amendment.

**Fix:** make a `t` of ±∞ E101, citing RFC-0015 §S11. Alternatively,
mark it **[author]** and take it to the owner as a departure from
RFC-0015. Criterion 8 then names the case either way.

### SF2. The licence paragraph overstates what D216 decided (RFC:922-928)

§S12 says D216 "already allows GPL code in the build", so "this adds no
new obligation". What D216 ruled is narrower:
- D216 (option B) allows two GPL **crates** to be linked, so a
  distributed binary carries GPL-3.0-or-later obligations.
- It also says "The source files remain Apache-2.0".

A line-for-line port of `hclust.f`, `kmns.f` or MASS's `lda.R` puts
GPL-derived text into Bund2's own source files, which D216 says stay
Apache-2.0. That is a different question from linking a GPL crate:
- D216 is about what a binary carries;
- this is about what the repository's sources are.

RFC-0019's ports raised the same question and did not answer it either.

This review's predecessor made the same error (third review, N5, "the
RFC adds no new exposure").

**Fix:**
- Replace "adds no new obligation" with an accurate statement.
- Take the source-licence question to the owner as a new decision.
  Options include:
  - a GPL notice on the ported files;
  - a statement in `LICENSE` or `NOTICE` that the workspace as a whole
    is GPL-3.0-or-later;
  - clean-room descriptions in place of line-for-line ports.
- Name RFC-0019's ports in the decision too.

## Notes

- **N1.** The `nstart` work-cap clause, "`nstart` counting as 1 with a
  MATRIX of centres", sits under the **[author]** marker of choice 9.
  That is a consequence of the owner's `nstart` ruling, not a change to
  choice 9. D278's new note should say so in one clause, or the RFC
  should cite the note there (RFC:721-723).
- **N2.** `nstart` 0 with a MATRIX is E101, criterion 5's new bullet.
  R accepts it silently (`kmeans.R:98`, `:100` and `:126` each test only
  1 or ≥ 2). This follows RFC-0016 §S1's options rule ("a value of the
  wrong … range is E101"), so it is not a departure that needs a
  ruling, but §S6 could say which rule decides it.
- **N3.** MASS's `lda` drops the priors of empty levels,
  `prior <- prior[counts > 0L]` (`lda.R:92`). §S1's `levels` must name
  every label present and no other, so no level is empty and the line
  never applies. It is still worth a clause beside SF2's new `prior`
  sentence (RFC:792-795), so the port does not carry the line over.

## Assumed but not stated

Nothing new beyond §S13. B1's premise, that R's QR is of a square root
of E, was an unstated assumption. It is now shown false rather than
assumed.
