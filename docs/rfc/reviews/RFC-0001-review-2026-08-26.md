# RFC-0001 review — first pass, 2026-08-26

Scope: `docs/rfc/RFC-0001-value.md` at `fee3661`, read against the pinned
submodules. Every `path:line` cited by the RFC was opened in this session;
every count was re-measured (`cargo xtask layout`, `cargo xtask corpus`,
`cargo xtask cite`, and `grep` over `tests/golden/`). Consistency checked
against RFC-0000, `decisions.md`, `defects.md`, `open-questions.md` and
`ERRATA.md`.

Verdict: **do not accept.** The RFC names one blocker (F29/F30) and there are
three more of the same weight, all of them contradictions with registers this
RFC claims to consume:

- `dup` as an `Rc` bump does not mint a fresh identity, which is what F13's
  disposition, D20 and D1's "clone-equal versus dup-unequal" all require (C2).
- The design's central claim — that CoW split points coincide *exactly* with
  the reference's id-regeneration points — has a counterexample on the hottest
  path in the language, `TS::push`'s `set_tag` (C3).
- `curr` is not a constant. It is the `Iterator` cursor, and the design deletes
  the field (C4).

The measurement work behind the RFC is sound and reproduces exactly. The damage
is in the preservation analysis, not in the numbers that justify the shape.

---

## What this pass confirms as correct

Stated first, because the pass is otherwise negative.

**Every layout figure reproduces.** `cargo xtask layout` prints the reference
replica at **176** bytes, candidate D at **16 / align 8**, `Heap` at **72** and
`HeapTagged` at **80** — so "the header grows from 72 to 80" is exact. Scalar
construct and clone are **0 allocations, 0 bytes**.

**Every golden measurement reproduces**, with three exceptions listed in §B.
All renderings show `q: 100.0`, `attr: []`, `curr: -1` with zero variance.
`dt: 6` appears 43 times and `dt: 7` 30 times, and every one of those 73 has a
`String` payload — I re-measured the payload, not just the tag.

**The conformance arithmetic is right.** `tests/golden/CONFORMANCE.txt` reads
`0/64`; `find tests/golden -name '*.golden'` counts 64 (45 examples + 12 tests
+ 7 probes); `cargo xtask corpus` reports the suite at 57/132, and 57 + 7 = 64.
The 63 → 64 move is recorded in RFC-0000's provenance table at line 464, as
criterion 3 claims. `tests/probes/valuemap-hash-eq.bund` exists and its golden
confirms F29 empirically.

**The `dup` chain is exactly as described.** `dup` → `dup_one`
(`create_aliases.rs:18`) → `dup_in_current_stack` (`ts_stack_op.rs:30`) →
`val.dup()` (`ts_stack_op.rs:34`) → `to_binary`/`from_binary`/`nanoid!()`
(`dup.rs:7-12`). Verified link by link.

**D4's grounding holds.** `cast_int` returns `i64` (`cast.rs:17`) and
`Val::I64(i64)` (`types.rs:72`). The narrowing argument against NaN-boxing is
correct as stated.

**Criterion 4 is accurate and honestly labelled.** Both crates exist under
`crates/`, and RFC-0000:429 does record D-2 as deferred.

## A. Citation defects

Three citations do not say what the prose says. In each case the *claim* is
true; the line pointed at is not the line that shows it. Note that
`cargo xtask cite` passes all three — see §D6.

**A1. `set.rs:41` does not show `Value::set` dup'ing.** RFC:49-50 —
"`Value::set` does the same on every map write
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/set.rs:41`)." Line 41 is `VALUEMAP => {`, a match
arm inside **`set_vmap`**, a different function. The `self.dup().unwrap()` in
`Value::set` is at `set.rs:15`. (`set_key_raw` does it too, at `:75`.) The
claim is true and worth keeping — the dup'd `res` is then discarded on the
`Val::Map` path in favour of `v.clone()` (`set.rs:17-24`), so the round trip is
pure waste — but that is a stronger point than the RFC makes and it needs
`set.rs:15`.

**A2. `set.rs:61` does not show `q` being written.** RFC:290-291 — "The `q`
field is written by `set` on the non-map path
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/set.rs:61`)." Line 61 is `return res;`, in
`set_vmap`'s catch-all. The `res.q = self.q` assignments are at `set.rs:33`
(`Value::set`, the function the prose names), `:60` (`set_vmap`) and `:93`
(`set_key_raw`). Q18 in `open-questions.md:14` carries the same wrong line; its
companion citation `push.rs:164` is correct.

**A3. `value_tag.rs:71-72` shows registration, not writing.** RFC:114-116 —
"`attribute` and `tag` write `attr` and `tags`
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/values/value_tag.rs:71-72`)." Those
two lines are `vm.register_inline(...)` calls inside `init_stdlib`. The write
is `value_tag.rs:13` — `vm.stack.push(value_val.attr_add(attr_val))` — reaching
`attr.rs:18-21`. This matters more than a normal citation slip, because §C5
turns on `attribute` actually being able to move `attr` off `[]`.

match. All three constant claims still hold across all 250.

## C. Claims contradicted by the source or by an accepted register

### C1. The equality section is materially incomplete, and contradicts D1 and D13

RFC:98-104 — "`PartialEq` … falls back to `self.id == other.id` **only when the
types differ** (`:15`, `:26`, `:34`)."

`eq.rs` has a catch-all at `:45-55`. For every payload other than `I64`, `F64`,
`String` and `Time`, `==` **is** id comparison — `eq.rs:53` — whether or not the
types differ. Two `Val::Map`s, two `Val::List`s, two `Val::Bool`s, two
`Val::Null`s: id, not content. The section also omits the `Val::Time` arm
(`:37-44`, a fourth by-content type) and the `CINTEGER`/`CFLOAT` special cases
(`:47-52`).

This is not a nitpick, because both decisions the RFC consumes are built on the
line it omits:

- D1: "**Equality.** For every non-scalar type, `==` *is* id comparison —
  `reference/rust_dynamic/src/eq.rs:53` … Two structurally identical lists are
  unequal unless they share an id."
- D13: "`Clone` copies the id and `==` compares ids for every non-scalar type
  (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/eq.rs:53`), so `A == A.clone()` is true before
  and after."

So the preservation table's "Equality by content, cross-casting int/float —
**Preserved exactly**" states the wrong contract. The contract is: by content
for four payload kinds, by identity for the other sixteen.

Consequence the RFC has to answer. `Bool` and `Nodata` are **inline** arms of
`BundValue` and carry no identity at all, yet in the reference two separately
constructed bools compare unequal. Two mitigations, both of which the RFC
should state rather than leave to a reader:

- The `==` **word** cannot reach it. `stdlib_logic_compare` accepts only
  `INTEGER | FLOAT | CINTEGER | CFLOAT | TIME` and `STRING` and bails otherwise
  (`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/logic/logic_compare_fun.rs:17-69`,
  registered at `:236`). So `true true ==` is an error, not `false`.
- `PartialEq` is still reachable through `HashMap<Value, Value>` — which is
  exactly F29/F30 — and through any `Vec`/`HashMap` of `Value` in the stdlib.

F30 carries the identical error ("only falls back … when the types differ
(`:15`, `:26`, `:34`)"), and is where the RFC inherited it. `defects.md` is
append-only, so this wants a new entry correcting F30, not an edit.

### C2. `dup` as an `Rc` bump contradicts F13, D20 and D1

RFC:191-193 — "`dup` becomes an `Rc` bump. That is the single largest
behavioural-cost change in this RFC and it changes no observable result,
because the reference's `dup` mints a fresh id and CoW mints one at the same
moment." The preservation table repeats it: "Observably identical: both yield
an equal value with **a fresh identity**."

An `Rc` bump yields two handles onto one `HeapValue`, hence one identity slot.
There is no fresh identity, and the split does not happen at `dup` — CoW splits
at *mutation*.

Against the registers:

- **F13's disposition is not this.** "Disposition: **FIX** — `dup` becomes a
  structural clone **plus fresh identity**." D20 restates it verbatim: "Bund2
  implements `dup` as a structural clone plus fresh identity — F13,
  dispositioned FIX."
- **D1 names `dup.rs:11` as a reset site.** "The lazy slot therefore has to be
  *shared across clones* and reset only at the sites that mint fresh identity
  today: `reference/rust_dynamic/src/dup.rs:11`, `attr.rs:7`, `push.rs:165`,
  `set.rs:16,31,43,58,76,91`, `bincode.rs:95`, `id.rs:6`. **Clone-equal versus
  dup-unequal is the contract to preserve.**" An `Rc` bump makes `dup` behave
  like `Clone`, which is the one thing D1 says it must not do.
- **The RFC's own §Current behaviour says so.** RFC:94-95 — "`dup` mints a
  fresh id explicitly (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/dup.rs:11`)." Confirmed:
  `dup.rs:11` is `res.id = nanoid!();`.

`dup` is a mint-without-mutation site. It is the counterexample to "split
points coincide exactly," and the RFC cites it two pages before making the
claim. The observable consequence runs through F29/F30 (a dup'd key and its
original become the same `ValueMap` key), through `Ord`'s id fallback (§E3),
and through `.id` (`base_classes.rs:16`), which is public surface on the base
`Object` class.

### C3. `set_tag` is a second counterexample, and it sits on the hottest path

`TS::push` calls `value.set_tag("stack", &curr.stack_id())` on **every push**
(`ts_push.rs:25`; `push_to_stack` at `:51`) — the RFC cites both lines, for the
`tags` claim. `set_tag` is a plain in-place `HashMap::insert` with **no id
regeneration and no stamp refresh** (`tags.rs:4-6`).

So here is a mutating operation the reference performs on every push that mints
nothing. Under `Rc::make_mut` with `tags` in the header, it splits the `Rc`.
The split points therefore do **not** coincide exactly with the
id-regeneration points, and §Value semantics' emphasised claim is false as
written. Three consequences the RFC does not address:

1. **The `dup` win is largely cancelled.** `dup_in_current_stack` pushes the
   duplicate (`ts_stack_op.rs:36`), so every `dup` of a heap value is
   immediately followed by a `set_tag`, hence a `make_mut`, hence a clone of
   the header. The Motivation section rests on `dup` becoming free.
2. **The split does not repair C2.** `Rc::make_mut` *copies* the id; it does
   not mint one. So even after the push splits, the dup and its original are
   still id-equal.
3. It is unclear whether the design intends `tags` in the header at all — see
   C6.

### C4. `curr` is the `Iterator` cursor, not a constant

RFC:117-118 — "`q` and `curr` have no word at all"; RFC:206-208 — "`q`, `attr`
and `curr` are **not fields**."

`impl Iterator for Value` (`iter.rs:4-6`) uses `self.curr` as its cursor. It
reads it at `:7,19,33,45,59,75` and writes it at
`:11,24,27,37,50,53,63,83,93,96`. The final chain (`iter.rs:92-97`) applies to
**any** value type: `curr` toggles `-1 → 0 → -1`, yielding the value itself
once. So `curr` is live mutable state on every value, not only lists.

Every golden shows `curr: -1` because iteration resets the cursor on
exhaustion (`iter.rs:27,53,86,96`), not because the field is constant. The
evidence the RFC offers for the constant is equally consistent with a cursor
that always runs to completion before anything renders — and the second reading
is the true one.

Deleting `curr` removes the state `Iterator` needs. D2 already relies on that
iterator existing: it cites the METRICS materialisation path at `iter.rs:67,82`
and `carcdr.rs:127,203` as a reader of `stamp`. This is a preservation gap, not
a rendering question.

### C5. `attr` can be driven off `[]` by a registered word

RFC:225 — "`q`, `attr`, `curr` rendered as constants … **Preserved as text.**"

`attribute` is a registered inline word (`value_tag.rs:71`) whose body pushes
`value_val.attr_add(attr_val)` (`value_tag.rs:13`), and `attr_add` appends to
`attr` (`attr.rs:18-21`). So `<v> <a> attribute debug.display_stack` renders a
non-empty `attr: [...]` in the reference and `attr: []` under a constant
rendering. No golden catches it — `attribute` has zero corpus uses — but this
is a **known** writer, unlike `q`.

The RFC raised the hazard for `q` (Q18, `[UNGROUNDED]`) and not for `attr`,
which is the more certain of the two. Either `attr` is a field, or `attribute`
is out of scope — and D14 is OPEN, so the RFC may not assume the latter
(CLAUDE.md: never adopt an OPEN decision's default silently).

On `q`, the opposite result, offered so Q18 can be closed rather than carried:
I found **no word-reachable writer**. `set_q` and `calc_q` (`q.rs:4,8`) have
zero callers in `rust_multistackvm`, `rust_multistack` or `Bund`. The only two
paths to `q: 0.0` are `Value::new()` via `Value::none()`
(`create_special.rs:20`, used once, at `debug_debug.rs:20`, and only as a
display fallback that never reaches the stack) and `Value::map`'s fall-through
(`map.rs:108`) — and neither `map` nor `map_float` has any caller outside
`rust_dynamic`. Everything else copy-propagates `self.q` (`set.rs:33,60,93`,
`push.rs:164`). Q18's answer looks like "no", and it is groundable.

### C6. `HeapValue` has no `tags` field

§The heap object gives four fields — `id`, `stamp`, `dt`, `payload`. §Rendering
says "`tags` **is** a field, because it varies." The struct as written cannot
carry it. Given C3, where `tags` lives is not cosmetic: it decides whether
every push splits the `Rc`.

`Payload` is also never defined, though the whole `dt`-vs-payload independence
argument and the byte-identity claim both depend on its arms — see §F1.

### C7. `Val::Null` carries two `dt` tags, so the one-to-one claim fails

§The heap object justifies the 16-byte shape this way: "the tag is only ever
ambiguous for heap types — `NONE`, `BOOL`, `INTEGER` and `FLOAT` are one-to-one
with their payloads — so it rides in the header."

`NONE` is not. `Val::Null` is written with `dt: NONE` (`value.rs:37,39`, via
`Value::new`/`Value::none`) **and** with `dt: NODATA` (`create_special.rs:11,13`,
via `Value::nodata`). Since `BundValue::Nodata` is an inline arm carrying no
header, it cannot distinguish `dt: 0` from `dt: 97`.

`NODATA` is not decorative: `nodata` is a registered word aliased `|` and `∅`
(`create_aliases.rs:40,41`), it is the sentinel that terminates the `*`
fold-family loop (D12, `math_op.rs:38-52`), and it has its own arms in `conv`
(`conv.rs:741`) and `len` (`len.rs:95`). No corpus program uses it, so no
golden catches it.

The fix is cheap — two inline arms instead of one, still 16 bytes — but the
justifying sentence is wrong as it stands, and the payload→`dt` table has no
`Val::Null` row at all.

### C8. The payload→`dt` table is incomplete, against the RFC's own citation

- `Val::Map` omits **MESSAGE** (`create.rs:186`, `res.dt = MESSAGE` on a
  `Value::dict()`) and **ASSOCIATION**. The very next sentence quotes
  `get.rs:7`, whose arm list contains both.
- `Val::List` omits **PAIR** (`create.rs:166`, `res.dt = PAIR` on a
  `from_list`; `cast_pair` matches `Val::List` at `cast.rs:106-111`).
- No `Val::Null` row (C7); no `Val::Lambda`, `Val::Matrix`, `Val::Metrics`,
  `Val::Time`, `Val::Json`, `Val::Operator`, `Val::Embedding`, `Val::Token`,
  `Val::Error`, `Val::Exit` rows.

If the table is meant to be illustrative, say so; if it is the preservation
contract for the tag axis, it is missing entries.

### C9. `Cell<u64>` for `id` conflicts with three things the RFC also claims

None of this makes a `u64` wrong. It makes the RFC silent on a mapping it
needs, in three places:

**(a) The byte-identical wire format.** The reference serialises `id: String`
(`value.rs:17`), a 21-character nanoid (`create.rs:10`). bincode
length-prefixes a `String`, so a `u64` rendered as `"7"` produces different
bytes. D1 resolved to "lazy nanoid derived from counter plus VM seed
(**preserves format**)" — `Cell<u64>` is not that unless the RFC gives the
expansion.

**(b) Criterion 5.** The golden normaliser matches the literal pattern
`id: "` — a **quoted** string (`xtask/src/golden/mod.rs:169,177`). A field
printed as `id: 7` is not normalised, and all 250 renderings then differ.

**(c) `.id` is program-visible surface.** `register_method_id` pushes
`Value::from_string(value.id)` (`base_classes.rs:16`), registered at `:91` and
exposed as a PTR attribute on the base `Object` class at `:98`. The id's
*spelling* is returned to Bund code, not just its uniqueness. (Reachability is
narrow — `VM::m` bails unless the top of stack is `OBJECT`,
`multistackvm_object.rs:45-56` — but see C10.)

### C10. ERRATA is not reconciled

`ERRATA.md`'s first entry stands unretired: "`00-jit-feasibility.md` §4.1 ->
superseded by the OOP finding in `03-metaprogramming-oop-debugger.md` §2.2:
`.id` and `.timestamp` are public API on the base `Object` class, **so the
value cannot shrink to 16 bytes**."

RFC-0001 specifies 16 bytes and supersedes only the `05-rfc-roadmap.md` §6 row.
It owes a second errata line, and the grounding that earns it is available:
`VM::m` peeks and bails unless `type_of() == OBJECT`
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_object.rs:45-56`), and `OBJECT`
is a `Val::Map` (`create_map.rs:149`) — always heap, always carrying the
header. So `.id` and `.timestamp` are unreachable on a scalar and do not
force identity inline. Without that line the RFC contradicts a standing errata
entry rather than superseding it.

### C11. The JSON row does not say which paths keep the loss

Table row: "JSON round trip losing identity — **Preserved exactly**, including
the loss." But once `dup` stops serialising (D20, F13), `dup` of a JSON value
no longer round-trips through text and no longer loses identity — F13 requires
that deviation be stated: "for JSON values the reference round-trips through
`serde_json` text …; a structural clone preserves the value exactly." RFC:144-145
describes the reference's behaviour correctly and then the table claims blanket
preservation. It needs to name where the loss survives (`wrap`/`unwrap`
`bincode.rs:95`, `compile`, `save.*`) and where it does not (`dup`).

## D. Acceptance criteria, one at a time

**1 — checkable, and true today.** `size_of`/`align_of` in a `bund2-value` unit
test, plus `cargo xtask layout`'s candidate-D row, which prints `16 / 8`.

**2 — not checkable as written.** "Constructing and cloning a scalar allocate
**0** times, measured by the counting allocator in `cargo xtask layout`." The
allocations table reports rows for candidates **A and B only**; there is no D
row. A and D have the same inline shape, so the fix is one line in
`xtask/src/layout/mod.rs` — but until it lands, nothing evaluates this
criterion against the candidate the RFC specifies.

**3 — checkable, and true today.** Checked by `cargo xtask conform` against
`tests/golden/CONFORMANCE.txt`, which reads `0/64`. Aside: the header comment
*inside* that file still says "59 goldens", stale prose in the artifact the
criterion points at.

**4 — checkable, and honestly labelled vacuous.**

**5 — checkable, but wrongly numbered and not passable as specified.** The
number is 250 across 28 goldens (B3). It is checked by `cargo xtask conform`
against the F14/F15-normalised goldens — worth naming, because the normalisation
is what makes lazy identity invisible here (§F4). As specified it cannot pass:
not while `curr` is a constant (C4), not while `attr` is a constant if
`attribute` is in scope (C5), and not with an unquoted `id` (C9b).

**6 — checkable, but near-vacuous as evidence about citations.**
`cargo xtask cite` reports **0 defects** on the tree as it stands — including on
A1, A2 and A3 — because it checks that the file and line exist and that a
quoted token is within three lines, not that the line means what the prose
says. The tool says so itself: "It cannot check that a line means what the
prose says; that still needs a reader." Criterion 6 is fine to keep; it should
not be read as "the citations are right."

**Missing criteria.** Three claims in the preservation table have no check:

- *Byte-identical wire format* — the strongest claim in the table and the
  easiest to test: serialise a fixture with the oracle, serialise with Bund2,
  compare bytes. It is also the one C9a says is presently unmet.
- *`A == A.clone()` true and `A == A.dup()` false* — D1 calls this "the
  contract to preserve," and it is precisely what C2 breaks. A probe under D21
  would capture it from the oracle.
- *The F28 fix* — the table claims "Deliberately fixed" and nothing verifies it.
  `ensure_stack_with_capacity` has zero corpus uses, which is exactly the case
  D21 authored probes exist for.

## E. Behaviours the preservation analysis does not enumerate

E1. **`curr` / `Iterator`** — C4.

E2. **`attr` writable by `attribute`** — C5.

E3. **Ordering is absent entirely.** D1 names ordering as one of the three
internal readers of identity: "`reference/rust_dynamic/src/ord.rs:175,183,191,199`
fall back to `self.id.cmp(&other.id)`." Confirmed — `Ord::cmp` has by-content
arms only for `I64`, `Time` and `String`, and everything else lands on
`ord.rs:199`. F12 dispositions `Ord::cmp` **FIX**. RFC-0001 does not mention
ordering in any section. Two questions it owes: which of `Int`/`Float`/`Bool`/
`Nodata` can reach an id-ordering fallback when scalars carry no identity, and
whether F12's FIX belongs to this RFC or a later one.

E4. **Equality for the sixteen non-scalar payload kinds** — C1.

E5. **`Send`/`Sync`.** The reference's `Value` is `Send + Sync` — `String`,
`f64`, `u16`, `Vec`, `HashMap`, all of them. `Rc<HeapValue>` with `Cell` fields
is neither. D13 forces `Rc::make_mut`, so the choice is not free, but the
consequence — a value can never cross a thread boundary — constrains **D6**
(async granularity) and **D7** (concurrent VM count), both OPEN. The RFC
disclaims D14's relevance explicitly and says nothing about D6/D7.

E6. **Where `tags` lives, and the cost of writing it on every push** — C3, C6.

## F. Assumed but not stated

1. **That `Payload` mirrors `Val`'s 20 arms in the same declaration order.**
   bincode encodes enum discriminants positionally, so byte-identity requires
   it. `Payload` is never defined in the RFC.
2. **That serialisation is the materialisation point for `id` and `stamp`.**
   D20 says so; the RFC never repeats it, and nothing in §Design says a
   `to_binary` forces both `Cell`s.
3. **That `id: 0` and `stamp: 0.0` are reserved sentinels** that a real mint can
   never produce. The RFC writes "0 until observed" for both with no note.
4. **That the goldens normalise `id` and `stamp` (F14) and dict member order
   (F15).** "Nothing can tell: no word reads an id without observing it"
   (table, D1 row) is true partly because `debug.display_stack` output has the
   id and stamp normalised out before comparison
   (`xtask/src/golden/mod.rs:169-188`). That is load-bearing and unstated.
5. **That `BundValue` is single-threaded** — E5.
6. **That `dt` keeps the reference's exact `u16` values including the 92–99
   gap**, so it can be neither renumbered nor narrowed to `u8`.
7. **That `Debug` is hand-written, not derived**, and reproduces the field
   order `id, stamp, dt, q, data, attr, curr, tags` *and* the payload arm
   spellings — `String(...)`, `I64(...)`, `ValueMap({...})` — which the goldens
   capture verbatim (`tests/golden/probes/valuemap-hash-eq.golden`). The RFC
   says `Debug` "must reproduce the reference's text exactly" only in respect of
   `q`, `attr` and `curr`.
8. **That `Nodata` means `NONE` rather than `NODATA`** — C7.
9. **That F28's fix is in scope for a value RFC.** It is in `rust_multistack`,
   not `rust_dynamic`. F28's own disposition does say "Record the divergence in
   RFC-0001", so this is sanctioned — but the RFC states the fix without
   restating the mechanism or citing `ts_push.rs:47-48`, where the wrong
   length is read.

## What would make this acceptable

Not a rewrite. The measurement is sound and the shape is almost certainly
right; the preservation analysis has to catch up with it.

1. Fix A1–A3 and B1–B3.
2. Rewrite §"Equality is by content" to state the real contract, and correct
   F30 by a new defect entry (C1).
3. Decide `dup`: either it is a structural clone plus a fresh identity, as F13
