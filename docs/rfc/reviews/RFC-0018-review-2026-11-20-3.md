## Blocking

### B1. Criterion 3's 10⁻⁶ fails on correct code under a non-canonical link

RFC lines 553-557 and 788-797 set criterion 3's tolerance at a relative 10⁻⁶ because "Convergence is quadratic, so when the rule stops the iteration the coefficients can still be some 10⁻⁸ from the solution". That holds for a canonical link, where IRLS is Newton's method. For any other link IRLS is Fisher scoring, and it converges linearly. R's rule stops on the deviance, `abs(dev - devold)/(0.1 + abs(dev)) < control$epsilon` (`glm.R:308`). Near the optimum the deviance moves with the square of the coefficient error, so a deviance change of 10⁻⁸ leaves a coefficient error near 10⁻⁴ standard errors before the last step. A linear step then only multiplies that by its rate.

Criterion 3 names five non-canonical fits: the binomial under probit and cloglog, the Gamma under log, the Gaussian under log, and the Poisson under identity. The last is the halving example at RFC lines 830-838: x = 1…8, y = 1, 1, 2, 0, 6, 2, 0, 6. R's loop, re-implemented, reproduces the RFC's account: the first step is valid, the second is halved once, and it stops at iteration 7. It stops at (0.4353914409699764, 0.4032463464511164). The solution of the score equations is (0.4353888491624697, 0.40324692240833976), where both scores are below 3·10⁻¹⁴. The intercept is off by a relative **5.95·10⁻⁶**, six times the tolerance. Dobson's design under identity is off by 1.6·10⁻⁵, and under sqrt by 3.6·10⁻⁵. The canonical fits do meet it: the Gamma under inverse at 8·10⁻¹², the Gaussian and Gamma under log at about 1.5·10⁻⁷.

Since criterion 3 compares "the estimate … against the fixture" (line 836), and the fixture solves the score equations by Newton's method (line 780), a correct `fit.glm` fails criterion 3. The RFC's own disclaimer, "a reason to look, not a bug proven" (line 795), does not make the criterion pass.

**Fix**, one of:
- compare non-canonical IRLS fits at a wider relative tolerance, such as 10⁻⁴, and say why;
- have the fixture script run R's iteration and stopping rule in mpmath, so that it reproduces where IRLS stops, and keep 10⁻⁶ against that;
- keep 10⁻⁶ for canonical links only, and correct the "quadratic" sentence at lines 555 and 792-793.

The second also makes K11's rationale ("one more step would break the agreement with `fit.logistic`") unnecessary to restate.

### B2. Dobson's treatment effect is exactly 0, and the criteria compare it at a relative tolerance

Dobson's counts total 50 under every treatment: 18 + 17 + 15, 20 + 10 + 20 and 25 + 13 + 12 (`glm.Rd:336-338`). Under the Poisson log link, the MLE of each treatment coefficient is therefore exactly 0. The deviance with and without `treatment` is the same, so Δdeviance is exactly 0, the LR statistic is 0, and p is 1. The re-implementation gives treatment coefficients of −3.7·10⁻¹⁷ and −1.1·10⁻¹⁷, and a deviance difference of +1.8·10⁻¹⁵. Rounding could equally have made it negative.

The criteria compare these numbers against the fixture:
- Criterion 3 checks "the Poisson model of Dobson's randomised trial" against the fixture (lines 820-824). That reads as its coefficients, `se`, `z` and `p`, at the relative 10⁻⁶ of lines 788-791.
- Criterion 6 checks `model.compare` "of the Dobson model without and with `treatment`" against the fixture, and `model.anova` of the same design (lines 855-860). Their `deviance` and `statistic` are exactly 0.

A relative tolerance against an exact 0 fails for any floating-point implementation. The one absolute fallback in the RFC is for "a p below 10⁻⁶" (line 797), which does not apply here.

The RFC also does not say what a negative Δdeviance does. R clamps it in two places:
- `anova.glm`'s table uses `pmax(0, -diff(resdev))` (`glm.R:521`);
- `stat.anova` sets a negative statistic to `NA` "rather than p = 0" (`anova.R:34`, `:43`).

As written, the F or χ² tail of a negative statistic decides the answer.

**Fix:**
- give the criteria an absolute floor for quantities whose exact value is 0, for example an absolute 10⁻¹⁰ on coefficients, statistics and deviance differences;
- or replace the Dobson comparison with a pair that differs;
- and state in §S7 that a Δdeviance below 0 is taken as 0, as R's `anova.glm` takes it, so the statistic is 0 and p is 1.

## Should fix

### SF1. An intercept-only `fit.lm` has an F statistic of 0/0

§S3's `f` is ((TSS − RSS)/(k − i))/(RSS/(n − k)) (line 442). For the intercept-only design, k = i = 1, so the numerator divides by zero. B12, as §S1 states it (lines 302-303), makes a non-finite answer E114. So `fit.lm` refuses the simplest model, and so does `model.anova` under `"gaussian"` and `"identity"`, whose first row is "the intercept-only model … fitted as any other row" by `fit.lm`'s computation (lines 657-662).

R does not compute an F there. It sets r² and adjusted r² to 0 and leaves out `fstatistic`, guarded by `if (p != attr(z$terms, "intercept"))` (`lm.R:338-344`).

Also, r² = 1 − RSS/TSS is exactly 0 there only if RSS and TSS round alike. RSS comes from the QR residual and TSS from the mean.

**Fix:** state the k = i case: `r2` and `adj.r2` 0, and `f`, `f.df` and `f.p` left out (or NONE), as R leaves them out.

### SF2. Without an intercept, the null deviance is infinite under several links

§S4's null deviance without an intercept is "at μ = linkinv(offset)" (line 544), and so is `model.anova`'s no-intercept first row (line 664). With no offset, that is linkinv(0):
- `identity` gives μ = 0;
- `sqrt` gives 0² = 0 (`family.R:107`);
- `inverse` gives 1/0 = ∞ (`:119`);
- `1/mu^2` gives 1/√0 = ∞ (`:113`);
- the binomial's `log` gives μ = 1.

At each of those the family's deviance is infinite or not a number for any y not at that μ. Examples are the Poisson at μ = 0 with a y > 0, the Gamma at μ = ∞, and the binomial at μ = 1 with a y < 1. R reproduces the formula (`glm.R:368`) and answers the non-finite value. Under B12 Bund2 would refuse the whole fit with E114. That is a valid no-intercept Gamma, inverse Gaussian, or Poisson identity fit, lost to a null deviance nobody asked for. Criterion 6's no-intercept `model.anova` uses Dobson under log, where linkinv(0) = 1, so no criterion meets this.

**Fix:** state that a non-finite `null.deviance` is left out of the fit (or NONE), and what `model.anova`'s first row then holds. Alternatively, refuse `model.anova` without an intercept under these links, with a text.

### SF3. The valid range of η is never stated, so `sqrt` does not halve where R does

Halving applies to "an η or μ outside the family's valid range" (lines 497-499). §S4's table gives only μ's (line 473), and the clamps table says `identity`, `inverse`, `sqrt`, `1/mu^2` have "none; the valid-range test and halving apply" (line 525). R's halving test is `valideta(eta) && validmu(mu)` (`glm.R:289`), and `valideta` is the link's, not the family's:
- `sqrt`: η > 0 (`family.R:109`);
- `1/mu^2`: η > 0 (`:115`);
- `inverse`: η ≠ 0 (`:121`).

Under `sqrt` a step to η < 0 gives μ = η² > 0. That μ passes the Poisson's μ > 0, with a finite deviance. R halves it; the RFC as written accepts it, and the fit then sits on the other branch of a non-monotone link. The Poisson under `sqrt` is one of the links §S4 offers. No criterion fits it.

**Fix:** add a "valid η" column to the links, citing those three lines, and say the halving test is both.

### SF4. Without an intercept, an interaction with the fully coded factor is singular

Without an intercept, the first factor term codes "m: every level its own column" (line 355). "An interaction's columns are the products of its members' columns" (line 358). Read literally, `[ "dose" "site" [ "dose" "site" ] ]` with `intercept` false codes:
- `dose`;
- `site.east`, `site.north`, `site.south`;
- `dose:site.east`, `dose:site.north`, `dose:site.south`.

The three products sum to `dose`, so the design is singular, and `fit.lm` refuses it with E106 naming `dose:site.south`. The same happens to any no-intercept design whose first factor enters an interaction. That is the shape criterion 1's "with `intercept` false" sentence (line 806) invites.

The other reading is that a member's columns in an interaction are its contrast columns. That gives `dose:site.north` and `dose:site.south`, six columns of full rank, which matches what R's `model.matrix` codes for `~ 0 + dose * site`. [UNGROUNDED here: R's rule is in C, `src/library/stats/src/model.c`, not read for this review.] The text does not say which reading it means.

**Fix:** say that inside an interaction a factor is coded by its m − 1 contrast columns, whatever its main effect's coding. Add the no-intercept interaction to criterion 1, with its six names.

### SF5. Criterion 9's snapshot, recorded "as RFC-0016 recorded `infer.snapshot`", sees only the first number of a LIST

Criterion 9 (lines 874-880) checks `stat.lm`'s and `fit.logistic`'s answers "f64 bit for f64 bit" against a snapshot recorded as `infer.snapshot` was. That snapshot's writer records `format!("{:016x}", floats(&v)[0].to_bits())` per key (`infer/tests.rs:372`): one float. Every key RFC-0016 snapshotted was a scalar (`fixtures/infer.snapshot`, 49 lines). `stat.lm`'s `coef`, `se`, `t` and `p` are LISTs, and so are `fit.logistic`'s. Recorded the same way, the snapshot would check the intercept's bits and none of the slopes', so the factoring of `stat_lm` could move a slope's last bit unseen.

**Fix:** state that every element of a LIST is recorded, in order.

### SF6. The Poisson "rates numerically 0" refusal does not catch the case it is there for

Lines 537-541 refuse a fitted Poisson μ below 10ε (R's `eps <- 10*.Machine$double.eps`, `glm.R:322`, `:327-328`). The reason given is that "a group of zero counts drives its coefficient without bound". It does, but the deviance rule stops the iteration long before μ gets near 10ε.

The deviance rule stops when |Δdev|/(0.1 + dev) < 10⁻⁸ (`glm.R:308`). The zero group adds about 2·n_g·μ to the deviance, so the iteration stops once μ is around 10⁻⁹/n_g × (0.1 + dev), not 10⁻¹⁵. Re-implemented on an intercept and an indicator, with counts 3, 5, 4 and 0, 0, 0, R's loop stops at iteration 19:
- the indicator's coefficient is −22.69;
- the smallest μ is 5.6·10⁻¹⁰, five orders above 10ε = 2.2·10⁻¹⁵.

Neither R's warning nor this refusal fires. The fit is answered with the large finite coefficient and standard error the paragraph says it prevents. The refusal fires mostly when an offset (a tiny exposure) makes μ small by design, which is a valid fit.

**Fix:**
- restate the rationale: the refusal is R's warning made a refusal, nothing more;
- mark the zero-group case as answered;
- name in criterion 3 the input that produces the refusal (for example a log offset of ln 10⁻¹⁶).

The first review's SF6 asked for exactly this case, so the fix it received does not do what it says.

### SF7. Under the logit, R warns from |η| > 30, not 33.7

Lines 533-536 say that under the logit, Bund2's refusal at |η| ≥ 36.04 comes "later than R's warning, which comes once μ is within 10ε, at about |η| ≥ 33.7". That is the exact logistic's threshold, ln(1/(10ε)). R's logit is not the exact logistic. Beyond |η| = 30 it sets μ to 1/(1 + ε) or ε/(1 + ε) (`family.c:83-84`), so 1 − μ or μ is about ε, within 10ε. R's warning (`glm.R:323-325`) therefore fires for every |η| > 30. Between 30 and 33.7 the exact logistic's distance from 0 or 1 is 9.4·10⁻¹⁴ to 2.2·10⁻¹⁵, outside 10ε, but R never computes it. The RFC cites this piecewise rule itself at lines 172-175.

**Fix:** "R's warning comes at |η| > 30, where its piecewise logit saturates (`family.c:83-84`)".

### SF8. K12 checks w·y but not the trials w, and R rounds a half to even

R's binomial AIC uses `dbinom(round(m*y), round(m), mu)` with m = w for a one-column response (`family.R:357-359`). K12 refuses a w·y more than 10⁻³ from an integer (line 475, line 696), but nothing checks w itself. Two rows show the gap:
- w = 2.5 and y = 0 passes, since w·y = 0;
- w = 2.5 and y = 0.4 passes, since w·y = 1.

The AIC is then a binomial density on round(2.5) trials, which is the "wrong AIC" K12 exists to prevent (line 916). The rounding also differs. R's `round` takes a half to the even digit, so `round(0.5)` is 0 and `round(2.5)` is 2 (`src/library/base/man/Round.Rd:57-59`). Rust's `f64::round` takes it away from zero, so 2.5 becomes 3. So "rounds m·y and m, as R's does" (line 552) cannot be implemented with `f64::round` and match R on such a w.

**Fix:** under the binomial, refuse a weight more than 10⁻³ from an integer, as K12 refuses w·y. Or state the rounding (`f64::round_ties_even`) and accept the AIC.

### SF9. A Poisson response may be non-integer, and its AIC is then R's +∞

The Poisson response is "≥ 0" (line 476). The AIC is "the family's AIC, R's formula" (line 573), which for the Poisson is `-2*sum(dpois(y, mu, log=TRUE)*wt)` (`family.R:165`). R's `dpois` answers its zero, so −∞ on the log scale, for any non-integer x, with a warning (`src/nmath/dpois.c:79`; `R_D_nonint_check`, `src/nmath/dpq.h:124-128`). So R's Poisson AIC on a non-integer response is +∞.

Under B12, Bund2 would refuse the whole fit with E114. Alternatively an implementer computing y ln μ − μ − lnΓ(y + 1) would answer a finite AIC that R does not. A Poisson fit to a rate or a non-integer abundance is common, with or without weights. The RFC says neither.

**Fix:** state one of these:
- a non-integer Poisson `y` is E101, as K12 does for the binomial;
- the fit is answered and its `aic` and `bic` left out;
- the continuous extension is used, marked **[author]**.

### SF10. A leverage of 1 makes 0/0, not ∞, and the test for it is not stated

§S5 refuses "an hᵢ of 1, which makes either infinite" with E114 (lines 603-604). At hᵢ = 1 the fit passes through yᵢ, so rᵢ = 0. The standardised residual and Cook's distance are then 0/0, not ∞; R turns only an infinite value into NaN (`lm.influence.R:172`, `:262`). In floating point, hᵢ is rarely exactly 1. A factor level with one row, which is common, gives hᵢ = 1 − δ with δ near ε. The outcome then depends on rounding: a finite but meaningless value, or ±∞, or NaN. The criterion that should see this has no case for it (criterion 4).

**Fix:**
- state the test as a tolerance, for example 1 − hᵢ ≤ 10⁻¹⁰, as the rank test uses 10⁻¹⁰;
- say whether `model.residuals` `"standardized"` refuses in the same way (line 597 defines it but §S5's refusal sentence names only `model.influence`);
- add a single-row level to criterion 4.

### SF11. `model.anova`'s first row and its `df` and `deviance` columns are not defined

§S7 lists the columns `term`, `df`, `deviance`, `resid.df`, `resid.deviance`, `statistic` and `p`, "the first row's last two NONE" (lines 666-669). The first row has no previous model, so its `df` and `deviance` (the differences) have no value either. R's first row has `NA` in all four (`glm.R:520-521`, `c(NA, …)`). As written, `df` and `deviance` must hold something in the first row, and the text does not say what. Criterion 6 checks "the stated frame" (line 858), and the frame is not stated.

**Fix:** "the first row's `df`, `deviance`, `statistic` and `p` NONE", and write the Dobson frame into criterion 6 as criterion 1 writes its matrix.

The writer lists every such native, promotable or `# not reached:` (`lib.rs:1106-1115`, `:1336-1342`), and the file has none of the three (`grep frame\\. tests/golden/PROMOTABLE.txt`). The comparison reads only entry lines, so the test still passes. Regenerating for M1–M5 will add them beside the model words. The Preservation row "no existing name line moves; its count line changes" (line 738) holds, but the diff will carry three names this RFC does not add. - **N4. E106's new suffix needs `Refusal` changed.** `Refusal::Singular` has no fields (`refuse.rs:37`, `:73`, and its text `"the matrix is singular"` at `:104`). Naming a column (line 698) needs a field or a variant, and every existing `Refusal::Singular` site must keep its text. `refuse.rs` belongs in the Preservation table, with its tests' texts unchanged.
- **N5. Separation refused under the other binomial links is an [author] choice not listed.** Line 531 refuses a μ within 10ε under probit, cloglog, cauchit and log, where R only warns. `fit.logistic`'s precedent covers the logit on 0/1 data only. The Poisson's analogue is in the [author] list (line 935); this one is not.
- **N6. A column of no kind.** §S2 refuses it in a term (line 356). D259's fifth choice accepted one in `frame.matrix` and `frame.aggregate`, and `frame_matrix` takes `Kind::Unknown` (`frame/summary.rs:602`). Here such a column cannot be fitted anyway: every cell is NONE, so `"refuse"` names a NONE and `"omit"` leaves no rows. Say which refusal comes first.
- **N7. Research 07 §3.2 says "a design MATRIX"** (`07-statistical-research.md:111`). §S2 answers a MAP holding one. That is a refinement for ERRATA beside K1's six narrowings.
- **N8. A quantile can be E110.** The intervals' q (line 630) comes from `dist.rs`'s `Family::quantile`, which RFC-0016 §S1 notes "can be E110 as any polish can". §S8 has no row for it.
- **N9. μ′ can underflow to 0.** Line 508 says μ′ is never 0 in the valid range. Under `inverse` μ′ = −1/η² (`family.R:120`), which is −0 for |η| above about 1.3·10¹⁵⁴; `1/mu^2` similarly. This is an edge, but the claim is absolute.
- **N10. `model.compare`'s F under a fixed dispersion.** If `test` is `"F"` on a binomial or Poisson pair, R's `anova.glmlist` takes the denominator df as ∞ (`glm.R:660-661`) and warns (`:670-675`). §S7 always takes `big`'s residual df (line 649). Either is defensible; say which, or refuse F there.

## What the RFC assumes and does not state

- **U1. The response as a term, weights or offset.** Nothing stops `response` from also naming a term, or `weights` or `offset` from naming the response or a term. R drops a response that appears on the right-hand side. Say whether each is E101.
- **U2. `model.predict`'s new frame may type a column differently.** For example a string column where the fit coded a factor, a factor whose `levels` list differs from the spec's, or a factor where the fit read a number. Only "a level the fit never saw" (line 612) is stated.
- **U3. The order of `model.matrix`'s refusals.** D259's first three choices fixed the order for `frame.join`, `frame.matrix` and `"refuse"`. §S2 states the NONE order only, and §S8 lists rows without an order. Criterion 1's "each row … with its exact text" depends on an input meeting one failure only.
- **U4. `model.anova`'s `family`.** It "takes `fit.glm`'s options" (line 657), where `family` is required (line 469). Whether `model.anova` requires it, or defaults to Gaussian, is not said.
- **U5. Refusals inside a refit.** The second review's U2 and U3 (a refusal while `model.anova` refits; the null refit's first step) are, by the RFC's own line 82, "not yet stated". They still are not. The same applies to separation and the Poisson-rate test in a null refit.
- **U6. The halving cap counts per step.** "At most 100 times" (line 499) does not say whether per step, as R's inner loops count (`glm.R:274-277`, `:293-296`), or in total, as `halvings` sums (line 576).
- **U7. Which of §S3's keys a GLM fit has.** "§S3's keys where they apply" (line 559) leaves `sigma`, `r2`, `adj.r2` and `f` open for a Gaussian `fit.glm`. `fit.lm`'s key table (lines 433-448) also omits `null.deviance` and `df.null`, which the paragraph at lines 457-461 defines.
- **U8. `model.compare` across words.** A `fit.lm` fit and a `fit.glm` fit have different key sets (`iterations`, `halvings`, `linear`, `working.weights`). The "malformed fit" check (line 709) must accept both shapes for every `model.` word that reads a fit.
- **U9. Nesting by name assumes the same frame.** Two fits with the same family, link, `y`, `weights`, `offset` and nested names can still come from different frames whose columns differ under one name. The check (lines 642-644) cannot see that, and a negative Δdeviance (B2) is one symptom.
- **U10. `conf` and `test` are read by the model words' own reader.** It must read `conf` as RFC-0016 §S1 does (a FLOAT; an INTEGER is E101), and `na` with `frame/opts.rs`'s texts (the second review's U4).

- SF1–SF4 and SF10 are cases where the design as written refuses or mis-codes a valid model. SF5 makes criterion 9's promise checkable. SF6–SF9 correct or complete claims about R.
- The notes and assumptions can go into the same revision.
