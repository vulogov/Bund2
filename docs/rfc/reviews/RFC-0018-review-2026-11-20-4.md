## Blocking

### B1. `model.predict` inherits §S2's step 7, so it refuses ordinary predictions

§S6 says "The order of refusals is §S2's, from step 5 on, with the response not read" (803-804). §S2's step 7 is "The rows kept: none left, then a factor term with one used level, then two columns of one name, then n ≤ k, then the cap" (484-485). Applied to a new frame, as written:
- **n ≤ k.** Predicting at fewer new rows than the fit has coefficients is refused. One new row against criterion 1's six-column design is "1 observations for 6 coefficients". That is the commonest use of the word.
- **One used level.** A new frame whose `site` cells are all `north` is refused as "has one used level". Yet §S6 codes "by the fit's `spec`" (785-788), not by the levels the new rows use.
- **Unused levels.** Step 7 sits on §S2's "Unused levels" rule (440-448). If the new frame's factor were re-coded from its own used levels, a prediction frame would get a different column set from the fit.

None of these is what §S6 means, and the [author] ruling D262 #21 covers column kinds only. Criterion 5 does not catch it, because the test chooses its own new frames. This came out of the second review's U2 fix: the order was added without a check of which steps apply.

**Fix:** state that `model.predict` applies step 5 (kinds as coded, D262 #21), step 6 (NONE under `"refuse"`, then the cells of `offset` and `weights`), and from step 7 only "none left" and the cap. The columns and levels are the spec's, whatever levels the new rows use. Add to criterion 5 a prediction at one new row of a factor design.

## Should fix

### SF1. Criterion 9's "`fit.rs`'s tests pass unchanged" fails if `fit.lm` and `fit.glm` live in a module of their own

`fit/tests.rs:76-82` collects every registered name that `starts_with("fit.")` and asserts `ours.len() == WORDS.len()`. `WORDS` is `fit.rs`'s three names, `fit.logistic`, `fit.logistic.` and `fit.nls` (`fit.rs:361`). `fit.lm`, `fit.lm.`, `fit.glm` and `fit.glm.` make that count 7 against 3.

§S9 asks for "a counting test per module, as RFC-0016's and RFC-0017's modules have" (953-954). Read that way, the model words get their own module and `WORDS`, and the unchanged test fails. The RFC never says where the fit words live. (`infer/tests.rs:79` counts `stat.lm*` only and is not affected.)

**Fix:** say that `fit.lm` and `fit.glm` join `fit.rs`'s `WORDS`, which makes the existing test their counting test. Or say that `fit/tests.rs`'s filter is narrowed, and change criterion 9's "unchanged" to name that one edit.

### SF2. A logical column is a factor in R, so "as R codes it" is wrong without an intercept

§S2 codes a logical as one column, 1.0 for `true` (417). It says the first factor term without an intercept is fully coded "as R codes it" (419), and "A logical column is not a factor" (490). R treats a logical as a two-level factor:
- `model.matrix.default` takes `is.factor(x) || is.logical(x)` as a factor (`models.R:652`);
- `model.c` gives a logical `nlevs` 2 (`src/library/stats/src/model.c:439-443`);
- without an intercept, the dummy coding goes to "the first factor found" with `nlevs > 1` (`model.c:461-475`), and that includes a logical.

So with `intercept` false:
- `[ "treated" ]` is one column here and two in R (`treatedFALSE`, `treatedTRUE`). That is a different model, with the `false` group's mean forced to 0.
- `[ "dose" "treated" ]` is likewise a smaller column space than R's.
- `[ "treated" "site" ]` spans the same space as R's but codes it differently. R dummy-codes `treated` and contrast-codes `site`, where §S2 fully codes `site`.

The RFC records none of this, and the "[K7]" ruling covers which kinds are coded, not this consequence.

**Fix:** state it as a difference from R, citing `models.R:652` and `model.c:439-443`, `:461-475`. Say that a logical is never fully coded, so that a no-intercept design with a logical and no factor fits the `false` group at 0. Alternatively, code a logical as a two-level factor where R does. That changes K7's coding and needs the owner.

### SF3. "The contrast columns lose nothing" is false for an interaction of three or more members

§S2 says that "Inside an interaction, a factor is always coded by its m − 1 contrast columns", and that "K6 has every member as an earlier term, so the contrast columns lose nothing" (424-431). K6 requires only the members, "not the two-way interactions among them" (407-409).

R's `TermCode` decides each factor's coding inside a term from the term's other members. It removes the factor and asks whether the remainder is contained in a single preceding term. If not, the factor is coded by dummies (`model.c:1715-1749`). For `[ "a" "b" "c" [ "a" "b" "c" ] ]` with factors a, b, c:
- removing `a` leaves {b, c}, which no single earlier term contains;
- so R dummy-codes each factor in the three-way term, and §S2 contrast-codes it.

The two designs span different spaces. The sentence holds for two members, which is criterion 1's case. K6's ruling stands; the claim about it does not.

**Fix:** restrict "lose nothing" to an interaction whose lower-order margins are all earlier terms. State that with only the members present, §S2's three-way columns differ from R's (`model.c:1715-1749`), as K6's option B describes. Also cite `model.c:461-475` for line 419's no-intercept rule.

### SF4. `model.anova`'s tests and its Gaussian rows are underdefined

§S7 gives `model.compare`'s F its denominator df ("`big`'s residual df", 835-836), and refuses `test` `"F"` under a fixed dispersion (847-851). For `model.anova` it says only that it "takes `fit.glm`'s options and `test`" and that "each row's test uses the full model's dispersion" (866-867, 890-891). It leaves three things open:
- **F's denominator df.** R's `anova.glm` uses the full model's residual df (`glm.R:548-551`), and the text does not say so.
- **`test` `"F"` on a Poisson or binomial `model.anova`.** Whether it is E101 as in `model.compare`. §S8's row (935) names no word.
- **Gaussian identity rows.** These are "fitted by `fit.lm`'s computation" (868-870), yet the text says "Every refusal of §S4 applies" (876). §S3's own refusals are not mentioned: a constant `y − offset` with an intercept, and a perfect fit. The first refuses the intercept-only row of any constant-`y` Gaussian design, which `fit.glm` would fit.

**Fix:** state each of the three, and add the F denominator to criterion 6's Gamma case.

### SF5. The 10⁻¹² agreement on AIC needs the logistic path's AIC to be its deviance plus 2k

§S4 gives the binomial logit on 0/1 data `fit.logistic`'s W, z and deviance (746-756). The AIC is still "the family's AIC, R's formula, plus 2k" (740), that is −2 Σ ln `dbinom`(y; 1, μ). For a 0 at a large η, ln(1 − μ) through μ loses digits. Recomputed against 50 digits, the relative error of −2 ln(1 − μ) is:
- 1.5·10⁻¹¹ at η = 15;
- 1.8·10⁻⁹ at η = 20.

The softplus form stays at 10⁻¹⁷. One such row puts the AIC outside criterion 3's relative 10⁻¹² against `fit.logistic`'s `dev + 2k` (`fit.rs:342`). On 0/1 data with unit weights, the two AICs are equal mathematically, since every saturated term is 0.

**Fix:** on that path, take `aic` = `deviance` + 2k, as `fit.logistic` does, and say so in the three-item list at 747-751.
