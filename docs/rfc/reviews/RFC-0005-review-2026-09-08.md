**What is right.** The reference citations that exist are accurate.
`execute.rs:26-30` really is the `PTR | STRING | CALL` arm handing the name to
`vm.call`. `multistackvm_inline.rs:41-67` really is `i_direct` falling through
from the VM's inline table to the stack layer's, and `:69-75` really is `i`
resolving aliases ahead of it. Every §3.x quotation from the feasibility study
is verbatim and in context — §3.2a's `free_memory(self)` and the three
consequences, §3.2b's guard-and-branch directive and the OSR corollary,
§3.2c's target list, §3.2e's pin instruction, §3.2g's return-value protocol,
§3.1's s390x tail-call note. The `=0.135.0` pins are in `Cargo.toml:50-54` for
all five crates; `bund2-jit` and `bund2-ir` are twelve lines each with no code.
D9, D10, D12, D16's openness, D20, D35, D39 and D40 are all read correctly.
The Q27 provenance ("the study's own open question 7") is exact
(`00-jit-feasibility.md:660-662`), and the roadmap's joint assignment of tier
pinning is exact too (`05-rfc-roadmap.md:243`).

The design's spine — pointer-keyed cache with a strong reference, every call
indirect, guard-and-branch with no bail, caps as correctness rather than
tuning — is the right shape and correctly derived from D35, D16 and §3.2a/b.

The problems below are concentrated in three places: §S1's arithmetic and what
it omits, §S4's under-read dispatch chain, and a direct contradiction with an
**Accepted** RFC.

## 1. Blocking

### B1 — §S5 contradicts RFC-0004 (Accepted) on what an `Opaque` site does

RFC-0005 §S5:

> `Opaque` means no static claim is available and the generic path is the only
> path.

RFC-0004 §S1, `docs/rfc/RFC-0004-stack-effects.md:262-265`, status
**Accepted (2026-09-07)**:

> **`Opaque` is not a failure of the design; it is the honest answer** […]
> RFC-0005 reads the same enum: a `Fold` or an `Opaque` site **bails to
> Tier 0**, which is exactly D12's accepted barrier.

These are different mechanisms with different consequences. "Generic path in
the compiled function" means the body still promotes and Tier 1 emits a
slow-but-compiled arm. "Bails to Tier 0" means the body does not compile at
all. RFC-0004 also states the saturation rule
(`RFC-0004-stack-effects.md:284`): *anything composed with `Opaque` is
`Opaque`*. Combine that with §S6's own admission —

> A body containing `!` has no static depth, so promotion stops there. `!` is
> among the five most-used words in the corpus.

— and RFC-0004's rule says any lambda anywhere downstream of `!` is
unpromotable. ERRATA puts `!` at 69 invocations across 39 of 132 programs,
**13 of them inside a lambda body**. Under RFC-0004's reading, a large
fraction of the corpus's compilable units are not compilable, which is a
first-order fact about what Tier 1 can deliver and the RFC never states it.

An RFC may not silently take the more favourable reading of an accepted RFC's
sentence about itself. Either RFC-0005 states the deviation and RFC-0004 gets
an amendment, or §S5 adopts "bails to Tier 0" and §S6's promotion analysis is
rewritten around it. Today it does neither, and CLAUDE.md's named failure mode
applies exactly: *a required deviation filed as a non-deviation, so no
criterion covers it*.

### B2 — §S4 stops one call short: the resolution chain has five tiers, not two

§S4 cites `execute.rs:26-30` for `vm.call` and then
`multistackvm_inline.rs:41-67` / `:69-75` for "the resolution chain behind it",
describing it as "the fall-through from the VM's inline table to the stack
layer's, with alias resolution ahead of both".

`vm.call` does not reach `i()` directly. `multistackvm_call.rs:7-9` is two
lines and delegates:

```rust
pub fn call(&mut self, name: String) -> Result<&mut VM, Error> {
    self.apply(Value::call(name.clone(), Vec::new()))
}
```

`multistackvm_apply.rs:16-60` is the actual chain, and **D16 already declares
its order contract** (`docs/registers/decisions.md`, D16 point 3):

> **The resolution chain is observable in full and its order is contract**:
> command, then `$`-forced internal, then alias, then lambda, then inline
> (`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_apply.rs:16-60`)

Read in this session, `apply` does, in order: `is_command` → `c()` (`:16-17`);
the **`autoadd`** branch, which does not call at all but pushes the CALL onto a
pulled value (`:19-27`); the `$` bypass to `call_internal_word` (`:33-35`);
alias resolution (`:39-42`); the lambda table via `lambda_eval` (`:46-54`); and
only then `i()` (`:59`).

Three of those are load-bearing for this RFC and none appears in it:

- **The lambda tier sits ahead of the inline tier.** This is where every
  compiled body is *reached from*. §S4's "every inter-word call loads a pointer
  from a runtime-owned slot" is a claim about a slot table that must model a
  lambda hit, a command hit and an inline hit as one dispatch, and the RFC
  never says so.
- **`autoadd` changes what a CALL *means*.** In autoadd mode a name is not
  called; it is appended to a value. A compiled body that assumes a call site
  is a call is wrong under `:` / `;`. The study raised this as its own open
  question 4 ("Is `autoadd` ever toggled dynamically?"). The RFC has no row for
  it in Preservation and no open question.
- **The `$` bypass** skips the lambda check (F26, ERRATA), so `$name` can
  never reach compiled code for a lambda of that name — a guard the RFC's
  indirect-slot design has to encode.

§S4 also double-resolves aliases without noticing: `apply` resolves at `:39-42`
and `i()` resolves again at `:71-75`. Harmless, but it is the tell that the
chain was read from `i()` outward rather than from `call` inward.

This is the second of CLAUDE.md's two named recurring failures. The RFC read
`execute.rs` and `multistackvm_inline.rs` and asserted what happens between
them; `multistackvm_apply.rs` is the file in between and it is not cited.

### B3 — the two citations §S1's entire argument rests on are stale, and `cite` cannot see them

§S1:

> `Vm::push` does not store the value it is handed, it stores
> `v.with_tag("stack", name)` (`crates/bund2-interp/src/lib.rs`), and
> `with_tag` (`crates/bund2-value/src/lib.rs`) boxes the scalar …

Opened in this session:

- `crates/bund2-interp/src/lib.rs` is `pub fn from_rc(name: Rc<str>) -> Self`
  inside `impl Stack` — a constructor that interns the stack name. It is not a
  push and there is no `with_tag` on it. There is no `Vm::push` in the file at
  all: the pushes are `Stack::push` at `:125`, `Stack::push_as` at `:137`, and
  the tag write is `:147` — and it is now **conditional**,
  `BundValue::Heap(_) => v.with_tag(…)`, because D41 moved the scalar case into
  an interned `StackSym`.
- `crates/bund2-value/src/lib.rs` is inside `mod tests`, in
  `rendering_does_not_mint_an_identity`. `with_tag` is at `:2018`.

Both are pre-D41 line numbers that moved when D41 landed **the same day**. The
RFC's §S1 update announces D41 in the section immediately below and leaves the
citations that D41 invalidated standing above it.

Worse, this is not caught by anything:

- **Criterion 8 names a tool that cannot decide it.** `cargo xtask cite`
  extracts only paths beginning with the literal `reference/`
  (`xtask/src/cite/mod.rs`, `let pat: Vec<char> = "reference/".chars()…`).
  A `crates/…:N` citation is invisible to it. The run I did reports 0 defects
  over 1782 citations *with both of these wrong*. Criterion 8's wording —
  "`cargo xtask cite` resolving every `path:line` in this document" — is
  therefore false for precisely the two citations that are broken.
- **The same stale pair has propagated to two other places**:
  `docs/registers/open-questions.md:13` (Q25's row) and
  `crates/bund2-bench/benches/interpret.rs` (the `value` group's doc
  comment) both repeat both citations verbatim, sourced from this RFC. Fixing
  the RFC alone leaves two copies.

### B4 — §S12 does not exist

Cited twice as normative:

- §S3: "D35 records that this is 'an invariant to test, not merely to
  document'. **§S12 makes it a criterion.**"
- Preservation table, row 1: "strong `Rc` in the cache (D35); tested, **§S12
  criterion 3**".

The RFC's sections are S1–S11 followed by unnumbered *Preservation analysis*,
*Alternatives considered*, *Acceptance criteria*, *Open questions*. The
intended referent is acceptance criterion 3, which does exist and does say the
right thing. But D35's one explicit demand on this RFC — "an invariant to
test" — is discharged by a pointer to a section that is not there, in both
places it is discharged.

### B5 — §S7's counter is unspecified, and it is where the false-hit hazard actually lives

§S7 sets a promotion threshold of "64 evaluations of one body". A counter
keyed per body needs a key, and the RFC never says what it is. Both answers
break something the RFC claims:

- **If the counter keys on the `Rc` pointer without holding a strong
  reference**, it has exactly the hazard §S3 identifies for the cache: a body
  is freed at count 40, the allocator reuses the address, and an unrelated cold
  body inherits 40 evaluations. That is not a false *hit* — no wrong code runs
  — but it defeats the threshold, and the threshold is load-bearing for D3
  (§S7's own column: "eval'd bodies are fresh `Rc`s per call, so their count
  never reaches 1"). Under address reuse a REPL's successive eval bodies can
  land on the same address and accumulate.
- **If the counter holds a strong reference**, then §S7's last row —
  "the cache pins ≤1024 bodies alive" — is false. The *cache* pins 1024; the
  *counter table* pins every body ever counted, unbounded, which is precisely
  the heap consequence D35 asked this RFC to state once for both.

D35's requirement is quoted correctly and then applied to only half the
structure that keys on pointers. Criterion 3 tests the cache and not the
counter.

### B6 — §S1's ratio arithmetic does not hold, in either table

The baseline table gives an average word at 100–120 ns and
`value/push_pull/balanced` at **126.9 ns**, then concludes `with_tag` is
"roughly 60–75% of an average word". But a balanced push/pull round trip
(126.9) is *more expensive than an entire average word* (112 ns for
`native_call`, which itself performs pushes and pulls). One of the two numbers
cannot mean what the section says it means — the benchmarks are not on a
common basis (`push_pull` times a `Vm` round trip directly; `dispatch/*` times
`Interp::eval` over a stream and divides by word count), and the RFC divides
one into the other anyway. The 25%-dispatch figure, and therefore the "Amdahl
bounds a perfect Tier 1 at about 1.3×" conclusion, both descend from that
division.

The update repeats the shape. "The value layer is now ~10 ns of a ~25 ns word"
takes `push_pull/balanced` = 10.1 against `literal_push` = 24.5 ns/word. But
`literal_push/w2000` is `1 drop` repeated — **two** words per push/pull pair
(`crates/bund2-bench/benches/interpret.rs`, "2000 words"). Per *word* the
value layer is ~5 ns of 24.5, not ~10 of ~25. The conclusion (dispatch is now
dominant) probably survives; the arithmetic offered for it does not.

## 2. Significant

### S1 — the one benchmark that isolates dispatch is never reported

`crates/bund2-bench/benches/interpret.rs` defines
`dispatch/nop_nl/w1000`, with this comment at `:114-115`:

> 1000 words, no operands at all: the cheapest word in the language, isolating
> dispatch from anything the word then does.

That is the direct measurement of the quantity §S1 exists to decide. It is
absent from both of §S1's tables. Instead the section infers dispatch **by
subtraction** — word cost minus value-layer cost — which silently attributes
the word's own work (`+` really does have to add) to dispatch. The gate's
second half, "dispatch has become the bottleneck", is the conclusion of that
subtraction and is never checked against the benchmark built to check it.

The update table also drops `dispatch/dup_drop`, which D41 records at
**44.9 ns/word** — nearly double the two rows the RFC does quote. Selecting the
two cheapest rows to establish "a ~25 ns word" is not a neutral summary.

### S2 — §S1's pre-D41 conclusions are left standing above the update that reverses them

Lines 118–140 still read as present tense: "Dispatch is at most a quarter of
the time", "**Amdahl bounds a perfect Tier 1 at about 1.3× on this
workload**", "**So the gate's answer is: not yet**", and a numbered
prerequisite list. The update at line 142 says the opposite. A reader who
stops at the bold "not yet" — and it is bolded, while the update's heading is
not — gets the wrong answer.

Concretely, the update never restates the number that the whole section is
about: **what does Amdahl bound Tier 1 at now?** The header still says "the
question changes from 'should this be built' to … whether the design is
right", but the expected payoff is the reason to build it and the RFC's only
computed payoff is the superseded 1.3×.

### S3 — the header's status line is self-contradictory

Lines 3–11 say, in order: "it does not pass"; then "D41 fixed that"; then "The
gate is met and §S2-§S11 are live". A status field cannot assert both. Compare
the *Summary*, which ends "That is the design. The measurement in §S1 says it
should not be built yet" — the pre-D41 verdict, unamended, three lines after
the header says the gate is met.

### S4 — the Open questions section is stale against D41 and Q29

Q25 is listed as open, with "§S1 requires `value/push_pull/balanced` under
20 ns and deliberately does not say how" and "this RFC is blocked on it in the
sense that its own premise is". `docs/registers/open-questions.md:17` records
**Q29 as resolved by D41 and implemented**, and D41's own "Implemented,
2026-09-08" block says the gate is met. The register's Q25 row already carries
"Option 1 done … residue is Q29". The RFC's Q25 entry, and its claim of being
blocked, are both superseded by a decision the RFC itself cites two sections
earlier. Q29/D41 is not listed at all as the resolution.

### S5 — ERRATA now disagrees with the RFC, and no new line records the reversal

`docs/research/ERRATA.md`'s last entry supersedes §2.1's table with RFC-0005
§S1 and ends:

> §2.2's gate — "Project B is worth doing only if …" — **does not pass**.
> Boxing is the bottleneck; dispatch is not.

That is now false, by D41 and by this RFC's own update. CLAUDE.md makes
`docs/research/` immutable and ERRATA the place a contradiction is recorded;
an ERRATA entry that has itself been overtaken needs a further line, not a
silent divergence. The RFC's header claims the supersession is "Recorded in
`docs/research/ERRATA.md`" — the recording exists but says the opposite of
what the RFC now concludes.

### S6 — §S6's claim about the study's table is wrong as written

> §2.1's only row promising more than ~1.5× is "stack-slot promotion + type
> guards — ~2–5×".

`00-jit-feasibility.md:249-253` has four other rows: value representation
rework ("large multiple"), symbol interning + slot table ("large multiple"),
and IR + threaded interpreter ("~2×"). The intended claim is "the only row
*that needs Cranelift*", which the table's last column supports. As written it
is false, and it is the premise of §S6's opening sentence.

### S7 — D12's barrier is asserted, never designed, and collides with §S5's vocabulary

§S6 says the `*` fold family "still has to be *represented*, because D16 means
one may appear at run time". D12 (`decisions.md:495`) says what representation
was accepted: "**A site using a fold bails to Tier 0**; nothing else changes."
RFC-0004 repeats it. But under §S4 *every inter-word call is indirect through a
slot* — so a compiled body cannot know at compile time whether a computed name
will resolve to `**`. "Bails to Tier 0" is not implementable for a computed
call site, and §S5 has separately forbidden the word: "Guard and branch […]
**Never guard and bail out**."

The RFC uses "bail" in two senses (a compile-time refusal to promote; a runtime
deoptimisation) without ever distinguishing them, and never says what a
compiled body actually does when an indirect call lands on a `Fold` at run
time. The Preservation row "a `*`-family word crossing a promoted region → D12's
barrier, represented not assumed" names the guard as the thing that is missing.

### S8 — §3.2d and §3.2f are not addressed, and §3.2f contradicts §S8

The RFC works through §3.2a, b, c, e and g. Two are skipped:

- **§3.2d, relocation range.** "Calls use 32-bit relocations (±2GB) […] use
  [`ArenaMemoryProvider`], and route runtime-helper calls through an
  indirection table so helper addresses are never a relocation-range problem."
  §S7's 1024-function cap is justified solely by `free_memory` being
  all-or-nothing; the ±2GB arena is a second, independent bound on the same
  knob and it is not mentioned.
- **§3.2f, the ArrayCall gap.** "the workarounds — passing `argc: usize,
  argv: *mut Value` — **defeat tail calls** and force everything onto the
  stack. Bund's design should not depend on this landing." §S8 is a whole
  section premised on `return_call` being usable and §S6 is premised on values
  living in `Variable`s rather than on the stack. §3.2f says the calling
  convention a dynamically typed language ends up with takes both away. The
  RFC does not reconcile them or even acknowledge the tension.

### S9 — the loop-body argument omits that the loop driver stays interpreted

§S5: "rely on loop bodies being separately compilable lambdas — which in Bund
they are, because `times`, `loop`, `map` and `while` all take a LAMBDA
operand."

The operand claim is true and I verified all four
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/logic/times_fun.rs:13`,
`loop_fun.rs:12`, `map_fun.rs:12`, `while_fun.rs:11`) — but it carries no
citation, and reading one level further changes the conclusion.
`times_fun.rs:17-21` is the driver:

```rust
for v in 0..n {
    vm.stack.push(Value::from_int(v));
    match vm.lambda_eval(lambda_val.clone()) {
```

The loop **is a native**. Every iteration returns to the runtime, pushes the
index, clones the lambda value, and re-enters through `lambda_eval`. So Tier 1
compiles the body and not the trip, and the per-iteration overhead — a native
dispatch plus `lambda_eval` — is exactly what a JIT would normally remove and
here cannot. For the workload §S6 is about (`1 2 +` inside `{ } times`) that
overhead may dominate the compiled body. The RFC presents loop bodies as the
thing that rescues the no-OSR constraint without noting what the constraint
still costs.

One thing that happens to work and should be said: `lambda_val.clone()` on a
`BundValue::Heap` shares the payload `Rc` — `Clone for BundValue` is
`crates/bund2-value/src/lib.rs`, `payload: Rc::clone(&self.payload)`
at `:1947` — so the pointer key is stable across iterations. That is the same
check D35 ran for `dup` (`:1102-1114`), and it is what makes a `times` body
promotable at all. It is unstated.

### S10 — the `jit` feature's default is never stated, and it decides §S10's own argument

§S10 requires Tier 1 to be "a `cargo` feature that compiles out cleanly,
exactly as `string.grok` is (D40)". D40's resolution is specific: grok is
`optional = true`, behind a feature, **off by default**
(`decisions.md`, D40, "the default authorises…"). The RFC never says whether
`jit` is on or off by default. It matters: on by default, the *default build*
is restricted to Cranelift's four targets, which destroys §S10's own
portability claim; off by default, criterion 2's "same N/M with the feature on
and off" is a two-configuration CI matrix nobody has scoped.

## 4. Preservation analysis — what the table misses

The table's six rows are all real and correctly guarded. The section's opening
— "Tier 1 preserves everything by construction, because it adds no word and
changes no word's behaviour" — is the sentence that lets the rest be thin.
Behaviours the design could change that have no row:

1. **`autoadd`** (B2). Under `:` / `;` a CALL is appended, not called. A
   compiled call site that assumes a call is a preservation failure, not a
   speed difference.
2. **The `$` bypass** (F26). `$name` skips the lambda tier. Compiled code
   reached through the lambda tier must not be reachable through `$`.
3. **Resolution *order*.** D16 point 3 makes command → `$` → alias → lambda →
   inline **contract**. Compiled dispatch reproduces an order, and no row says
   which.
4. **Error text.** `bail!` concatenates context at every Rust level it passes
   through (`multistackvm_lambda_eval.rs:17`, `execute.rs:45`,
   `bundcore_eval.rs:33`), and open-questions Q21 (line 1074) records that
   RFC-0003's frame loop already changes that nesting. A compiled frame changes
   it again — different frames, different `context` string, and goldens capture
   error text. §S11 says errors stay a return-value protocol; it does not say
   the *text* is unchanged.
5. **Stack-effect guards firing where the interpreter would not.** §S5 orders
   guards by declared effect, and RFC-0004 §S2 (F18) records that fourteen
   words' *declared* guards disagree with their probed arity and that the
   declared guard **loses**. A compiled arity guard built from the declared
   effect would reintroduce exactly the error F18 decided against.
6. **The stack tag on a promoted value.** D41 rule 2 is "the one rule whose
   violation is silent": boxing a tagged scalar must carry the `StackSym` into
   the header map. Compiled code that boxes — anything that materialises a
   value, which §S6's promotion does at every materialisation point — has to
   honour it. Nothing in the RFC mentions D41's rule 2, and the failure mode is
   a golden printing `tags: {}`.
7. **Timing and ordering of `stamp`.** D2's sampled `.timestamp` and D20's
   materialisation points: a compiled body that elides an intermediate value
   elides its stamp sample. D20 is listed in the header as consumed and never
   used in the body — this is the row it would have produced.
8. **`Vm::report` delivery** (D36). §S11 keeps `Result`; it does not say a
   compiled body still routes `Warning`/`Notice` through the reporter, or that
   a stack snapshot taken inside a promoted body sees the same stack the
   interpreter would (promotion to `Variable`s means intermediate values are
   not *on* the stack).
9. **Threading.** The study's open question 6 notes `JITModule` is `Send` but
   not `Sync`; D41's interner is thread-local; pointer keys are `Rc`. The
   roadmap assigns a "shared JIT compile service" to RFC-7 (`:242`). §S9
   engages RFC-0007 for pinning and not for this.

## 6. Consistency with the registers

- **D3.** §S3's paraphrase ("eval'd code is structurally unable to hit the
  cache") matches D3's **amendment** (2026-08-29) and not D3's headline status
  line, which still reads "Eligibility falls out of the **content-hash**
  compiled cache". Citing "D3" bare points at a status line the amendment
  reversed. More substantively, §S3 states the blanket "Eval'd code does not hit
  the cache" and drops D3's explicit carve-out: *a snippet that calls
  `register` installs a named lambda and "is JIT-eligible by the normal word
  path whatever this decision says"*. Under §S7's threshold that lambda is a
  stable `Rc` and promotes normally — correct behaviour, but the RFC's sentence
  denies it.
- **D33.** §S6 says "This RFC does not take D33's default". D33 records no
  `Default:` field; it records a **Recommendation** (option 2) and a "what is
  implemented meanwhile" (option 1). What §S6 actually does — require the
  generic path while D33 stands, and name option 2 as what would unlock the
  lowering — is right and well-judged. The wording should match the entry.
- **D5, D20.** Both declared "consumed" in the header and used nowhere in the
  body. D5's substance (write-once ⇒ no invalidation) is the reason §S3 needs
  no invalidation section and is never said. D20's substance is the
  materialisation-point rule that §S6's promotion has to respect and never
  mentions (Preservation item 7).
- **Q22 is ambiguous as cited.** `docs/registers/open-questions.md` contains
  **two** rows numbered Q22 — line 46 ("resolved — three answered, one
  unprobeable, one defect", about probes) and line 1075 (the compiled-cache
  threshold, which is the one §S7 answers). Same for Q21 (lines 47 and 1074).
  A bare "Q22" does not resolve. This is a register defect rather than the
  RFC's, but §S7 is the entry that has to live with it.
- **`value/with_tag/scalar`** (§S1's table) is named
  `with_tag/scalar_unique` in the source (`interpret.rs:137`). A reader running
  the filter gets nothing.

## 7. What the RFC assumes and does not state

1. That the `jit` feature is **off by default** (S10).
2. That **promotion takes effect at the next call**, not mid-execution — the
   no-OSR constraint makes this mandatory and §S5 only says "do not enter
   compiled code mid-loop".
3. That **the promotion counter is not the cache** and has its own lifetime
   rules (B5).
4. That **`clone`ing a lambda value preserves the payload pointer**, which is
   what makes any `times`/`loop` body promotable (S9).
5. That **one `JITModule` serves one `Interp`** — nothing says whether the
   cache is per-VM or process-global, and `Rc` keys plus D41's thread-local
   interner force per-thread whether or not the RFC intends it.
6. That **the 1024 cap is on live entries, with no eviction** — §S7 says
   "cap", the demotion row is about the *recompile* cap, and nothing says what
   happens to body 1025. "Leaves the fifth interpreted" (criterion 6) implies
   no eviction; §S7 does not say it.
7. That **compiled code and interpreted code share one `Stack`**, so a
   promoted body's `Variable`s must be spilled before any word that inspects
   the stack (`.s`, `debug.display_stack`, a `Diagnostic` snapshot, or any
   `Opaque` call) — the materialisation discipline §S6 needs and never defines.
8. That **guards derive from `StackEffect` as declared**, and that F18's
   fourteen declared-vs-probed disagreements do not make those guards wrong
   (Preservation item 5).
9. That **`bund2 build` (RFC-0006) reuses this lowering**. §S10 invokes D10 for
   `--emit=native` and §S4's "no direct relocation to a `FuncId`" is a JIT
   constraint that AOT does not share; whether AOT inherits the indirection is
   unstated and is RFC-0006's premise.
10. That **`Intrinsic`** — named in the D9 line and in §S10 — is a thing
    RFC-0002 defines. It is never introduced here, and §S10's "D9 applies" is
    the only place a reader learns Tier 1 has intrinsics at all.

## 8. Recommendation

**Revise before Proposed.** The design is sound and the reference reading that
exists is accurate; the defects are concentrated and fixable.

Blocking, in order:

1. **B1** — reconcile with RFC-0004 on `Opaque`, and state the consequence for
   how much of the corpus is promotable. This is the one that changes what the
   RFC claims Tier 1 can do.
2. **B2** — read `multistackvm_apply.rs:16-60` and rewrite §S4's chain around
   it; add `autoadd`, the `$` bypass and the lambda tier to Preservation.
3. **B3** — fix both `crates/…` citations, and fix the two copies in
   `open-questions.md:13` and `interpret.rs:124-128`. Reword criterion 8 to
   name what `cite` actually checks, or extend `cite`.
4. **B4** — replace both `§S12` references with "acceptance criterion 3".
5. **B5** — specify the counter's key and lifetime; extend criterion 3 to it.
6. **B6/S1/S2** — rebuild §S1 on a common measurement basis, report `nop_nl`
   and `dup_drop`, restate the post-D41 Amdahl bound, and fold the update into
   the section instead of appending it under a superseded conclusion.

Then: S4 (drop the stale Q25 framing, add Q29/D41), S5 (an ERRATA line for the
gate reversal), S3 (one status verdict, and fix the Summary's last line), S6,
S7, S8, S9, S10, and the criteria marked "no tool" in §3.

The RFC's best instinct is the one in its own §S1 — that a gate should be a
number and not a paragraph. Criteria 4, 6 and 7 have not been held to it.
