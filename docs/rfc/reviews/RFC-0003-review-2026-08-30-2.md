## 1. Blocking

### B1 — criterion 3's dump mechanism is non-deterministic for exactly the construct D2 changes

Criterion 3 says `"<source>" compile debug.display_stack` renders the
reference's stream "in the same `Value { … }` text the normaliser already
handles". The first half is true — I ran it and it works:

    "1 2 + { 3 } ( 4 ) :a `b @m" compile debug.display_stack

renders one `Value { … }` per token, nested lambdas included, on a single
unwrapped line.

The second half is false. The normaliser rewrites two things and only two:
`id: "…"` and `stamp: …` (`xtask/src/golden/mod.rs:164-202`, and its own tests
at `:749-763`). `Value::context()` mints its payload with `nanoid!()` and puts
it in `data`, not in `id` (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/create_special.rs:22-33`,
the `data: Val::String(nanoid!())` at `:28`). Measured, two runs of
`"( 4 )" compile debug.display_stack`:

    dt: 21, q: 100.0, data: String("hYxj_rCxmWSb0I6zVS_HX")
    dt: 21, q: 100.0, data: String("A2Kuhr25-lKmOwZlFfwv8")

After normalisation those two lines still differ. So criterion 3 fails on any
program containing `( … )` — which is the one construct D2 and D34 change, and
therefore the one the criterion most needs to decide. It also fails
oracle-against-oracle, before Bund2 exists.

Three routes, all cheap, none stated: extend the normaliser with a rule for an
anonymous CONTEXT payload; state that the parity comparison masks `data` for
`dt: 21` values whose payload is not a source-derived name; or exclude `( … )`
programs from criterion 3 and let criterion 5's D34 clause carry them. Pick
one in the RFC — the normaliser is Bund2's, so extending it is a change this
RFC owns.

### B2 — criterion 3 and D2 contradict each other, and the RFC does not carve out the deviation

Criterion 3 says: "render the reference's stream that way, render Bund2's the
same way, and compare the **normalised text**." Three constraints follow and
"must be honoured rather than discovered" — the EXIT drop, quote escaping, F21.

A fourth is missing and it is the largest. D2 is a *deliberate deviation*: a
`( … )` nested in a block lowers in place instead of hoisting. So for any
program with a nested `( … )` the two streams **must** differ, by design and by
D34's approval. Criterion 3 as written turns D34's approved deviation into a
criterion failure.

The corpus makes this cheap — exactly one of the 132 programs uses `( … )` and
it is top level
(`reference/Bund/examples/bund_dynamic_demos/create_lambda_on_the_fly_in_the_context.bund:10,20`,
re-confirmed) — but "no corpus program trips it" is not the same as "the
criterion states its exception". Criterion 3 is the criterion that decides D1's
grammar claim; it cannot also silently decide D2's.

### B3 — D3's identity key is a new materialisation point for lazy identity, and `dup` makes a body permanently uncacheable

D3 keys the compiled cache on "the body's **identity**". RFC-0001's D1 and D2
make `id` and `stamp` **lazily** generated, and decisions-D20 enumerates where
laziness ends: "serialisation is a materialisation point … confined to
operations that genuinely persist: `save.lambdas`/`save.stacks`/`save.aliases`,
`compile`, and `wrap`" (`docs/registers/decisions.md:834-862`).

Executing a lambda is not on that list, and under D3 it becomes one. Every body
that reaches the promotion threshold must have an `id` to be keyed by, so
Tier-0 execution materialises identity on a value that may never be serialised.
That is a change to D20's enumeration and it belongs stated against RFC-0001,
the way the RFC already states the `bund2-ir` redraw against RFC-0000.

The sharper half is `dup`. D20 continues: "Bund2 implements `dup` as a
structural clone plus fresh identity — F13", on a word reached by 38 of 132
programs. A `dup`'d lambda therefore carries an identity the cache has never
seen, and can never see, because every `dup` mints another. `dup ... !` is
uncacheable in principle. That may be entirely acceptable — but it is the
observable consequence of choosing identity over content, and D3 spends four
paragraphs on why content hashing is not adopted without naming it.

D3 also does not say what the key is when identity is **unset**. Under lazy
identity that is the normal state of a freshly parsed body.

### B4 — criteria 1 and 4 interfere: criterion 4's probes enter criterion 1's denominator and its 42

Criterion 1 requires `conform --parse-only` to report "how many of the 69
goldens' sources parse without error", rising to "**at least 42**, the count of
goldens using `{` or `}` outside string literals."

I re-measured and both numbers are right — 42 code-brace, 49 anywhere, 44 under
a naive line-based strip. But the 42 splits **37 suite programs + 5 probes**,
and the 69 splits 57 + 12 (`tests/golden/HERMETIC.txt` and `tests/probes/`,
enumerated together by `capture_jobs`, `xtask/src/golden/mod.rs:76-99`).

Criterion 4 then requires five new probes under `tests/probes/` per D21, of
which **two carry braces and deliberately do not parse** — `{}` and
`{ 1 println}`. Landing criterion 4 therefore:

- raises criterion 1's denominator from 69 to ~74;
- raises the brace-carrying count from 42 to 44;
- adds two sources that can never appear in the parse-only numerator.

So criterion 1 is either measured against a stale 42/69 or becomes
unsatisfiable at 44. Pin the measure to a named set — "the 57 suite programs of
`HERMETIC.txt` as of this RFC" is the honest denominator, since the probes are
ours and self-set. Note also that a criterion whose target is partly composed
of files we author is a criterion we can satisfy by writing files.

While there: the parenthetical explains the 42/49 gap but not the *measure*.
The measure is "outside string literals, **with string state tracked across
line boundaries**"; without the cross-line half the answer is 44, because two
of the seven `format`-placeholder goldens hold their braces in multi-line
strings. This was §5.1 of the previous review and is still only implicit.

## 3. A notation collision that has already caused one blocker

**N1 — the design sections D1–D8 collide with decisions.md D1–D8, and the RFC
uses both namespaces in the same sentences.**

The register holds D1 `.id` format, D2 `.timestamp` precision, D3 tier policy,
D4 integer width, D5 lambda body mutability, D7 concurrent VM count, D8
external word packages. This RFC's design sections are D1 grammar, D2 scoped
blocks, D3 BundIR, D4 frame loop, D5 errors carry position, D7 conditional
registry, D8 inline caches. Every one collides.

They are then mixed within a section:

- RFC:376, inside design-D3: "keyed on the body's **identity**, as D5 resolved"
  — register D5, while design D5 is "Errors carry position".
- RFC:384, inside design-D3: "Every `BundValue` carries a lazily minted `id`
  and a sampled `stamp` (D1, D2)" — register D1 and D2, while design D1 is the
  grammar and design D2 is scoped blocks.
- RFC:211: "what makes D7's 'populated at construction' non-trivial" — this
  RFC's D7, appearing 300 lines before it.
- D4a and D4b read as amendments to register D4 (integer width) on first
  encounter.

The first review's B1 was precisely a reader failing to tell two D-labels
apart. Rename the design sections — §1…§8, or S1…S8 — or prefix every register
reference as `decisions-D5`, which the RFC already does once ("decisions-D3's
Status line", in the previous review's language) and nowhere else.

## 4. Preservation analysis — behaviours it does not enumerate

**P1 — `atom` has its own character class, and it is strictly narrower than
`name`. `:my-word` is a parse error.** New.

RFC:97-99 says `element` admits seventeen punctuation characters "which is why
`←`, `Σ` and `$` are ordinary name characters. `:` and `@` are **not** in
`element`, which is what separates `atom` and `stack` from `name`."

The separator is not the sigil. `atom` is `":" ~ aelement+` where `aelement` is
`ASCII_ALPHANUMERIC | LETTER | "." | "_"` (`bund.pest:26,38`) — no `-`, `+`,
`?`, `!`, `/`, `*`, `|`, `&`, `#`, `%`, `<`, `>`, `=`, `^`, `,`. Confirmed on
the oracle:

    :foo { "hi" println } register  foo    ->  hi
    :my-word { "hi" println } register     ->  parse error at 1:1
    :foobar println                        ->  foobar
    :foo-bar println                       ->  parse error at 1:1

So `foo-bar` is a legal word name and `:foo-bar` cannot be written, which means
the `:name { … } register` idiom — §7's own first example — cannot name a
kebab-case word. The preservation row "`atom` → STRING … preserved exactly"
hides a rule a re-implementation reusing the name lexer for atom bodies would
silently widen, and widening it would newly accept programs the reference
rejects. That is the same class of error D1 spends a page avoiding for the
trailing-whitespace rule, and this one has a corpus-facing consequence the
whitespace rule does not.

**P2 — `stack` is `"@" ~ LETTER+`, so `@s1` is a parse error.** Repeat of the
previous review's P4, still unstated. `bund.pest:27`; confirmed, failing at
1:1, while `@s` works. No digits, no `_`, no punctuation — a third distinct
character class, narrower than both `element` and `aelement`.

**P3 — F49 has a twin on floats.** Repeat of the previous review's P3, still
unstated. `float` shares `digits` (`bund.pest:23` against `:49`), so `1.0_5`
parses and fails conversion the same way. Confirmed:

    1_000 println   ->  Error converting INT to VALUE: lexical parse error at index 1
    1.0_5 println   ->  Error converting FLOAT to VALUE: lexical parse error at index 3

D1's trap list and the preservation row name `1_000` only. A port that fixes
`vm/integer.rs` alone leaves `vm/float.rs` behind.

**P4 — `endcontext` carries a value out to the workbench, conditionally, and
D4 does not say the context frame's exit action does that.** New.

D4a says "a context is a **frame with an exit action**". The exit action's
content is never stated, and `endcontext` does three things, not one
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/ctx.rs:5-27`): if the current stack is
non-empty (`:9`) it pulls the top value and pushes it to the **workbench**
(`:10-16`); then it drops the stack (`:18`); then it pops `stacks_stack`
(`:20`). Confirmed on the oracle:

    ( 7 ) take println          ->  7
    ( 1 drop ) debug.display_workbench  ->  empty

That carry-out is the *only* way a value leaves a `( … )`, and it is the whole
mechanism behind the corpus's one use of the form. It has no preservation row.
D4a's guard fix (F60) is specified against `endcontext`'s message; its
value-moving behaviour is specified nowhere, and D34 now routes every `( … )`
through it.

**P5 — `execute.`'s error prefix, and what "proceeds exactly as `execute`"
does to it.** New.

`err_prefix` is threaded through the recursion alongside `op`
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/execute.rs:42,67`) and is `"EXECUTE."`
for the workbench wrapper, `"EXECUTE"` for the main-stack one (`:113,120`). It
reaches the user: `EXECUTE. returned error during unfolding the list: …`
(`:51`), `EXECUTE. can not obtain key for DICT execute` (`:59`),
`EXECUTE. returns: NO DATA` (`:100`).

D4b says `execute.` "**proceeds exactly as `execute`**: the recursion passes
`FromStack`". Read literally that swaps the prefix too, and every nested error
message from `!.` changes text. Read as intended it means only the `op`
argument changes. One clause settles it; the preservation row "`execute`'s
eight arms and their errors | preserved exactly" is the row it collides with.

**P6 — `execute.` on an OBJECT changes which error a program sees.** Repeat of
the previous review's P5, still unstated. With the `:117` guard corrected,
`execute.` with an empty main stack reaches `execute_object`, whose own guard
bails `Stack is too shallow for inline EXECUTE.OBJECT`
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/bund_execute/execute_object.rs:8-10`),
where today the wrapper rejects it earlier with `Stack is too shallow for
inline execute()` (`:118`). D4b's "two arms, not four" answers the `op`
question, correctly; it does not answer this one, and the RFC now cites
`execute_object.rs:8-20` — the very lines — for a different purpose.

**P7 — D8 and the dynamic call site; D8 and the three inline tiers.** Repeat
(previous review's P7, and the one before's P5). D8 puts a monomorphic
`(Symbol, generation) → resolution` cache on "call sites". Two boundaries stay
unstated: `execute`'s CALL arm reaches `vm.call` rather than `apply`
(`execute.rs:30`), and under D16 it is the site whose target is least
predictable — exactly where a monomorphic cache thrashes; and ERRATA records
**three** inline tiers, `i_direct` trying the VM's table
(`multistackvm_inline.rs:42`) then the stack layer's (`:52`), so "a resolution"
is a three-way answer and the RFC never says which.

**P8 — criterion 3's mechanism cannot observe D1's terminator change.** New,
and it is a scoping note rather than a defect.

`compile` appends `\n` before parsing (`bund_interpreter.rs:29`), exactly as
the four evaluation sites do. So the reference side of the parity comparison is
always the post-append parse, and D1's one behavioural claim — that admitting
end-of-input as a terminator accepts the same language the append accepts — is
the one thing the harness structurally cannot test. Criterion 4's
`1 2 +`-without-a-newline probe is what tests it, and criterion 3 should say it
does not.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

**1 — checkable, and the number is right, but see B4.** `conform --parse-only`
is correctly declared as new work; no `parse_only` exists in `xtask/src/`.

**2 — checkable, and still names no tool.** Depth, wall clock and scope are all
stated. The preamble promises each criterion "names the tool that decides it"
and this one names none. It cannot be a probe — a probe golden captures output
and the failure mode here is a process that dies — so it is a Rust test.
Repeat of the previous review.

**3 — see B1, B2, P8.** The mechanism is the right idea and the RFC deserves
credit for finding it in the language rather than instrumenting `reference/`. I
verified it renders nested structure faithfully, that `compile` drops EXIT as
claimed, and that the run inherits F21 as the RFC says. One further assumption
it rests on: `debug.display_stack` renders through `comfy_table` with
`ContentArrangement::Dynamic` (`debug_display_stack.rs:20-31`), which does not
wrap only because the harness's stdout is not a terminal. It held at
`COLUMNS=60` in my runs; it is still an assumption about a third-party
renderer's non-tty behaviour, and one line saying "captured non-interactively"
retires it.

**4 — checkable, correctly placed under `tests/probes/` per D21, and every
probe reproduces.** Add `1.0_5` (P3). Note the interference with criterion 1
(B4).

**5 — checkable, and every oracle baseline reproduces.** Two carry-overs.
`current_name` (RFC:750) is not the reference's spelling — it is
`current_stack_name()` (`conditional_ctx.rs:37`); if `current_name` is Bund2's
own name, say so. And the D34 clause tests that a lambda containing a `( … )`
"opens and closes its own context on every call, leaving the caller's stack
unchanged" — it should also assert the workbench carry-out (P4), since that is
what the corpus's one user of the form depends on.

**6 — checkable, and still says "both".** D6 now correctly names three callers;
criterion 6 still tests "visible from both `bund.eval` and the script path", so
a change at the single entry point could be invisible from the debugger — the
one caller whose behaviour differs. Repeat of the previous review.

**7 — accurate about the tool, and still self-demonstrating.** A run today
reports 2 exact-match blocks across all docs and neither is in this RFC; the
RFC contains exactly one fenced block and it carries no citation info string
(`:249-253`, the corpus idiom). So C1 through C6 all pass `cite` today, which
is the criterion arguing for itself for the third review running. The F21 header
claim also holds: `cite` reports 5/5 byte-verified, and the oracle's own error
output prints `~/.cargo/registry/…/bund_language_parser-0.14.0/…` paths.

## 6. Consistency with the registers, the accepted RFCs, and ERRATA

**Clean, checked:** D34's resolution is reproduced accurately, including the
"lower in place", the "top-level unchanged, so no golden moves" conclusion, and
the grounding paragraph on why the hoist is not an alternative scoping rule —
which the RFC paraphrases from `decisions.md:1600-1611` faithfully. F58, F59,
F60, F61 and F62 all exist in `defects.md` (`:1547, 1578, 1666, 1714, 1739`)
and agree with the RFC. Q21 and Q22 exist at `open-questions.md:987-988` and
say what the RFC says they say. D5's consumption, D3's amendment and its
reversal of mechanism, and D11's explicit discharge are all faithful. D16, D21
and D27's use is faithful. F48's caveat is correctly scoped. The "restates
§1.1" position now agrees between the header and the alternatives section, and
`05-rfc-roadmap.md:30-41` bears it out.

**6.1 — B3's register consequence.** D3's identity key touches decisions-D20
and F13. Neither is cited anywhere in this RFC.

**6.2 — "Blocked on: nothing" is still optimistic by one item.** The header
says nothing blocks; the Open questions say D8 keys on `(Symbol, generation)`
and RFC-0002 "records its generation overflow policy as unresolved". RFC-0002
is itself **Proposed**, not Accepted, and D7 and D8 both build on it. Either
scope D8 so the overflow policy cannot reach it, or say the header means "no
*decision* blocks".

**6.3 — decisions-D3's Status line still contradicts its own amendment**
(`decisions.md:125`ff): the Status says "content-hash compiled cache", the
amendment below reverses it to identity. Not this RFC's to fix, and the RFC
cites the amendment correctly — but this is the exact reading error that
produced the first review's B1, and B3 above is its descendant.

**6.4 — F56's count.** Third review running: F56 says "45 aliases from this
table minus this one"; `create_aliases.rs:4-45` holds **42**, which I recounted.
RFC-0003 does not repeat the number and does adopt the disposition.

**6.5 — D3's parse-cache observation still has no home.** The amendment
preserves the observation that the reference re-parses identical strings on
every call, and that keying *parses* by source text is independent of how
compiled code is keyed. RFC-0003 owns the parser and the evaluator, mentions no
parse cache, and hands nothing to RFC-0005. Take it or assign it. Repeat.

   covers `id` and `stamp` only. B1.
2. That criterion 3 tolerates D2's approved deviation. B2.
3. That materialising a body's identity to key the compiled cache is compatible
   with lazy identity, and that a `dup`'d lambda's cache miss is acceptable. B3.
4. That criterion 1's 42/69 is measured before criterion 4's probes land. B4.
5. That `atom` and `name` share a character class. They do not — P1.
6. That `stack` and `name` share one. They do not — P2.
7. That the float handler needs no separate trap entry — P3.
8. That the context frame's exit action reproduces `endcontext`'s conditional
   carry-out to the workbench, not merely the stack drop — P4.
9. That `execute.`'s `err_prefix` survives D4b's "proceeds exactly as
   `execute`" — P5.
10. That `execute.` on an OBJECT keeps its current error text after F53 — P6.
11. That `execute`'s dynamic CALL site is in or out of D8's scope, and which of
    the three inline tiers a cached "resolution" names — P7.
12. That criterion 3 is not the thing that tests D1's terminator change — P8.
13. That a reader can tell design-D5 from decisions-D5 — N1.
14. That `debug.display_stack`'s table renderer does not wrap under capture.
15. That criterion 2 is a Rust test rather than a probe. Repeat.

    well as by lambdas. `raise` bails from a native (`raise.rs:16`) and today

    `bail!` chain. D4 describes the unwind only in terms of frames. Repeat.
17. That the fifth `bund_parse` caller (`compile.rs:15`, which does not append)
    is out of scope because D11 found it unreachable from Bund. The RFC now
    names the caller and still does not make the argument.
