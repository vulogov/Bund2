## Should fix

### SF1. Not every R power function solves with `extendInt`, and the proportions' intervals are uncited (lines 212-216, 421-423)

The RFC says each function "solves for the one parameter given as
`NULL`, by `uniroot` over a stated interval with `extendInt`" (line 214).
§S4 then solves "over R's intervals and with R's interval extension".
- **`power.anova.test` uses no `extendInt`.** Its solves are
  `uniroot(…, c(2, 1e+05))` for n, `between.var·c(1e-07, 1e+07)` for
  `within.var`, `within.var·c(1e-07, 1e+07)` for `between.var` and
  `c(1e-10, 1 − 1e-10)` for `sig.level` (`power.anova.test.R:45-55`).
  Its n's upper end is 10⁵, not `power.t.test`'s 10⁷.
- **The proportions' solves are outside the cited range.** `:83-117` is
  `power.prop.test`'s signature and power bodies. Its `uniroot` calls
  are at `power.R:121-135`. n is solved over `c(1, 1e7)`, p1 over
  `c(0, p2)` and p2 over `c(p1, 1)`, each `extendInt = "yes"`, and
  `sig.level` with `"upX"`.
- **n's domain differs.** §S4 says "`n` must be ≥ 2" for every word.
  R's proportions solve starts at n = 1, and its t solve at 2. This is
  a departure from R for `power.prop` that the RFC does not list.

**Fix:**
- cite `power.R:53-64`, `:121-135` and `power.anova.test.R:45-55` for
  the intervals;
- say that `power.anova` has no extension in R, and either keep that or
  state Bund2's own;
- state `power.prop`'s n domain against R's.

### SF2. `optim.root`'s `extend` is called R's `extendInt` but is a different rule (line 503)

§S6 gives `extend` the values `"no"`, `"yes"`, `"up"` or `"down"`,
calling it "R's `extendInt`, doubling the interval at most 60 times".
R's `uniroot` (`nlm.R:50-53`, `:68-73`, `:85-111`) differs on all three
points:
- **The values mean something else.** R's are `"no"`, `"yes"`,
  `"downX"` and `"upX"`. `"upX"` and `"downX"` declare that f
  *increases* or *decreases* through its root (`Sig` = 1 or −1). They do
  not name a direction to extend in. `:53`'s comment maps them to
  crossings "down" and "up".
- **The step is different.** R does not double the interval. It moves
  each end by δ = 0.01·max(10⁻⁴, |end|) (`:85`), and doubles δ each
  time round (`:111`).
- **The cap is different.** It is `maxiter`, shared with the iteration
  cap and 1000 by default, and R stops with "no sign change found in %d
  iterations" (`:93-95`). There is no 60.

Since §S4 solves power "with R's interval extension", the power words'
answers near the ends of R's intervals depend on which rule is meant.

**Fix:** either port R's rule (`Sig`, the 1% step, the doubling and
`maxiter`) and keep R's value names, or call `extend` Bund2's own and
drop "R's `extendInt`". In either case, state which rule §S4 uses.

### SF3. The BCa default's citation stops before the rule, and its first half is commented out (line 281)

The RFC cites `bootfuns.q:1638-1650` for "`empinf` estimates L by
regression when R ≥ n, and by the jackknife only when R < n". The first
review verified it as "Yes, `:1644-1648`". But:
- **`:1640-1645` are comments**, an older rule commented out.
- **The active rule is `:1646-1653`:**
  - a supplied `t` gives `"reg"`;
  - `stype == "w"` gives `"inf"`, the infinitesimal jackknife;
  - an ordinary or balanced bootstrap gives `"jack"` when
    `boot.out$R < n`, and `"reg"` otherwise;
  - anything else gives `"jack"`.
- **The R < n test itself is at `:1651`**, outside the cited range.

The RFC's claim holds for `boot`'s defaults: `stype = c("i", "f", "w")`
picks `"i"`, and `boot.ci` passes `t = t.o`, which is NULL unless the
caller gave `t` (`bootfuns.q:891`, `:953-955`). But it holds only for
those defaults.

**Fix:** cite `:1646-1653`. Say "for an ordinary bootstrap with an
index statistic (`stype "i"`, `boot`'s default)", and note that a
weighted statistic gets the infinitesimal jackknife. N4's ruling is
unaffected.

### SF4. `swilk.c:3-4` is the copyright notice (line 250)

"Shapiro–Wilk is Royston's AS R94 (`swilk.c:3-4`)". Lines 3-4 are
` *  Copyright (C) 2000-2016   The R Core Team.` and a blank comment
line. The algorithm is named at `:5` ("Based on Applied Statistics
algorithms AS181, R94") and `:42` ("ALGORITHM AS R94 APPL. STATIST.
(1995) vol.44, no.4, 547-551"). Cite `:5`, `:42`. The first review
marked this citation verified.

## Notes

- **N1. `integrate.R:19-35` covers `dqags` only** (line 271). The
  infinite-range call to `C_call_dqagi` is at `:49`. Add it, since the
  sentence cites `dqagi` too.
- **N2. `power.chisq` has no R source.** §S4's table calls it the
  noncentral χ² upper tail at λ = n·w². It is Bund2's own, so "R's
  intervals" do not exist for it. State the solve intervals for `w`,
  `n`, `df` and `sig.level`, or say `df` cannot be solved for. `df` is
  an integer, and Brent's method over it is not meaningful.
- **N3. `power.prop`'s p1 and p2 solves pick one side.** R solves p1 in
  (0, p2) and p2 in (p1, 1) (`power.R:124-130`), so a solved p1 is
  always below p2, although |p1 − p2| makes both sides roots. With
  `extendInt = "yes"`, R's extension can also leave (0, 1), where §S4's
  domain refuses p1 and p2. State Bund2's interval and what happens at
  the boundary.
- **N4. On the reviews themselves.** Two citations the first review
  marked verified are not (SF3, SF4). The second review's remark that
  those 46 "were not re-opened line by line" was the right caution.
  Every citation has now been opened once against active lines.

## The criteria, Preservation and consistency

Unchanged from the second review, with one addition. Criterion 4's
round-trip clause ("each solved parameter, put back, gives the stated
power to 10⁻⁹") is checkable for `power.anova` and `power.chisq` only
once SF1 and N2 state their intervals. Without them, a root outside an
unextended interval is an E110 that no criterion predicts.
