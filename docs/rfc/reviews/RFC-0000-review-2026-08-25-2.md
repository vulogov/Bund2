# Review: RFC-0000 — Architecture, crate boundaries, and the tier model

- Reviewed: 2026-08-25 (second pass)
- Document: `docs/rfc/RFC-0000-architecture.md` at `5145b35`, Status: Draft
- Prior review: `RFC-0000-review-2026-08-25.md`, against `4aadaad`. That file is
  the record the RFC responded to in `209d527` and `5145b35`; this one is
  written alongside it rather than over it.
- Reference SHAs: verified against `git submodule status`. All six match the
  RFC header. `git status --porcelain` is empty in every submodule, before and
  after a full `cargo xtask golden` run.
- Method: every `path:line` in the RFC was opened and re-read in this session.
  Every acceptance criterion was executed. Six claims were checked against the
  oracle binary directly rather than by reading.

Verdict: the RFC improved materially — the supersession is now recorded in
ERRATA, `autoadd` and the fourth registration table are documented, criterion 3
is honestly demoted, and `cargo xtask cite` exists and passes. Four defects
remain or were introduced:

1. **Acceptance criterion 4's first clause fails at this commit**, and the RFC
   says it is vacuous when it is not (§3.4). The rewrite fixed the clause the
   prior review flagged and broke the clause it had passed.
2. **Two new count defects** the new `cite` checker cannot see: "six of the
   seven internal dependencies" (it is eleven of twelve) and "29 call sites"
   (it is 27) — §1.1, §1.2.
3. **The oracle is not built from the pinned submodules.** Its five internal
   dependencies resolve from crates.io, three of them at *older* versions than
   the submodules the RFC cites. The `src/` trees happen to be identical today;
   nothing checks that, and criterion 5 cannot (§3.5, §6.1). This is the most
   consequential finding in the review.
4. **Preservation still omits five behaviours**, three of which are now
   contradicted by material the RFC itself added (§4).

## 1. Citation defects

Every `reference/...:N` in the RFC was opened. 41 of 45 say what the RFC
claims. The four that do not:

### 1.1 "Six of the seven internal dependencies" — it is eleven of twelve

Motivation, second bullet. There are **twelve** internal dependency edges
across the reference, not seven:

    reference/Bund/Cargo.toml:13,14,23,24,43            5   ">=0.*.*"
    reference/bund_language_parser/Cargo.toml:17        1   ">=0.*.*"
    reference/bundcore/Cargo.toml:12,17,18              3   ">=0.*.*"
    reference/rust_multistack/Cargo.toml:14             1   ">= 0.*.*"
    reference/rust_multistackvm/Cargo.toml:21           1   ">=0.33.*"
    reference/rust_multistackvm/Cargo.toml:22           1   ">= 0.*.*"

Eleven unbounded, one bounded. The sentence's own enumeration lists ten
unbounded edges plus the exception — so "six of the seven" contradicts the list
that immediately follows it in the same sentence.

Where "seven" comes from is worth saying, because it shows the sentence is two
claims fused: excluding `Bund`, there are exactly seven internal edges, six
unbounded and one bounded. That is a true and interesting statement. It is not
the statement the enumeration makes.

**`reference/rust_multistackvm/Cargo.toml:22` is missing from the enumeration
entirely** — `rust_multistack = ">= 0.*.*"`. The RFC cites that file three
lines earlier for the dependency-graph claim, so the edge is known and dropped
only from the pin count. That is the same defect shape as the prior review's
§1.1 (`Bund/Cargo.toml:14` omitted from an enumeration introduced by "every"),
in the paragraph that was rewritten to fix it.

Two of the eleven are spelled `">= 0.*.*"` with a space
(`rust_multistack/Cargo.toml:14`, `rust_multistackvm/Cargo.toml:22`), so the
quoted string in the prose is not the string in two of the files it covers.

### 1.2 "29 call sites" — it is 27

"Word registration is three tiers, not two", fourth paragraph:
`register_function` "fills a separate `functions` map from 29 call sites across
`reference/rust_multistack/src/stdlib/`."

Counted exactly: `grep -rho "register_function" reference/rust_multistack/src`
returns 30 occurrences = 27 `ts.register_function(` call sites + 1 definition
(`ts_functions.rs:6`) + 2 inside `unregister_function`. The 27 sites are in
`current.rs`, `stack_move.rs`, `clear.rs`, `dup.rs`, `swap.rs`, `fold.rs`,
`drop.rs`, `push.rs`, `rotate.rs`, `ensure_stack.rs` and `workbench.rs`.

The error originates in `docs/registers/defects.md:333` (F19), which also says
29, and is inherited here — the same pattern as the prior review's §1.3, where
the RFC inherited an off-by-one from D16. The registers are append-only, so the
RFC cannot fix F19 in place, but it should cite 27 rather than propagate 29.

Note where 29 plausibly came from: it is the `take` corpus count two paragraphs
above. Both numbers are in the same section.

### 1.3 "`ptr` … pushes a PTR naming a word" — it applies, it does not push

`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/artefacts.rs:88` is
`return vm.apply(Value::ptr(name.clone(), Vec::new()));`. There is no push in
`stdlib_ptr_inline`. The push happens later, in `apply`'s default arm
(`multistackvm_apply.rs:99`).

The prior review raised this (§1.8) and it is unfixed. It matters more now than
it did then, because the RFC has since added the `autoadd` paragraph: at
`multistackvm_apply.rs:89-97` the default arm **appends to the value on top of
the stack instead of pushing** when `autoadd` is set. So the RFC now asserts on
one page that `ptr` pushes, and demonstrates on the same page that whether
anything pushes is mode-dependent. The omitted hop is the whole content of the
correction.

Also, `:80-93` ends mid-function; `stdlib_ptr_inline` runs to `:99`.

### 1.4 "those are the only two causes `tests/golden/UNSTABLE.txt` tags" — false

Registers section, second bullet: "F14 and F15 are why 18 of 77 hermetic
programs could not be captured — those are the only two causes
`tests/golden/UNSTABLE.txt` tags."

The file tags three causes. Counted over its 18 program lines: 13 `F14`, 2
`F15`, **3 `F17`**. Its header block names F17 as a root cause alongside the
other two (`UNSTABLE.txt:12-20`).

This is an over-correction. The prior review (§1.6) found F17 wrongly listed as
a capture cause *against the file as it then stood*; the file was subsequently
corrected to tag F17, and the RFC was corrected in the opposite direction. The
RFC's very next sentence — "F17 is why three of the eighteen stayed
unreproducible" — is correct and contradicts the one before it.

## 2. Claims that need a citation and lack one

Six of the prior review's six are still uncited. Two are new.

1. **The startup decomposition.** "a bare process spawn is 1.4 ms,
   `bund --version` … is 11.0 ms, an empty program is 15.8 ms" and "about
   9.6 ms of every run". Grounded in `docs/registers/decisions.md:870-878`
   (D28's measurement table), which I confirmed reproduces every figure. Still
   no pointer. The RFC's own rule is that a factual claim about existing
   behaviour points somewhere.

2. **"Each embeds data or a native library."** D28 says the same thing at
   `decisions.md:886`. Neither grounds it in the crates. It is defensible for
   `duckdb`/`hyphenation`/`lingua` and hard to defend for `reqwest`. Also
   `rusqlite` with `bundled` (`Bund/Cargo.toml:131-133`) is a bundled native
   library that the list omits, and it is the one the world file actually uses.

3. **"each handler in `src/vm/` converts one token to a `Value`."**
   `bund_language_parser/src/vm/` holds fifteen files: twelve token handlers
   matching the twelve `value` alternatives, plus `mod.rs`, `eoi.rs` and
   `unknown.rs`. "each" is wrong for three of the fifteen.

4. **"the scope filter in `cargo xtask corpus` guarantees a suite program
   invokes no gated word."** This is the load-bearing claim under "no golden's
   *content* changed". It is true — `xtask/src/corpus/classify.rs:418-478` —
   and carries no pointer.

5. **The LOC/file table.** "Measured over `src/` at the pinned SHAs" names no
   command and no file-set rule. It reproduces exactly under `*.rs` beneath
   `src/`, but only because I guessed the same rule twice.

6. **`take` 29 / `drop` 16.** Correct; no source given. ERRATA attributes them
   to `cargo xtask corpus`.

7. **New: "`autoadd` is toggled by the `:` and `;` commands."** This is the
   sentence that makes the `autoadd` finding actionable and it is the only
   claim in the paragraph with no citation. It is grounded:
   `reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/autoadd.rs:28-29` registers both via
   `register_command`. Two further facts belong with it and are not stated:
   autoadd **cannot nest** (`autoadd.rs:6-8`, `:17-19` both bail) and both
   toggles require a non-empty current stack (`:9-11`, `:20-22`).

8. **New: "`swap` is both an inline word and an alias; the deduped count is
   617."** No citation, and the underlying fact is sharper than the sentence —
   see §4.3.

### 3.4 Criterion 4 — the first clause fails today, and the RFC says it is vacuous

> `cargo tree -p bund2-interp` does not list `bund2-value` as reaching back
> into it, and `cargo tree -p bund2-stdlib` does not list `bund2-jit`. … Also
> vacuous today, because both crates are empty scaffolds.

Executed:

    $ cargo tree -p bund2-interp
    bund2-interp v0.0.0
    ├── bund2-api v0.0.0
    │   └── bund2-value v0.0.0
    ├── bund2-ir v0.0.0
    │   ├── bund2-syntax v0.0.0
    │   │   └── bund2-value v0.0.0
    │   └── bund2-value v0.0.0
    └── bund2-value v0.0.0

`bund2-value` appears four times. The criterion, executed literally, **fails at
the commit that states it** — and it will fail forever, because `bund2-interp`
depending on `bund2-value` is the intended direction
(`crates/bund2-interp/Cargo.toml:11`).

The rule is `bund2-value` must not depend on `bund2-interp`. The forward graph
that shows that is `cargo tree -p bund2-value`, which prints one line — the
root, no dependencies (`crates/bund2-value/Cargo.toml` has an empty
`[dependencies]`). The rule holds; the criterion does not check it.

The prior review flagged the *second* clause as reversed. The rewrite adopted
"use the forward graph, not `--invert`" and applied it to both clauses, but the
two rules point in opposite directions: for `stdlib`→`jit` the forbidden edge
starts at `stdlib`, so `-p bund2-stdlib` is right; for `value`→`interp` the
forbidden edge starts at `value`, so the check is `-p bund2-value`. Clause two
is now correct and passes (`cargo tree -p bund2-stdlib` lists only `bund2-api`,
`bund2-ir`, `bund2-syntax`, `bund2-value`).

"Vacuous today, because both crates are empty scaffolds" conflates empty source
with empty dependency graph. The manifests are wired; both trees are populated.
Clause two is a real check that really passes. Clause one is a real check that
really fails, for a reason that has nothing to do with the property it guards.

## 4. Preservation analysis — what it still does not enumerate

The section now has six items, up from five, and the denominator paragraph is a
real improvement: it states plainly that both denominators moved and that two
goldens stopped existing. Five behaviours remain inside the blast radius. Three
of them are ones the RFC's own new material demonstrates.

### 4.1 `autoadd` is documented in Current behaviour and absent from Preservation

The RFC now describes `autoadd` well — this was the prior review's first
blocking item and the description is accurate. It was added to **Current
behaviour**, not to **Preservation analysis**, which is where the prior review
asked for it and where this review's brief requires it: Preservation must
enumerate every behaviour the design could change. "Three registration tiers:
preserved exactly" covers the inline fallthrough; nothing covers the mode flag
that decides whether resolution happens at all.

Four things the description omits, all of which a reimplementer needs:

- **"`apply` first tests `self.autoadd`" is wrong about the order.** The
  command check at `multistackvm_apply.rs:16` comes first, and `autoadd` is
  tested at `:19` inside its `else`. This is not pedantry: `:` and `;` are
  registered with `register_command` (`autoadd.rs:28-29`), so the only reason a
  program can ever turn autoadd *off* is that commands are dispatched ahead of
  the autoadd branch. Get the order wrong and collection mode is a trap door.
- **The CALL arm is one of three.** `apply`'s CONTEXT arm pushes the context
  value instead of switching stacks (`:72-73`), and the default arm appends
  every non-CALL, non-CONTEXT value to the top-of-stack value (`:89-97`). Under
  autoadd, literals change meaning too, not just names.
- **`apply_in` has its own, different autoadd behaviour.**
  `multistackvm_apply_in.rs:15-16` pushes to the *named* stack rather than
  appending to a value, with two more branches at `:45` and `:62`. Two
  functions, two semantics for one flag.
- **Autoadd cannot nest and requires depth ≥ 1** (`autoadd.rs:6-11,17-22`).
  `: :` is an error, not a nested collection.

### 4.2 The dead fourth table holds six names that exist nowhere else — two of them behind live aliases

The RFC's F19 paragraph is correct: nothing reads the `functions` map, and
porting it "would add a name space the language does not have." It stops one
step before the consequence.

Of the 27 `register_function` names, **six are registered in no other table**:
`dup_in`, `from_workbench`, `push`, `push_to`, `stacks_left` and — at the
`rust_multistack` layer — `dup`. Confirmed against the oracle
(`target/oracle/release/bund script --file …`):

    1 2 stacks_left     → i(stacks_left) for stack returned: Inline stacks_left not registered
    1 2 <-              → i(stacks_left) for stack returned: Inline stacks_left not registered
    1 2 push_to         → i(push_to) for stack returned: Inline push_to not registered
    1 2 dup_in          → i(dup_in) for stack returned: Inline dup_in not registered
    1 2 from_workbench  → i(from_workbench) for stack returned: Inline from_workbench not registered

`<-` and `←` are live aliases (`create_aliases.rs:22-23`) pointing into the
dead namespace. `dup` and `push` survive because the VM and `Bund` layers
register them separately (`create_aliases.rs:18`,
`Bund/src/stdlib/functions/values/push.rs:74`).

So the table is not inert: it is the reason five spellings resolve to a
specific error rather than to a word, and one of those errors is reachable
through an alias a program can write. A `bund2-interp` that drops the table
must reproduce *that* error, and the RFC's instruction to RFC-0002 ("the slot
table absorbs three, not four") does not say so.

Two further notes on F19 as the RFC leans on it: its call-site count is wrong
(§1.2), and its `disposition` field is **empty** (`defects.md:355`). The RFC's
own Registers section states that an empty disposition blocks any work item
touching that area — which is RFC-0002's slot table, the thing the paragraph
exists to instruct.

### 4.3 An alias shadows a differently-arity inline word, and the RFC records it as a counting nuance

The acceptance table's first row: "`swap` is both an inline word and an alias;
the deduped count is 617."

True, and the *reason* it matters is not the count. Alias resolution precedes
inline lookup (`apply.rs:39` before `:59`), so `swap` never reaches the inline
registration at `rust_multistack/src/stdlib/swap.rs:98`. It resolves to
`swap_one`, and the two have **different stack effects**:

- `stdlib_swap_in_current_inline` (registered `swap`, `swap.rs:23-29`) pulls an
  operand `n` and performs an n-deep swap.
- `stdlib_swap_one_in_current_inline` (registered `swap_one`, `swap.rs:31-52`)
  takes no operand and swaps the top two.

Confirmed against the oracle: `1 2 3 swap` leaves `1, 3, 2` — three values,
top two exchanged, nothing consumed as a count. The n-swap is unreachable.

This is a preservation contract, not a tally: RFC-0002's slot table must let an
alias shadow an inline word of a different arity rather than merging the two
namespaces or preferring the concrete registration. It belongs in Preservation
and probably in `defects.md`.

### 4.4 A JSON value does not round-trip through the world file as itself

Preservation: "The contained values do not [change]: they remain
bincode-serialised `Value`s, and D20's rule that serialisation materialises
lazy identity is unaffected."

`reference/rust_dynamic/src/bincode.rs:9-22`: `to_binary` special-cases `JSON`.
It converts to `STRING`, then calls `Value::json_wrap`
(`rust_dynamic/src/create_special.rs:241-252`), which mints a **new** value —
fresh `id` from `nanoid!()`, fresh `stamp`, `dt = JSON_WRAPPED`, `q = 100.0`,
and empty `attr`, `curr` and `tags`. `from_binary` (`bincode.rs:54-59`) unwraps
by calling `Value::json(...)` (`create_special.rs:205-216`), which mints
another new value the same way.

So for JSON values, a world-file round trip does not materialise lazy identity
— it **discards and replaces** identity, timestamp, quality, attributes and
tags. That is more than D20 describes, and the redb migration inherits it. The
prior review raised this (§4.7); the sentence it contradicts is unchanged.

Related and already registered: F13 (`defects.md:125-140`) records the same
round-trip under `dup` and dispositions it FIX. The world-file path is the same
code and is not covered by that entry.

### 4.5 The conformance gate compares *normalised* output, and Preservation never says so

`tests/golden/README.md:11-13`: each golden records "every named stack's
contents, the workbench, the exit status, the error text, and stdout." Every
golden header records what was removed before comparison:

    # normalised: id and stamp (F14), dict member order (F15), ANSI stripped

Each normalisation is separately grounded — F14 and F15 are dispositioned FIX
with the reasoning that neither has an order to preserve, and ANSI stripping
cites D15 (`xtask/src/golden/mod.rs:138-140`). The grounding is fine. The
omission is that **Preservation analysis is the section whose job is to say
this** and it does not: the RFC's criterion 1 makes conformance the regression
gate, and three classes of observable output are excluded from that gate by
design. "A word behind a disabled feature is absent, not different, and no
*retained* behaviour changes" is the closest the section comes, and it is about
feature gating, not normalisation.

The corollary is bigger and also unstated: **error text is inside the
contract.** Goldens pin it. A Bund2 that produces a correct result with a
differently-worded error fails conformance. Nothing in RFC-0000 tells a later
RFC that error strings are preserved behaviour.

### 4.6 `!` dispatches on eight arms and its stack effect is input-type dependent

Unchanged from the prior review (§4.5). The Tier model writes the D16
constraint as "Tier 1 cannot statically devirtualise `!`", which frames `!` as
a call indirection. `reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/execute.rs:26-98`
handles `PTR | STRING | CALL` (`:27`), `LIST` (`:37`, recursing over elements),
`MAP | INFO | CONFIG | ASSOCIATION` (`:55`), `CONDITIONAL` (`:84`), `CLASS`
(`:87`), `OBJECT` (`:90`), `LAMBDA` (`:93`) and an error arm (`:96`).

The dict arm **pulls a second value off the stack as a key** (`:56`). So `!`'s
arity depends on the runtime type of its operand, and a Tier 1 speculation
guard has to model arity, not just a target. Since RFC-0000 is where the tier
constraint is fixed, that belongs here rather than by reference to D16 §4.

## 5. Consistency with accepted RFCs and with ERRATA

There are still no accepted RFCs. Against ERRATA, the registers and the
research corpus:

### 5.1 The supersession is now recorded — and the scope problem moved rather than closed

`ERRATA.md:54-58` records it, and its wording matches the RFC header exactly,
including the deliberate exclusion of §1.1. Both blocking items from the prior
review's §5.1 and §5.2 are addressed as stated.

What the narrowing did not consider: `04-consolidated-architecture.md` §2 and
§3 are not layout documents. §2 is "How close is this to the original idea"
(drift, cost, gains). §3 is twelve subsections of forward proposals — §3.2
inline caches, §3.3 type feedback from Tier 0 into Tier 1, §3.6 superinstruction
mining, §3.8 differential fuzzing across the three tiers, §3.12 the benchmark
corpus. Several of those *are* tier-model claims, so under "RFC-0000 is the
statement of record for … tiering" they are superseded by a document that says
nothing about them.

That is the same "worst of both" the prior review identified for §1.1,
relocated to §3: a reader can no longer rely on §3.2 and has nothing to rely on
instead. §1.1 is now the only place in the corpus that draws the crate diagram
*and* it is explicitly not superseded, which is coherent but leaves §1.1's
24-byte value claim (contradicted by D4, no errata) as live text alongside it.

### 5.2 "18 of 77 hermetic programs" is a fourth denominator the pinning table does not pin

The Registers section quotes 77. The acceptance table says hermetic programs =
**80**. `HERMETIC.txt` holds 57.

The arithmetic does close: 80 − 3 (D15, out of scope) = 77; 77 − 18
(unreproducible) = 59; 59 − 2 (D28) = 57. The table itself spells that out.
But the label "hermetic programs" means 80 in the table and 77 four paragraphs
earlier, and the RFC's stated purpose for that table is that "a later RFC
citing either can tell whether it has shifted." `UNSTABLE.txt:5` uses 77 too,
and calls them "hermetic-classified". For the RFC whose job is fixing
vocabulary, this is the one place the vocabulary should be airtight.

### 5.3 The coverage row over-attributes, and its arithmetic does not close

    | coverage | 140/586 | 121/497 | D28 moved 120 words out of scope |

Two problems. 586 − 497 = **89**, not 120, and one of that 89 is the `swap`
dedup, so 88 words newly left scope. And the 120 is not D28's: `cargo xtask
coverage` reports it as "out of scope **by decision**", and
`xtask/src/corpus/classify.rs:418-478` attributes it to three decisions — D15
(`console`), D26 (`internaldb`) and D28 (`ai`, `image`, `bus`, `forecast`,
`statistics`). Counting `register_inline` names under those paths gives roughly
31 for D15 and 4 for D26 before aliases.

This is the row that exists to stop exactly this kind of drift.

### 5.4 "Three dispatch tiers, three live tables" undercounts live tables

The RFC's own resolution order, three paragraphs above, is: command → `$`-forced
→ alias → lambda → inline, with inline having two tiers. That is commands,
aliases, lambdas and two inline tables — five live name-keyed lookups, not
three. `xtask/src/corpus/registry.rs:50-67` enumerates seven namespaces, and
`docs/research/05-rfc-roadmap.md:110` calls it "the five name-keyed tables".

"Three live tables" is true only if "table" silently means "inline word table".
Since the sentence's payload is an instruction to RFC-0002 — "the slot table
absorbs three, not four" — the ambiguity is load-bearing: an RFC-0002 that
absorbs three has not been told where commands and lambdas go.

### 5.5 Register rows the RFC points at are still stale

Not RFC-0000's bugs, but its Registers section presents these files as the
shared state and its Terminology defines "Probe" by D21:

- `open-questions.md:16` still lists **Q16 as Open**: "`cargo xtask golden` …
  never captures them — all six sit at `0/6`, asserting nothing." `cargo xtask
  coverage` reports "6/6 captured, 0 structural problem(s)" and I verified the
  six goldens capture. Flagged in the prior review (§5.5); unchanged.
- `open-questions.md:15` (Q15) still quotes "140 in-scope words" and "446 words
  unimplemented". The RFC restates Q15 in its Open questions without noting the
  figures moved to 121/497.

### 5.6 Where the RFC restates ERRATA, it restates it accurately

The three-tier finding, `take` 29 / `drop` 16, `!` as the spelling of
`execute`, the twelve `value` alternatives — all match. As before, the errors
the RFC inherits come from the *registers* (F19's 29), not from ERRATA.

## 6. Assumed but not stated

### 6.1 That the oracle is built from the pinned submodules — it is not

Terminology: "**Oracle** — `reference/Bund` built at the pinned SHA, the
authority on what a program does."

`reference/Bund/Cargo.toml` declares its five internal dependencies as registry
versions (`:13,14,23,24,43`), not path dependencies. There is no
`[patch.crates-io]` anywhere in the repo — `.cargo/config.toml` contains only
the `xtask` alias. So the documented build command resolves those five from
crates.io. What the 381 MB binary actually links:

    bund_language_parser-0.14.0     submodule at 80377728f45b is 0.15.0
    bundcore-0.7.0                  submodule at 3b0b8ba219a6 is 0.8.0
    rust_dynamic-0.49.0             submodule at ceb27c96fa10 is 0.50.0
    rust_multistack-0.33.0          matches
    rust_multistackvm-0.38.0        matches

Three of the five submodules the RFC pins by SHA are *ahead of* the code the
oracle runs. The goldens in `tests/golden/` were produced by the crates.io
copies.

The good news, and it is why this is an unstated assumption rather than a
retraction: I diffed all five. **Every `src/` tree is byte-identical** to the
registry copy the oracle links. The differences are confined to `Cargo.toml`
(the version bump), `README.md`, a `Makefile`, release notes and one test file.
So every `path:line` citation into `reference/*/src/` does resolve against code
the oracle runs, today.

Nothing establishes that, and three things assume it:

- The Terminology definition of Oracle is false as built, and it is the
  definition the whole citation regime rests on.
- Criterion 5 is presented as the thing that "catches an accidental edit to the
  oracle, which would invalidate every citation in every RFC." A clean
  `reference/` submodule says nothing about which code was linked. The
  submodules are not build inputs.
- The mechanism is F8, the RFC's own second finding. `">=0.*.*"` means a new
  upstream publish silently changes the oracle — and therefore the goldens —
  with every SHA in the header unchanged and every submodule clean. The RFC
  cites this hazard as a reason for the monorepo without noticing it is live in
  its own methodology.

The fix is small: either add `[patch.crates-io]` path overrides so the oracle
builds from the submodules, or add an xtask check asserting the linked crates'
`src/` trees match the submodules, and state which one the project relies on.

### 6.2 Where AOT lives

Unchanged from the prior review (#1). The Tier model says "AOT is the
`cranelift-object` build"; the layout table gives AOT no crate and labels
`bund2-jit` "Tier 1; optional, feature-gated". The scaffold answers it —
`crates/bund2-jit/Cargo.toml:23` declares `aot` alongside `jit`, both pulling
`cranelift-codegen`/`-frontend`/`-module` — so AOT and Tier 1 are one crate
under different features. Under a literal reading of the table, an AOT build
links a crate the RFC calls Tier 1 and calls optional. Say it.

### 6.3 A third boundary rule exists in the scaffold and is not in the RFC

Also unchanged (#2). `crates/bund2-runtime/Cargo.toml:20-22` carries the
comment "bund2-async depends on bund2-runtime, so it must NOT appear here", and
`aot`/`jit` add `dep:bund2-jit` accordingly. That is a real invariant, enforced
today, absent from both the layout table and criterion 4.

### 6.4 Whether the twelve-member list is closed

Unchanged (#4). Criterion 4 names boundaries crate by crate, so adding a
workspace member silently adds an unchecked boundary, and nothing says whether
a later RFC may add one.

### 6.5 The measurement platform

Unchanged (#6). 381 MB, 1.4 / 11.0 / 15.8 ms and the dependency graph are one
machine, one OS, one linker; nothing records which.

Sharpened by §6.1 and by a finding of its own: three goldens embed the capture
machine's absolute paths.

    tests/golden/probes/execute-arm-not-executable.golden
    tests/golden/probes/execute-arm-class.golden
    tests/golden/examples/code_snippets/tryexcept_demo_divide_to_0.golden

each contain lines like

    │ Location ┆ /Users/gandalf/.cargo/registry/src/index.crates.io-…/
                 rust_multistackvm-0.38.0/src/stdlib/execute.rs:97:21 │

Two of the three are D21 probes. Three consequences the RFC does not carry:
those goldens are unreproducible on any other machine (different `$HOME`,
different registry hash); **Bund2 can never match them**, since it has no
`rust_multistackvm-0.38.0` in a cargo registry, so criterion 1's target has a
structural ceiling of 60/63 unless those three are accepted as deviations; and
they pin the linked crate version, which §6.1 shows is not the pinned SHA.

### 6.6 That "gated" and "out of scope" coincide

Unchanged (#7), and now demonstrably not one-to-one — see §5.3. The Feature
policy names seven subsystems; `coverage` attributes 120 words to three
decisions; criterion 2's denominator is that mapping's output and the RFC
attributes all of it to D28.

### 6.7 That feature-gating cannot change a *surviving* word

Unchanged (#8), and it is the claim "no golden changes" rests on. The RFC
argues correctly that a gated word is absent, not different. It does not
address a surviving word whose behaviour differs when a gated crate is absent —
a `format`/`display` path that falls back, a word that reports which subsystems
are available. `bund/console` (D15, 31 registered names) sits in presentation,
which is where that most plausibly breaks.

### 6.8 D3, D9 and D10 are OPEN, bear on the tier model, and go unmentioned

Unchanged from the prior review (§4.9); listed under "worth doing" there and not
acted on. All three are still OPEN (`decisions.md:130`, `:239`, `:245`):

- **D3 — tier policy for `bund.eval` output** (default "permanently Tier 0").
  RFC-0000 defines Tier 1 as "optional, feature-gated, never required for
  correctness" without saying that whether a class of code is *eligible* for it
  is unresolved. D16 flags the interaction explicitly.
- **D9 — third-party CLIF lowerings through `bund2-api`.** The layout table
  gives `bund2-api` as "the stable surface a word package compiles against";
  whether that surface includes lowerings is D9, and the answer changes what
  the boundary means.
- **D10 — may `bund2 build` require `cc`.** "AOT is the `cranelift-object`
  build" is a toolchain claim and D10 is the toolchain decision.

CLAUDE.md forbids adopting an OPEN decision's default silently. None is
adopted here — they are simply absent, which reads the same to a reader.
