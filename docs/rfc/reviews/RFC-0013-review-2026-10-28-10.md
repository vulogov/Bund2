## Should-fix

### S1. D171 does not call `push_frame` "the one place" the guard is taken

The guard bullet says the interpreter polls "where a body starts running",
and then: "D171 calls that 'the one place', which it no longer is."

D171's text is: "A process-wide flag the thread sets and
`Interp::push_frame` takes — the one place a body starts running".

"The one place" there qualifies *where a body starts running*, and that is
still true. D42 says the same (`push_frame` is the one place a
body starts running). D171 doesn't claim `push_frame` is the only taker.
What F210 and F104 did overtake is D171's "What it does not cover" bullet,
which says that a native that allocates in one call is not interrupted,
with `seq` as "the known case".

`-9.md` made the same misreading ("D171 says the same: 'the one place a
body starts running'"), and revision 11 carried it into the RFC.

**Fix:** say that F210 and F104 overtook D171's "What it does not cover"
bullet. `seq` now asks the guard as it builds, and `io.graph` does too.
`push_frame` remains the one place a body starts running.

- **N1. "The engine's poll (S4) is the third" counts natives.** Under
  "Natives poll it too", "the third" means the third native, after `seq`
  and `io.graph`. Counted with the interpreter, the engine's poll is the
  fourth taker. A word ("the third native to poll it") avoids the
  ambiguity.
