# RFC-0001 review — second pass, 2026-08-26

Reviewing `docs/rfc/RFC-0001-value.md` at commit `0b650ae`, revised after
review 1 (`RFC-0001-review-2026-08-26.md`).

Method: every `reference/...:N` citation in the RFC was opened and read in this
session. Every count was re-derived from the corpus, the goldens or
`cargo xtask`. Four claims were put to the oracle directly
(`target/oracle/release/bund --nocolor script`).

Verdict: **do not accept**, but the reasons have changed shape. Review 1's ten
remediation items are substantially done: the citation defects A1–A3 are fixed
and now point at lines that say what the prose says, the three counts B1–B3
reproduce exactly, `dup` is no longer an `Rc` bump, `curr` and `attr` are
fields, `Nodata` and `None` are split, and the ERRATA line exists. The RFC is
much closer to its own evidence than it was.

What blocks acceptance now is different: **the design contradicts three of its
own preservation rows**, and two of those contradictions are visible in a
two-word Bund program. Separately, the F29/F30 blocker the RFC names has since
been decided by the owner, and neither the decision register nor the RFC has
absorbed it.

## A. Citation precision

Neither of these is a defect in review 1's sense; both point at a real line
that supports the sentence. Both point at the *least reachable* instance.

**A1. `set.rs:33` is one of three `q` copies, and the one the map path does not
take.** RFC:173. `res.q = self.q` appears at `set.rs:33`, `:60` and `:93` — the
scalar catch-alls of `set`, `set_vmap` and `set_key_raw`. The MAP arm of `set`
does not reach `:33` at all: it returns `Value::from_dict(m)` (`set.rs:21`),
whose `q` is the constructor's own `100.0` (`create_map.rs:36`). The claim "set
copies it" is true only of the scalar arm.

**A2. `push.rs:165` is the arm that words do not reach.** RFC:93 cites
`push.rs:165` as a per-mutation re-mint site. That line is in `Value::push`'s
scalar catch-all (`push.rs:162-167`). The callers that actually exist —
`map_fun.rs:31` for the `map` word, `bund_object.rs:86,89,93` for object
construction — push into a `Value::list()`, which takes the `LIST | RESULT` arm
and returns `Value::from_list(data)` (`push.rs:48`). That arm mints through the
constructor, and it does more than mint: see B3.

Note also that the Bund word spelled `push` is **not** `Value::push`. It is
`push_list_base` (`Bund/src/stdlib/functions/values/push.rs:10`), which
`conv(LIST)`s both operands (`:34,43`). Nothing in the RFC says which of the
two it means.

**A3. The payload→`dt` table is still incomplete — review 1's C8 is not
fixed.** RFC:70 lists `Val::Map` as carrying `CLASS`, `CONDITIONAL`, `CONFIG`,
`CURRY`, `INFO`, `MAP`, `OBJECT`. It also carries **`MESSAGE`**: `Value::message`
builds a `Value::dict()` and assigns `res.dt = MESSAGE`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/create.rs:181-186`). `MESSAGE` appears in the
`get.rs:7` arm the RFC quotes verbatim eight lines later (RFC:79), so the table
contradicts its own next paragraph. `ASSOCIATION`, also in that arm, has no
constructor anywhere in `rust_dynamic` — it is read by `get`, `set`, `conv` and
`reduce` and written by nothing, which is a finding in its own right and is not
recorded.

## B. Claims contradicted by the source

### B1. Scalars have no identity, so three preservation rows cannot hold

The design puts `Bool`, `Nodata` and `None` inline in `BundValue` (RFC:206-213)
and identity in `HeapValue` (RFC:231). Scalars therefore carry no identity —
this is the whole point of candidate D, and `cargo xtask layout` measures it.

Three rows of the preservation table then say things the design cannot do:

- **"Equality: content for four payload kinds, identity for sixteen —
  Preserved exactly, including `Bool` comparing by identity"** (RFC:353).
  `Val::Bool` reaches `eq.rs:45` and compares `self.id == other.id` (`:53`).
  Under the design a `Bool` has no id. Two separately constructed `true`s are
  **unequal** in the reference and can only be **equal** in Bund2. `Val::Null`
  — both `NODATA` and `NONE` — is in the same position.
- **"Ordering falling back to `id.cmp` — Preserved exactly"** (RFC:354). Same
  problem, same arm: `ord.rs:199` needs an id that a `Bool` or a `Nodata` does
  not have.
- **Criterion 4** (RFC:398-400) requires `A == A.clone()` true and
  `A == A.dup()` false "for a list, a map, a lambda **and a bool**". For the
  bool that criterion is **unsatisfiable as designed**: there is no identity
  slot to clear, so `dup` cannot make a bool unequal to its source. The
  criterion is well chosen — it is exactly the right test — and the design
  fails it.

This is not fatal to the shape. The escape is available and cheap, and the RFC
has to pick one and say so: either bools and nulls are heap values (paying 176
→ 16 only for the numeric scalars), or the RFC states the deviation — content
equality for `Bool`/`Null` — and argues its unobservability.

That argument is available, and it is stronger than it looks, but the RFC does
not make it. See C1: the `==` word never reaches those arms.

### B2. Criterion 3 is arithmetically wrong, and its harness cannot check it

Criterion 3 (RFC:392-394): "`dup` of a heap value allocates **1** time — one
header — against the reference's serialise-and-deserialise. Same harness."

`HeapValue` carries `tags: BTreeMap<String, String>` and `attr: Vec<BundValue>`
by value (RFC:235-236). A fresh header must clone them. And `tags` is never
empty for a stack-resident value: `TS::push` writes `value.set_tag("stack",
&curr.stack_id())` on every push (`reference/rust_multistack/src/ts_push.rs:25`),
which is why all 258 golden renderings show `tags: {"stack": "main"}`. Cloning
a one-entry `BTreeMap<String,String>` allocates the node and both `String`s. So
`dup` allocates at least four times, not one.

"Same harness" is also not true. `cargo xtask layout`'s allocation table has
rows for *construct a scalar*, *clone a scalar*, *construct a 1-element list*
and *clone a list* for candidates A, D and B. **There is no `dup` row for any
candidate.** Nothing in the repository measures criterion 3.

### B3. `set` and `push` do not split a value — they reset its header

This is the largest omission in the preservation analysis, and it is
observable in two words of Bund.

The RFC's value-semantics section (RFC:299-313) models every re-minting
mutation as an `Rc::make_mut` split: same contents, new identity. That is not
what the reference does on the heap arms. `set`'s MAP arm returns
`Value::from_dict(m)` (`set.rs:21`), `set_vmap` returns `Value::from_valuemap(m)`
(`set.rs:48`), and `Value::push`'s LIST arm returns `Value::from_list(data)`
(`push.rs:48`). Each of those constructors writes a **fresh header**:

    attr: Vec::new(), curr: -1, tags: HashMap::new()

— `create_map.rs:33-40` (`from_dict`), `create_map.rs:44-52` (`from_valuemap`),
`create_list.rs:20-28` (`from_list`). `set` then restores only `dt`
(`set.rs:22`). `attr`, `curr` and `tags` are **destroyed**. `make_mut` copies
them.

Confirmed against the oracle:

    dict 99 attribute "b" 2 set        ->  ... dt: 11, ..., attr: [], ...
    1 2 attribute                      ->  ... dt: 2, ..., attr: [Value{...I64(2)...}], ...

The control shows `attribute` populating `attr` exactly as RFC:146-152 claims.
The first line shows a subsequent map `set` wiping it. A Bund2 that splits by
`make_mut` renders `attr: [Value{...I64(99)...}]` there. That is a `Debug`
divergence, and `Debug` is captured by 29 goldens.

The preservation table has no row for this. Neither does the design section.

### B4. `Value::push` on a `RESULT` returns a `LIST`

`push.rs:37` matches `LIST | RESULT` and returns `Value::from_list(data)`
(`:48`), whose `dt` is `LIST` (`create_list.rs:23`). Unlike `set`, there is no
`raw_value.dt = self.dt` restore. So pushing to a `RESULT` silently changes its
type to `LIST` — 92 becomes 9. The RFC's first preservation row says `dt` is
"carried verbatim"; this is a path where the reference does not carry it. Not
recorded in `defects.md`, and reachability from a word is unverified.

## C. Behaviours the preservation analysis does not enumerate

### C1. The `==` word cannot reach the identity-equality arms at all

`stdlib_logic_compare` accepts `INTEGER | FLOAT | CINTEGER | CFLOAT | TIME` for
both operands, or `STRING` for both, and bails with "COMPARE: unsupported
operand #1" otherwise
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/logic/logic_compare_fun.rs:17-70`).
`==` and `!=` are the only words that reach `PartialEq for Value`
(`:23,26,52,55`); the only other consumer in the tree is the
`HashMap<Value,Value>` inside `Val::ValueMap`, which F29 leaves unreadable.

So the "identity for sixteen payload kinds" contract — the load-bearing
justification for lazy identity in D1, and a preservation row in this RFC — is
**not reachable from any Bund program**. That is not an error in the RFC, and
it does not make the row wrong. It is the missing sentence that would let B1 be
resolved by deviation rather than by redesign, and it belongs in the RFC
because a reader currently cannot tell that `A == A.dup()` is a Rust-level
statement rather than a Bund-level one.

### C2. `stamp` has no row, no section, and no criterion

D2 is listed as consumed (RFC:5). It appears nowhere else in the document
except as a field in the struct (RFC:232). D2 says, in terms:

> This is a **deviation from 100% preservation**, and it must be listed in the
> Deviate section of whichever work item implements `Value`.

Three consequences D2 enumerates — `.timestamp` becoming observation time, the
METRICS `"ts"` field becoming iteration time, `get_timestamp_diff` becoming a
difference of observation times — appear in no preservation row here. Nor does
the mechanics: `regen_id` resets `stamp` alongside `id`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/id.rs:6-7`), and `set`/`push` refresh it
(`set.rs:32,59,92`, `push.rs:166`), which under deferred sampling means
resetting the `Cell` to unobserved. §"`dup` is an identity reset" says what
happens to identity and is silent on the stamp.

`.timestamp` is reachable — `register_method_timestamp` pushes
`Value::from_float(value.stamp)`
(`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions/oop/base_classes.rs:25`) — though only on
an `OBJECT`, since `VM::m` bails for any other type
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_object.rs:50-56`). That last
fact is worth stating in the RFC: it is why `.id` and `.timestamp` never need
to answer for a scalar, and it is a better argument than the one D1's scan
gives.

### C3. `Send`/`Sync` — review 1's E5, still unaddressed

The word does not appear in the RFC. `Rc<HeapValue>` with `Cell` fields is
neither `Send` nor `Sync`; the reference's `Value` is both. D6 and D7 are OPEN
and both are about threads.

### C4. Q18 closed on an incomplete scan

Q18 records `q` as grounded because "no word writes it: `set` copies it and
`push` copies it". Two writers exist that the scan did not find:

- `Value::set_q` (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/q.rs:8-9`) and `calc_q` (`:4-5`).
- `impl Add`, `Sub`, `Mul`, `Div for Value` average the operands' `q` and write
  it back — `reference/rust_dynamic/src/math.rs:416,419`, `:429,431`,
  `:440,442`, `:451,453`.

The conclusion survives, and only just: the arithmetic words bypass the
operator impls entirely and call `Value::numeric_op` directly
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/math/math_op.rs:8,103`,
`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions/values/merge.rs:12`), so `impl Add` and
friends are dead. `q` is constant in reach *because the `+` word does not use
`+`*. An implementer who wires Bund2's `+` through a Rust `Add` impl
reintroduces the write, and `q` is a rendered constant by then. That is worth
one sentence in the RFC and a correction to Q18.

### C5. `tags` changes container type

`HeapValue` declares `tags: BTreeMap<String, String>` (RFC:235); the reference
is `HashMap<String, String>` (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/value.rs:24`). For
two or more tags the `Debug` rendering order changes from nondeterministic to
sorted. This is very likely an improvement and is probably masked by the golden
normaliser's dict-member-order pass (F15), but it is an unlisted deviation in a
`Debug` output the RFC elsewhere insists must reproduce exactly.

### C6. Nested values do not carry the stack tag

RFC:140-144 says `tags` "is written on the hottest path… so a value on the main
stack renders `tags: {"stack": "main"}`". True for stack-resident values. Values
*inside* a container render `tags: {}` — confirmed against the oracle with
`list 99 attribute 7 push`, whose elements show `tags: {}` while the container
shows `tags: {"stack": "main"}`. The RFC's field-versus-constant argument is
unaffected; the sentence as written over-generalises.

## D. Acceptance criteria, one at a time

1. **16 bytes / align 8** — checkable, and reproduces today (`cargo xtask
   layout`, row D). But see the note below on what candidate D actually is.
2. **0 allocations for a scalar, candidate D** — checkable, reproduces (`D:
   construct a scalar 0`, `D: clone a scalar 0`).
3. **`dup` allocates 1** — **not checkable and not true.** B2.
4. **Clone-equal / dup-unequal, incl. a bool** — checkable, and the design
   fails the bool case. B1.
5. **`conform` does not fall below 0/65** — checkable; the mark reads 0/65 and
   RFC-0000's provenance table records both denominator moves (63→64, 64→65).
   Note `CONFORMANCE.txt`'s own comment block still says "59 goldens".
6. **258 renderings across 29 goldens, 255 with `attr: []`** — checkable, and
   all four numbers reproduce exactly.
7. **Wire format byte-identical for one value of each of the 20 payload kinds**
   — checkable in principle; nothing in `xtask` captures oracle bytes today, so
   this needs a harness that does not exist. Say which one.
8. **`.id` is 21 characters over the nanoid alphabet, no collisions in one VM**
   — the first half is checkable; "two values minted in one VM never collide"
   names no bound and no test. How many values, over what operation?
9. **`cargo tree -p bund2-value` lists no `bund2-interp`** — checkable, vacuous
   today, correctly labelled as such.
10. **`cargo xtask cite` reports zero defects** — passes today. Weak by
    construction, as the RFC's own review-history note says: it reports 33
    advisories, nine of them against RFC-0001, and it cannot check meaning.

**Criteria 1 and 2 measure a type that is not the one in §Design.** Candidate D
in `xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:128-161` has **five** arms — `Int`, `Float`, `Bool`,
`Nodata`, `Heap` — and no `None`; the RFC's `BundValue` has six. Its
`HeapTagged` carries `id`, `stamp`, `dt`, `payload` and nothing else; the RFC's
`HeapValue` adds `curr`, `tags`, `attr` and puts the payload behind a **second
`Rc`**. Neither difference moves `size_of::<BundValue>()`, and RFC:225's "the
discriminant has room" is almost certainly right — but the Summary says the RFC
is "accepted against [the measured figures], not against argument", and this is
argument. Either extend candidate D to the real shape or state the gap.

**Still missing a criterion:** the F28 fix (review 1 asked for it; the
preservation table still claims "Deliberately fixed" with nothing verifying
it), and now `stamp` (C2) and the header-reset behaviour (B3).

## E. F29/F30 — the blocker has been decided, and nothing has absorbed it

The owner decided on 2026-08-26: **hash by content, and mirror `set`'s
pass-through in the `get` word.** The RFC is now stale in four places — the
status line (RFC:3), the `Hash by identity` row (RFC:355), the `valuemap
write-only` row (RFC:365), and the Open-questions bullet (RFC:425-434, "This
RFC takes neither").

More importantly, **there is no entry in `docs/registers/decisions.md`.** F29's
disposition still reads "undecided… Blocked pending a decision alongside D29",
and F30's still reads "RFC-0001 must state which". Until a D-entry exists,
RFC-0002 and the `valuemap` work item have nothing to cite, and the next
session cannot tell the decision from a draft's assumption.

Four things the decision needs before it is implementable, none of which are
anywhere yet:

**E1. Content hashing is sound, and the RFC should say why.** The `Hash`/`Eq`
contract is one-directional: `a == b` must imply equal hashes. Unequal values
may collide freely. So hashing by content while equality stays identity-based
for the sixteen kinds is *correct* — scalar keys become findable, and
list/map/lambda keys stay identity-equal and merely share buckets. It also
removes hashing from D1's list of identity-materialisation triggers, which is a
small win the RFC should bank explicitly.

**E2. `PartialEq for Value` is asymmetric, and content hashing has to pin which
answer is canonical.** `eq.rs:10-17` casts the *other* operand to `i64`;
`eq.rs:18-26` casts it to `f64`. So `I64(42) == F64(42.5)` is true and
`F64(42.5) == I64(42)` is false. Confirmed against the oracle:

    42 42.5 ==     ->  false
    42.5 42 ==     ->  true

`Value` also asserts `impl Eq` (`eq.rs:58`), which requires symmetry, and
`Val::ValueMap` is a `HashMap` keyed by it. A content hash must decide whether
`42` and `42.5` share a bucket. This is not recorded in `defects.md` and is not
mentioned in F30, which discusses the `Hash`/`Eq` mismatch only.

**E3. "Hash by content" needs a per-arm rule for the float-bearing arms.**
`Val::F64`, `Val::Metrics`, `Val::Operator` and `Val::Json` all carry `f64`,
which is not `Hash`. NaN, `-0.0` versus `0.0`, and the `I64`/`F64` cross-type
equality from E2 all need an answer. Twenty arms, and the RFC currently
specifies zero of them.

**E4. The `get` mirror is a change to a *word*, not to the value.** F29 says no
read path exists and cites `get.rs:7`. There is a second obstacle it does not
record: the `get` word stringifies the key before `Value::get` is ever called —
`tag_name_val.cast_string()` at
`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/values/value_dict.rs:54` — while `set`'s
`VALUEMAP` branch passes the raw `Value` through to `set_vmap` (`:16-19`). So
the two halves of the word pair disagree about key type, and no change confined
to `rust_dynamic` can fix it. `?key` needs the same treatment
(`value_dict.rs:97`). Which RFC owns that — this one, or RFC-0002's word table
— is unstated.

Note also that F29 says `has_key` "has the same arm" as `get`. It does not:
`has_key.rs:7` omits `MESSAGE`, which `get.rs:7` includes.

## F. Assumed but not stated

Carried forward from review 1, still true: `Payload` is used (RFC:237) and
never defined, so its arm order — which bincode encodes positionally, and which
criterion 7 depends on — is assumed. Serialisation as the materialisation point
for both `Cell`s is D20's, never restated here. `id: 0` and `stamp: 0.0` as
reserved sentinels. The golden normaliser stripping `id`/`stamp` (F14) and dict
order (F15), which is what makes lazy identity invisible. Single-threadedness.
`dt` keeping the reference's exact `u16` values including the 92–99 gap. `Debug`
being hand-written and reproducing the field order *and* the payload arm
spellings.

New to this pass:

1. **That `.id` and `.timestamp` can only be called on an `OBJECT`.** `VM::m`
   bails with "VM there is no value of type OBJECT in the stack" for anything
   else (`multistackvm_object.rs:50-56`). This is what makes identity-free
   scalars safe, and the RFC leans on the conclusion without the premise.
2. **That the Bund word `push` is not `Value::push`.** A2.
3. **That `conv` can rewrite `dt` in place** (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/conv.rs`),
   so `dt` is mutable state behind the `Rc`, not a construction-time constant.
4. **That `ASSOCIATION` has no writer** and is a read-only tag in the dispatch
   arms.

## What would make this acceptable

1. Resolve B1: either bools and nulls become heap values, or the RFC states
   content equality for them as a deviation and grounds its unobservability on
   C1's type filter in `logic_compare_fun.rs:17-70`. Criterion 4 has to change
   either way.
2. Fix criterion 3 (B2), and add a `dup` row to `layout`'s allocation table so
   whatever number replaces "1" is measured rather than asserted.
3. Add the header-reset behaviour to the preservation analysis (B3), with a
   disposition. It is the one finding here that changes what `set` and `push`
   have to do.
4. Add a `stamp` row and D2's three deviations (C2).
5. Add `MESSAGE` to the payload→`dt` table (A3), and qualify A1, A2 and C6.
6. Record the F29/F30 decision in `decisions.md` and update the RFC's four
   stale spots, then answer E2 and E3 — the decision is not implementable until
   the float and asymmetry rules exist.
7. Add criteria for the wire format's harness (7), the F28 fix, and `stamp`;
   bound criterion 8's collision claim.
8. Extend candidate D to the shape §Design actually specifies, or say plainly
   that the six-arm/second-`Rc` difference is argued rather than measured.

## Defects this pass found that are not in the register

Recommended, not written — the register is the owner's to append:

- **Asymmetric `PartialEq`**: `42 42.5 ==` is false, `42.5 42 ==` is true
  (`eq.rs:10-26`), while `impl Eq` (`eq.rs:58`) asserts symmetry. Oracle-
  confirmed. Constrains the content-hash decision directly.
- **`get` stringifies the valuemap key** (`value_dict.rs:54`) while `set` does
  not (`:16-19`) — a second, independent reason F29 cannot be fixed inside
  `rust_dynamic`.
- **`set`/`push` clear `attr`, `curr` and `tags`** via `from_dict`/`from_list`/
  `from_valuemap`. Oracle-confirmed. Whether this is a defect or intended is a
  preservation call; either way it is unrecorded.
- **`Value::push` on a `RESULT` yields a `LIST`** (`push.rs:37,48`), losing the
  tag `set` is careful to restore.
- **`ASSOCIATION` has no writer** in `rust_dynamic`.
