## Blocking

### B1. Cohen's d interval is scaled the wrong way (§S12, line 612)

§S12 says d's interval is "the ncp whose t-tail is α/2 at the observed t,
… scaled by √(nₐn_b/(nₐ+n_b))". `stat.effect.cohend`'s d is the mean
difference over the pooled SD (`effect.rs`, the module doc). The pooled
two-sample t is t = d·√(nₐn_b/(nₐ+n_b)), so the noncentrality is
δ = d·√(nₐn_b/(nₐ+n_b)), and an end for d is
δ_end·√((nₐ+n_b)/(nₐn_b)). That is the reciprocal of the stated factor.
Read as "multiply by", the interval would be wrong by a factor of
nₐn_b/(nₐ+n_b), which is 25 at nₐ = n_b = 50. Criterion 7 checks it
against "the fixture's noncentral-t inversion", which would be written
from the same sentence.

**Fix:** state the end as δ_end·√(1/nₐ + 1/n_b). Also state which ncp each
end is: the lower end's ncp leaves the upper tail α/2 at the observed t,
and the upper end's leaves the lower tail α/2.

### B2. Criterion 10's interval check has no fixture it can be checked against

Criterion 10 says "each interval equals the fixture's formula applied to
the word's own replicates" (line 764). The replicates come from Bund2's
`StdRng`. The mpmath fixtures are static files written by Python
(acceptance criteria preamble), and Python cannot reproduce `rand` 0.8's
ChaCha stream. So no committed fixture can hold "the word's own
replicates", and the criterion names no checker that can run.

**Fix: choose one route.**
- The interval arithmetic gets a crate-internal function. The fixture
  applies `boot`'s formulas to a committed replicate vector at 50 digits,
  and the test runs the internal function on that vector.
- Or the test re-implements `norm.inter`, `basic.ci`, `perc.ci` and
  `bca.ci` independently in Rust over the word's replicates. This is
  weaker, since the same author writes both.

The first matches how RFC-0018's criteria were written. Criterion 10's
other clauses (repeatability, equality with a hand loop) stand.

## Should fix

### SF1. Two `bootfuns.q` citations are off (lines 256, 523)

- **Line 256**, `:1199-1209`, is cited for w = Φ⁻¹(#{t* < t₀}/R) and
  a = ΣL³/(6(ΣL²)^1.5). In `bca.ci`, w is at `:1205`, its check at
  `:1206`, a at `:1211` and its check at `:1212`. Lines 1199-1204 are
  the function's opening (`t.o <- t`, the `is.null` branch). The range
  should be `:1205-1213`.
- **Line 523**, `:1200`, `:1206`, is cited for "`boot` stops too" when w
  or a is not finite. The stops are at `:1206` and `:1212`. `:1200` is
  `t <- boot.out$t[,index]`.

### SF2. `stat.count` is cited to a file that does not register it (line 109)

Line 109 says the reference's `stat.` names are "the `stat.count` and
`stat.mean.*` families" and cites `statistics.rs:185-215`. Those lines
register `stat.mean.*` only. `stat.count` and its forms are registered
in `reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions/statistics/count.rs:52-55`. Add
that citation.

Also on line 106: "513 names" is the number of distinct literal
`register_inline("…")` names, as stated. Coverage's denominator is 602
in-scope words (§Current behaviour quotes COVERAGE 577/602), which the
reader may take for the same registry. Say that the 513 are literal
registrations, and that coverage's in-scope set is a different count,
or drop the number.

### SF3. The noncentral t is named AS 243 but described as a different series (lines 322-330), and criterion 2 asks for an ncp where AS 243 fails

- **What AS 243 does.** Lenth's algorithm sums from the Poisson term 0
  upward: R's `pnt` sets p = ½·exp(−λ/2) at the first term
  (`pnt.c:100-101`). It underflows to 0 when λ = ncp² is large. R then
  warns and returns 0 (`pnt.c:105-110`), and it returns 0 outright for
  ncp > 40 in one tail (`pnt.c:75`). Its own comment says the result is
  unreliable for ncp > 100 (`pnt.c:49-50`).
- **What §S2 describes.** A series that "starts at the Poisson mode and
  sums outward", which is not AS 243.
- **Criterion 2** asks for "a large ncp (100)" (line 711), which AS 243
  cannot reach.

**Fix:** drop "as Lenth's AS 243, which R's `pnt` is" and describe the
mode-outward series as Bund2's own. Note that R's `pnt` answers 0 or
inaccurate values beyond ncp ≈ 38 to 40, so R is not a check there; the
50-digit fixture is. Or keep AS 243 and lower criterion 2's ncp. The
first is what §S2's other two families already do.

### SF4. The studentized range accepts df in (1, 2), which R refuses (line 354)

§S3 takes df > 1. R's `ptukey` and `qtukey` both return NaN with a
warning for df < 2 (`ptukey.c:321`; `qtukey.c`, `if (df < 2 || rr < 1
|| cc < 2)`). Accepting (1, 2) is a defensible choice, since the integral
is defined there. But it is a difference from R that §What R does does
not list, and that criterion 3's grid should include. State it, or use
df ≥ 2.

### SF5. Three refusals and rules are stated without their source, or not stated at all

- **CMH's per-stratum size** (line 440): "A stratum with n < 2 is E101,
  as R stops on it". R's text is "sample size in each stratum must be
  > 1" (`mantelhaen.test.R:49-50`). Cite it. R also stops on any table
  dimension below 2 (`:28`), which for a LIST of 2×2 MATRIXes is a
  non-2×2 stratum. §S5 does not say what a 3×2 MATRIX in `tables` is
  (E102 or E101).
- **Nelder–Mead's stop** (line 561): "the spread of values falls below
  `reltol`·(|f| + `reltol`)". In R, f is the value at the start, computed
  once (`appl/optim.c:302`), and the test is VH ≤ VL + convtol
  (`:367`). Say "|f(x0)|" and cite both.
- **Shapiro–Wilk on identical values.** R refuses them ("all 'x' values
  are identical", `shapiro.test.R:28`). §S5's refusals do not mention a
  constant sample. `swilk` would divide by a zero sum of squares there.

### SF6. A Hessian that is not positive definite is filed under E106, whose text says "singular" (line 590)

§S11 refuses a non-positive-definite Hessian at `par` with "E106 (`the
matrix is singular`, or a text naming the non-positive pivot)".
- E106's template is "the matrix is singular" (`refuse.rs`,
  `Refusal::Singular`). An indefinite Hessian is often not singular, so
  the text would be false.
- The alternative, "a text naming the non-positive pivot", is not a
  text, so criterion 12 cannot check it.

**Fix:** choose one.
- E101 on `start` or `loglik`, with a stated text ("the Hessian of
  −loglik at par is not positive definite: pivot 2 is −0.3");
- or E106's `SingularColumn`-style variant with its own template, which
  RFC-0018 added for designs (`refuse.rs`, `Refusal::SingularColumn`).

### SF7. `alternative` takes different values in `power.` than everywhere else, and NONE as an option value is new (lines 371, 377)

- **`alternative`.** RFC-0016 §S1 fixes it to `"two.sided"`, `"less"`
  or `"greater"`, and §S1 of this RFC repeats that. `power.t` and
  `power.prop` take `"two.sided"` or `"one.sided"` (line 371), as R's
  do. A program would pass `"greater"` to `power.t` and get E101. Either
  say plainly that `power.`'s `alternative` is R's sidedness, a
  different key meaning, and name it `sides` (1 or 2); or accept
  `"less"` and `"greater"` as one-sided.
- **NONE as an option value** (line 377): "unless the program sets it
  explicitly to NONE". RFC-0016 §S1's options rules make a value of the
  wrong type E101, and no options MAP in the family takes NONE today.
  This is a new rule, and the options reader `power.` uses needs it. It
  should be stated as such in §S1, with its interaction with RFC-0017's
  NONE (D252).

## Notes

- **N1. Which function each named statistic is** (lines 499, 836). "Each
  computed by the named `stat.` word's own function": `"mean"` could be
  Bund2's `stat.mean` or the reference's `stat.mean.arithmetic`, and
  `"sd"` and `"var"` could each be one of two variance conventions. Name
  the word, as RFC-0017 named `frame.aggregate`'s.
- **N2. The initial simplex** (line 561). R's step is 0.1·max|x0ᵢ| over
  all coordinates, one size for every vertex (`appl/optim.c:313-318`
  loop), which §S10 states. Worth saying it is one size, not per
  coordinate, since that is the surprising part.
- **N3. Named statistics on a frame.** `boot.sample` takes a frame
  (N12), and the named statistics are defined "over a LIST of numbers".
  A name with frame data is unspecified: E101, presumably.
- **N4. `stat.test.permutation`'s default statistic** is chosen "when
  the operand is NONE" (§S9). That is a NONE operand, under D252's rule
  that numeric operands refuse NONE. Say it is a deliberate exception,
  or use `"mean.diff"` explicitly with no default.
- **N5. The power solve's interval for `sd` and `delta`** depends on
  the other's value (R: `delta * c(1e-7, 1e7)`). With `delta` 0 the
  interval is empty. §S4's domains do not refuse `delta` 0 when solving
  for `sd`.
- **N6. `rng.derive`'s cost to other streams.** Preservation notes that
  derive advances the generator. Worth adding to §S7 that a program
  wanting the parent's stream unchanged by derivation must derive all
  child seeds first.
- **N7. `stat.effect.cohend.opts` and the counting tests.** §S13 names
  `dist/tests.rs`, `infer`'s and `fit/tests.rs`. It does not name
  `describe/tests.rs`'s rule that every other `stat.` name outside
  `stat.count*` and `stat.mean*` is in `describe`'s `WORDS` or excluded,
  which `effect.rs`'s names already go through (RFC-0016 §Preservation
  analysis). `stat.effect.cohend.opts` must join `effect`'s `WORDS`.
- **N8. Cost of `fit.mle`'s profile intervals under D39.** Each profile
  end is a root-find whose every step is a full re-optimisation of the
  other k − 1 parameters. §S11 has no cap on total lambda calls; only
  `optim.`'s per-run caps bound it, to about k·2·1000·100·2k calls. D39
  allows a program's own cost, but a stated cap or an E111 on the
  product would make the worst case visible.

## Preservation

The table is complete for what the words touch, with two additions:
- **The counting tests** (N7): `describe/tests.rs` as well as the three
  named.
- **The `SWITCHES` and `REENTERING` audits** (`lib.rs`,
  `every_native_that_changes_the_current_stack_is_named` and its
  sibling). They scan source for stack switching and evaluation. The
  new lambda words are unaffected only if they reach the VM solely
  through `dynamics.rs`'s `call` and `kb::bridge::ask`, which are
  already listed. A word that calls `ask` or `scoped_call` itself would
  fail the scan until listed. Say so in §S13, so that a milestone that
  writes a reader of one FLOAT beside `call` (§S1) knows where it must
  live.

## What the RFC assumes and does not state

- **U1.** That `rand` 0.8's `StdRng` is ChaCha12, and that `seed_from_u64`
  of distinct derived seeds gives streams that do not overlap. This is
  Q66, marked [UNGROUNDED]: stated, but not as an assumption the
  criteria lean on. Criterion 9 does not lean on it.
- **U2.** That `boot.sample`'s draw order is replicate-major (all n draws
  of replicate 1, then replicate 2). Criterion 10's equality with "a
  Bund program's own loop of `rng.choice.replace`" requires it, and
  §S8 does not say so.
- **U3.** That a frame resampled by row keeps its levels and column
  order, as `Frame::rows` does (`frame/check.rs`, `Frame::rows`). §S8
  says only "by row".
- **U4.** That the one-FLOAT lambda reader §S1 mentions ("a sibling of
  it reading one FLOAT") refuses as `call` does: E112 with `call`'s
  "a LIST of n finite numbers" text adapted. The text is not given.
- **U5.** That `optim.integrate`'s infinite-end STRINGs are the only
  STRINGs either end may be. Any other STRING is presumably E101, but
  this is not stated.
- **U6.** That R 4.4.2 is available on macOS from CRAN for the owner's
  machine. N10 rules that it is installed, but the RFC does not say
  whether a later R would be refused if 4.4.2 cannot be installed.
