# RFC-0014 review: Draft revision 9, 2026-11-03 (eighth review)

Adversarial review, independent of the seven before it: the RFC and SMYSL
were read first, and the earlier reviews only afterwards, to drop
findings already raised and answered. Nothing was edited or built, apart
from running `cargo xtask cite`. Read against Bund2's working tree at
61ed15a, the `reference/` submodules (whose `git status` is empty), and
SMYSL at 4f5ecb9 ("Merge 1.14.0"), opened file by file. SMYSL citations
are relative to its crate directory, as the RFC writes them. The
revision 9 changes were checked hardest: §Current "The library" and "The
locks", §S1's CI steps, §S4's rule T reason and `smysl.label`, §S5's
measure `Input`, §S6's runner, §S8, §S9's amended R25 and the unlock
function, the plan, Preservation, criteria 10, 12, 15, 17 and 18, and
D245's 2026-11-03 note. R25 was tested against every write path in
`ShardLog::append`, `Library::append_records` and `Library::add`, and
against every case of `shards::stamp`.

**Verdict: Ready once the should-fix items are answered. 0 blocking,
3 should-fix, 12 notes.** D244 and D245 are not re-argued. The amended
R25 does what revision 9 says it does in the direction that matters: no
whole record of a failed batch escapes it (see "R25, tested" below). Its
torn-tail clause errs the other way further than the RFC admits. It keeps
the locks after refusals that write nothing, and the RFC says in so many
words that those keep no lock (S2). The other two should-fix items are
older: `smysl.create` has no lock sequence (S1), and SM02's text has a
slot most SMYSL errors cannot fill (S3).

### R25, tested

The question was whether the amended rule can miss a whole record of a
failed batch. It cannot, apart from what R22 already concedes:

- **Inside `ShardLog::append`** (`shards.rs:341-385`), every step that
  changes a file changes its length, except one: the torn-tail cut
  (`:376-379`), after which a write can restore the old length. The
  torn-tail clause covers that cut. `create(true)` on an absent log makes
  a file of length 0, which the RFC counts as 0 before. So that step
  changes nothing.
- **In `append_records`**, every failure after a log is written leaves
  that log longer: `ShardLog::read` of a later new shard, a later
  shard's `append`, or `Store::append` (`library.rs:1364-1382`).
  `append_uids` cannot fail (`shards.rs:562-599`). `Library::add` writes
  only objects before its one `append_records` (`library.rs:1247-1317`),
  and objects are written to `objects/tmp` and renamed into place
  (`smysl-text/src/objects.rs:241-264`).
- **The layout marker** is rewritten before any log
  (`library.rs:1357-1362`). It is not a log, and R25 does not see it.
  That is not a lost record, but see N3.
- **Primary shards** cannot reach the comparison. A build without
  `store-redb` fails the open on one (`shards.rs:307-321`), so no
  primary log enters the stamp.
- **The arithmetic.** The stamp's length is the sum of `len` over the
  shards with `len > 0` and the catalog. Logs with `len = 0` add nothing,
  and with one live log the stamp is that log's length, which is the same
  sum. For each log the open read, its on-disk length is `len` plus
  `torn`. So the total on disk minus the stamp's length is the sum of the
  torn tails, and "exceeds" means "some log is torn". That holds in every
  case of `stamp`. It needs two things the RFC does not state:
  - the files measured are exactly the files the open read. Note N1
    gives cases where they are not, all of which over-lock;
  - no log is shorter on disk than the length decoded from it. That can
    fail only if something truncates a log after Bund2's second open. One
    of R22's writers can do that, by cutting what it took for a torn tail
    (`shards.rs:376-379`). A torn tail elsewhere can then be masked. This
    is inside R22's hazard, but "It never misses a whole record" should
    say "outside R22's hazard".

So the rule never misses a whole record. Where it errs, it over-locks,
and it over-locks more than the RFC says. That is S2.

## Should fix

### S1. `smysl.create` has no lock sequence. §S9's step 1 cannot open a library that does not exist

The RFC says:

> "W" marks the words that write; every write is made under §S9's locks.
> (§S4; `smysl.create` is marked W)

> Every write takes SMYSL's locks … then **re-opens** the library:
> 1. open the library; 2. read its shard names … (§S9)

> `smysl.create` writes, which is why it waits for M3 rather than joining
> `smysl.open` in M2. (Implementation plan, M3)

For a new root there is no library to open at step 1. `Library::open`
reads the `LIBRARY` marker and fails without it
(`smysl-text/src/library.rs:303-304`). `Library::create` writes that
marker, then calls `open` (`:288-299`). SMYSL never creates a library
under its locks. `text add` calls `Library::create` and only then takes
the locks (`smysl:src/main.rs:12645-12656`), and so does `with_library`
with `create` set (`:8618-8631`). The RFC does not say which order
`smysl.create` uses, or whether it locks at all.

The order matters, because `create` is not safe against a concurrent
open. It checks `marker.is_file()` and then writes the marker with
`std::fs::write` (`library.rs:290-293`). That truncates the file and then
writes it, so it is not atomic. Opens take no lock, so any reader can
open the library at that moment. A reader that finds the marker empty
fails with "layout `` is not `smysl/library/2`" (`:304-319`). Neither
SMYSL nor the RFC guards against this.

**Needed.** State `smysl.create`'s sequence. One option is SMYSL's: run
`Library::create` under the process mutex and without SMYSL's locks,
because there is nothing yet to lock, then return. Another is to take the
library lock first (`Lock::acquire` creates the root's directory,
`smysl-graph/src/lock.rs:139-144`), then create and re-open. Either way,
say what a concurrent open of a half-created library meets: SM01 or
SM02. Say also why M3 is the right home if `create` takes no SMYSL lock.

### S2. R25's torn-tail clause keeps the locks after refusals that write nothing, which the RFC says cannot happen

§S9 under R25 says:

> **So does a failed call over a library that held a torn tail.** …
> A failed call over such a library keeps the locks whatever the lengths
> say.

and a few lines later:

> So every refusal that writes no log leaves no lock: `check_loggable`'s
> (`:1347-1354`), a language-less `add`, an I/O error before the first
> shard.

D245's note says the intent is "that the locks stay **exactly** when part
of a batch may be on disk".

The two sentences contradict each other whenever any log holds a torn
tail. The torn-tail test counts the logs' total on disk against the
stamp, so it cannot tell which log is torn, or whether the call reached
it. The cases are not only the unreachable `check_loggable` one.
`Library::add` refuses, before it writes anything, on:

- a `carry: "text"` the licence does not permit (`SMY-E402`,
  `library.rs:1148`);
- a reader cap other than input size (`:1155-1156`, `Budget`);
- a structure the reader cannot build (`:1167-1174`);
- a part policy that finds no part (`NoParts`, `:1184-1190`), which an
  empty `text` is likely to meet.

All four are refusals of the program's own input. Over a library with a
torn tail in any log, each one now keeps every lock. The word answers
SM02 "left locked", and the library refuses all writers until an
operator runs `bund2 smysl-unlock`. A torn tail is what a crashed SMYSL
process leaves, and also what R22's writers leave. SMYSL tolerates it
silently. So the trigger is not exotic: one crash, then one mistyped
`smysl.text.add`, locks the library for every writer. Criterion 15's
"A failure that writes nothing leaves no lock" passes only because its
fixture has no torn tail.

The over-approximation can be made exact cheaply. A cut followed by a
write is the only way a log's contents can change while its length stays
the same. For each log that is torn before the call, Bund2 can record
its tail: the bytes past the decoded length. `smysl_core::from_cbor_seq`
is public and gives that length, as `ShardLog::read` uses it
(`shards.rs:35,239-251`). After a failure, that log is unchanged exactly
when its length is unchanged and its tail is byte-for-byte the same.

**Needed.** Do one of these:

- make the test per log, as above, so that a refusal that writes nothing
  keeps no lock even over a torn library; or
- keep the total-based clause, withdraw "every refusal that writes no log
  leaves no lock", state the case in §S9, Preservation and D245's note
  (whose "exactly" is then wrong), and add a criterion 15 case: a
  `smysl.text.add` refused for `NoParts` over a torn library keeps the
  locks.

The first restates R25's test again. That is the owner's to rule, as the
2026-11-03 amendment was.

### S3. SM02's text has a `<code>` slot that most of its causes cannot fill, and the message already carries the code

§S8 gives SM02 the text `SMYSL refused it: <code> <message>`, for "SMYSL
refused the operation, or a SMYSL call failed with an I/O error",
including a lock that could not be linked and a primary shard.

- **Most `LibError` variants have no code.** `LibError::code()` returns
  `None` for `Io`, `BadParam`, `NoParts`, `Unreadable`, `NotText`,
  `BadAlias`, `Redacted` and `DerivedContention`
  (`smysl-core/src/error.rs:858-878`). Every I/O error SM02 names is
  `LibError::Io`: the hard-link failure (`smysl-graph/src/lock.rs:177`),
  the primary shard (`smysl-text/src/shards.rs:314-320`), and the
  read-only log of criterion 15 (`shards.rs:375`). So is SMYSL's own
  language refusal (`BadParam`, `library.rs:1196-1202`).
- **The code is already in the message.** `LibError`'s `Display` begins
  with the code wherever there is one, for example
  `"{}: {cap} limit {limit} exceeded by {saw}"` with `Code::E440`
  (`error.rs:905-916`), and `"{}: {what} is locked by {held_by}"`
  (`:925-926`). Read as `Display`, `<message>` repeats `<code>`.
- **R25's answer has its own text.** §S9 says the word "answers SM02,
  saying the library is left locked". §S8 gives that no text.

Criterion 14 asks for "a test asserting §S8's text" for each code, and
criterion 15 asserts the "left locked" answer. Neither can be written
until the text is defined for an error with no code.

**Needed.** Define SM02's text for a `LibError` with no code (for
example, `SMYSL refused it: <message>`). Say whether `<message>` is
`Display`, and if it is, drop the separate `<code>`. Give a
`ShapeError`'s form too: it has a code, through `ShapeError::code`
(`error.rs:143-150`). Give the "left locked" text.

## Notes

- **N1. R25's files and its error arm.**
  - **Which files.** The RFC names "`log/catalog.cbor` and every
    `*.cbor` under `log/shards`", each read at the path `log_path`
    gives. `shards::list` is public and returns exactly the
    `(name, path)` pairs the open reads (`shards.rs:87-113`). It should
    be the source. A directory listing differs from it in three cases:
    - a stray `log/shards/catalog.cbor`, which `list` maps to the
      catalog's own path and the open never reads;
    - a file stem that does not round-trip through `name_of_stem` and
      `stem`, such as one holding `:`;
    - a `.primary` marker, which `list` names and the listing does not.

    Each of the first two adds bytes to the disk total that the stamp
    lacks. So the torn-tail clause fires on every failed call, for good.
  - **Errors.** The RFC gives no arm for a `metadata` or `read_dir` call
    that fails, before or after. Under "Bund2 does not panic", this
    needs one, and it should keep the locks.
- **N2. Two places omit the torn-tail clause.** The guard's description
  in §S9 forgets the locks "while the thread is panicking … or after a
  failed call that changed a log's length". The Preservation row "SMYSL's
  lock as a crash notice" also names only a panic and a changed length.
  Both should add "or that ran over a torn tail", or whatever S2 makes of
  it.
- **N3. A failed write can raise the layout.** In a layout-1 library,
  `append_records` rewrites the marker before the first log
  (`library.rs:1357-1362`). The write is non-atomic, as in S1. If the
  first shard then fails with nothing written, R25 drops the locks, and
  the library is now layout 2 with no new shard. If the marker write
  itself fails part way, the library cannot be opened at all
  (`:304-319`). The Preservation row "A library's layout" says only
  that a write that *creates* a shard raises it.
- **N4. "Undecodable tail" should be "truncated tail".** See the second
  list in "Citations checked". Any other decode error fails the open,
  and that is SM02, not a tolerated tail. R25's torn-tail clause
  inherits the same definition.
- **N5. The plan's CI count is stale.** §S1 lists seven steps. M1 claims
  "five of §S1's seven CI steps, all but the probe's nextest run and
  clippy run … and the `smysl_demo` run". Seven less three is four. M10's
  "the fifth CI step, which runs it" is revision 8's numbering: the demo
  run is now the third bullet of §S1. Also, §S1 calls the `CC=false`
  build "the same build" as the feature matrix's `cargo check`, while
  criterion 3 says `cargo build -p bund2-cli`. Name one command.
- **N6. Criterion 15's torn-tail case names no failing call.** "Over a
  library whose log was given a torn tail by hand before the open, a
  failed call keeps the locks although no length changed." M3 has only
  `smysl.unit`. The read-only log of the failed-append case would serve:
  give the read-only log the torn tail, so that the open fails before
  the cut. Say so.
- **N7. "Warnings from appends" have no source.** `append_records`
  returns `Result<(), LibError>` (`library.rs:1341`). It maps
  `Store::append`'s error and drops its report (`:1379-1382`), so a
  library write yields no warnings. What a write does report is in
  `Added`: `lossy` and `objects_redacted` (`library.rs:163-187`), which
  SMYSL's `text add` prints (`smysl:src/main.rs:12679-12692`).
  `smysl.text.add` answers only `{ mid, parts }` and drops both. Either
  map them, or say they are dropped. Then criterion 18's "a write's
  warnings" has nothing to drive.
- **N8. Bund2 leaves SMYSL's index sidecar stale.** SMYSL's `text add`
  calls `complete_index` under the locks and `persist_index` afterwards
  (`smysl:src/main.rs:12667,12680`; `library.rs:416-424,494-509`).
  §S4's `smysl.text.add` does neither. So a library Bund2 writes keeps a
  sidecar whose stamp no longer matches, and every open rebuilds the
  catalog tables (`library.rs:390-398`) until a SMYSL write persists one.
  That costs speed, not meaning, and bears on Q54. A Preservation row
  should say it.
- **N9. Measure results: the no-basis arm cannot be reached, and the arm
  that can has no mapping.** `scope::resolve` always gives a basis
  (`smysl-measure/src/scope.rs:113-117`), so §S5's `SMY-W483` case does
  not arise from `smysl.measure`. `to_unit_on` can still refuse a result
  that ran, as `NotPersisted::Unwritable`: a value past a payload
  integer, or a unit that does not build, both `SMY-E489`
  (`smysl-measure/src/lib.rs:416-417,446-458`). Its `uid` and where its
  diagnostic goes are unstated. That diagnostic is not in
  `MeasureResult::diagnostics`: `to_unit_on` returns it.
- **N10. §S4's `Transformed` sentence.** Drop "from the unit they were
  computed from", or cite something that says it (see "Citations
  checked"). The argument does not need it.
- **N11. Labels within one `smysl.append`.**
  - **SM08's text.** It is `label <label> binds <n> units`. For the
    bind-time refusal revision 9 added, where the library binds the
    label to one other unit, it would read "binds 1 units" and not say
    why the binding was refused.
  - **Two MAPs, one label.** The rule checks only what "the library
    already binds". Two label MAPs in one `smysl.append` that bind one
    label to two units would write both and leave the label ambiguous.
  - **Order within a batch.** The RFC does not say whether a label
    bound earlier in a LIST resolves `grounds` or `deps` later in the
    same LIST.
- **N12. The `--noio` stubs' effects.** D119's stubs are registered
  `StackEffect::opaque(0)` (`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/data.rs`, `register`).
  §S8 does not say whether each `smysl.` stub keeps its word's fixed
  effect or becomes opaque. That changes what `bund2 check` says of a
  program run under `--noio`.

## Preservation: what is missing

- **A library locked by a refused input** (S2). Over a library with a
  torn tail, a `smysl.text.add` that SMYSL refuses before it writes
  anything leaves every lock in place.
- **The layout raised by a failed write** (N3). The layout row covers
  only a write that creates a shard.
- **SMYSL's index sidecar** (N8). It is left stale by every Bund2 write,
  where SMYSL's own `text add` rewrites it.
- **The crash-notice row** omits the torn-tail clause (N2).
- **What `smysl.text.add` drops** (N7): `lossy` and the count of redacted
  objects, which SMYSL reports.

## Consistency

- **D245.** The 2026-11-03 note matches revision 9's text, but says the
  locks stay "exactly when part of a batch may be on disk". The RFC
  itself says the test over-approximates, and S2 shows by how much. The
  note's word "exactly" is contradicted by the design it records. The
  ruling is not re-argued here.
- **D244 R20.** "a batch `check_loggable` refuses keeps no lock". Under
  the torn-tail clause this fails over a torn library (S2).
- **D119.** Consumed, and the conditional's handler is now stubbed as
  D119 stubs `csv`'s and `sqlite`'s. Only the stubs' effects are open
  (N12).
- **RFC-0013.** §S6's runner matches §S11: it uses `eval_lambda`, the
  lambda runs on the current stack, and `bund2 check` joins the per-row
  arm. The stated difference over a missing `lambda` stands. §S7's node
  table matches §S15 and D230.
- **ERRATA.** `docs/research/ERRATA.md` does not mention SMYSL or
  RFC-0014, and nothing here contradicts a research document.
- **Registers.** Q51's and Q57's revision 9 notes in
  `docs/registers/open-questions.md` match the RFC. Q51's note inherits
  N4's "torn" wording.

## Assumptions the RFC makes but does not state

1. **A library exists before a write's first step**, which
   `smysl.create` breaks (S1).
2. **A torn tail lies in a log the failed call touched** (S2).
3. **Every SMYSL error has a code** (S3).
4. **The files R25 measures are the files the open read, and reading
   their lengths cannot fail** (N1).
5. **No log is shorter on disk than its decoded length while Bund2 holds
   the locks.** Only R22's writers can break this, and when they do they
   can mask a torn tail ("R25, tested").
6. **Each word makes one `append_records` or `Library::add` call per
   acquisition of the locks.** R25 snapshots the lengths "before it
   calls", per call. A word that made two calls, such as units and then
   their attestations, could have the second fail with nothing written.
   It would then drop the locks with the first batch on disk, which is
   half of the word's write. Say that `smysl.unit`, `smysl.append`,
   `smysl.retract` and `smysl.prove` each write one batch. Or take the
   snapshot once, before the first call.
7. **A refused `smysl.text.add` writes no objects worth reporting.**
   Every refusal in `add_with` before line 1243 writes nothing, and a
   failure after it leaves only objects no manifest names. That holds
   (`library.rs:1148-1317`), and the RFC relies on it without saying so.
