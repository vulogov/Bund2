## Blocking

**B1 — Under `--noio` alone a program reads any file it can name and fetches
any `http://` URL, and runs what it gets. §B3a's two lists for the flag name
neither route, and D119 was ruled without them.**

D78, in the paragraph that rules out the word "sandbox": "a `--noio`
artefact can still pull and evaluate remote code", and "a builder who
believed `--noio` protected that person would be wrong. RFC-0006 states what
each flag leaves ungated, by name."

§B3a, "What `--noio` leaves ungated": "Of the 550 names the flag does not
touch … these reach outside the program". Eight bullets follow — two data
files (gated since D119), the history files, standard input, the host's
identity, the command line, the clock, standard output, ending the process.
Then: "a runner who reads `restrictions --noio` that way is wrong about
standard input, the address of the machine and the history file."

The reference (`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions/bund/`):

- `bund_use.rs:31` — `use` hands its operand to `get_file_from_uri`, and
  `:33` evaluates the answer. Its registration is switched on `cli.noeval`
  alone (`:74-80`).
- `bund_eval.rs:65` — `bund.eval-file` hands its operand to
  `get_file_from_file`, which is `get_file_from_uri(format!("file://{}", …))`
  (`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/helpers/file_helper.rs:57-59`), and `:67`
  evaluates the answer. Switched on `cli.noeval` alone (`:117-124`).

Bund2 does the same: `use_word` and `bund_eval_file_base` both call
`fetch_uri` (`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/host.rs`,
`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/singles.rs`), and `--noio` stubs neither.

Measured 2026-10-09. `lib.bund` is `"B" println`; the listener answers
`/lib.bund` with `"A" println`. Each program is the whole file.

| program | oracle, `--noio` | `bund2 script --noio` | bundle built `--noio` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `"http://127.0.0.1:<port>/lib.bund" use` | `A` | `A` | `A` |
| `"file:///<dir>/lib.bund" use` | `B` | `B` | `B` |
| `"/<dir>/lib.bund" bund.eval-file` | `B` | `B` | `B` |
| `"/<dir>/lib.bund" file println` (control) | `bund FILE functions disabled with --noio` | the same | — |

The listener received the request from all three runners.
`bund2 build --inspect` on the first bundle prints `restrictions   --noio`.
And `"/etc/hosts" bund.eval-file` under `--noio` opens the file on both
binaries: each fails in the parser, and the oracle's report prints the line
it stopped at.

**How the list lost it.**

- The 2026-09-29 text said `--noio` "does not gate fetching at all". The
  third review showed that was half wrong — `url` and `file` are `--noio`
  stubs — and the correction kept the wrong half's replacement and dropped
  the right half. What is left of `use` under `--noio` is one clause, "`use`
  is gated by `--noeval`", in a paragraph about the other flag.
- The 2026-10-08 rewrite says how the ungated list was checked: "every file
  under `reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions` that touches a file, a socket
  or a process and carries no `disabled with` stub". `bund_use.rs` and
  `bund_eval.rs` each carry a stub — `disabled with --noeval`
  (`bund_use.rs:63`, `bund_eval.rs:106`). The filter asked whether a file
  has a stub, not whether it has this flag's.
- `bund.eval-file` is in the RFC six times and D54 not at all. Every
  mention is of the word as evaluation. None is of it as a read.

**Why it blocks.**

- It is D78's own requirement, and the route missing is the one D78 gives
  as the reason for the requirement. The seventh to tenth reviews each
  blocked on a route `--noeval` left unnamed; this is the same finding for
  the other flag, and the remote one.
- **D119 was decided on the survey this is missing from.** D119: "Running
  every registered name under the flag showed two words that read a file and
  are not gated." There were six. `"<path>" bund.eval-file` reads a file the
  program names exactly as `csv` does, and D119's "What it does not change"
  lists what stays ungated — standard input, the host's address and name,
  the clock, the history file — without it. So the owner gated the two
  words that read a data file while the four that read a file and run it
  were not on the page. Whether the ruling would have named two words or six
  is not something the RFC can assume either way.
- The preservation row for the flags repeats the short list: "for `--noio`
  that includes two words that write a history file, standard input, and the
  host's address and name."
- No criterion sees it. Criterion 10 tries `--noio`'s floor with `fs.cwd`.

**What it needs.** A ruling, in the shape of D119 or of D122, and it is not
mine to predict: gate the four under `--noio` — a deviation from the
reference, as D119 was, and one that makes `--noio` and `--noeval` overlap —
or leave them and name them, as D122 and D123 did. Either way: the four in
§B3a by name, under the flag they escape; the summary sentence and the
preservation row corrected; the method sentence saying which flag's stub it
looked for; and a note on D119 that its survey was short.

**B2 — The fifteenth review's B1 stands, and is reproduced.** §B7 says "for
two shapes they decide differently" and "in none do the two evaluate
different text". That review found twenty shapes of response, in three
directions, of which D129 approves one. Tried again here, independently, with
a different listener:

| the response | oracle | Bund2 |
|---|---|---|
| a `302` with a `Location`, a body, no `Content-Length` | `url` returns the body; `use` evaluates it | `url` returns the empty string; `use` evaluates nothing and the program goes on |
| **a `301`, the same** — not tried by the fifteenth review | the same | the same |
| a `302` with a `Content-Length` (control) | the body | the body |
| `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` given twice | the fetch fails: "chunk hex-length char not a hex digit" | **`use` evaluates the body** |
| a plain `200` (control) | evaluates | evaluates |

So the redirect is not one status, and it is the missing length that
decides it: with a length the two agree. The fourth row is the direction
nothing approves — Bund2 runs what the reference will not read.

Nothing here adds to what that review asks for. It is listed as blocking
because it is, and because this review's verdict would otherwise read as
though one finding stood between the RFC and acceptance.

## Should fix

**S1 — The fetch has eight words, and the RFC and D54 say six.** The
preservation row is headed "`use`, `use.`, `url`, `url.`, `file`, `file.`",
and was corrected at the fourteenth review from three. §B7: "`url` and
`url.` share `fetch_uri` and all of this." D54: "The reference fetches
through libcurl in three places". There is a fourth,
`bund_eval.rs:65`, and Bund2 follows it through `fetch_uri`
(`singles.rs`, `bund_eval_file_base`, whose comment says so). So every row
of §B7 that is about a `file:` path is about `bund.eval-file` too, and it is
the one of the eight that both takes a bare path and evaluates.

with a literal space". D125's table has `file` and `use`; this is the
third word, and with `use` one of the two for which "reads" means "runs".
No defect found. A heading, a sentence and a row.

**S2 — The sentence that says how the ungated list was checked names six
files, and there is a seventh.** "…is `conditional_csv.rs`,
`conditional_sqlite.rs`, `debug_debug.rs`, `debug_shell.rs`, `ai/ollama.rs`
and `internaldb/mod.rs`. The last two register words Bund2 does not have."
`bus/globals.rs` registers `global`, `global*` and `?global` with no gate

But the sentence is a claim about the reference, it is short by a file,

## What the RFC assumes and does not state

1. **A word that has a stub for one flag has been looked at for the
   other.** B1. The method sentence filters on the presence of a stub.
2. **The words that fetch are the ones D54 lists.** S1.
3. **D119's survey was the whole of what reads a file under the flag.** B1.
   The ruling "names two words and gates two"; the RFC reads that as the
   flag's line having been drawn, and it was drawn around what was shown.
4. **Evaluation and I/O are two groups of words.** Four words are both, and
   each flag's section treats them as the other's.
5. **`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions` with a stub or in the list of
   six is everything that reaches outside.** S2.
6. **A redirect's body comes with a length.** B2. With one, the two
   binaries agree; without, they do not, for `301` and `302` both.
7. Carried from the fifteenth review, all unchanged: two clients that send
   the same request read the same answer; a response `ureq` accepts is one
   libcurl accepts; a fetch that succeeds fetched something; a `no_proxy`
   entry has no length; a proxy's scheme ends in exactly two slashes; the
   operating system names the C library.
8. Carried from the eleventh to fourteenth reviews: the sentinel cannot
   occur in what a build writes; no golden fails to parse; `--inspect`
   reports what a header says and not what the runtime does; the executable
   on disk is the one running when `build` copies it; a proxy variable that
   is not UTF-8 is unset.
9. Carried, and stated in the RFC: the compiler does not fold reads of the
   region; the in-place write works on ELF and PE; `/usr/bin/codesign` is
   present and real; a refusal is text and not an exit status.
