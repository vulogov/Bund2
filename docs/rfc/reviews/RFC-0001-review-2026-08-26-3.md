# RFC-0001 review — third pass, 2026-08-26

Reviewing `docs/rfc/RFC-0001-value.md` at commit `f62be91`, revised after
reviews 1 (`RFC-0001-review-2026-08-26.md`) and 2
(`RFC-0001-review-2026-08-26-2.md`).

Method: every `reference/...:N` citation in the RFC was opened and read in this
session — 49 distinct citations across nine `rust_dynamic` files, two
`rust_multistack` files, three `rust_multistackvm` files and one `Bund` file.
Every count was re-derived from the corpus, the goldens or `cargo xtask`.
`cargo xtask layout` and `cargo xtask cite` were run. Four claims were put to
the oracle directly (`target/oracle/release/bund --nocolor script --file`).

Verdict: **do not accept.**

Review 2's remediation list is largely done, and the two findings that changed
the design — the header reset (F34) and the scalar-equality deviation — are
both now present, argued and dispositioned. What blocks acceptance is that the
scalar repair was applied to **one** field and not the other four. Review 2
found that a `Bool` has no identity slot; the same is true of its `tags`, its
`attr`, its `curr` and its `stamp`, and the goldens show all of them populated
on scalars. The RFC's own probe golden is the counterexample.

Second, one load-bearing empirical claim is wrong in a way the cited evidence
contradicts: the 2^53 equality result has its operands reversed, and the golden
the RFC says pins it records the opposite answer. Verified on the oracle both
ways.

## A. Claims contradicted by the evidence

### A1. Scalars have no header, and 29 of the 258 renderings show one

This is review 2's B1, repaired for `identity` and left standing for
everything else in the header.

`BundValue` (RFC:206-214) makes `Int`, `Float`, `Bool`, `Nodata` and `None`
inline arms with no heap object. `HeapValue` (RFC:231-239) holds `identity`,
`stamp`, `dt`, `curr`, `tags` and `attr`. A scalar therefore carries **none of
those six fields**. `cargo xtask layout` confirms the intent rather than
contradicting it: `D: construct a scalar 0`, `D: dup a scalar (no header at
all) 0`.

But `TS::push` writes a tag on **every** push, of every value, with no type
test — `value.set_tag("stack", &curr.stack_id())`
(`reference/rust_multistack/src/ts_push.rs:25`, and `push_to_stack` at `:51`).
The RFC knows this: criterion 3 uses it as the reason `dup` costs four
allocations rather than one, and `xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:152-155` repeats it
in a doc comment. It applies to integers too.

Counting the goldens by top-level `dt`:

| | count |
|---|---|
| renderings total | 258 |
| of those, scalar (`dt` 0, 1, 2, 3, 97) | **34** |
| scalar renderings with a non-empty `tags` | **29** |
| scalar renderings with a non-empty `attr` | **3** |

The three with a populated `attr` are in `tests/golden/probes/value-fields.golden`
— **this RFC's own probe**, cited at RFC:146-153 as the evidence that makes
`attr` a field rather than a constant:

    Value { id: "<id>", stamp: <stamp>, dt: 2, q: 100.0, data: I64(1),
            attr: [Value { … dt: 2 … data: I64(2) … tags: {"stack": "main"} }],
            curr: -1, tags: {"stack": "main"} }

`dt: 2` is `INTEGER`. Under the design that value is `BundValue::Int(1)` — a
bare `i64` with nowhere to put an `attr` list, a tag map, or the `curr` and
`stamp` the same line prints. The two `dt: 97` renderings in the same golden
are `BundValue::Nodata` carrying `tags: {"stack": "main"}`. Twelve `dt: 1`
renderings are `BundValue::Bool` doing the same.

Three consequences, all internal to the RFC:

- **Criterion 6 is unsatisfiable as written.** It demands the `Debug` rendering
  reproduce the reference's text for all 258 renderings "including … `attr`,
  `curr`, `tags` as real state". For 29 of them the design has no state to
  render.
- **Criteria 2 and 3 contradict criterion 6.** "Constructing and cloning a
  scalar allocate **0** times" and "`dup` of a scalar allocates **0**" are true
  only of a scalar that carries no tag map. `TS::push` gives every stack
  scalar a one-entry `BTreeMap<String, String>`, which is precisely the
  allocation criterion 3 counts for the heap case.
- **The preservation row at RFC:490** — "`set_tag` mutating in place without
  minting | **Preserved exactly.** `make_mut` copies the id, which is what an
  in-place write does" — has no meaning for a scalar. There is no `make_mut`,
  no id, and no map.

The RFC's scalar section (RFC:318-342) argues the identity deviation carefully
and then stops. `tags`, `attr`, `curr` and `stamp` on scalars are not mentioned
in the section, in the preservation table, or in any criterion.

This is not fatal to the shape, and the escape is the same one review 2
supplied for identity: either the scalar arms gain a rarely-populated side
channel, or `Bool`/`Nodata`/`None` and the numerics keep a header when they are
stack-resident, or the RFC states the deviation and argues its observability.
The last route is the hard one here, because unlike identity — normalised away
by F14 — **`tags` and `attr` are not normalised, and 29 goldens capture them.**
Whatever the answer, it changes criterion 6 and probably criteria 2 and 3.

### A2. The 2^53 result has its operands reversed, and the golden pins the opposite answer

RFC:397-400:

> - **Widen both ways** is not transitive above 2^53: on the oracle
>   `9007199254740993 == 9007199254740992.0` is **true**, because widening
>   `2^53+1` to `f64` loses the low bit …

and RFC:393: "both failures are pinned in `tests/golden/probes/eq-asymmetry.golden`".

Both halves fail.

**The notation.** The RFC writes `42 == 42.5` for the truncating direction,
which is `I64(42).eq(F64(42.5))` — `eq.rs:13`. The probe source agrees: its
Bund line is `42.5 42 ==` and its label is `42 == 42.5`, because "`a b ==`
computes `b.eq(a)`" (`tests/probes/eq-asymmetry.bund:5`). So the RFC's `x == y`
means `x.eq(y)`.

Under that notation `9007199254740993 == 9007199254740992.0` is
`I64(…993).eq(F64(…992.0))`, which takes `eq.rs:13` and **truncates**:
`9007199254740993 == 9007199254740992` → **false**. The claim needs the
operands the other way round.

**The oracle.** Run in this session:

    9007199254740992.0 9007199254740993 ==   ->  false    (I64.eq(F64), truncate)
    9007199254740993 9007199254740992.0 ==   ->  true     (F64.eq(I64), widen)

**The golden.** `tests/golden/probes/eq-asymmetry.golden` contains one 2^53
line and it reads:

      (2^53+1) == 2^53.0 ->
    false

So the probe captures only the truncating orientation, and records `false`.
The orientation that answers `true` — the one the RFC's argument needs — is
**not in the probe at all**. "Both failures are pinned" is false: the widening
failure is unpinned.

The underlying argument survives untouched, because the non-transitivity of
widen-both-ways is a property of the *proposed rule*, not of the reference:
`9007199254740993` and `9007199254740992` both widen to the same `f64`, so
under that rule they would be equal to each other. The RFC dresses that up as
an oracle observation and gets the observation wrong.

**The same error is in the registers**, which is where the RFC inherited it:
`docs/registers/decisions.md:1194-1197` (D30's amendment) and
`docs/registers/open-questions.md:40` (Q20) both state
`9007199254740993 == 9007199254740992.0` answers `true` on the oracle. Fixing
the RFC alone leaves two register entries asserting a result the oracle
contradicts.

**Criterion 12 inherits it.** RFC:586-587 asserts
"`9007199254740993 == 9007199254740992.0` is false". Under exact comparison
Bund2 answers false — and so does the **reference**, in that orientation. The
criterion therefore tests nothing: it asserts agreement, not the deviation.
The deviation is `9007199254740992.0 == 9007199254740993`, which the reference
answers `true` and Bund2 must answer `false`. That orientation is not asserted
anywhere. (Criterion 12's `42` cases do not have this problem — they assert
both orientations, and the first one is a real deviation.)

### A3. The ordering row contradicts F12's recorded disposition

RFC:497:

> | Ordering falling back to `id.cmp` | **Preserved exactly**, including F12's
>   inconsistency with `lt`. |

`docs/registers/defects.md:95-100` says the opposite:

> Disposition: **FIX** — Bund2 implements `Ord::cmp` with a proper `Val::F64`
> arm, consistent with `partial_cmp`. No golden regenerates: the defective arm
> is unreachable through `sort`.

One says preserve the defect, the other says fix it, and `CLAUDE.md` makes the
register the shared state across sessions. The RFC consumes no decision that
overrides F12 and does not name the disagreement. A work item reading the
preservation table and a work item reading `defects.md` will implement
different things.

Note also that F12's own premise is wrong in a way the RFC has already
side-stepped without saying so. F12 opens "`partial_cmp` handles `Val::F64`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/ord.rs:19-21`)". Lines 19-21 are inside **`lt`**
(`fn lt` at `:9`), not `partial_cmp` — `partial_cmp` is `:6-8` and is
`Some(self.cmp(other))`, so it cannot disagree with `cmp` at all. The RFC's
prose says `lt`, which is correct; the register says `partial_cmp`, which is
not. Worth an appended correction, since the RFC cites F12 by name.

For the record, F12's *reachability* claim does hold: `lt`, `le`, `gt` and
`ge` are all four overridden (`ord.rs:9,48,87,126`), so no comparison word
reaches `Ord::cmp`. The RFC's "currently unreachable" is right.

### A4. "twelve heap kinds" and "sixteen" — two rows, two numbers, neither correct

RFC:491: "Equality: content for four payload kinds, identity for **twelve**
heap kinds".
RFC:498: "`hash` mirrors `eq`: content for the four content-compared kinds,
identity for the other **sixteen**."

These describe the same partition of the same 20 arms and disagree. Neither is
right for the design:

- 20 `Val` arms, 4 content-compared (`I64`, `F64`, `String`, `Time`), 16
  reaching the catch-all at `eq.rs:45`. **Sixteen** is the reference's count.
- Of those 16, `Val::Bool` and `Val::Null` are scalar arms in this design and
  compare by **content** under the deviation at RFC:339-342. So the number of
  heap kinds compared by identity is **fourteen**, not twelve and not sixteen.

The hash row is worse than a miscount: "identity for the other sixteen" assigns
an identity hash to `Bool`, `Nodata` and `None`, which under this design have
no identity. See D4 below.

Separately, both rows count by payload arm, which the RFC's own opening thesis
says is the wrong axis. `Val::List` compares by **content** when `dt` is
`CINTEGER` or `CFLOAT` (`eq.rs:47-52`) and by identity when it is `LIST`,
`RESULT` or `PAIR`. Equality is a function of (arm, `dt`), and a count per arm
cannot express that.

## B. Citation precision

### B1. "The `==` word accepts only …" — it also accepts `STRING`

RFC:332-334:

> The `==` word accepts only `INTEGER | FLOAT | CINTEGER | CFLOAT | TIME` on
> both operands
> (`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/logic/logic_compare_fun.rs:17-20`)
> and bails otherwise, so `PartialEq` on a bool is **not reachable through
> `==`**.

`:17-20` is correct and says what is quoted. But `stdlib_logic_compare` has a
second accepting arm: `STRING` at `:47`, taking `STRING` on both sides for
`Ops::Eq` and `Ops::Ne` (`:48-60`), bailing only for the ordering operations.
Review 2 recorded this as "or `STRING` for both" (`logic_compare_fun.rs:17-70`);
the RFC narrowed the citation to `:17-20` and kept the word "only", which turns
an imprecision into a false statement.

The conclusion survives — `STRING` is not `Bool` — but this is exactly review
1's A-class defect: a citation that resolves, is quoted accurately, and
supports a sentence that is wrong. It matters because this sentence is the
whole reachability argument that converts A1's sibling deviation from a
redesign into a deviation. Cite `:17-70` and say "accepts only numeric and
time types on both operands, or `STRING` on both".

### B2. The payload→`dt` table is still missing `MESSAGE`, and now also `PAIR`

Review 2's A3 asked for `MESSAGE`. It is still absent. RFC:70:

> | `Val::Map` | `CLASS`, `CONDITIONAL`, `CONFIG`, `CURRY`, `INFO`, `MAP`, `OBJECT` |

`Value::message` builds a `Value::dict()` and sets `res.dt = MESSAGE`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/create.rs:181-186`), and `MESSAGE` appears in the
`get.rs:7` arm the RFC quotes verbatim nine lines later at RFC:79. The table
still contradicts its own next paragraph.

New this pass — RFC:72:

> | `Val::List` | `CFLOAT`, `CINTEGER`, `LIST`, `RESULT` |

`Value::from_pair` calls `Value::from_list` and sets `res.dt = PAIR`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/create.rs:164-167`). `PAIR` is a fifth `dt` over
the `Val::List` payload, and it behaves differently from the other four:
`Value::push` matches `LIST | RESULT` (`push.rs:37`) and `Value::set` matches
`LIST | RESULT` (`set.rs:8`), so a `PAIR` falls to each function's scalar
catch-all and is **replaced wholesale** rather than appended to. That is a
`Val::List`-payload value on which the two container mutations do not mutate
the container — the strongest example the RFC has of its own "dispatch is on
`dt`, not on the payload" thesis, and it is not in the table.

The same incomplete table is copied into `xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:128-137`, and
that comment also asserts "`NONE`, `BOOL`, `INTEGER` and `FLOAT` are one-to-one
with their payloads" — the claim RFC:221-226 explicitly retracts, since
`Val::Null` carries both `NONE` and `NODATA`. The harness comment should follow
the RFC.

### B3. `set.rs:33` and `push.rs:165` are still the arms nothing reaches

Review 2's A1 and A2, unqualified.

RFC:174 — "`set` copies it (`set.rs:33`)". `res.q = self.q` appears at `:33`,
`:60` and `:93`, all three in scalar catch-alls. The MAP arm of `set` never
reaches `:33`: it returns `Value::from_dict(m)` at `:21`, whose `q` is the
constructor's own `100.0` (`create_map.rs:36`).

RFC:93 — `push.rs:165` as a per-mutation re-mint site. That line is in
`Value::push`'s scalar catch-all (`:162-167`). The `LIST | RESULT` arm returns
`Value::from_list(data)` (`:48`) and mints through the constructor instead —
and, per F34, resets the header while doing it.

Both citations resolve and both support the sentence. Both point at the least
reachable instance, and in `push`'s case at the one arm where F34 does *not*
apply. One clause each.

### B4. `eq.rs:59-62` starts on a blank line

RFC:388 cites `:59-62` for `impl Eq`. The impl is `:60-62`; `:59` is blank.
Inherited from `defects.md:769` and `decisions.md:1183`. Trivial, listed only
because `cite` cannot catch it.

### B5. `Ord::cmp`'s "otherwise" omits the `CINTEGER` arm

RFC:118-120: "`Ord::cmp` has the same shape: content for `I64`, `Time` and
`String`, and `self.id.cmp(&other.id)` otherwise". The catch-all also compares
`CINTEGER` by content (`ord.rs:196-198`), so "otherwise" is not quite the
fallback. More usefully: `eq` handles **both** `CINTEGER` and `CFLOAT` by
content (`eq.rs:47-52`) while `cmp` handles only `CINTEGER` — a `CFLOAT`
compares by content and orders by id. The RFC says the two have "the same
shape"; they differ by one arm, and both are reachable through the comparison
words, which accept `CFLOAT` (`logic_compare_fun.rs:18`).

## C. Claims that need a citation and lack one

**C1. "no word writes it" — `q`.** RFC:175: "neither changes it, and no word
writes it." Three writers exist and are uncited: `Value::set_q`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/q.rs:8-9`), `Value::calc_q` (`:4-5`), and
`impl Add`/`Sub`/`Mul`/`Div for Value`, which average the operands' `q` and
write it back through `set_q` (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/math.rs:416,419`,
`:429,431`, `:440,442`, `:451,453`).

The conclusion survives on a premise the RFC never states: the arithmetic words
bypass the operator impls entirely and call `Value::numeric_op` directly
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/math/math_op.rs:8,103`), so
`impl Add` and friends are dead code. `q` is constant in reach *because the `+`
word does not use `+`*. Review 2 raised this as C4 and it is unchanged. It
deserves a sentence, because an implementer who wires Bund2's `+` through a
Rust `Add` impl reintroduces the write against a field the RFC has by then
made a rendered constant. Q18 (`open-questions.md:39`) carries the same
unqualified phrasing and should be corrected alongside.

**C2. "`from_list` and `from_valuemap` do the same"** (RFC:351) — uncited.
They do: `create_list.rs:26-28` and `create_map.rs:50-52`. F34 carries the
citations; the RFC should too.

**C3. "29 of the 65 goldens"** (RFC:182) — the denominator is **66**.
`CONFORMANCE.txt` reads `0/66`, `find tests/golden -name '*.golden'` returns
66, and criterion 5 in this same document says `0/66`. The RFC contradicts
itself by one across two sections. (Separately, `CONFORMANCE.txt`'s own comment
block still says "59 goldens" — review 2's G, unfixed, and criterion 5 points
at that file.)

**C4. `Payload` is used and never defined.** RFC:238 declares
`payload: Rc<Payload>` and no section says what `Payload` is. Criterion 7
compares wire bytes for "each of the 20 payload kinds", and bincode encodes
enum discriminants positionally, so arm order is a wire-format contract. Review
2's F carried this; still open.

## D. Behaviours the preservation analysis does not enumerate

### D1. `Send` and `Sync`

Still absent — review 1's E5, review 2's C3. The reference's `Value` is `Send`
and `Sync` by derivation. `Rc<HeapValue>` with `Cell` fields is neither.

This is no longer merely unlisted. **RFC-0002 already depends on it and
attributes the position to RFC-0001** — RFC-0002:389-390: "making a registry
*shareable across threads* is a separate problem that starts with whether
`BundValue` is `Send`, **and RFC-0001 says it is not**." RFC-0001 says nothing
of the kind; the word does not appear. A dependent RFC is citing a statement
that does not exist.

Nothing in the reference spawns a thread (`grep` for `thread::spawn` /
`tokio::spawn` over `Bund/src`, `rust_multistackvm/src`, `rust_dynamic/src`
returns nothing), so no observable behaviour changes. That is the argument the
row should make, in one line, so RFC-0002 and RFC-0007 have something real to
cite.

### D2. `dup`'s effect on `stamp` and `curr`

The reference's `dup` is a bincode round trip (`dup.rs:7-12`) that mints a
fresh `id` at `:11` and **preserves everything else** — `stamp` and `curr` are
serialised fields and come back verbatim. RFC:266-298 says what `dup` does to
identity and is silent on the other two.

The harness has already chosen an answer the RFC has not stated:
`xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:185-198` resets both — `stamp: Cell::new(0.0)` at
`:189` and `curr: Cell::new(-1)` at `:191` — on every `dup`. Under D2 a reset stamp means the copy
reports a *later* `.timestamp` than its source, where the reference reports the
same one. Under D2's laziness that is arguably right, but it is a second
deviation riding inside the first, decided in a measurement harness rather than
in the RFC.

### D3. `dup` of a JSON value stops round-tripping through text

RFC:199-200 states the current behaviour: "Because `dup` is built on this pair,
`dup` of a JSON value is a round trip through text." The design removes that
round trip (`dup` becomes a header allocation plus an `Rc` bump on the
payload), and the preservation table dispositions the two facts separately —
"JSON round trip losing identity | **Preserved exactly**" and "`dup` as a
bincode round trip | **Deliberately changed**" — without ever addressing their
intersection.

Whatever `serde_json` value → string → `serde_json` value does to a JSON
payload beyond identity (number formatting, object member order) is applied by
the reference's `dup` and will not be applied by Bund2's. That is a third row,
and it is the kind of thing a golden finds rather than a reader.

### D4. The hash rule has no case for `Bool`, `Nodata` or `None`

RFC:420-423: "Content-compared kinds (`Int`, `Float`, `String`, `Time`) hash
their content; identity-compared kinds hash their identity."

Under this design `Bool`, `Nodata` and `None` are **content**-compared
(RFC:339-342, the deviation) and have **no identity**. They match neither
clause. The `Hash`/`Eq` contract makes this mandatory rather than cosmetic:
`true == true` under the deviation, so the two must hash alike, so they must
hash by content — but the rule as written assigns them an identity hash they
cannot produce.

Criterion 10 makes it checkable and then trips over it: "a valuemap keyed by a
freshly built equal *scalar* finds its entry." A bool is a scalar. As specified,
a bool key has no defined hash.

D30's amendment settles the float-bearing arms for `F64` but says nothing about
non-integral floats either: `42.5` must hash somehow, and `42.5 == 42.5` forces
equal hashes. Presumably the bit pattern; unstated in both documents.

### D5. Three more dead `dt` constants, and one dead `Val` arm that breaks criterion 7

RFC:495 dispositions `ASSOCIATION` as "**Deliberately omitted.** A `dt`
constant with no constructor and therefore no values." Grepping every
`dt: <CONST>` and `.dt = <CONST>` write across all five crates finds three
constants in exactly the same position and not mentioned anywhere:

- `LITERAL` (5) — no writer
- `LARGE_FLOAT` (23) — no writer
- `TOKEN` (99) — no writer

Meanwhile the first preservation row says `dt` is "carried verbatim as a `u16`
over the same **42 constants**", which the `ASSOCIATION` row already
contradicts by one and these three contradict by four.

Sharper: **`Val::Token(String)` is never constructed anywhere in the reference
tree** — `grep -rn "Val::Token"` over all crates returns nothing but the
declaration at `types.rs:69`. Criterion 7 reads:

> **The bincode wire format is byte-identical to the reference** for one value
> of each of the 20 payload kinds, compared against bytes captured from the
> oracle.

There is no oracle run that produces a `Val::Token`, so bytes cannot be
captured for it and the criterion cannot be satisfied for 20 of 20. Either the
criterion drops to the constructible kinds and says which, or it names another
source for the bytes. (Review 2 already asked criterion 7 to name a harness;
that is still unanswered — nothing in `xtask` captures oracle bytes today.)

### D6. D2's other consequences

D2 (`decisions.md:96-107`) says its deviation "**must be listed** in the
Deviate section of whichever work item implements `Value`" and enumerates
three. The RFC's stamp section (RFC:362-381) lists one — two never-observed
values sharing a stamp. Still missing:

- the METRICS `"ts"` field becoming iteration time (`iter.rs:67,82`,
  `carcdr.rs:127,203`);
- `get_timestamp_diff` (`timestamp.rs:22`) becoming a difference of observation
  times;
- and the mechanics D2 spells out at `decisions.md:116-117` — `set` and `push`
  refresh the stamp (`set.rs:32,59,92`, `push.rs:166`), which under deferred
  sampling means resetting the `Cell` to unobserved. The RFC's F34 section
  covers `attr`, `curr` and `tags` on those paths and does not mention `stamp`.

Review 2's C2 asked for all of this. One of four is done.

### D7. The RFC withdraws a sentence that is still standing in D13

RFC:301-307 withdraws "CoW split points coincide exactly with the points where
the reference regenerates the id", with `set_tag` as the counterexample. That
sentence is D13's, verbatim, at `decisions.md:338-339`, where it is the
load-bearing justification for adopting `Rc::make_mut`:

> So clone-on-write does not approximate the semantics — **its split points
> coincide exactly with the points where the reference regenerates the id.**

The RFC consumes D13 and withdraws D13's argument without saying so. The
registers are append-only, so the repair is an appended note on D13, not an
edit — but nothing currently records that a session reading D13 is reading a
claim RFC-0001 has retracted.

### D8. The layout scan's case analysis is incomplete, and D1 rests on it

`open-questions.md:818-832` concludes scalars need no identity because the `==`
fallback fires "in exactly two situations": both operands non-scalar, or
operands of mismatched kind (where the answer "is **provably always false**").

There is a third: **both operands scalar and of the same kind.** `Val::Bool`
against `Val::Bool`, and `Val::Null` against `Val::Null`, reach `eq.rs:45` and
then `:53`, and the answer is not provably false — `A == A.clone()` is `true`
for a bool in the reference.

RFC:318-342 is right about this and treats it as a deviation. What it does not
say is that the scan D1 cites as settling "where identity lives" has a hole in
its enumeration, and that the deviation exists *because* of the hole. Same
paragraph also asserts "`Hash` is only reached through VALUEMAP, whose keys are
Values that already have a header" — which D30 has since inverted by making
scalar keys the useful case.

## E. Acceptance criteria, one at a time

1. **16 bytes / align 8** — checkable, reproduces (`layout`, row D). Caveat
   below.
2. **0 allocations for a scalar** — checkable, reproduces. But see A1: it
   measures a scalar that carries no stack tag, and 29 golden renderings say a
   stack scalar has one.
3. **`dup` 4 allocations / 649 bytes, scalar 0** — checkable and exact.
   `D: dup a list … 4 649`, `D: dup a scalar … 0 0`. Fully repaired from
   review 2.
4. **Clone-equal / dup-unequal for list, map, lambda; explicitly not bool** —
   checkable, and the deviation is asserted rather than hidden. Good criterion.
5. **`conform` ≥ 0/66** — checkable; the mark reads 0/66 and RFC-0000's
   provenance table records all three denominator moves (`RFC-0000:465-468`).
6. **258 renderings across 29 goldens, 255 with `attr: []`** — all four
   numbers reproduce. **Not satisfiable by the design** for 29 of the 258.
   A1.
7. **Wire format byte-identical for each of the 20 payload kinds** — **not
   satisfiable**: `Val::Token` has no constructor, so the oracle cannot produce
   the bytes. Also still names no harness, which review 2 asked for; nothing in
   `xtask` captures oracle bytes today.
8. **`.id` is 21 characters over the nanoid alphabet; two values minted in one
   VM never collide** — first half checkable. Second half still names no bound
   and no test, which review 2 asked for. How many values, over what operation?
   Also unstated: `.id` is only callable on an `OBJECT` (`VM::m` bails
   otherwise, `reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_object.rs:50-55`),
   which is what makes identity-free scalars safe.
9. **`cargo tree -p bund2-value`** — checkable, vacuous today, correctly
   labelled.
10. **`valuemap "k" 42 set "k" get` leaves 42; scalar keys find, list keys
    don't** — checkable and well chosen. Except that "scalar" includes bool,
    which D4 leaves without a hash rule.
11. **`.timestamp` returns a float; stamps non-decreasing once observed** —
    checkable in principle. Written as though `.timestamp` applies to any
    value; it applies only to an `OBJECT`, same citation as 8. Say so, or the
    test cannot be written.
12. **Equality across int and float is exact, symmetric, transitive** — the
    `42` clauses are checkable and assert both orientations. The 2^53 clause
    asserts an orientation the reference already agrees with, so it tests
    nothing. A2.
13. **`cite` reports zero defects** — passes (0 defects, 36 advisories over
    1213 citations). The criterion's own note about what `cite` cannot check
    is the right thing to have written, and this pass proves it again: B1 is a
    citation that `cite` accepts and that misleads.

**Criterion 1 and 2 still measure a five-arm enum.** Review 2 asked for
candidate D to be extended to the shape §Design specifies, or for the gap to be
stated. The *header* was extended and now matches `HeapValue` field for field,
including `curr`, `tags`, `attr` and the second `Rc` — good. The *enum* was
not: `xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:203-209` has `Int`, `Float`, `Bool`, `Nodata`,
`Heap` — five arms, no `None`. The RFC's `BundValue` has six. RFC:225-226 says
"It costs nothing: the discriminant has room", which is almost certainly right
and is argument, against a Summary that says the RFC "is accepted against
[the measured figures], not against argument". Add the arm; it is a one-line
change to the harness.

**Still missing a criterion:** F28's fix (asked for by review 1, still claimed
"Deliberately fixed" with nothing verifying it), F34's header reset (review 2
asked; the section exists, no criterion does), and F35's `dt` preservation on
`push`.

## G. Assumed but not stated

Carried forward and still true: `Payload` is never defined, and its arm order
is a wire-format contract (C4). Serialisation as the materialisation point for
both `Cell`s is D20's and is never restated here. `identity: 0` and
`stamp: 0.0` as reserved sentinels — nothing says a real counter can never be
0. The golden normaliser stripping `id`/`stamp` (F14) and dict order (F15),
which is the only reason lazy identity is invisible. Single-threadedness.
`dt` keeping the reference's exact `u16` values including the 92–99 gap.
`Debug` being hand-written and reproducing field order *and* payload arm
spellings. `tags` changing container from `HashMap` (`value.rs:24`) to
`BTreeMap` (RFC:235), which sorts a rendering the RFC elsewhere insists must
reproduce exactly — probably masked by F15, still unlisted.

New to this pass:

1. **That `TS::push` writes the stack tag only on heap values.** It writes it
   on every value (`ts_push.rs:25`, restated in the harness at
   `xtask/src/layout/mod.rs:153-156`), and 29 golden renderings prove it for
   scalars. This is A1, and it is the largest unstated assumption in the
   document.
2. **That `dup` resets `stamp` and `curr`.** The harness does; the RFC does
   not say; the reference preserves both (D2).
3. **That `Bool`/`Nodata`/`None` hash by content.** Forced by the `Hash`/`Eq`
   contract under the RFC's own scalar deviation, and specified nowhere (D4).
4. **That a non-integral float hashes by its bit pattern.** D30 specifies
   integral floats, `-0.0` and `NaN`, and stops.
5. **That the arithmetic words bypass `impl Add`**, which is the only reason
   "no word writes `q`" is true (C1).
6. **That `BundValue` is neither `Send` nor `Sync`**, which RFC-0002 already
   treats as settled here (D1).
7. **That `LITERAL`, `LARGE_FLOAT` and `TOKEN` are dead like `ASSOCIATION`**,
   and that `Val::Token` has no constructor at all (D5).
8. **That `.id` and `.timestamp` are only reachable on an `OBJECT`**
   (`multistackvm_object.rs:50-55`). This is what makes identity-free scalars
   safe, and the RFC leans on the conclusion without the premise — review 2's
   F1, unchanged.
9. **That `dt` is not mutable behind the `Rc`.** `HeapValue.dt` is a plain
   `u16`, not a `Cell`. That is correct — the only in-place `dt` writes in the
   tree are on freshly constructed values (`create.rs:166,186`,
   `set.rs:22,49,82`, `bund_object.rs:35`, `merge.rs:83`) — but it is a
   verified fact the RFC should record rather than an accident of the struct
   definition.

## Defects this pass found that are not in the register

Recommended, not written — the register is the owner's to append.

- **`Val::Token` has no constructor anywhere in the reference.** The arm exists
  at `types.rs:69` and nothing produces it. Same class as F36. Breaks
  criterion 7 as written.
- **`LITERAL` (5), `LARGE_FLOAT` (23) and `TOKEN` (99) have no writer**, the
  same condition F36 records for `ASSOCIATION` (94). Four of the 42 `dt`
  constants are unreachable, not one.
- **`PAIR` is a `Val::List` payload on which `set` and `push` do not mutate the
  container.** `from_pair` (`create.rs:164-167`) makes it; `push.rs:37` and
  `set.rs:8` match `LIST | RESULT` only, so a `PAIR` falls to each function's
  scalar catch-all and is replaced wholesale. Sibling to F35 and arguably
  worse, since F35 at least appends.
- **F12's citation `ord.rs:19-21` names `partial_cmp` and points at `lt`.**
  `partial_cmp` is `ord.rs:6-8` and delegates to `cmp`, so the two can never
  disagree; the real inconsistency is `lt`/`le`/`gt`/`ge` against `cmp`.
- **`eq` compares `CFLOAT` by content (`eq.rs:50-51`) and `cmp` does not**
  (`ord.rs:194-200` has a `CINTEGER` arm only), so a `CFLOAT` is equality-
  compared by content and ordered by nanoid. Both are reachable: the compare
  word accepts `CFLOAT` (`logic_compare_fun.rs:18`).
