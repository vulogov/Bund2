## Blocking

### B1. A one-column frame with a NONE loses a row through `frame.csv.text` and `frame.csv.parse`

**The RFC says** (S6): `frame.csv.text` "writes NONE as an empty field **[P6]**", and "`frame.csv.parse` of `frame.csv.text` of a frame, with an empty options MAP, is `frame_eq` to it, factors aside". The frame reader "tokenises with `csv-core` or a reader of its own".

**What the source and a probe show.**
- In a one-column frame, a NONE row is written as an empty line.
- `csv-core` ignores empty lines. Its doc says "Empty lines (that do not include other whitespace) are ignored" (`csv-core-0.1.13/src/reader.rs:94`), and its record-start state discards a terminator (`:988-990`).
- Probed through `csv`, which reads with that core: a file `x\n1\n\n3\n` run without `column` and with `:lambda { println }` prints `[ 1 :: ]` and `[ 3 :: ]`, two rows and not three.
- The `csv` crate's own writer knows this. It quotes "an empty field … if it is the only field in a record" (`csv-1.4.0/src/writer.rs:331-332`; `csv-core-0.1.13/src/writer.rs:232-233`). Under D254, though, a quoted `""` reads back as an empty STRING, so that cure is closed to this RFC.

**Why it matters.**
- Writing `[ 1 NONE 3 ]` in a one-column frame and reading it back gives a frame of two rows. There is no refusal, and S6 says that cannot happen.
- An implementer who writes through the `csv` crate's `Writer` instead gets `""`, which reads back as an empty STRING: the same silent change in another form.
- P6 (NONE written empty), D254 (a quoted field is text) and `csv-core`'s blank-line rule collide here, and no ruling chooses between them. A one-column frame is ordinary: one variable read from a file, or the answer of `frame.select` with one name.

**Suggested fix.** Take it to the owner; it is a change to the text format. Options include:
- The frame reader treats an empty line as a record of one empty field when the header has one column. This needs a reader that sees empty lines, which `csv-core` does not offer.
- `frame.csv.text` writes NONE in a one-column frame as the first `na` string, `NA`.
- A one-column frame with a NONE is refused by `frame.csv.text` with E101.

Whatever is chosen, say what the reader does with an empty line in a file of more than one column: E102, or skipped as today. Add a one-column frame with NONE to criterion 6's round trip.

### B2. `frame.aggregate` answers ±∞ from finite cells, which B12 and the frame check forbid

**The RFC says.** S1: "**Every frame a word answers passes the check** (S2)". S2's check refuses a NaN or ±∞ cell (B12). S5: `"mean"` is computed by "`stat.mean.arithmetic` (`series.rs`, `amean`)", "bit for bit"; `"sum"` is "a left-to-right f64 sum". S8 has no E114 row, and no E114 appears anywhere in the RFC.

**What the source and probes show.**
- `amean` divides an unchecked sum (`series.rs:285-287`). Probed: `[ 1.0e308 1.0e308 ] stat.mean.arithmetic println` prints `inf`.
- RFC-0015's own words refuse the same overflow. Probed: `[ 1.0e308 1.0e308 ] vec.sum println` is `VEC.SUM: E114 the answer overflows a FLOAT: Σ xᵢ`, and `[ 1.0e308 -1.0e308 1.0e308 ] stat.var println` is `STAT.VAR: E114 the answer overflows a FLOAT: the variance`.
- D250 B12, consumed by this RFC, is "refuse (E101, E114), never NaN or ±∞ from finite operands" (`decisions.md:17186`). RFC-0016 §S1 applied it to every estimate (`RFC-0016-statistical-inference.md:182-186`).

**Why it matters.**
- A group of two large cells makes `frame.aggregate` either answer a frame its own check refuses, or refuse with something the RFC does not state. Its `"mean"` is "bit for bit" `stat.mean.arithmetic`, which is the one statistic with no overflow check.
- `"var"` and `"sd"` share `stat.var`'s code, which refuses E114 with its own word's prefix (`STAT.VAR:`). Which word's name a `frame.aggregate` refusal carries is not said.
- Criterion 9 cannot be met as written, and criterion 5 has no case for it.

**Suggested fix.**
- Every statistic whose answer is not finite is E114, `FRAME.AGGREGATE: E114 the answer overflows a FLOAT: <column>.<statistic> of group [ … ]`, and it refuses the whole word, as RFC-0016 §S1 does.
- Define `"sum"` as `vec.sum`'s code (`biomath/vector.rs:71-74`), which already is a left-to-right sum with this refusal (N9).
- Add an S8 row and a criterion 5 case.

## Should fix

### S1. D255's refusal reads per field, so a string column holding `inf` would be refused

**The RFC says** (S6): "a field that infers as FLOAT and parses to NaN or ±∞ (`NaN`, `inf`, `-inf`, `+inf`) is E101". D255 says the same: "An unquoted field that infers as FLOAT".

**What the source and a probe show.**
- `infer` is per field: `infer("inf")` is FLOAT whatever its neighbours (`data.rs:79-83`, `:105-115`). A column's kind is a separate step (`column_kind`, `:119-125`).
- Probed: a column `status` of `inf`, `ctl` through `csv` answers `[ inf ::  ctl :: ]`, and its first cell's `type` is `String`. A string column holding the word `inf` (an abbreviation, a code) is ordinary.

**Why it matters.**
- Read per field, `frame.csv.parse` refuses that file, though nothing in it is a number.
- Read per column, the rule applies only where the column is numeric. The RFC's next sentence ("`nan` … infers as a string, and its column becomes a string column") suggests that reading, but does not say it.
- D255's text does not settle it either. If the per-column reading is wanted, it is a clarification of the ruling, to be recorded beside D255.

**Suggested fix.** "In a column inferred FLOAT, a field that parses to NaN or ±∞ is E101." Add the `status` column to criterion 6.

### S2. An INTEGER field too large for i64 is still a late failure with a non-E1nn text

**The RFC says** (S6): over every row, "no late cell disagrees" with the column's kind. Criterion 9: every word answers or refuses with an E1nn.

**What the source and a probe show.**
- `is_int` is digits only (`data.rs:101-103`), so `99999999999999999999` infers INTEGER, and `cell`'s `parse::<i64>()` then fails (`:143`).
- Probed: a column holding it through `csv` is `CONTEXT.RUN: Error finishing dataframe: could not parse `99999999999999999999` as dtype `i64` at column 'x'`.
- D254's note has the same gap: a field "that matches the integer pattern" in a mixed column "is an INTEGER", even when it does not fit one.

**Why it matters.** It is the one late failure that judging every row does not remove, and the RFC says none remains. Large identifiers (accession numbers, barcodes) are a common way to meet it.

**Suggested fix.** State a rule; no ruling covers one. Either E101 naming the field, row and column, or read it as FLOAT, making the column numeric FLOAT. Add a criterion 6 case.

### S3. `frame.text`'s worked example fits only a reading the rule does not state

**The RFC says** (S6): "numeric cells right-aligned, the rest left-aligned", "each column as wide as its widest cell". D257 S9 says "numbers right-aligned, the rest left".

**Counting the example** (`RFC-0017-frames-and-missing-values.md:451-454`; the lines are `g····x`, `a····1`, `b··2.5`, `NA··NA`, with `·` a space and no trailing spaces):
- `g` is 2 wide (`NA`) and `x` is 3 wide (`2.5`).
- `a····1` is `a` left-padded to 2, one separator, then `1` right-aligned in 3. It fits.
- `b··2.5` fits the same way.
- `NA··NA` fits only if the NONE in `x` is right-aligned. A NONE is not a numeric cell, so by the rule as written it is "the rest", left-aligned: `NA·NA·`, or `NA·NA` with trailing padding dropped.
- `g····x` fits only if the header `x` is right-aligned. A column name is not a numeric cell either: by the rule, `g··x··` or `g··x`.

**Why it matters.** Criterion 8 compares exact text. An implementer following the rule fails it; one following the example has guessed the rule. The example also leaves two questions open:
- Whether a left-aligned last column is padded with trailing spaces. The example's last column is right-aligned, so it never shows.
- Whether the header counts toward a column's width. Every name in the example is one character, so it never decides.

**Suggested fix.**
- Make the rule per column: every cell of a numeric column, its NONE and header included, is right-aligned; every other column is left-aligned.
- State that the header counts toward the width, and that no line has trailing spaces.
- Use a header wider than its cells, and a left-aligned last column, in the example.

### S4. S8 is not the complete list S1 says it is, and its "…" hides the operand

**The RFC says** (S1): "Each is listed with its text in S8 **[D256 S3]**". D256 S3: "Criterion 2 lists each text".

**Refusals stated elsewhere with no S8 text, or with no code at all:**

- `frame.matrix`'s non-numeric column, empty `names`, and zero rows (E101: no text).
- `frame.aggregate`:
  - a non-numeric statistic: S8's row joins it to "a group too small" but gives only the group text;
  - an unknown statistic name, or a `stats` entry that is not a two-item LIST: no code;
  - an empty `by` or empty `stats`: unstated whether refused.
- `na.count`, `na.omit`, `na.fill` given a non-LIST: no code.
- `frame.select` or `frame.sort` given a repeated name. Unrefused, `frame.select` answers a frame with two columns of one name, which the check then refuses with a text naming the answer, not the operand the program wrote. This is the case the first review's S7 fixed for `frame.aggregate`.
- CSV text that is not a STRING; a `sep` that is not one byte (S8 below).

**The elided operand.** "`…` domain:" abbreviates the first row's `frame is outside <w>'s domain:`. For an index (`row 7 of a frame of 5 rows`), a name (`no column "dose"`), a join key or a group, the operand at fault is `rows`, `n`, `names`, `by` or `stats`, not `frame`. The template's `<operand>` slot (`RFC-0015-biomathematics.md:840`) should name it.

**Suggested fix.** One S8 row per refusal above, with its operand written out. Criterion 2 covers the frame rules; name which criterion covers the rest (S7 below).

### S5. A LIST or MAP cell in a numeric column has two codes

**The RFC says** (S2): "A column holding a number and a cell that is neither a number nor NONE is E103 naming that cell; any other mixture, **or a cell of another type (a LIST, a MAP), is E101**". S8 orders "a numeric column's non-number cell" (E103) before "any other mixture" (E101), and the check "stops at the first".

**Why it matters.** `[ 1 [ 2 ] ]` is "a number and a cell that is neither a number nor NONE" (E103), and holds "a cell of another type" (E101). S8's order picks E103, and S2's last clause says E101. Criterion 2 asks for the code per rule.

**Suggested fix.** Say which wins. The simplest is to apply E103 only when the odd cell is a scalar (STRING or BOOL), and to make a LIST or MAP cell E101 in any column, with S8's order changed to match.

### S6. The Open questions list misses choices no ruling covers, and three passages extend a ruling under its mark

The list names four author's choices. These are of the same kind and are not listed:
- **`frame.join` refuses a shared non-key name (E101).** D257 S6 rules columns, rows, keys and the cap, and says nothing of shared names. R's `merge` suffixes them [UNGROUNDED as R's]. Refusing them is a choice.
- **An emptied group refuses the whole of `frame.aggregate`** (S5: "A group with no cells for a statistic … is E101 naming the group"). D256 S1 and S7 do not rule it, and the second review's S5, which proposed it, is not among the findings D257 ruled ("B2, B3, S6, S9, S10"). Under `"omit"` this means one all-NONE group refuses every group's answer, though `"count"` could answer 0.
- **The 10⁸-byte cap on CSV text** (S6, E111). D257 S6 rules the join's 10⁸ rows; no ruling sets a text cap.
- **A ragged CSV row is E102**, where `csv`'s reader is `flexible(true)` and pads a short row with nulls (`data.rs:160`, `:200`). The first review listed this as an assumption; no ruling took it.
- **`frame.with`**: a replaced factor column loses its levels, and a new column is appended last.
- **BOOLs sort false first.** D108's total order puts BOOLs in rank 4, "no order" (`decisions.md:12089`). `frame.sort`'s order is its own, which is fine, but it is a choice.

And three passages say more than the ruling they cite:
- **`factors` "a name that is not a string column is E101 [D257 S10]"**: D257 S10 rules "a name in `factors` that is not a column". Refusing an existing numeric or logical column follows from the listed `frame.factor` choice, so cite that choice too.
- **`frame_eq` "ignores q" [D257 B2]**: D257 B2 does not mention q, and D32 (`decisions.md:1639`) says q's propagation is preserved, so a helper blind to q cannot catch a regression in it. Criterion 3 checks `na`'s q separately, which is enough for this RFC, but the choice is the RFC's.
- **"another result name" [D256 S7]**: D256 S7 rules collisions with a `by` column or `.dropped`. Adding a result-name collision is harmless and natural, but unmarked.

**Suggested fix.** Add the six choices to §Open questions' "Choices this revision makes" paragraph, and mark the three extensions as the RFC's own.

### S7. The criteria do not check several rules the RFC states, ruled ones included

- **D257 S10's `factors` E101**, and `factors` making a factor with sorted levels (S6 "The round trip": "then with its levels sorted (criterion 6)"). Criterion 6 tests neither.
- **P8's `{ matrix, dropped }`**: criterion 5 tests `frame.matrix` only for its three refusals and through `frame.complete`, never under `"omit"`.
- **The M1 words**: `frame.new`, `.nrow`, `.ncol`, `.names`, `.column` and `.rows` are checked by criterion 1 (registered) and, for `frame.column`, by criterion 5's model path. No criterion checks their answers, `frame.nrow` of a zero-column frame (S4's "0 rows"), or `frame.rows`' sorted keys.
- **The `.` forms**: criterion 1 checks they exist, and nothing checks that they take the top operand from the workbench and answer there.
- **`frame.factor`** refusing a numeric column; an empty CSV header name; `frame.csv.text` quoting a header name that holds a comma.
- **The new cases above**: B1's one-column round trip, B2's overflow, S1's `inf` string column, S2's i64 overflow.

### S8. The reader paragraph leaves out what `csv-core` does, and Q65 is answerable now

**The RFC says** (S6): "the `csv` crate's `StringRecord` does not say [whether a field was quoted], so the frame reader tokenises with `csv-core` or a reader of its own … a leading byte-order mark is [UNGROUNDED; Q65]". `sep` is "one character".

**What the crates show.**
- **No quote flag in `csv-core` either.** `read_field` answers `ReadFieldResult::Field { record_end }` (`csv-core-0.1.13/src/reader.rs:263-283`) and writes the unescaped bytes. A reader built on it learns of a quote only by looking at the input bytes it consumed, which is workable but not stated.
- **It strips a leading UTF-8 BOM** (`strip_utf8_bom`, `reader.rs:607-619`, called from `read_field` and `read_record` at `:540` and `:597`; its test at `:1594`).
  - Probed: a file of bytes `ef bb bf 78 2c 79 0a 31 2c 32 0a` (BOM, `x,y`, `1,2`), run with `:column "x"`, prints `[ 1 :: ]`, so the header name is `x`.
  - Q65 is answered for `csv` and for a `csv-core` reader. Only "a reader of its own" leaves it open, as a choice.
- **It skips empty lines** (B1), and treats CR, LF and CRLF each as a terminator (`reader.rs:91-92`).
- **It never errors** (`reader.rs:86-87`: "will never return an error and will always find *a* parse").
  - Probed: a file `x\n"unterminated\n1\n2\n` through `csv` answers one field holding the rest of the file, with no refusal. A malformed file is refused by `frame.csv.parse` only if the damage happens to change a row's width.
- **The delimiter is a byte** (`csv-core-0.1.13/src/reader.rs:178`, `csv-1.4.0/src/reader.rs:172`: `delimiter(u8)`). "One character" admits `"§"`, two bytes, and says nothing of `sep` `"\""` or `"\n"`.

**Suggested fix.**
- Close Q65 with the citation and probe, as Q63 was closed, and state the BOM rule in S6.
- Say `sep` is one ASCII byte other than `"`, CR and LF, else E101.
- State that a malformed quote is not refused, or add a rule if it should be.
- Say how the reader learns that a field was quoted.

## Notes

**N1.** "`==` with NONE as an operand is `… unsupported operand #1`" (§Current behaviour). With NONE deeper it is `#2`: `'null' json json.to_value 1 ==` is `== returns error: COMPARE: unsupported operand #2`, and `1 'null' json json.to_value ==` is `#1` (probed).

**N2.** S3: "`na.omit` hands *one* column to an RFC-0015 word, which refuses NONE: `"dose" frame.column na.omit stat.mean`". `stat.mean` is the reference's word, built in `series.rs` (`:501`, `:528`), not RFC-0015's. The example still holds: it refuses NONE through `get_data`.

**N3.** Registers by subject. `frame.sort` and P7's byte order agree with D33 (INTEGER against FLOAT by exact value, `decisions.md:1709`) and D106 (strings by code point, `:12198`; UTF-8 byte order is code-point order). Neither is cited, and D108 is not either (`:12089`). D32 (q is reserved and its propagation preserved, `:1639`) bears on whether moved cells keep their q, which the RFC does not say. `frame.csv.text`, JSON and `frame.aggregate`'s fresh FLOATs all reset it.

**N4.** "Q63 is answered (B3)" (§Open questions) sits beside "D250 (B3, B12, B15)" in the header's consumed list. D250 B3 is "Numeric vectors" (`decisions.md:17177`); write D257 B3.

**N5.** Criterion 5's model path compares `stat.lm`'s answers "each key's FLOAT by `to_bits`". `stat.lm` answers LISTs under `coef`, `se`, `t`, `p` and FLOATs under `r2`, `df` (`infer.rs:903-908`). Say "every FLOAT, in every LIST", so the test compares the LISTs cell by cell.

**N6.** The key `na` means a LIST of strings to `frame.csv.parse` and `"refuse"`/`"omit"` to `frame.aggregate` and `frame.matrix`. D255 uses the first (`{ na: [ "" "NA" "NaN" ] }`), so this is ruled, but one sentence in S6 would prevent a reader from passing one where the other is meant.

**N7.** RFC-0016 §S1 says "D252's future `na` key would be an addition to these MAPs" (`RFC-0016-statistical-inference.md:201-202`). This RFC adds no `na` key to RFC-0016's words and keeps them refusing NONE (S1). That is consistent with D252, but worth a sentence, since RFC-0016 anticipated otherwise.

**N8.** In `frame.text`, a STRING cell `NA` prints exactly as NONE does, which weakens P12's reason ("visible in a table"). A STRING holding a line break breaks the table, and a 10⁶-byte STRING widens every line to 10⁶ bytes. The second review's S9 asked for a long STRING in the example, and D257 S9 is silent on truncation, so no truncation is the rule by omission. Saying so would be clearer.

**N9.** D256 S1 rules that `sum`, `min` and `max` "have no word". `vec.sum` is RFC-0015's left-to-right sum with an E114 check (`biomath/vector.rs:71-74`, registered at `:139`). `math.min` and `math.max` exist too (`series.rs:499`, folding from NaN, `:228-243`). The ruling's outcome (define them in the RFC) is unaffected, but its premise is wrong in the same way D252's two findings were. A dated note beside D256, and `"sum"` defined as `vec.sum`'s code, would fix both (B2).

**N10.** In a CSV, a quoted number in a column whose unquoted fields are numbers is a STRING cell in a numeric column, so the frame check refuses the file with E103 (`column "x" is not a LIST of numbers: "1" at row 1`). A file written with every field quoted reads as all string columns. Both follow from D254; neither is said. `csv` reads the first case as `[ 1 ::  2 ::  abc :: ]` (probed, file `x\n"1"\n2\n"abc"\n`), which is why a user will be surprised.

**N11.** S7 says each new `UNVARIED` name gets "its reason in the audit's comment block", but does not give the reasons, as RFC-0016 §S8 did in a table (`RFC-0016-statistical-inference.md:630-636`). Each one's unvaried deepest operand is a frame, `left`, or `frame` and `name`; a one-line table would do.

**N12.** "`PROMOTABLE.txt` and `bund2 effects` regenerated" (S7, criterion 10). `bund2 effects` prints the registry; it has no checked-in output. `tests/golden/EFFECTS.txt` is a hand-written register of differences from `docs/arity.md`, read by `cargo xtask effects` (`xtask/src/effects/mod.rs:56`). RFC-0016 §S8 has the same wording. The clause checks nothing for `bund2 effects`.

**N13.** Whether a `[ column "count" ]` pair's column counts as "a column a statistic reads" is unstated. Under `"refuse"` that decides whether a NONE in it is E101, and under `"omit"` whether its row is dropped.

**N14.** Still unstated from the second review's N11: what `frame.csv.text` and `frame.text` write for a zero-column frame, and the kind of an all-NONE `by` column in `frame.join` (S2 gives it "no kind until a cell says one", and the join requires "one kind in both frames"). A `by` column named `.dropped` would also collide with `"omit"`'s added `.dropped`, a case S5's result-name rule does not reach.

**N15.** A CSV refusal names "row 7". Whether that is the data row (1 = the first after the header) or a line of the text, which differs once a quoted field spans lines, is unstated.

## What the RFC assumes and does not state

- **That the tokeniser's leniency is acceptable.** `csv-core` never refuses, skips empty lines, strips a BOM, and ends a record at a lone CR (S8). Every one of these reaches `frame.csv.parse` unless its own reader differs.
- **That a field's quoting can be recovered** from `csv-core`'s consumed input, since no API reports it (S8).
- **That `column_kind`'s merge is the frame reader's too.** S6 says `infer` and `is_int` are shared "by a visibility change only". The rule "integers with floats are floats, anything else a string" is `column_kind` (`data.rs:119-125`), which is not named, and `Kind` (`:60-66`) must become visible with `infer`.
- **That `"sum"` and the other statistics are finite** whenever their cells are (B2).
- **That INTEGER cells of a numeric column convert to f64** for every statistic, so `"min"` and `"max"` of INTEGERs above 2⁵³ are not exact, and `"sum"` is an f64 sum.
- **That cells moved by `frame.take` and the rest keep their q**, and that the CSV, text and JSON paths do not (D32; N3).
- **That `file` hands over valid UTF-8.** `frame.csv.parse` takes a STRING, so it never sees bytes. What `file` does with a non-UTF-8 file was not checked here.
- **That a `.` form taking `opts` from the workbench is wanted.** `frame.sort.`, `frame.csv.parse.`, `frame.aggregate.`, `frame.join.` and `frame.matrix.` take their options MAP from the workbench by D111's rule. The rule makes it so, but a reader may expect the frame there.
- **That `na.count`, `na.omit` and `na.fill` take a LIST only.** They are not RFC-0015 §S3's "one series" words (`RFC-0015-biomathematics.md:375-376`), which would have four forms and accept a METRICS or a drain. That is why they have two forms; the RFC does not say so.
- **That no new crate dependency is needed.** `csv-core` is in the lock already, as `csv`'s dependency (`Cargo.lock:1478`). Using it directly adds it to `bund2-stdlib`'s `Cargo.toml`, a change neither the Preservation table nor criterion 10's `cargo deny` mentions.

## Verdict

**Not ready. 2 blocking, 8 should-fix, 15 notes.** Every citation into `reference/` and every Bund2 symbol says what the RFC says it does. The probes reproduce, and the health numbers are today's. Both earlier reviews' blocking findings are resolved, and D253–D257 are stated as ruled, apart from the three extensions in S6.

The blocking pair are new:
- **B1:** writing NONE as an empty field loses rows in a one-column frame, because the tokeniser skips empty lines. The rulings in play (P6, D254) leave no in-format fix without an owner decision.
- **B2:** `frame.aggregate`'s `"mean"`, copied bit for bit from a word with no overflow check, can answer ∞, against B12 and the RFC's own check. The fix is E114, as RFC-0016 already does.

The should-fixes are mostly statements:
- S1 and S2 are cases in the ruled CSV path that the rulings did not settle;
- S3 makes the `frame.text` rule fit its own example;
- S4 and S5 complete and correct the refusal table;
- S6 lists choices no ruling covers;
- S7 fills criteria gaps;
- S8 reports what `csv-core` does and closes Q65.

B1 and S2 need the owner. Everything else is drafting.
