## 1. Blocking

### B1 — §S6's "55 opaque sites" is the wrong total, and it is the wrong category's number

§S6 line 433 reads:

> `bund2 check` over the 160 corpus programs and probes abandons at **55 opaque
> sites**

followed by a five-row table (`!` 32, `times` 7, `if` 6, `loop` 5, `graph!` 5)
that sums to exactly 55.

Run over the corpus roots RFC-0004 accepted against
(`xtask/src/corpus/mod.rs:23` — `reference/Bund/examples` recursive plus
`reference/Bund/tests`, plus the probes), `bund2 check` reports **128 abandoned
sites**, attributed in its own "analysis stopped at" tallies as:

| stop reason | sites |
|---|---|
| `whose effect is not a fixed pair` — **this is `Opaque`** | **61** |
| `which declares no effect` | **55** |

The five rows in the RFC's table are each individually correct. **The total is
not.** The opaque set is 61, not 55, and the table silently drops six sites
across five words:

| word omitted | sites |
|---|---|
| `?true*` | 2 |
| `pull.` | 1 |
| `map` | 1 |
| `?true` | 1 |
| `?.` | 1 |

That 55 matches the table's sum *and* matches the count of the **other**
category is a coincidence, and it is the kind that hides a mistake rather than
revealing one. The RFC's number is right about nothing it claims.

Three consequences, none cosmetic:

1. **`map` is in the omitted set.** §S5 line ~357 names it explicitly — "`times`,
   `loop`, `map` and `while` all take a LAMBDA operand" — and then §S6's table,
   which exists to measure exactly those, leaves it out.
2. **The whole `?` conditional family is omitted**, four sites. §S6's very next
   sentence (line 444) is "**Eighteen of them are ordinary control flow, and
   `if` is one.**" 18 = `times` 7 + `if` 6 + `loop` 5. Adding `map`, `?true`,
   `?true*` and `?.` — all ordinary control flow — makes it **23 of 61**. The
   sentence is the load-bearing one for §S5's second rule, and both its
   numerator and its denominator are wrong.
3. **The 55 "declares no effect" sites are never mentioned at all**, and they
   stop the same analysis. See S4 below for why that is not merely a bookkeeping
   omission.

The claim also carries **no citation and no command**. §S6 is the section that
decides §S5's specialisation rule; it is the last place in this document that
should rest on a number a reader cannot regenerate. The command is:

    for f in <corpus roots>; do bund2 check --file "$f"; done

### B2 — criterion 11's command does not exist, and it guards the RFC's own stated blocker

The header (line 19) names §S8's frame consumption as "the outstanding blocker",
and §S8 answers it with an entry guard whose verification is criterion 11
(lines 913–914):

        cargo xtask depth                       # feature off — Met at 100,000
        cargo xtask depth --features jit        # must read the same

Run:

    $ cargo xtask depth --features jit
    xtask depth: unknown argument `--features`

`depth::run_cmd` accepts exactly `--depth`, `--timeout` and `--require`, and
returns `Err` on anything else (`xtask/src/depth/mod.rs:221-238`). The flag has
never existed.

It is not a missing-flag problem either, and this is the part that needs a
design answer rather than a patch. `depth` shells out to a **prebuilt binary** —
`target/release/bund2`, else `target/debug/bund2`
(`xtask/src/depth/mod.rs:86-94`) — so the feature has to be compiled into
`bund2` before `depth` runs, and `xtask`'s own feature flags could not affect it
even if they were parsed. Criterion 11 needs either a documented two-step
(`cargo build --release --features jit` then `cargo xtask depth`), which cannot
distinguish the two builds and so cannot report the A/B the criterion asks for,
or a real `--features` path through `bund2_binary`.

The Preservation table's row for the same risk (line 718) makes the same
unrunnable promise: "`cargo xtask depth` at 100,000 with the feature on".

Criterion 11 also asks for two numbers the command could not produce even if it
ran — "the depth at which compiled bodies begin declining" and "the per-entry
cost of the check" — neither of which `depth` reports. Those need a
`crates/bund2-bench` benchmark and an instrumented run, and the criterion should
name them.

### B3 — ERRATA's live conclusion still contradicts the RFC [carried]

The Supersedes bullet (lines 36–41) says §2.1's expectation table is superseded
"Recorded in `docs/research/ERRATA.md`". The entry exists and was partially
updated, but it **ends** (`docs/research/ERRATA.md`, final paragraph):

> so §2.2's gate — "Project B is worth doing only if Project A's measurements
> show that dispatch and boxing are still the bottleneck" — does not pass.
> Boxing is the bottleneck; dispatch is not.

Present tense, and it is the entry's conclusion. The RFC's §S1 update (lines
196–231) says prerequisite 1 is met and "§S2–§S11 are therefore live". The
mid-paragraph sentence "**That corroboration is itself withdrawn,
2026-09-09**" withdraws the *Amdahl* corroboration, not the gate verdict, and
the paragraph then re-derives the gate verdict from the 73.1/126.9 figures D41
superseded.

So a reader who follows the RFC's own pointer lands on a document asserting the
opposite of the RFC. Research documents are immutable and ERRATA is the
mechanism, so the fix is a further ERRATA line recording D41 — not an edit to
the existing one. Until that line exists, the Supersedes bullet's "Recorded in
ERRATA" is not true of the thing it matters for.

### B4 — the Open questions section lists Q25 as live and this RFC as blocked on it [carried]

Lines 943–950:

> **Q25 — what replaces the stack tag on `push`?** §S1 requires
> `value/push_pull/balanced` under 20 ns and deliberately does not say how. …
> **RFC-0001's**, not this RFC's, and this RFC is blocked on it in the sense
> that its own premise is.

This is flatly contradicted by the same document at line 203 ("Prerequisite 1 is
met … `push_pull/balanced` is 9.8"), by **D41** (`docs/registers/decisions.md`
line 2127, "the stack tag rides in the value's padding for scalars"), and by
**Q29**, which records itself as "**Resolved as D41 and implemented**".

The question is answered. Leaving it in the Open questions section as a live
blocker is the single most misleading line in the document for anyone deciding
whether this RFC can move to Proposed — it says the RFC is blocked on its own
premise when the premise is met.

The register is also stale here: Q25 still stands as an ordinary open row
(`docs/registers/open-questions.md:13`) with no resolution marker, while Q29
immediately below it (line 17) resolves the same question. One of the two needs a
pointer to the other.

## 2. Significant

### S1 — the benchmark names in §S1 are still not benchmark IDs [carried]

§S1's tables name `dispatch/literal_push`, `dispatch/dup_drop`,
`dispatch/native_call`, `dispatch/literal_only`, `value/with_tag/scalar`,
`value/clone/scalar`, `value/push_pull/balanced`. The IDs Criterion actually
registers (`crates/bund2-bench/benches/interpret.rs:109-127, 140-190`):

| RFC name | actual ID | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `dispatch/literal_push` | `dispatch/literal_push/w2000` | prefix-matches |
| `dispatch/dup_drop` | `dispatch/dup_drop/w3000` | prefix-matches |
| `dispatch/native_call` | `dispatch/native_call/w4000` | prefix-matches |
| `dispatch/literal_only` | `dispatch/literal_only/w1000` | prefix-matches |
| `value/clone/scalar` | `value/clone/scalar` | exact |
| `value/push_pull/balanced` | `value/push_pull/balanced` | exact |
| **`value/with_tag/scalar`** | `value/with_tag/scalar_unique`, `value/with_tag/heap_shared` | **names nothing; two benchmarks exist and they differ by ~an order of magnitude** |

The four `dispatch/*` truncations are survivable because Criterion filters on
prefix. `value/with_tag/scalar` is not: it is the ID behind the 73.1 ns figure,
the two real benchmarks measure the unique and shared arms respectively, and
which one 73.1 came from is exactly the distinction §S1's own next paragraph
turns on ("mints and clones only on the shared arm, which the production path
does not take"). ERRATA propagates the same non-existent ID.

### S2 — 9.8 ns against the register's recorded 10.1 ns

The RFC gives `value/push_pull/balanced` as **9.8 ns** at lines 8, 200, 203, 426
and 779. **Q29** records the chain as "126.9 -> 96.7 -> 52.0 -> **10.1** ns"
(`docs/registers/open-questions.md:17`).

The prior review flagged the RFC quoting both. It now quotes one consistently —
and it is the one the register does not have. Neither figure is cited to a run.
Both clear the 20 ns gate so nothing turns on it, which is precisely why it will
not get fixed unless it is named: one of the two documents is recording a
measurement that was never taken.

### S3 — §S4's discharge of §3.2d covers inter-word calls; §3.2d is about helper calls [carried]

§S4 (lines ~340–350) claims: "**This also discharges §3.2d.** … the study's
mitigation is to … 'route runtime-helper calls through an indirection table so
helper addresses are never a relocation-range problem'. The indirect-slot rule
above *is* that indirection table".

Re-read at source: §3.2d's mitigation names **runtime-helper** calls
(`docs/research/00-jit-feasibility.md`, §3.2(d)). The indirect-slot rule as §S4
states it covers **inter-word** calls: "Every inter-word call loads a pointer
from a runtime-owned slot and calls it indirectly."

Those are different call sets. Runtime helpers are calls from compiled code into
Bund2's own Rust runtime — the stack push/pull, the sync at an opaque site
(§S5's second rule requires one), the generic arithmetic path a type guard
branches to. The RFC names them repeatedly ("everything after it runs through
runtime helpers") and never says they go through a slot. The discharge is
asserted over a set the rule does not cover.

Either extend the rule to helpers explicitly, or state that helpers are direct
calls inside the arena and that `ArenaMemoryProvider`'s contiguous reservation
is what bounds them — which is the other half of §3.2d and is what the RFC
adopts, but it is adopted for the module and never connected to the helpers.

### S4 — 55 sites hit words that declare no effect, and §S5's guard ordering assumes every body has one

§S5 states: "**RFC-0004 orders the guards.** A word's declared or inferred
`StackEffect` gives the arity a guard must check before entry."

B1's measurement shows **55 sites** where `bund2 check` stops because the word
**declares no effect** — a distinct category from `Opaque`, and one the RFC
never mentions. For those call sites there is no arity to guard on, declared or
inferred.

That is not an `Opaque` site and §S5's second rule does not cover it. Three
readings are possible and the RFC picks none: the site is treated as opaque
(promotion stops, and the promotable region is smaller than §S6 implies); the
guard is omitted (and the "RFC-0004 orders the guards" sentence has an
unstated exception); or such words are ineligible for compilation entirely
(which needs saying, and interacts with §S7's threshold).

This is the substantive half of B1. The count is a bookkeeping error; this is a
hole in the design.

### S5 — the health metric is quoted without its ceiling

The RFC quotes **73/86** at lines 230, 683 and 764. CLAUDE.md's health-metric
section is explicit:

> `conform` also prints a **`CEILING`** … Quoting `N/M` without it overstates
> the remaining work.

`cargo xtask conform` prints `CONFORMANCE 73/86 (+7 approved deviation(s))` and
`CEILING 79/86 (6 golden(s) still to reach it)`. The ceiling appears nowhere in
this document. Criterion 2 ("`conform` reads the same N/M with the feature on
and off") is unaffected in substance — a zero-movement criterion does not need
the ceiling — but the four narrative quotations do.

### S6 — D3's recorded status names a cache key D35 replaced [carried]

§S3 says "**D35 resolves the key: the body's `Rc` pointer**" and then "D3's
amended resolution rests on exactly this".

D3's recorded status (`docs/registers/decisions.md:130-131`):

> **RESOLVED — no eval-specific tier rule. Eligibility falls out of the
> **content-hash** compiled cache, bounded by that cache's cap.**

Content-hash keying is the option D35 considered and did not take — the RFC's
own Alternatives section says so ("Content-hashed cache keys instead of pointer
keys. D35 considered and rejected this for now"). So §S3 asserts agreement with
a register entry that names the rejected key.

The *argument* survives the substitution — §S3's own sentence, "eval'd bodies
are fresh `Rc`s per call, so their count never reaches 1, let alone 64", is
sound under pointer keying — which is why nobody has noticed. Registers are
append-only and a status may be changed; this is a status that should be.

### S7 — the register has two Q22s [carried]

§S7's heading is "Tiering policy: threshold, cap, demotion — **Q22**, answered
here". `docs/registers/open-questions.md` has **Q22 at line 49**, resolved, about
five unprobeable words, and **Q22 at line 1078**, open, about the compiled-cache
threshold and cap. §S7 means the second. A reader grepping `Q22` finds the first.

The register is where the fix belongs, but the RFC could disambiguate by quoting
the question rather than only its number.

### S8 — D41 is credited with the whole 126.9 → 9.8

The header (line 8) says "**D41 changed that** … push/pull 126.9 → 9.8 ns".
D41's own entry (`docs/registers/decisions.md:2129-2132`) attributes the first
and larger portion elsewhere:

> Options 1 and 2 of RFC-0001's amendment took `value/push_pull/balanced` from
> **126.9 ns to 52.0 ns** without changing any layout.

D41 is the 52.0 → 9.8 (or 10.1, per S2) step. §S1's update does say "RFC-0001's
Q25 amendment records the chain", which is a pointer rather than an attribution;
the header does not, and the header is what a reader of the status line sees.

### S9 — "D33's default" is not something D33 declares

§S6: "This RFC does not take D33's **default**: it requires mixed-kind
comparison to take the generic path".

D33 (`docs/registers/decisions.md:1515-1564`) has **no `Default:` line**. It has
three options, a **Recommendation** (option 2), and a "What is implemented
meanwhile" section naming **option 1**, "because preserving the reference is the
default and needs no decision". So the RFC's rule — generic path, reference
answers — *is* what is implemented today, and the RFC is not withholding
anything; it is describing the status quo. CLAUDE.md's prohibition is on
silently adopting a default, and this is the opposite failure: announcing a
refusal of a default that does not exist.

§S6 also names only option 2 as unlocking the lowering. **Option 3** — "Extend,
and reject mixed kinds instead" — would too, and differently: there would be no
mixed-kind comparison to lower and the generic path would be a bail. Worth one
clause, because the two resolutions imply different code.

### S10 — `multistackvm_call.rs:8` is quoted non-verbatim

§S4 renders it as `self.apply(Value::call(name, Vec::new()))`. The line is:

    self.apply(Value::call(name.clone(), Vec::new()))

The `.clone()` is dropped. Nothing turns on it and `cite` cannot see it (the
quotation is inline, not a fenced block with a `path:line` header). Flagged
because §S4's whole point is that this chain "is contract", and a contract
quoted approximately invites the next reader to quote it approximately again.

### S11 — the withdrawn `with_tag` diagnosis is still live in the bench source

§S1 says of the four-allocations description: "**No line is cited for that,
deliberately: the code no longer exists.** … it is kept in the past tense for
that reason." That is the right disposition for the RFC.

But `crates/bund2-bench/benches/interpret.rs:130-137` still states it in the
present tense, as the doc comment on the `value` group:

> `with_tag` boxes the scalar, clones the whole `HeapValue` including its
> `BTreeMap` of tags, inserts two freshly allocated `String`s, and wraps the
> result in a new `Rc`

`crates/bund2-value/src/lib.rs:2039-2054` takes `Rc<str>` and does the identity
+ clone only on the `None` (shared) arm, as the RFC correctly says. Not an RFC
defect — but the RFC's claim that the description is confined to the past tense
is true only of this document, and the surviving copy sits on the benchmark
whose numbers §S1 argues from. Q25's register row carries the same stale text.

### S12 — "160 corpus programs and probes" is 161 here

§S6 line 433 says 160; RFC-0004's acceptance says "160 programs, 132 corpus plus
28 probes". Enumerating the roots at `xtask/src/corpus/mod.rs:23` plus
`tests/probes/*.bund` (non-recursive) gives **132 + 29 = 161**, and including
`tests/probes/features/` gives 162. The drift is small and RFC-0004 owns the
figure, but §S6 restates it as if measured.

### S13 — "runnable" is claimed for criteria that need a tier that does not exist

Lines 767–775 say the `--features jit` criteria "became runnable on 2026-09-09"
and that "before that none of 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 or 12 could be executed
at all", then correctly marks criterion 2 as vacuous.

Of those nine: **11 cannot run at all** (B2). **4, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 12** all
require compiled code to inspect, promote, sync or A/B, and there is none —
`crates/bund2-jit/src/lib.rs` is twelve lines of lint configuration. **7 runs
and reads zero by construction**, as `crates/bund2-bench/Cargo.toml:22-27` says
in its own words: "there is no tier behind it yet, so with the feature on the
benchmarks measure the same thing and the A/B reads zero."

So the honest statement is that **two** criteria run (2 and 7), both are
vacuous, and one (11) is unrunnable as written. The RFC applies the "vacuous
until a tier exists" caveat to criterion 2 alone, which reads as though the
others are in a different state.

| 8 | **yes, and passing** | `cargo xtask cite` — verified, no finding against RFC-0005 |
| 9 | **yes** | a `bund2-bench` benchmark at run lengths 1/4/16/64, 5% band, crossover reported. The "crossover is reported, not optional" rewrite closes the loophole the prior wording left |
| 10 | **yes** | A/B on one compiled body; fails below 1.2× on `1 2 + drop`. Deliberately falsifiable, and the RFC says so |
| 11 | **NO — the command does not exist** | B2. And the two numbers it asks for (declining depth, per-entry cost) are not produced by `depth` at all |
| 12 | **yes** | a probe that pushes, promotes, syncs and dumps, with `conform` green |

So eleven of twelve are checkable and name a tool. The exception is the one
covering the failure mode the RFC itself calls its outstanding blocker.

One gap across the set: **no criterion covers a Cranelift compilation failure.**
If `define_function` returns `Err` — a CLIF verifier error, an out-of-memory
arena, an unsupported lowering added later — the RFC never says what happens.
D37 forbids the panic; the correct behaviour is presumably "fall back to Tier 0
for that body and report a `Notice`", which is exactly §S11's and D36's shape.
It is unstated and untested.

## 5. Preservation analysis — what the table does not enumerate

The table has eleven rows and is much stronger than the prior draft's: the
machine-stack row (B7 of the last review) and the D41 stack-symbol row are both
present, and both are the reviewer-found kind the RFC now credits. What is still
missing:

1. **`unregister` against a live slot.** §S4 names `unregister` as one of the
   three word-table mutators that force indirect calls, and the table has a row
   for *redefinition* but none for *removal*. A compiled caller holding a slot
   whose word was unregistered must produce the reference's "not registered"
   error through `Vm::report`, not call a stale or null pointer. Under D37 the
   difference is an abort.

2. **`alias` retargeted after compilation.** §S4 concludes "a slot must name the
   *resolved* target, not the spelling", which is right and is what makes
   re-aliasing observable: if `foo` is aliased to `bar` when a caller is
   compiled and re-aliased to `baz` afterwards, a slot keyed on `bar` never
   sees it. The table's redefinition row does not cover this — the *alias*
   moved, not the word.

3. **The `$` path resolves aliases once, not twice, and skips `apply`'s
   resolution entirely.** §S4's step 4 presents `$name → call_internal_word` as
   a terminal, and attributes "double alias resolution" to step 7 alone. Read
   one call further, as CLAUDE.md requires: `call_internal_word` strips the
   sigil and calls `i()`
   (`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_call_internal_word.rs:7-8`),
   and `i()` resolves aliases (`multistackvm_inline.rs:71-72`). So `$foo`
   resolves **once**, in `i`, while plain `foo` resolves **twice** — in `apply`
   at `:39-40` and again in `i`. On a two-deep alias chain the two spellings
   reach different targets. A slot keyed on "the resolved target" is therefore
   ambiguous for `$`-prefixed names, and the RFC's rule does not say which
   resolution it means. ERRATA already records the related correction (F26: `$`
   does not bypass alias resolution, it skips the lambda check only); §S4's
   framing does not contradict ERRATA but does not absorb it either.

4. **Diagnostic fidelity from compiled code.** D36 requires a structured
   `Diagnostic` carrying severity, reason, **Bund source location** and an
   optional stack snapshot, delivered through `Vm::report`
   (`crates/bund2-api/src/lib.rs:220`). A compiled body has no interpreter loop
   holding the current stream position. If a guard or a helper fails inside
   compiled code, what fills the location field? §S11 settles the *protocol*
   (return-value, not unwinding) and says nothing about the *content*. A
   `Diagnostic` whose location is absent under `--features jit` and present
   without it is a behaviour difference, and 39 goldens already prove the
   project's error text is compared.

5. **Identity and `stamp` under promotion (D13, D20).** A promoted region that
   never materialises a `BundValue` also never mints an identity. D20 enumerates
   materialisation points; D35 found an unlisted one. Whether a value that lived
   only in a `Variable` and was then synced has the same `.id` as one that never
   left the stack is not addressed. Criterion 12 covers the *stack symbol*; it
   does not cover `.id` or `.timestamp`.

6. **`Warning` and `Notice` from compiled code.** D36 makes these one line on
   stderr rather than a full report. A compiled body that emits them at a
   different point in the program — or not at all, because a promoted region
   skipped the word that would have emitted one — changes stderr, which goldens
   capture.

7. **`Fold` at run time.** §S6 says D12's barrier "still has to be
   *represented*, because D16 means one may appear at run time". Representing it
   is a `*`-family word arriving through `!` at a call site that was compiled
   assuming a fixed depth. The Preservation table's row says "D12's barrier,
   represented not assumed" — which names the guard as the thing being asserted.
   What *detects* the arrival at run time is unspecified.

8. **Counter thrash under repeated `bund.eval`.** §S7 caps the counter at 4096
   and evicts the coldest. D3's whole argument is that eval'd bodies are fresh
   `Rc`s per call — so a loop calling `bund.eval` inserts a *new* counter entry
   every iteration and evicts genuinely hot bodies, permanently starving them of
   promotion. That is not a correctness failure, so it does not belong in the
   Preservation table, but it defeats §S7's threshold in exactly the workload
   D3 exists to reason about, and §S7 should say so.

9. **The `autoadd` guard's granularity.** §S4 requires an entry guard "and
   decline to run when it is set". `autoadd` is VM-wide and mutable, and `:`/`;`
   are reachable by computed name through `!`. A body that enters with the flag
   clear, calls `!` on a name that turns out to be `:`, and continues — the
   entry guard has already passed. §S4 handles this by making `:` and `;`
   opaque sites, which stops *promotion*; it does not stop the *compiled calls
   after that point in the same body* from being wrong, because the guard was an
   entry guard and there is no OSR to re-check with. Either every post-opaque
   call re-checks the flag, or the RFC should say that promotion stopping is
   sufficient because everything after an opaque site is a runtime helper that
   re-enters the interpreter's dispatch. The second is probably true and is not
   stated.

## 7. What the RFC assumes and does not state

1. **That `jit` is not a default feature.** Criterion 2's "on and off" requires
   it. True today (`crates/bund2-jit/Cargo.toml:20`, `default = []`) and
   unstated; if it ever becomes a default, the criterion silently stops being an
   A/B.

2. **That the counter's `Weak` and the cache's `Rc` are over the same
   allocation.** §S7's non-reuse argument — "a live `Weak` keeps the allocation,
   and therefore the address `Rc::as_ptr` returns, out of circulation" — is only
   true if both structures hold references to the *same* `Rc`. If a body is
   cloned anywhere between counting and compiling, the addresses differ and both
   the pinning claim and the false-hit argument fail.

3. **That compiled code never reads the body's `Vec<BundValue>` after
   compilation.** This is what would make S1's "the body's contents are dropped
   on the last strong reference, while the address stays reserved" safe for the
   counter. The RFC states it for the counter and never says it for compiled
   code, which is where it matters more.

4. **That the `autoadd` entry guard is sound at entry granularity.** §9 above.

5. **That every compilable word has a `StackEffect`.** S4 above — 55 measured
   sites say otherwise.

6. **That a demoted body stays demoted across `unregister`/`register` of the
   same name.** §S7 says demotion is "permanent, per body", and D35 keys on the
   body's `Rc`. A re-registered word has a *new* body and therefore a new key,
   so the permanent demotion is trivially escapable by re-registering — which is
   precisely the "REPL redefiner" §S7's recompile cap exists to stop. Either the
   demotion is per-slot (contradicting "per body") or the cap is escapable.

7. **That the machine-stack headroom check can be written portably and without
   `unsafe`.** §S8 requires it on every compiled body entry and criterion 11
   budgets 2 ns for it. Rust has no stable stack-limit API; the usual
   implementations take the address of a local and compare against a
   thread-recorded base, which needs the base captured at thread start. Nothing
   says where that happens, and for a thread Bund2 did not spawn it may not be
   knowable.

8. **That §S1's medians are reproducible.** No machine, no date, no baseline
   name, no CPU governor. Every gate decision in the document rests on them.

9. **That the 86 goldens exercise a promoted body at all.** Criterion 12 says
   plainly that none does today. So criterion 2's "conformance moves by exactly
   zero" is guaranteed by the goldens not reaching the tier, not by the tier
   preserving behaviour — a green criterion 2 will be evidence of nothing until
   a golden runs compiled code. The RFC notes this for criterion 12's own risk
   and does not draw the conclusion for criterion 2.

10. **That the AOT path shares this design.** `crates/bund2-jit/Cargo.toml:23`
    gates `aot` on `cranelift-object` in the same crate, and §S10 requires both
    features to build. AOT has no `JITModule`, so §S4's slot rule, §S7's caps
    and §S3's `Rc` keying either apply differently or not at all. The RFC is
    titled "the Cranelift backend" and covers only the JIT; RFC-0006 is not
    named as owning the difference.

## 8. Recommendation

**Revise before Proposed.**

The design has improved substantially. §S8's frame-consumption problem is
correctly identified and correctly classified as conformance-and-D37 rather than
performance; §S7's `Weak`/`Rc` split and its willingness to say that D35's
stated rationale does not hold are the strongest passages in the document;
criteria 3, 9 and 10 were rewritten from unfalsifiable into falsifiable. Every
`path:line` in the document is accurate.

What blocks it is that two of its load-bearing artefacts do not survive being
run. §S6's promotability measurement — the section that decides §S5's
specialisation rule — reports a number that is neither the total nor the
category it names, and its table omits `map`, which §S5 names one paragraph
earlier. Criterion 11, the only check on the correctness problem the header
calls the outstanding blocker, invokes a flag that does not exist against a tool
that runs a prebuilt binary. Both are the same failure: a claim that reads as
measured and was not re-run.

Fix B1–B4, answer S3 and S4 (the two that are design holes rather than
bookkeeping), and this is ready for Proposed with the remainder as revision
notes.
