# RFC-0001 review, 2026-08-27 (review 7)

Full pass, not the focused one. Tree: `91c6a46`, RFC at 1317 lines. Every
`reference/…:N` citation in the document was opened. Reference submodules
verified clean (F44's remediation holds).

**Verdict: do not accept.**

The design work is close. Review 6's five substantive items were all acted on
and acted on well: the mutation taxonomy grew the third class `attr_add`
needed, `ValueMap` went back to a `HashMap` with the reasoning spelled out,
`dup`'s treatment of the other five header fields is now stated, `Send`/`Sync`
has a section RFC-0002 can cite, and D7 no longer asserts the property lazy
sampling gives up.

What blocks it is that **the repairs landed in the design body and not in the
sections that quote it**. Six places still assert what the body now retracts —
including one where the body says *in terms* "the reason two earlier drafts
gave for this no longer holds" and two other sections go on giving it. And the
first citation defect in three passes: `bund_bbus.rs:177` does not say what the
RFC claims, and the "six callers" it belongs to is seven.

## B. The repairs did not propagate

`lint` cannot see any of these: each is a section agreeing with an older
version of another section.

**B1. §"Value semantics" still says `set_tag` is the only reachable
minting-free mutation** (line 553):

> Mutations that mint nothing, **of which `set_tag` is the only one
> reachable**, are split points too…

§"One policy…" class 3, twenty lines earlier, exists to retract exactly this:

> An earlier draft said `set_tag` was the only reachable one. **It is not**,
> and this RFC establishes the others itself several pages earlier…

Review 6's finding was acted on in one section and not the other, so the
document now states the claim and its retraction, both in the present tense,
under adjacent headings.

**B2. §"Value semantics" also files `attr_add` as a CoW split** (line 551):

> **every mutating operation that the reference gives a fresh id to is a CoW
> split point** (`set.rs:…`, `push.rs:165`, `attr.rs:7,13,19` — and `attr_add`
> is explicit about it, calling `regen_id` at `attr.rs:19`).

Class 2 says `attr_add` is *not* a split — it is "Regenerate in place", its own
class, "because a two-bucket partition has nowhere to put that". The same
sentence that names the third class in one section puts it back in the first in
another. Class 1's members (`set` on a map/list, `push`) are also listed here
as splits, where class 1 calls them rebuilds. As written, §"Value semantics" is
the two-bucket taxonomy the RFC replaced.

**B3. The Bool/Nodata/None preservation row and criterion D2 both give a reason
the RFC explicitly retracts.**

§"Equality for scalars is content" (lines 629-635):

> **The reason two earlier drafts gave for this no longer holds.** They argued
> that scalars *have no identity to compare*. Since review 3 made scalars
> boxable, a boxed scalar has a header and therefore an identity slot, **so the
> premise is false**…

Preservation row (line 820): "**Deliberately changed** — *scalars have no
identity to compare*."

Criterion D2 (line 927): "**Not for a bool**: *scalars carry no identity*, so
`true == true.dup()` is true in Bund2…"

Both use the premise the body declares false, one of them inside an acceptance
criterion. The replacement reason the body gives — equality must be stable
under boxing, so it must be content — is better and belongs in both places.

**B4. Two rows and one open question still say `ValueMap` is a `BTreeMap`.**

§"The heap object" now says, at length and correctly, that `Map` is a
`BTreeMap` and `ValueMap` **must** be a `HashMap` because D30's mechanism is
hashing. The declaration matches. Three places did not follow:

- Row F15 (line 817): "`Payload` uses `BTreeMap`, so **both rendering paths are
  ordered by construction**." Only one is; the `ValueMap` path is ordered by
  the renderer, which is the distinction §"The heap object" spends a paragraph
  drawing.
- Row Q19 (line 832): "`Payload::ValueMap` is a **`BTreeMap<BundValue,
  BundValue>`**".
- §"Open questions" (line 985): the same phrase again.

**B5. The `curr` row still says nothing advances it** (line 830): "**Preserved
as a field.** *Nothing advances it today*; RFC-0003 decides whether Bund2's
iteration does." §"Iteration" and §"`curr` is the iteration cursor" both say it
has six callers and is advanced. Reported in review 6; not fixed. The row also
still fails to record the RFC's actual decision — that Bund2's `next` uses a
locally owned cursor — which is a deviation with no row anywhere.

**B6. Three call-sites use the pre-partition criterion numbers.** The criteria
were renumbered to B1-B4 / V1-V2 / D1-D8, but §"Scalars have no header" says
"the very fact **criterion 3** uses" (line 568), "**Criterion 6** asks the
`Debug` rendering" (line 580) and "**Criteria 2 and 3** are restated against
that" (line 610). None of those exist. (`tests/probes/payload-arms.bund`'s
header comment says "criterion 7" for the same reason — repo-wide, not just
here.)

**B7. Leftover `29`** (line 581): "an inline scalar arm cannot reproduce any of
those **29**" — five lines after the same paragraph updates the figure to 40.
Review 5 found the identical leftover at the identical spot, then reading `24`.

**B8. Status line** (line 3): "revised through five reviews" — six.

## C. Internal contradictions in the new material

**C1. §"Hashing mirrors equality" enumerates the content-hashed kinds as four,
and this RFC has six.**

> Content-compared kinds (`Int`, `Float`, `String`, `Time`) hash their content;
> identity-compared kinds hash their identity.

That is the *reference's* partition. This RFC changes `Bool`, `Nodata` and
`None` to content equality (§"Equality for scalars is content"), and says so
elsewhere — "under D30 a bool key hashes by content anyway" (line 644). Taken
literally, §"Hashing" hashes a bool by identity while comparing it by content,
which breaks the `Hash`/`Eq` contract in the one structure that consumes it
(`Payload::ValueMap`, now a `HashMap`) and defeats D6's scalar-key assertion
for bool keys. The mirror rule is right; its enumeration is one revision stale.

**C2. `HashMap<BundValue, BundValue>` requires `Eq`, and `NaN` denies it.**

§"Hashing" states: "`NaN` is never equal to anything, including itself, so its
hash is unconstrained". `Eq` is the marker for a *reflexive* equality, and
`HashMap`'s key bound is `Eq + Hash`. A `BundValue` holding `Float(f64)`
therefore cannot honestly implement `Eq` — the same fault this RFC convicts the
reference of forty lines earlier: "`impl Eq` asserts the symmetry it does not
have (`:59-62`)". Bund2 fixes the symmetry and reintroduces the
non-reflexivity.

The consequence is concrete and reachable now that `get` reads a valuemap: a
`NaN` key can be inserted and can never be found, removed, or overwritten, and
repeated inserts accumulate. The reference's version of this is invisible
because F29 leaves no read path; D30 creates one. Either the RFC states that
`NaN` is rejected as a valuemap key, or it states that reflexivity is
deliberately broken and what that costs.

**C3. Rendering is a materialisation need in one section and not in the other.**

§"The heap object" (line 338): "`identity` and `stamp` are `Cell`s because
equality, hashing **and rendering** mint through `&self`."

§"Identity: lazy…" (line 420): "D1 enumerates the needs: `.id`, equality,
ordering, hashing, serialisation. **All five materialise.**"

Rendering is a sixth need, and it is not one of D1's. That is fine as an
extension — the `Debug` output prints `id:` and `stamp:`, so it must be — but
the RFC consumes D1 and should say it is adding to D1's list rather than
enumerating it.

**C4. The `.id`/`.timestamp` promotion rule does not close the hole it is
written for.**

New text (lines 599-606):

> The state is reachable: `"[1,2]" json json.to_value` leaves inner scalars
> with `tags: {}`, so a program can hold an unadorned scalar and then
> interrogate it. **So `.id` and `.timestamp` box**: observing either promotes
> the value, and the promoted form is what the stack holds.

Promotion works for a value sitting *directly on the stack*, which is what
`.id`'s `peek` sees (`base_classes.rs:12-16`). It does not work for the case
the RFC cites as its evidence. An inner scalar of a json-derived list is
reached with `get`, and `Value::get` returns `d.clone()`
(`reference/rust_dynamic/src/get.rs:11`) — a copy. Promoting the copy does not
write back into the list, so:

```
l 0 get .id      \ mints id X
l 0 get .id      \ mints id Y ≠ X
```

still yields two different strings where the reference yields one, because the
element remains `Int(1)` with nowhere to keep what was minted. The same holds
for `.timestamp`, and for `debug.display_stack` rendering the list twice — the
renderer takes `&self` through an `Rc<Payload>` and cannot promote either, so
the inner `id:` fields differ between two renderings of one list. F14
normalises that away in the goldens, which is why no criterion can catch it.

The honest positions are (a) promotion covers stack-resident scalars and inner
scalars are a stated deviation, or (b) a container's elements are boxed on
construction so they always have somewhere to keep an identity — which prices
`json.to_value` of a 1000-element list at 1000 headers. The RFC currently
implies (b) is unnecessary while its own example needs it.

**C5. "A scalar never touches the heap"** (line 292) is stated as a measured
property and retracted at line 609 ("holds only *before* a push"). The
retraction is explicit and well done; the earlier sentence should carry the
qualifier rather than leaving 300 lines between the claim and its limit.

## G. Consistency with accepted RFCs and ERRATA

**Improved and now correct.** §"`BundValue` is neither `Send` nor `Sync`"
closes the gap review 6 found: RFC-0002 lines 527-538 cite RFC-0001 for exactly
this, and RFC-0001 now says it, with the same reasoning (`Rc` is neither,
`Cell` is not `Sync`) and the same consequence for RFC-0007. No daylight
between the two documents.

**ERRATA** — consistent. The `05-rfc-roadmap.md` §6 supersession matches the
header; the retirement of `00-jit-feasibility.md` §4.1 matches §"Identity:
lazy…" and `base_classes.rs:16`.

**RFC-0000** — consistent on D20 (`bincode.rs:9-28`, `:54-69`,
`create_special.rs:205,207` are cited identically in both and both resolve),
and on the conformance gate. One thing to reconcile: RFC-0000 says `conform`
normalises "`id`/`stamp` (F14) and **map order** (F15)". RFC-0001's `ValueMap`
renderer sorts entries by their *rendered form*, and a rendered form contains
the `id` that normalisation later replaces with `<id>`. So Bund2's sort key
includes data the comparison erases, and two entries differing only in `id`
have an order the normaliser cannot reconcile. Probably harmless, since the
normaliser sorts members; it is an assumption the RFC leans on and does not
name.

**RFC-0002 `curr`** — RFC-0002 line 525 says `curr` is "a plain field precisely
so clones do not share a cursor", matching §"The heap object". Both are then in
tension with §"`curr` is the iteration cursor", which has Bund2's `next` not
touching the field at all. As specified across the three passages, `curr` is
written once to `-1` and never again — which is the design review 5 rejected.
If that is now intended, say it outright.

## H. Assumed but not stated

1. `HeapValue: Clone`, required by `Rc::make_mut` and by the split policy.
2. `identity == 0` is a sentinel the minting counter never produces.
3. `Payload::Scalar` never nests (F7).
4. `BundValue: Eq + Hash` is honest enough for `HashMap`, despite `NaN` (C2).
5. The `ValueMap` renderer's sort key is stable under the goldens'
   normalisation (G).
6. Promotion by `.id`/`.timestamp` writes back into the stack slot — the RFC
   says "the promoted form is what the stack holds", but `.id` is specified in
   the reference as a `peek`, so Bund2 is changing a read into a
   read-and-replace and does not say how.
7. Which `Rc` the split policy governs. There are two — `Rc<HeapValue>` and
   `HeapValue.payload: Rc<Payload>` — and D13 asks for "one policy governing
   both" the pointer and the slot. The payload `Rc` arrived with `dup` and the
   policy has never been restated over it. A payload split plausibly needs no
   materialisation, since identity lives above it; that is the argument, and it
   is still absent.
8. That `set_q`/`calc_q` only ever write a freshly constructed, unshared value
   — now that class 3 admits them as minting-free mutations, whether every
   arithmetic result is unshared decides whether arithmetic splits.
9. That the four `Val::Null` renderings at `dt: 0` versus `dt: 97` are
   distinguished on the wire (D4's probe reaches only `NODATA`).

## I. What is right

Worth recording so it is not relitigated:

- The three-class mutation taxonomy is correct, and class 2 is a real
  discovery: `attr_add` mints while preserving, which neither of the two
  earlier buckets could express.
- The `Map`-`BTreeMap` / `ValueMap`-`HashMap` split, and the three-way argument
  for why a total `Ord` over `BundValue` is unavailable (F12's FIX, `NaN`, and
  `lt`/`le`/`gt`/`ge` returning `bool` rather than `Ordering`), is exactly
  right.
- `dup`'s new paragraph on the other five header fields, and materialising the
  stamp before copying, closes a real hole with the right rule.
- The `dt`-reachability section is a model of how to state a negative claim:
  method named, method's failure record named, positive corroboration, and an
  explicit refusal to call it proof.
- §"`BundValue` is neither `Send` nor `Sync`" resolves a genuine cross-RFC gap.
- D7's rewrite is correct where the previous version asserted the opposite.
