## Blocking

### B1. `model.predict`'s response-scale intervals: an infinite end has two answers, and a finite end can be wrong

§S6 maps both link-scale ends through the inverse link, and takes `lower` as the smaller and `upper` as the larger, "since `inverse` and `1/mu^2` are decreasing" (906-910). That holds only inside the link's valid η (§S4, 642-646).
- **An infinite end.** The zero group, which §S4 answers on purpose (722-726, criterion 3), has an indicator coefficient −22.69 with se ≈ 2.4·10⁴ (from W = μ, the group's μ ≈ 5.6·10⁻¹⁰). A new row in that group has η̂ ≈ −21.3 and η̂ + 1.96·se ≈ 4.8·10⁴, so `upper` = exp(4.8·10⁴) = +∞. The header consumes D251 A3 (line 15), "An infinite interval end: **A** leave the key out" (`decisions.md:17227`). The body never applies A3, and §S1's general rule (385-386) makes the same case E114. The text gives two answers, and the one it states contradicts the consumed ruling.
- **An end across a pole.** Under `inverse`, a link-scale interval [−0.1, 0.5] maps to −10 and 2. §S6 answers `lower` −10 and `upper` 2, but the image of the interval is (−∞, −10] ∪ [2, ∞). Under `1/mu^2`, an end with η ≤ 0 is NaN, which E114 catches only by the general rule.
- **An end across the `sqrt` fold.** Under `sqrt`, [−1, 2] maps to 1 and 4, but the image is [0, 4]. With both ends negative, the mapped interval is the mirror of the right one.
- **An end outside `validmu`.** A binomial `log` interval's upper end can exceed 1, and a Poisson or Gamma `identity` lower end can be negative. Each is finite and monotone, but outside the family's range.

Criterion 5 cannot see any of this. Its Gamma `inverse` fit checks only that `lower` < `upper`, on data the test chooses. The point prediction has the same exposure at an η̂ outside `valideta`, though R's `predict.glm` simply applies `linkinv` there (`predict.glm.R:44`, `:61`).

**Fix:** in §S6, state per row:
- an end whose link-scale value leaves the link's valid η, or an interval that contains η = 0 under `inverse`, is refused (E101 naming the row and the link) or answered without that row's interval, whichever is chosen;
- an end that maps to ±∞ follows A3, or the text says why A3 does not apply. A3 is for a scalar key, and `lower` and `upper` are LISTs, so say whether the key goes, or the row is refused;
- whether ends outside `validmu` are answered as computed.

Mark the choices **[author]** for the owner. Add the zero-group fit, or an `inverse` fit with a wide interval, to criterion 5. Either drop A3 from the header or cite where it is applied.

## Should fix

### SF1. An interaction's member order and repeated members are undefined

§S2 accepts "a LIST of two or more STRINGs" (419-420), refuses a term that repeats (427-428, step 3 at 522-523), and requires every member as an earlier term (428-430). It says neither whether `[ "dose" "site" ]` and `[ "site" "dose" ]` are one term, nor what `[ "dose" "dose" ]` codes. R's interaction is the bitwise OR of its members' variable sets (`model.c:1343-1349`, `OrBits`, used by `InteractTerms` at `:1475-1489`). So in R `b:a` is `a:b`, which `TrimRepeats` then drops as a repeat, and `a:a` is `a`.

Read literally, §S2 does three things R does not:
- `[ "dose" "dose" ]` codes dose², a quadratic term R never makes from `:`;
- `[ "site" "site" ]` codes (m − 1)² products, each a copy of a main-effect column or all 0.0, which is E106;
- `[ "dose" "site" ]` beside `[ "site" "dose" ]` passes "not repeated" and the name check (`dose:site.north` ≠ `site.north:dose`), and meets E106 with nothing to say why.

Criterion 1 ("each row of §S8 for `model.matrix` … by a stated input") cannot test what is not specified.

**Fix:** state that an interaction's members are distinct (E101 naming the term, a new §S8 row) and that two interactions with the same members in any order are a repeat ("term 4 repeats term 3"). Cite `model.c:1343-1349`. Or accept R's semantics and say so. Either is an **[author]** choice for the owner.

### SF2. The design check does not state what the fit words and `model.anova` rely on

§S2 checks a design on entry: "each key present with its type, the MATRIX n × k, every LIST of its length" (558-560). The words then read more than that:
- **The intercept.** `fit.lm`'s `r2`, `adj.r2`, `f`, `null.deviance` and constant-`y` refusal (572-578, 590-591, 618-621), `fit.glm`'s null deviance (733-738), and `model.anova`'s first row (984-988) all depend on "with an intercept". Nothing says whether that is read from `spec.intercept`, from `assign`'s 0, or from a column of 1.0. A design whose MATRIX had its first column removed by a `linalg.` word, which §S1 invites (411-412), makes these disagree.
- **`assign`.** `model.anova` adds terms "in order, by `assign`" (961-962), and names rows from the terms (991-993). A hand-made `assign` that is not non-decreasing, skips a term, or disagrees with `spec.terms` in count has no stated meaning. R's `stat.anova` sets a 0-df row's statistic to `NA` (`anova.R:33`, `:42`); here a 0-df row is not even refused.
- **Finite values.** The MATRIX, `y`, `weights` and `offset` of a hand-made design may hold NaN or ±∞, or a weight ≤ 0. Criterion 8's palette has NaN. Whether that is E101, E103 or E114 is unstated. B12 covers only finite operands.
- **n > k.** §S3 refuses n ≤ k for `fit.lm` (572). §S4 and §S7 do not say it for `fit.glm` and `model.anova`. With an estimated dispersion, n = k divides by 0.
- **`spec`'s content.** `model.predict` codes a new frame from `spec`'s `terms`, `intercept`, `levels`, `logical`, `weights` and `offset` (861-890). The fit check (952-959) asks only that `spec` be present.

**Fix:** list the design invariants the check enforces, each E101 naming the key:
- `assign` non-decreasing from 0 or 1, every term from 1 to |`spec.terms`| present;
- `spec.intercept` true exactly when `assign` starts with 0 and that column is all 1.0, or the intercept read from one stated source;
- every value finite, and every weight positive;
- n > k.

Say that `model.predict` checks `spec`'s shape the same way.

### SF3. Two defaults differ from R and are neither marked nor stated as differences

- **`model.predict`'s `type` defaults to `"response"`** (865-866). R's `predict.glm` defaults to `"link"` (`predict.glm.R:20`, `type = c("link", "response", "terms")`). A program porting `predict(fit, newdata)` gets μ̂ where R gives η̂, silently. K16 rules the intervals, not this default, and D262's and D263's lists do not include it.
- **An interaction's columns vary the first member slowest** (445-447). R varies the first variable fastest: `addfactor` and `addvar` write the new factor's column k after all existing columns j, at k·ncx + j (`model.c:263-278`, `:292-305`, called at `:740-748`). So R's `a:b` is a2:b2, a3:b2, a2:b3, a3:b3, and §S2's is a2:b2, a2:b3, a3:b2, a3:b3. Names make the coefficients findable, but a program that compares by position with R's output gets them in another order.

CLAUDE.md asks that a choice made on the owner's behalf be marked, and neither is.

**Fix:** mark both **[author]** with R's behaviour cited, and add them to the list after D263 for a ruling. Or adopt R's: `"link"`, and the first member varying fastest.

## Notes

- **N1. The Gaussian identity null deviance without an intercept is finite** (744-747). "At those μ the deviance of any y away from them is infinite or not a number" fails for `gaussian`/`identity`, where linkinv(0) = 0 and Σ w y² is finite (`family.R:277`). The paragraph should except it. §S3 already computes it (620).
- **N2. The inverse Gaussian's deviance at μ = 0 is infinite, not finite** (655). (y − μ)²/(yμ²) (`family.R:528`) is finite for μ < 0 only. Say "for μ < 0".
- **N3. Non-finite coefficients from a solve.** R stops the loop, with a warning, when a solve gives a non-finite coefficient (`glm.R:249-253`). §S4 has no such case. Halving towards the previous coefficients cannot make an infinite one finite: on a later step it reaches E110 `IRLS step halving` after 100 halvings, and on the first step E101's "first IRLS step leaves … valid range" text. Both texts mislead. State the case, for instance as E114 naming the coefficient.
- **N4. What "a deviance of 0" tests** (771-775). Under the Gamma, each term −2(ln(y/μ) − (y − μ)/μ) is ≥ 0 mathematically, but at y ≈ μ rounding can make a sum a few ulps below 0. Then "a deviance of 0" is false, disp = dev/Σw < 0, and the AIC's shape 1/disp is negative, so the fit ends in E114 rather than the perfect-fit text. State the test as deviance ≤ 0, or as φ = 0, and say which.
- **N5. `model.anova`'s perfect-fit refusal: which row's text** (971-976, 978-980). A constant `y` makes every Gaussian identity row's RSS 0, the intercept-only row first. Say whether the refusal is raised at the first such row, with `, fitting the null model`, or only for the full model, with `, fitting the model through term "<last>"` or with no suffix.
- **N6. `fit.lm`'s standard errors must come from `stat.lm`'s lines, not from the new solve function** (605-608, 690-694). `stat_lm` takes se from the row sums of R⁻¹ squared (`infer.rs:894`). `weighted_solve`'s covariance is R⁻¹(R⁻¹)ᵀ by a matrix product (`fit.rs:267`), whose diagonal need not round alike. "`fit.glm` and `fit.lm` solve through a crate-internal function" could be read as taking `fit.lm`'s `se` from that function's covariance, which would break criterion 2's bits. Say that the new function is for the solve and the rank test, and that `vcov` is computed separately.
- **N7. The 10⁻¹² stopping margin assumes f64 deviance errors near 10⁻¹⁵** (1138-1140). Off the `fit.logistic` path, the logit's deviance through μ is already 1.8·10⁻⁹ off relative at η = 20 (recomputed above). Its ratio can then move by more than the margin, so f64 can stop one iteration from the 50-digit run with the script reporting nothing. ## What the RFC assumes and does not state

- **U1. Row numbers under `"omit"`.** `model.matrix`'s texts name frame rows ("weights row 4 is 0", 1013). `fit.glm`'s name design rows ("y row 3 is -1", 1022). Under `"omit"` the two differ, and a program cannot map a design row back. Say which numbering each word uses.
- **U2. A spec column missing from `model.predict`'s frame.** §S6 applies §S2's steps 5 to 7 (883-887). The "no column" check is step 3's, so the text for an absent term column is not stated.
- **U3. `model.anova`'s first row and `fit.glm`'s `null.deviance`.** With an intercept and an offset, the first row is "fitted as any other row" (985), from `mustart`. `fit.glm`'s `null.deviance` is refitted from the full fit's μ̂ (735-738), as R's `anova.glm` takes `object$null.deviance` for its first row (`glm.R:516`). Under a non-canonical link the two stopping iterates differ, so the two numbers are not equal. Say so, or take the first row from the same refit.
- **U4. "An offset", for the null refit.** R refits whenever an offset is supplied, zeros included (`glm.R:108`, `length(offset)`). §S4's `fit.logistic` path reads the offset from its values (813-814). The null-deviance rule (733-738) says neither. An all-zero offset column with an intercept gives the closed form under one reading and an IRLS refit under the other.
- **U5. The fixture needs `mpmath`.** D260 records that `mpmath` is not installed for the system `python3` (`decisions.md`, D260's fourth finding). Criteria 2–6 rest on the new script, so M2 assumes that setup step. Say where it is recorded, as `fit.py`'s header does for RFC-0015.
