## Blocking

### B1 — Criterion 5's table is false, and now contradicts criterion 13 inside the document

R:521-546 lists `debug`, `debug.shell`, `debug.display_memstat` and
`debug.display_distributed_info` as **not implemented**, and R:540 says
"`debug`/`debug.shell` are goldenable by no capture". Criterion 13 was
corrected on 2026-10-08 (R:956-966) to say a golden holds both. Criterion 5
was not. Today:

- all four are registered (`bund2 words`; IMPLEMENTED 500/505);
- `debug` and `debug.shell` are **covered** by
  `tests/golden/probes/debug-repl-words.golden`;
- the two display words sit in `coverage`'s "no golden can hold" list, which
  is the state criterion 5's wording was built to express and does not.

The acceptance review's B1 named both halves; only criterion 13's was taken.
A reader of criterion 5 is told the opposite of what criterion 13 says, three
hundred lines apart.

### B2 — The preservation contract still omits the four flags, and `--debugger` is a deviation nobody has filed

The flag table at R:113-121 puts `--debugger`, `--debug-shell`, `-d`/`--debug`
and `--profile` "in the contract". The Preservation table (R:378-393) has no
row for any of them. What a row would have to say:

- **`--debugger`**: in the reference it is the `debug` word applied to the
  program's whole text — `bund_debugger(&mut bc.vm, snippet)` at
  `run_snippet.rs:25` and `:72` — a per-term table, a per-term prompt, every
  typed line Bund. Bund2's `--debugger` is a stepping console with its own
  short forms that stops before every value (`main.rs:238-243`). That is a
  **deviation in a reference flag's behaviour**, and neither this table nor
  `docs/registers/decisions.md` records it as one. D84 decided the mechanism;
  nothing decided that the flag's user-visible behaviour changes.
- **`--debug-shell`**: appears once (R:114) and nowhere after. `main.rs`
  parses `--debugger`, `--noio` and `--noeval` and no other debug flag. Not
  implemented, not named as unbuilt, no row, no criterion. The acceptance
  review's B3 said so.
- **`-d`/`--debug`**: D90 narrowed it to `BUND_LOG_LEVEL` only (R:460-464).
  A narrowing of a reference flag is a deviation and the row is missing.
- **`--profile`**: the open question at R:984 asks whether it is in scope;
  the reference prints its table to stdout (`cmd/mod.rs:109`). Unbuilt, no
  row.

### B3 — The status line's "That is the whole gap" is still not the whole gap

R:8-14 names §D7's trace and the `--debugger` console's history. Still
specified, unbuilt, and under no criterion, verified today against

the status line has not changed. R:1277-1281 ("What this does not do") names
the source-location half and `words`/`classes`, which is the right place for
all of it, and the status line should point there rather than say "whole".

### B5 — RFC-0003's accepted Preservation row says the debugger is "preserved via S6's observer"; RFC-0008 says the observer is not the debugger

`RFC-0003-syntax-ir-and-tier0.md:709`: "The debugger's third eval loop, which
prints and steps | preserved via S6's observer". `:659-669` says the single
evaluator "is used by all three of today's callers: the top-level script path,
`bund.eval`, and `--debugger`", with "print-and-step for the debugger".

RFC-0008 §D1 builds a safepoint instead, and Alternatives (R:402-404) reject
"Making `eval_observed` the debugger". The `--debugger` path does not go

reference to RFC-0008 at that row (`grep RFC-0008` finds `:518` and `:759`
only). The first review's Consistency section raised exactly this on
2026-09-30. RFC-0008's open question at R:980-984 speaks of a *different*
RFC-0003 row — the observer's reach, for the trace — and leaves `:709` alone.
One of the two documents has to say which is the statement of record.

## Significant

### S1 — "Vacuous" rests on an invariant the RFC calls a tag check; it is not one

Criterion 3 (R:485-488) and D94's table say each `DUMP: error CASTING to …`
arm "sits behind the tag check that guarantees its cast". `debug_dump.rs:18`
dispatches on `value_val.dt`; `cast_int` (`cast.rs:17-24`) matches on
`self.data`, which is `Val::I64` or not. `dt` and `data` are **separate
fields** of `Value` (`value.rs:19,21`). The arm is vacuous only if no
constructor in `rust_dynamic` or any word yields `dt == INTEGER` with a
payload that is not `I64`. That may well be so; it is a property of the whole
reference, not of the two lines, and the RFC presents it as structural. Bund2's
choice — `Error::internal` for the mismatch — is right either way; the
justification should say what it rests on.

### S2 — `debug.display_distributed_info` is implemented as a D90-shaped deviation no decision names

Bund2's word reports `Diagnostic::warning("BUND must be in distributed mode.
You shall pass --distributed to CLI")` through `Vm::report`
(`terminal.rs:797-800`). The reference emits the same words through
`log::error!` (`debug_display_distributed_info.rs:23`), which at the default
filter is a timestamped stderr line with a Rust module path — the exact
situation D90 had to decide for `log.error`, and decided as "route through
`Vm::report` at `Warning`". D90's title and scope are the five `log.*`
words; `grep display_distributed_info decisions.md` finds nothing. The
Preservation row (R:388) says "**Preserved**, including its absence
behaviour", which is wrong on two counts: the line's form differs, and
`coverage` lists the word among the eleven "no golden can hold" for that
reason. Either D90 is extended by name or a row says "Deviation, D90's shape,
by analogy", and criterion 5's table (B1) says "implemented".

### S3 — `debug.dump`'s refusal above `MAX_WIRE_DEPTH` is called deferred; D114 decided it, for `send` only

R:491-493: "`DUMP: error converting to binary`, which Bund2 can reach above
`MAX_WIRE_DEPTH` where the oracle's encoder has no bound at all — RFC-0007
criterion 4's **deferred** question, now with a second word behind it." D114
(2026-10-08) resolved that question: the refusal stays, **as an approved
deviation, for `send`**. `debug.dump` is the second word the RFC says is
behind it, and D114 does not name it. So either D114 covers it and the RFC
should say so and stop calling it deferred, or `debug.dump` refusing where
the oracle does not is a second deviation with no entry.

### S4 — Research §3.3(g) is departed from, and the header still says "It departs nowhere today"

R:46-49: the research "is **followed** … It departs nowhere today." §3.3(g)
says `"…" debug` "becomes a thin wrapper: eval the string with the debugger
attached and a breakpoint on entry … now it steps *into* things." The owner
ruled on 2026-10-07 (R:1357-1362, D113's third note) that `debug` stays the
reference's word and a **new** word, `debug.run`, is the wrapper. That is a
departure from (g) by decision, and ERRATA has no entry for it. The
"Four departures" paragraph at R:988-998 predates the ruling and says the
flag spelling is the only deliberate departure. Two smaller ones, for the
same list: §3.3(a)'s `Interp::step()` and (h)'s "IR-op granularity …
`(depth, symbol, op, stack effect)`" are rewritten as a safepoint and
`(depth, symbol, value, stack effect)` (R:9, R:323) without saying the
research's terms do not exist in Bund2.

### S5 — Criterion W3 was met over "four" sites and the document then added three more stops

W3 (R:1256-1259): a line may be typed "at each of the four safepoint sites —
top level, loop head, breakpoint, and both watch hooks". Met, per
"What building Part A changed" (R:1295-1300), with one guard. After that:

- the native stop (`lib.rs:597`), added 2026-10-07 (R:1426-1460) — a typed
  line runs with the native's operands on the stack and the native not yet
  called, which is the shape §W2's `[UNGROUNDED]` worried about (R:1141-1146)
  moved from mid-push to pre-call;
- the alias stop (`:800`), same day;
- `Term` (`:1939`), with `debug.run`.

R:1453-1456 says a typed line at a native's stop that exits "keeps the native
from running" and names one test that types the armed word at its own stop.
That is two cases, not W3's claim re-made over seven sites. Either W3 is
restated to the sites that exist and `a_line_may_be_typed_at_every_kind_of_stop`
(`debugger_words.rs:124`) is shown to enumerate all seven, or the three
later sites are named as outside it.

### S6 — Criterion 11's count reading is of the tree of 2026-10-08; the loop file has eight commits since

R:744-753 records D117's reading — "the commit before D113 against the tree
of 2026-10-08". `git log --since=2026-10-09 -- crates/bund2-interp/src/lib.rs`
shows eight commits (D139-D141, D145-D149, F195-F203, `cargo fmt`), all
RFC-0005's. Whether any touched the per-step path this criterion protects is
not established here and is not the point: the acceptance review's B2 was
that a cost criterion had been measured on code that then changed, and the
fix was a reading on a dated tree. The criterion should say which tree the
reading holds for, or D117's method should be re-run — it is five runs of
`/usr/bin/time`, not a quiet evening.

### S7 — `debug`, `debug.shell` "belong with `password` and `bund.prompt` in `ACTS_ON_HOST`" is false twice

R:156-158. `ACTS_ON_HOST` (`bund2-stdlib/src/lib.rs:578-592`) is the
promotion audit's skip list for natives with a fixed effect and contains
neither word; both are opaque and would be skipped before the list is
consulted. And `debug-repl-words.golden` shows both run hermetically under a
capture, so "no hermetic golden can run them" is now contradicted by the
document's own criterion 13. First review M6, still open, and now wrong for a
second reason.

### S8 — Two claims about `debug.dump`'s bytes are platform statements written as facts

R:141 and R:385: "eight **little-endian** bytes". `bytemuck::bytes_of` is a
view of memory, so the order is the host's. The oracle and Bund2 agree on
every host the goldens run on because both are little-endian there; the
golden `debug-dump.golden` is itself host-specific in that sense and is not
in `tests/golden/hosts/`. The first review's S1 said "native-endian"; the
text was not changed.

### S9 — `Interp::step()` is specified and does not exist (see Citations)

§D1 opens with a method the build never produced. The rest of §D1 describes
what was built. The first sentence should go, or say "a safepoint".

### S10 — F139 is quoted at "the default threshold" and it measured at 64

R:309 and R:1230-1231: "F139 measured that no corpus program compiles a body at
the default threshold". F139 (`defects.md:7376-7384`) ran at "the shipped
threshold of 64"; D74 made the default 1024. The claim holds more strongly
and the citation says something else. First review M8, still open.

## Preservation analysis — what the table does not enumerate

Beyond B2 and B4, behaviours the design changes or could change, with no row:

1. **`debug`'s own error texts and silent editor failure.** `Stack is too
   shallow for inline DEBUG` (`debug_debug.rs:125`), `Casting debug snippet
   returns:` (`:138`), the two `Detected an Error posted on the stack` texts
   (`:66,69`), `Attempt to evaluate value … returned error` (`:78`), and
   `DefaultEditor` failing — the snippet is consumed and the word returns `Ok`
   (`:40-46`). First review M9, still open.
2. **`debug.shell` evaluates every line, empty ones included**
   (`debug_shell.rs:27-29`); `debug` advances on an empty line (`:85-87`).
   Bund2's two words are in `terminal.rs:416-540`; whether they match is
   unstated.
3. **Ctrl-C at a word's prompt.** In the reference it `break`s the inner
   loop: for `debug` that means *advance to the next term*
   (`debug_debug.rs:96-99`), for `debug.shell` it ends the word
   (`debug_shell.rs:36-39`). R:1420-1422 says Ctrl-C at the **console**
   detaches, finally. Nothing says what Ctrl-C does inside `debug`'s own loop
   in Bund2, and D112's "both the same way" would make it end the word, which
   is not the reference's `debug`.
4. **History is saved unconditionally in the reference** (`debug_debug.rs:119`,
   `debug_shell.rs:51`, `rustyline::save_history` after every session);
   Bund2 writes "only if a line was entered" (R:364-366). Since the location
   is already an approved change, fold this into the same row; today it is a
   second, unrecorded difference.
5. **`debug.display_distributed_info`'s line** — S2.
6. **`display_memstat`'s fallback line** (`:28,47`) — reproduced
   (`terminal.rs:763`), unstated.
7. **The conditional breakpoint's effect on the debuggee** — criterion 8
   proves it is none; it is still the one place the design could have changed
   program meaning and the first review asked for a row.
8. **Step granularity inside a synchronous native.** §D1 says step-into
   works by stopping inside the nested loop. A row should say what a user
   sees at `map` with `s`: the body's values, and never `map`'s own Rust
   state.
9. **Who pays `Frame`'s eight bytes**: every run, not debug sessions. The
   row says "Changed" and not for whom.
10. **`--noeval` and the words.** D120 and D121 are noted at the words;
    **D122** — `debug` is *not* gated and is named — is a ruling about a word
    in this RFC's contract and is not mentioned. `grep D122` finds nothing.
11. **D113.6 mid-run**: "the body that was running compiled when the word was
    called finishes so" (R:1339-1340) is stated; F203 (a body queued beneath

12. **`stack` at the console is the Bund word** (D113.4): stated at

## What the RFC assumes but does not state

1. **`dt` implies the payload variant** in `rust_dynamic` — S1.
2. **Native-endian is little-endian** on every host the goldens run on — S8.
3. **No embedder installs a tier after `for_debugging`.** `install_tier` is
   public under `jit` (`bund2-runtime/src/lib.rs:384`); criterion 10's `None`
   is true of the CLI's construction, not of the type.
4. **A typed line is sound at the native, alias and `Term` stops** — S5.
5. **The reporter-line mapping for a reference word that logs at ERROR** is
   D90's by analogy — S2.
6. **The `Console` seam has one implementor shape per transport and none
   but stdin exists.** The `debug.rs` module doc says "a host thread over
   a channel pair" is a second, and R:59-61 rests the TUI claim on the seam; nothing implements it, and D101 records the
   same state for `Input`. The TUI claim in the Summary rests on it.
7. **The D117 count stands for later trees** — S6.
8. **`BUND_LOG_LEVEL`'s "last `=`" narrowing** matches the forms users set.
   D90 records the narrowing; the RFC does not say what `BUND_LOG_LEVEL=bund=info,other=trace`
   reads as.
9. **The stepping sweep's two exclusions are the only programs that read
   stdin.** Derived from source by the test, per R:567-569 — stated, and
   fine; recorded here because "derived" depends on the derivation's word
   list staying complete as words are added (`debug.feed` queues, `input`,
   `input*`, `password`, `debug`, `debug.shell`, `bund.prompt`).
10. **`debug.feed` under `--debugger` at a terminal** reads the fed line
    before the console's next command — R:1413-1416 measured it; the order
    between the queue and `Stdio` is not written down.
