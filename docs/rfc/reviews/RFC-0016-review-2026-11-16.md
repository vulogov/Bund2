## Blocking

### B1. S2 gives the plain test words new refusals and new costs, against "refusals preserved exactly" and D251's "additive"

**Location.** S2's table and "Work (D39)" (lines 148-156, 190-194); S1 "Additive" (lines 131-133); Preservation row 3 (line 309); Summary (lines 35-37).

**Evidence.**
- Today `mannwhitney` ranks the pooled sample and sums: O((n+m) log(n+m)) time, no allocation beyond n+m, and no cap (`infer.rs:434-470`).
- S2 gives the **plain** `stat.test.mannwhitney` a Hodges–Lehmann `estimate` and interval. "The Hodges–Lehmann estimate forms n·m differences; above 10⁸ it is E111 before allocating" (line 190-191).
- So `a b stat.test.mannwhitney` with n = m = 10 001 answers today and is E111 after M3. At the cap it allocates about 800 MB of f64 before the median is taken.
- Fisher's plain word gains a conditional MLE and its interval by bisection, "at most 200 steps, else E110" (lines 192-194). Today `stat_test_fisher` has only E101, E102 and E111 (`infer.rs:293-354`). Each bisection step sums the support, which may hold up to 10⁸ tables (`SUPPORT_CAP`, `infer.rs:47`), so the plain word's work can grow 200-fold.
- The t and binomial intervals need a quantile, and every continuous quantile is "polished by bracketed Newton … 100 steps at most, E110 beyond" (`dist.rs:27-28`, `POLISH_CAP` `:57-58`). That is a new E110 path on the plain t tests and `stat.test.binom`.

**Problem.** Preservation row 3 says the test words' "refusals … [are] Preserved exactly". D251 defines "additive" as "RFC-0015's test answers gain keys and lose none". A program that worked yesterday and is refused today has lost more than keys. The Preservation table lists none of these refusals or costs, so no criterion covers them.

**Suggested fix.** Pick one, and state it in S2 and in Preservation:
1. On the plain words, **leave the key out** rather than refuse, when the extra computation would hit a cap or fail to converge. This matches A3's rule for absent keys. Keep the refusal for the `.opts` word, which the caller asked for.
2. Compute the Hodges–Lehmann median without the n·m array, with an O((n+m) log(n+m)) selection over the implicit sorted matrix of differences (Johnson & Mizoguchi 1978; Monahan 1984). That removes the E111 and the 800 MB.

Then add a criterion: on RFC-0015's existing fixtures **and** on a case at n·m above 10⁸, the plain word answers what it answered before. If option 1 changes what D251 A2 was shown, the owner should see it as a note on D251.

### B2. The Mann–Whitney estimate and interval are not defined, so criterion 6 cannot be checked

**Location.** S2's table, the `stat.test.mannwhitney` row (line 156); criterion 6 (lines 364-367); Q62.

**Evidence.**
- The row says the estimate is "the median of the n·m differences a − b". It says the interval is "R's asymptotic interval, by inverting the normal approximation with its continuity correction".
- The statistic being inverted, W(d) for a shifted by d, is a step function of d (`mannwhitney`, `infer.rs:439-461`, ranks then a sum). "The root" of W(d)'s corrected z minus a quantile is an interval of d, or a jump, not a point.
- Nothing in the RFC says which point is taken.
- R is not in `reference/` and not installed, so how R chooses is **[UNGROUNDED]**. From the reviewer's knowledge of R's `wilcox.test.default`, not checkable here: its asymptotic branch finds both ends **and** the estimate with `uniroot` at `tol = 1e-4`, and the estimate is the zero of the corrected statistic, not `median(diffs)`. It takes `median(diffs)` only in the exact branch, which Bund2's test never takes (RFC-0015 §S6: "the normal approximation").
- If that recollection holds, the RFC mixes the exact branch's estimate with the asymptotic branch's interval. R's printed ends would then be good only to about 1e-4, against criterion 6's 1e-8.

**Problem.** Criterion 6 checks against "the interval of the fixture's inversion". The fixture has to implement a definition the RFC does not give, and the implementer and the fixture author may choose differently. Both choices would be defensible and would disagree past the fourth digit.

**Suggested fix.**
1. Define the interval as Bund2's own, exactly. For example: the ends are the infimum and supremum of {d : |z_c(d)| ≤ z₁₋α/₂}, with z_c(d) the tie- and continuity-corrected statistic for a − d against b. These are order statistics of the differences, so they are exact.
2. State the estimate as the median of the differences, as a choice, and say it differs from R's asymptotic-branch estimate.
3. Give criterion 6 a hand-worked case small enough to enumerate (say n = 4, m = 5, with a tie).
4. Narrow Q62's Hodges–Lehmann item to "does R's `uniroot` answer agree with this definition to R's printed digits".

### S2. "What is linked" says the distributions are statrs's; they are Bund2's own

**Location.** §Current behaviour, "What is linked" (lines 113-116).

**Evidence.** The RFC says: "Student's t, the normal, the χ², the beta and the hypergeometric are `statrs`'s, through `dist.rs`'s `Family`". But `dist.rs`'s module doc says:

> "`statrs` 0.18 checks every family's parameters … it draws for the continuous families … The densities, tails, quantiles and moments are Bund2's own, over §S4's functions, because `statrs`'s ride on the incomplete beta and gamma §S4's note of 2026-11-14 replaced" (`dist.rs:15-21`).

`Family::tails` calls Bund2's `beta_both` and `gamma_both` (`dist.rs:586-640`). `Family::quantile` is Bund2's own polished Newton (`dist.rs:758-798`), and it is a private `fn`, so S2 must widen it.

**Problem.** The conclusion, that no crate is added, holds. The reasoning would send an implementer to statrs's quantiles, which RFC-0015 replaced because of their defects.

**Suggested fix.** Rewrite as "Bund2's own, in `dist.rs`'s `Family` (`tails`, `quantile`); statrs checks parameters only". Note that `quantile` becomes `pub(crate)`.

### S3. "less" and "greater" are defined for two-sample location only; four tests are left undefined and KS is reversed from R

**Location.** S2 (lines 175, 181-182, 184-188).

**Problem.** "'less' means the first parameter is less: … mean(a) − mean(b) < μ." That does not say what "less" means for:
- `t1`: presumably mean < μ₀;
- `binom`: the true p is below the operand p;
- `fisher`: the odds ratio is below 1, a single MATRIX with no "first parameter";
- `ks2`: which of D⁺ and D⁻ is "less".

For KS, R's convention, by the reviewer's recollection and **[UNGROUNDED]**, is that `alternative = "greater"` means F_a lies above F_b, so a is stochastically *smaller*. Read literally, the RFC's generic rule gives the opposite.

**Suggested fix.** Add a table with one row per test, giving H₁ for "less" and for "greater", and the statistic (D⁺ = max(F_a − F_b), D⁻ = max(F_b − F_a)) for KS. Say whether Bund2 follows R's KS sense or the "first parameter is less" sense. Add it to Q62.

### S5. `stat.test.chisq.opts` and `stat.test.chisq.table.opts` accept no key

**Location.** S2 "`.opts` variants" (lines 170-178).

- `conf` applies only to tests with an interval, which those two lack;

### S6. The effect sizes' formulas and domains break B12 at ordinary sizes, and their edge cases are unstated

**Location.** S5 (lines 231-242); criterion 9.

**Evidence and problem.**
- **Hedges' J** is given as Γ(df/2)/(√(df/2)·Γ((df − 1)/2)). Γ overflows f64 past about 171.6, so for n_a + n_b > 345 both Γs are +∞ and J is NaN. Under the house rule (`finite`, `operand.rs:64-78`) the word is E114, though the answer is near 1 and finite. At df = 1 (n_a + n_b = 3), Γ(0) is a pole and J = 0.
- **Cohen's d** has no refusal for a pooled SD of 0, though RFC-0015's tests refuse a constant series (`infer.rs:62-64,114-116`). Its minimum sample sizes are unstated.
- **Cramér's V** with min(r, c) = 1 divides by zero. A zero row or column total makes χ²'s expected count 0. `stat.test.chisq.table` refuses both (RFC-0015 §S6's M5 note), and S5 does not say V does.
- **Scope of the zero-cell rule.** Criterion 9's "a zero cell is E101" follows Cramér's V in the sentence. S5 applies it only to the odds and risk ratios, where a zero cell in an r×c table is legitimate.

**Suggested fix.**
1. Write J as exp(lgamma(df/2) − lgamma((df − 1)/2))/√(df/2), using Bund2's `lgamma` (`dist.rs:52`, imported from `special`).
2. Require n_a, n_b ≥ 2 (E101), and refuse a constant pair as the t tests do.
3. Give V the χ²-table's refusals and min(r, c) ≥ 2.
4. Scope criterion 9's zero-cell clause to the two ratios.

### S11. Criterion 3 freezes an existing Mann–Whitney defect, and S2's one-sided correction inherits it

**Location.** S2 "One-sided p" (lines 186-187); criterion 3.

**Evidence.**
- `mannwhitney` applies the correction as `z - 0.5 * z.signum()` (`infer.rs:461`). `f64::signum(0.0)` is 1.0, not 0.
- Probed: `[ 1.0 4.0 ] [ 2.0 3.0 ] stat.test.mannwhitney` (W = 2 = n·m/2) answers `p=0.6985353583033387`. The fixture's own formula uses `mpmath.sign`, which is 0 at 0 (`fixtures/infer.py:177-178`), and gives p = 1 there.
- `sleep` does not hit z = 0, so M5's test passes.

**Problem.** This is a Bund2 bug, not a reference one. Criterion 3 requires every existing key "unchanged in value on RFC-0015's fixtures", which happens to allow the fix, but nothing tests the fix. S2's "continuity correction toward the null" for one-sided p needs the same care. It is ±0.5 by direction, not by the sign of z.

**Suggested fix.**
1. Fix the bug in M3 with a test at W = n·m/2, and record it as a Bund2 bug in the milestone's notes.
2. State the one-sided correction as +0.5 for "greater" and −0.5 for "less", subtracted from W − nm/2, if that is the intended rule. **[UNGROUNDED]** as R's: add it to Q62.

## Notes

**N1. `t_p` is misdescribed.** "Twice the lower tail of |t|" (line 95) would be at least 1. The code takes twice the lower tail at −|t| (`infer.rs:80-82`), which is twice the upper tail of |t|.

**N2. The probe proves less than it says.** `1.23456 3 math.round → FLOAT_OP returns error: This Dynamic type is not float: 2` (line 85) is `math.round` refusing the INTEGER 3. The same error appears for `5 math.round` (probed). The reference's `cast_float` does not convert an INTEGER (`float_math.rs:99,155-156`). The arity conclusion stands, but the evidence for it is the citation `:93-100`, not the probe. B15 is the reason the new words differ here, and S6 could say so.

**N3. "The only seeded draws are `rng.uniform` and `dist.sample`"** (lines 65-67). RFC-0015 §S8 also lists `dist.sample*`, `popgen.wf*` and `sim.gillespie`.

**N4. One claim about `format` is uncited.** "A FLOAT renders as its STRING conversion does" (lines 80-81): the conversion is `dtoa`'s shortest form (`reference/rust_dynamic/src/conv.rs:127-130`, reached from `conv` at `:694-696`). The header pins `reference/rust_dynamic` and the body never cites it, so cite this line there.

**N5. Preservation's `format` and `math.round` rows omit their forms.** `format.` (`format.rs:72-132`, registered `:136`) is also preserved. Bund2 registers `math.round.` (D111's list; `bund2 words`), and `math.round.places` sits beside it.

**N6. Negative zero.** `{:.0}` of −0.4 is `-0`, and `{:.2}` of −0.001 is `-0.00` (run). So `-0.4 0 math.round.places` answers −0.0, and `string.fixed` writes `-0`. Say whether that is intended or normalised.

**N7. `string.sci`'s form is not R's or C's.** `sprintf("%.2e")` writes `1.23e+04`, and Rust writes `1.23e4` (run). A6's rationale is "what a statistician expects from R". Say that the exponent's form is a deliberate departure.

**N8. Places' ranges and types.** n is [0, 15] for `math.round.places` and [0, 17] for `string.fixed`, with no reason given. Only the first says INTEGER. Say whether a FLOAT `2.0` is accepted, and that an INTEGER x answers a FLOAT, as criterion 10's `120000.0` implies.

**N9. The two ratios are underspecified.**
- `stat.effect.oddsratio` does not give the table's orientation.
- `riskratio`'s `se` names no method; Woolf's is for the odds ratio, and the risk ratio's usual one is Katz's.
- The sample odds ratio differs from `stat.test.fisher`'s conditional-MLE `estimate`, two odds ratios for one table. Say so.

**N10. Not every one-sided far end is infinite.** S2's "as a one-sided interval's far end is" (line 164) holds for the t and Hodges–Lehmann intervals. For Clopper–Pearson the far end is 0 or 1, for Fisher's "less" the lower end is 0, and for Pearson it is ±1. All are finite, so they are answered. Say so, so that criterion 4's "one finite end" is not generalised.

**N11. R's estimates differ in shape** (reviewer's recollection, **[UNGROUNDED]**). For two samples, `t.test` reports both means, not their difference. `wilcox.test` computes an interval only with `conf.int = TRUE`. Neither affects the design, but Motivation's "print one" and S2's "that R's `htest` gives" overstate R's parity.

**N12. Research 07's effect sizes η² and ω²** (§3.4) are dropped from Phase A without a word. Say they wait for ANOVA.

**N13. §Open questions under-describes Q62** (see S1). The register's entry also covers the Spearman approximation and the three corrections.

**N14. The polish tolerance against criterion 4's 1e-10.** A quantile is polished to |cdf(x) − p| ≤ 10⁻¹², absolute in p (`dist.rs:60-61`). The error in x is about 10⁻¹²/pdf(x). That is fine for `sleep` at 95%. At df = 1 and conf = 0.999 it is about 10⁻⁶ in x, 2×10⁻⁹ relative, which fails 1e-10. Bound criterion 4 to the cases it names, or polish to a tolerance in x.

**N15. Clopper–Pearson at k = 0 and k = n.** Beta(k, n − k + 1) has a zero shape at k = 0, and Beta(k + 1, n − k) at k = n. The ends are 0 and 1 by definition. Say so before an implementer calls the beta quantile with a = 0.

**N16. Hommel's work is unbounded in the RFC.** R's algorithm, as Q62 describes it, is O(n²) in the number of p-values. D39 bounds internal loops on the data taken, and the RFC states no cap or complexity. Give one.

**N17. Tolerances depart from B11 without a reason.** RFC-0015's criteria use relative 1e-12 for closed forms (RFC-0015 §Acceptance criteria; `infer/tests.rs:86-89`). This RFC uses 1e-10 (line 345). B11's "at the RFC's tolerances" permits it, but say why.

**N18. Fisher's bisection has no stated bracket.** Bisection "on its logarithm" (line 193) needs a finite bracket on an unbounded log-odds axis, and a rule for the support ends. S2 already says the estimate is infinite at an end. State the bracket, or the expansion with its own cap.

## What the RFC assumes and does not state

- That an options MAP's keys are STRINGs, and that `alternative`'s value is a STRING. A wrong type is presumably E101.
- That the `.` forms answer on the workbench, as `bench!` makes every form do (`wb.rs:181-189`). So `math.round.places.` consumes n from the workbench on each call and cannot be reused over a loop. A reader may expect otherwise.
- That S6's LIST is not read by `get_data`, so a METRICS and a drain are refused, unlike `stat.quantile`, whose shape it shares.
- Which algorithm `rng.choice.weighted` uses: an alias table or a cumulative search. Only "within one version" reproducibility is promised, but the choice decides the stream.
- That Kendall's test inherits `stat.cor.kendall`'s O(n²) pair loop (`describe.rs:188-219`) with no cap.
- That a program will cope with an answer whose interval keys are sometimes absent. A3 rules it, but for Pearson at n = 3 and for B1's option 1 the absence depends on data, not only on `alternative`.
- That D252's future `na` option can be added to these `.opts` MAPs later as an addition. It can, since unknown keys are refused today, but it is worth a line.

## Verdict

**Not ready.** Two things block:
- **B1.** S2 gives the plain RFC-0015 test words new refusals (E111 on Mann–Whitney at n·m > 10⁸, E110 on Fisher's bisection and on the quantile polish) and new costs, while Preservation calls their refusals "Preserved exactly". Leave keys out instead of refusing on the plain words, or compute without the cap, and add the rows.
- **B2.** The Hodges–Lehmann estimate and interval have no exact definition, so criterion 6 cannot be checked. Define both as Bund2's own.

After those, the largest should-fix items are:
- S1: R claims unmarked, and missing from Q62;
- S3: what "less" means for t1, binom, Fisher and KS;
- S4: the key table behind criterion 3;
- S7: the prefix-count tests, the 22 UNVARIED entries, `docs/arity.md`;
- S10: R's printed values unstated and unsourced.

None of these needs a new owner ruling except possibly B1's option 1, which narrows A2 for capped cases. With them fixed, the design is sound and the rest is specification.
