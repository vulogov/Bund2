# Review: RFC-0000 — Architecture, crate boundaries, and the tier model

- Reviewed: 2026-08-25
- Document: `docs/rfc/RFC-0000-architecture.md` at `4aadaad`, Status: Draft
- Reference SHAs: verified against `git submodule status`. All six match the
  RFC header, and `git status --porcelain` is empty in every submodule, so
  every citation below resolves at the recorded SHAs.
- Method: every `path:line` in the RFC was opened and re-read in this session.
  Every acceptance criterion was executed.

Verdict: the RFC is well grounded. 38 of 43 citations say exactly what the RFC
claims. The defects are five citation errors (§1), a wrong-direction
acceptance check (§3.4), an acceptance criterion that cannot fail (§3.3), and
a Preservation section that omits nine behaviours the design could change
(§4) — of which `autoadd`, double alias resolution, and the fourth
registration table are the load-bearing ones.

---

## 1. Citation defects

### 1.1 "Every internal dependency in `reference/Bund/Cargo.toml:13,23,24,43`"

There are **five** internal dependencies, not four. Line 14 —
`bundcore = ">=0.*.*"` — is missing from the citation. Lines 13, 23, 24 and 43
say what the RFC claims (`bund_language_parser`, `rust_dynamic`,
`rust_multistackvm`, `rust_multistack`, all `">=0.*.*"`).

The claim survives the correction — line 14 is unbounded too — but "every"
with an enumeration that is not every is exactly the kind of thing F8 is about.
Read `:13,14,23,24,43`.

### 1.2 "The crates are pinned `">=0.*.*"` against each other" — false as stated

The bolded generalisation is broader than its citation and is contradicted by
`reference/rust_multistackvm/Cargo.toml:21`:

    rust_dynamic = ">=0.33.*"

That one is bounded below at a minor version. So it is not true that the
crates are pinned `">=0.*.*"` *against each other*; it is true that `Bund`'s
five internal dependencies are, which is what the citation supports. The
sharper version of the finding is also the more damning one: the pins are
*inconsistent* — four crates use `">=0.*.*"`, `rust_multistack/Cargo.toml:14`
uses `">= 0.*.*"`, and `rust_multistackvm` alone remembers to floor a version.
Restate the claim to what `Bund/Cargo.toml` shows, or widen the citation set.

### 1.3 `reference/rust_multistackvm/src/stdlib/execute.rs:28` — off by one

The RFC: "`execute` also accepts a bare STRING (`execute.rs:28`)".

Line 28 is `match &mut ptr_value.data {`. The bare-STRING acceptance is the
match arm at **line 27**: `PTR | STRING | CALL => {`. Line 28 is the payload
destructure that follows it and says nothing about accepted types.

This error is inherited from `docs/registers/decisions.md` D16 §4, which cites
the same wrong line. The register is append-only, so the RFC cannot fix it in
place — but the RFC should cite `:27` and not propagate the register's slip.

### 1.4 "`reference/bund_language_parser/bund.pest` is 55 lines"

It is **54** (1636 bytes, 54 newlines, file ends with a newline). The
substantive half of the sentence is right: `value` at `:7-20` has exactly
twelve alternatives.

### 1.5 "See `docs/research/ERRATA.md` for three corrections that predate this RFC"

`ERRATA.md` holds **five** entries, and has since `94c13e2`, which precedes
the RFC commit. The number three appears to come from that commit's subject
line ("three errata owed from the corpus session"), not from the file. Two of
the five — the `execute`-is-spelled-`!` correction and the three-tiers
correction — are the two RFC-0000 leans on hardest, so the undercount points
the reader away from its own evidence.

### 1.6 F17 is not a cause of the capture failures

The RFC: "F14, F15 and F17 are the reason 18 of 77 hermetic programs could not
be captured."

`tests/golden/UNSTABLE.txt` says "Root causes, **both** recorded as defects:
F14 … F15 …", and every one of its 18 lines is tagged `F14` or `F15`. Nothing
in the file attributes a capture failure to F17. The "18 of 77" figure itself
is correct and the file lists exactly 18 programs.

### 1.7 Minor: `stacks.rs:129,187` is in the opposite order to the prose

The sentence names the tables `LAMBDAS, ALIASES, STACKS, STACK_DATA, MODELS,
BOOTSTRAP`; the citation gives `stacks.rs:129,187` where `:129` is
`CREATE TABLE ... STACK_DATA` and `:187` is `CREATE TABLE ... STACKS`. A
reader pairing them positionally gets both wrong. Cosmetic, but this is a
citation-ordering convention worth fixing once.

### 1.8 Minor: `ptr` does not push, it applies

`artefacts.rs:80-93` reads correctly, but the function ends at `:88` with
`vm.apply(Value::ptr(name, Vec::new()))`, not a push. The push is the PTR arm
of `apply`, at `multistackvm_apply.rs:88-99` — which D16 cites and RFC-0000
drops. The omitted hop matters because `apply`'s PTR arm is also where
`autoadd` would intercept (see §4.1).

## 2. Claims that need a citation and lack one

1. **The whole startup decomposition.** "a bare process spawn is 1.4 ms,
   `bund --version` … is 11.0 ms, an empty program is 15.8 ms" carries no
   citation at all. It is grounded — `docs/registers/decisions.md:872-877`
   under D28 — and I confirmed the 381 MB independently, but the RFC's own
   rule is that a factual claim about existing behaviour points somewhere.
   Cite D28's table.

2. **"63 goldens cover 121 of 497 in-scope words."** No citation. I verified it
   by running `cargo xtask coverage`. Say that is where it comes from; the
   figure supersedes the 140/586 still recorded in `open-questions.md` §Q5,
   and a reader who checks the register instead of the tool gets the old one.

3. **"each handler in `src/vm/` converts one token to a `Value`."** No
   citation, and not quite true: `src/vm/` holds fifteen files, of which
   twelve are token handlers matching the twelve `value` alternatives, and
   `mod.rs`, `eoi.rs` and `unknown.rs` are not.

4. **"that is what the scope filter in `cargo xtask corpus` enforces."** No
   `path:line` into `xtask/src/corpus/classify.rs`. This is the load-bearing
   claim under "no golden changes" and deserves one.

5. **The LOC/file table.** "Measured over `src/` at the pinned SHAs" names no
   command and no file-set rule. It reproduces exactly under `*.rs` beneath
   `src/`, but only because I guessed the same rule.

6. **`take` 29 / `drop` 16.** The counts are right, but the RFC gives no
   source. `ERRATA.md` attributes them to `cargo xtask corpus`.

## 3. Acceptance criteria

Each was executed.

### 3.1 Criterion 1 — checkable, passes

`cargo xtask conform` prints `CONFORMANCE  0/63`. `tests/golden/CONFORMANCE.txt`
records `0/63`. Checked by: the command itself, which fails below the mark.

One defect in the artifact the criterion designates as the record:
`CONFORMANCE.txt`'s own prose says "**59 goldens** reach a fraction of the word
table" while its value is `0/63`. The file contradicts itself, and the RFC
points at it as the authority. `cargo xtask --help` is stale the same way
("77 goldens cover 140 of the 586 in-scope names", "446 words unimplemented").

### 3.2 Criterion 2 — checkable, passes

`cargo xtask coverage` prints `COVERAGE 121/497 (24.3%)`, with
`registered names 617`, `out of scope by decision 120`. Checked by: the
command.

The criterion says the denominator "moves again as D14 resolves". Understated:
`decisions.md:327` records D14 as "OPEN — being resolved **per word**, not per
subsystem", so the denominator moves word by word rather than in subsystem
steps. That is a different shape of instability than the sentence implies.

### 3.3 Criterion 3 — not checkable as written; cannot fail

`cargo build --workspace --no-default-features` succeeds (exit 0), so the first
half holds. The second half — "every gated subsystem absent from the
dependency graph, verifiable with `cargo tree --no-default-features`" — is
**vacuous**. No bund2 crate declares a feature for any of the seven gated
subsystems. The only features in the workspace are:

    crates/bund2-cli/Cargo.toml:18-21      default, aot, jit, async
    crates/bund2-jit/Cargo.toml:20-24      default, aot, jit
    crates/bund2-runtime/Cargo.toml:19-22  default, aot, jit
    crates/bund2/Cargo.toml:18-21          default, aot, jit, async

There is no `ai`, `image`, `bus`, `forecast`, `statistics`, `internaldb` or
`console` feature anywhere. `cargo tree` therefore cannot produce evidence for
or against the criterion, and no future state of the workspace can make it
fail. To be checkable it must name the seven features and the crate that
declares each, and name the third-party crates whose absence is the actual
observable (`lingua`, `duckdb`, `polars`, `arrow`, `viuer`, `zenoh`, …).

### 3.4 Criterion 4 — second clause checks the wrong direction; both pass vacuously

The design rule is:

> `bund2-value` must not depend on `bund2-interp`, and `bund2-stdlib` must not
> depend on `bund2-jit`.

The criterion is:

> `bund2-value` does not appear in `bund2-interp`'s reverse dependencies, and
> `bund2-jit` does not appear in `bund2-stdlib`'s.

The first clause is right. `cargo tree --invert -p bund2-interp` enumerates
crates that depend on `bund2-interp`; `bund2-value` appearing there would mean
value → interp, which is the forbidden edge.

The second clause is **backwards**. `cargo tree --invert -p bund2-stdlib`
enumerates crates that depend on `bund2-stdlib`; `bund2-jit` appearing there
would mean jit → stdlib, which is not the forbidden edge and is arguably
fine. The check that matches the rule is: `bund2-stdlib` must not appear in
`cargo tree --invert -p bund2-jit`.

Separately, both halves currently return only the root:

    $ cargo tree --invert -p bund2-interp
    bund2-interp v0.0.0 (…)
    $ cargo tree --invert -p bund2-stdlib
    bund2-stdlib v0.0.0 (…)

Nothing depends on either crate yet, so the criterion passes and will keep
passing whether or not the rule is respected, until the scaffold is wired.
That is not a reason to drop it, but the RFC should say the criterion is
latent — as written it reads as evidence the boundary holds, and at this
commit it is evidence of nothing.

### 3.5 Criterion 5 — checkable

`cargo xtask golden` exists and is documented. The criterion is well posed and
is the one worth keeping most. Note that `golden` reads `HERMETIC.txt` plus
the six probes and nothing else, so "a full run" is bounded — worth saying,
since the criterion's force depends on which paths it touches.

### 3.6 Criterion 6 — not mechanically checkable

"Every `path:line` citation in this document resolves at the recorded SHAs"
names no checker, and none exists. It was checked by hand for this review and
five citations did not hold (§1.1-§1.6). A criterion whose only implementation
is a careful reader is not an acceptance criterion; it is a hope. Either
specify `cargo xtask cite` (extract `reference/…:N` spans from `docs/`, assert
the file exists at the pinned SHA and has ≥ N lines) or demote it to process
text.

### 3.7 The criteria cover 2 of 12 boundary rules, and 0 AOT claims

The layout table states a boundary rule per crate — twelve of them. Criterion
4 checks two. The other ten are unchecked, and most are unfalsifiable as
phrased: "no VM internals", "source text to AST; no evaluation", "argument
parsing and the REPL; no language logic", "words only". These are the rules
that make the layout a design rather than a directory listing, and nothing
tests them.

Nothing in the criteria touches AOT, though the Design section makes three
AOT claims (retains the word table, retains the name resolver, moves
conformance by zero).

## 4. Preservation analysis — what it does not enumerate

The section names five items. Nine more behaviours are inside the blast radius
of what RFC-0000 fixes. The first three are the ones that would cost real
debugging.

### 4.1 `autoadd` suppresses resolution entirely — the "full resolution order" is not full

`multistackvm_apply.rs:19-27`. Between the command check at `:16` and the
`$`-forcing at `:33` there is a branch the RFC does not mention:

    if self.autoadd {
        match self.stack.pull() {
            Some(mut val) => { self.stack.push(val.push(value)); }
            None => { bail!("Autoadd found no working data on stack"); }
        }
    } else { … the resolution chain the RFC describes … }

When `autoadd` is set, a CALL is **appended to a value on the stack** and never
resolved. The CONTEXT arm does the same thing at `:72-73` — the context value
is pushed rather than switched to. So the chain the RFC declares a
preservation contract ("Full resolution order, from `apply.rs:16-60`") is one
arm of a two-arm branch, and the RFC's rendering omits the other arm and the
flag that selects it.

This matters concretely for RFC-0003: an IR that lowers a CALL node to a
resolve-and-invoke sequence has silently decided `autoadd` is always false.
D16 §3 calls the resolution chain "observable in full and its order is
contract" — that contract needs the mode flag in it.

### 4.2 Alias resolution happens twice, and exactly twice

`apply.rs:39-42` resolves an alias to `real_name`. It then calls
`self.i(real_name)` at `:59`, and `i` (`multistackvm_inline.rs:70-82`)
resolves an alias **again** before delegating to `i_direct`.

So an alias whose target is itself an alias resolves two levels deep, and a
three-level chain does not resolve. Both the two-level success and the
three-level failure are observable behaviour. "Three registration tiers:
preserved exactly" is about the *inline* tables and does not cover this; a
`bund2-interp` that resolves aliases once, or transitively to a fixpoint,
is correct-looking and wrong in opposite directions.

### 4.3 `$`-forcing does not skip alias resolution, whatever the comment says

`apply.rs:30-31` comments the `$` branch as "forcing to call internal function
**without lambda check or alias resolution**". Only the first half is true.
`call_internal_word` (`multistackvm_call_internal_word.rs:6-9`) strips the `$`
and calls `self.i(...)` — which is the alias-resolving entry point at
`multistackvm_inline.rs:71`. `$name` therefore skips the lambda check and gets
alias resolution anyway.

RFC-0000 reproduces the reference's framing ("a `$`-prefixed name forcing an
internal word") without flagging that the source comment is wrong about its own
code. Either the code or the comment is the bug; it belongs in
`docs/registers/defects.md` before RFC-0002 specifies slot lookup, because a
reimplementer reading the comment will build the documented behaviour.

### 4.4 There is a fourth registration table, and it is dead

The RFC's whole "three tiers, not two" finding — its best one — stops one table
short. `rust_multistack` registers into **two** independent tables:

    ts_functions.rs:6    register_function(name, AppFn)     — 12 call sites
    ts_inline.rs:5       register_inline(name, InlineFn)

`i_direct` consults only `stack.get_inline` (`multistackvm_inline.rs:52`).
`get_function` (`ts_functions.rs:25`) has exactly one caller in the entire
reference — `ts_functions.rs:36`, inside the same file — and none in
`rust_multistackvm`, `bundcore` or `Bund`. The function table is populated on
every boot and unreachable from word dispatch.

`workbench.rs:74-81` and `drop.rs:70-75` show the pattern: `drop` is registered
into both tables, `take` into only the inline one. So the tables are not even
mirrors.

"Bund2 keeps the three tiers as *tables* inside `bund2-interp`" is right about
dispatch and silent about this. An implementer working from the RFC alone has
two ways to go wrong: resurrect the dead table as a fourth dispatch tier, or
merge the two `rust_multistack` tables and change which function `drop`
resolves to. Say the function table exists, that it is unreachable, and which
one Bund2 keeps.

### 4.5 `execute` dispatches on eight arms, not two

"The world cannot be closed" describes `!` as name → word. `execute.rs:26-99`
handles: `PTR | STRING | CALL` (`:27`), `LIST` — recurses over elements
(`:37-54`), `MAP | INFO | CONFIG | ASSOCIATION` — **pulls a second value off
the stack as a key** and dispatches on it (`:55-83`), `CONDITIONAL` (`:84`),
`CLASS` (`:87`), `OBJECT` (`:90`), `LAMBDA` (`:93`), and an error arm (`:96`).

D16 §4 states this; RFC-0000's Design writes the Tier 1 constraint
("Tier 1 cannot statically devirtualise `!`") as if `!` were a call
indirection. The dict arm consumes an extra operand, so `!`'s *stack effect*
is input-type-dependent — a speculation guard has to model arity, not just a
target. Q5 records that the corpus reaches four of the eight arms, and D21's
six probes were authored precisely to cover the rest. Since RFC-0000 fixes the
tier model, the eight-arm shape belongs in its Preservation section rather
than by reference.

### 4.6 `execute.`'s depth guard reads the wrong stack

`execute.rs:116-120`: `stdlib_execute_from_workbench_inline` guards on
`vm.stack.current_stack_len() < 1` and then pulls from the **workbench**. The
inner `stdlib_execute_base_inline` re-checks correctly at `:14-18`, so the
outer guard is dead rather than harmful — but the error text differs by path,
and D18 preserves `.` forms as pairs with their base. Not in
`defects.md`. Worth one line there before RFC-0004 fixes arity.

### 4.7 `to_binary` special-cases JSON, so not every value round-trips as itself

Preservation says the world file's contained values "remain bincode-serialised
`Value`s". `rust_dynamic/src/bincode.rs:8-20` converts a `JSON` value to a
`STRING` before encoding. A JSON value therefore does not come back as a JSON
value. The redb migration inherits this whether or not it means to, and D20's
lazy-identity rule is stated for the general case only.

### 4.8 The conformance denominator already moved under D28

Preservation says "no golden changes" and "The M6 denominator narrows, which
D14 and D28 both record" — M6 being coverage. The conformance denominator
narrowed too. Commit `fe25906`: "That narrows the suite from 59 to 57 and
orphans two goldens, now removed." I confirmed it: `docs/bench-baseline.md`
lists 59 programs, `HERMETIC.txt` lists 57, and the difference is exactly
`examples/estimator_demo.bund` (statistics) and
`examples/forecast_mstl_demo.bund` (forecast).

Since criterion 1 makes conformance *the* regression gate and the RFC insists
the number must move by exactly zero, a decision consumed by this RFC having
already moved its denominator is the single most important thing the
Preservation section could say, and it says the other denominator instead.

### 4.9 Three OPEN decisions bear on the tier model and are not named

CLAUDE.md forbids adopting an OPEN decision's default silently. RFC-0000 is
scrupulous about D11 and D14. It does not mention:

- **D3 — tier policy for `bund.eval` output** (OPEN, default "permanently
  Tier 0"). This is a *tier* decision, and RFC-0000 is the tier-model RFC.
  D16 flags the interaction explicitly: "D3 may still put `bund.eval` output
  permanently at Tier 0". The Tier model section defines Tier 1 as "optional,
  feature-gated, never required for correctness" without saying that whether
  a whole class of code is *eligible* for it is unresolved.
- **D9 — third-party CLIF lowerings through `bund2-api`** (OPEN). The layout
  table gives `bund2-api` as "the stable surface a word package compiles
  against". Whether that surface includes `Intrinsic` lowerings is exactly D9,
  and the answer changes what the boundary means.
- **D10 — may `bund2 build` require `cc`** (OPEN). "AOT is the
  `cranelift-object` build" is a claim about the toolchain, and D10 is the
  decision about whether that toolchain may include a C compiler.

None is adopted silently — they are not mentioned at all, which is the same
outcome for a reader.

## 5. Consistency with accepted RFCs and ERRATA

There are no accepted RFCs; RFC-0000 is the first and is Draft. Against the
research corpus and ERRATA:

### 5.1 The supersession is declared but not recorded

The header says RFC-0000 supersedes `04-consolidated-architecture.md` §1-§3.
CLAUDE.md: "When an RFC contradicts one, record it in
`docs/research/ERRATA.md` — do not edit the original." **There is no ERRATA
entry for this supersession.** The five that exist are all pre-RFC corpus
corrections.

### 5.2 The supersession is scoped wrong

RFC-0000 claims §1-§3 "as the statement of record for layout and tiering",
then disclaims: "It makes no claim about value representation (RFC-0001),
symbols or the word table (RFC-0002), or the IR (RFC-0003)."

But §1.1 is a single section that mixes all of them. It states the crate
diagram *and*:

- "**Values.** 24 bytes, `Copy`, `{ tag: u64, payload: u64, birth: u64 }`"
- "**Symbols.** Every word name interned to `SymbolId(u32)` at parse time"
- "**Lambdas.** Canonical body stays `Vec<BundValue>` …"

Superseding §1-§3 wholesale while claiming nothing about those topics leaves
them superseded by a document with nothing to say about them — the worst of
both, since a reader can no longer rely on §1.1 and has nothing to rely on
instead. §1.1's 24-byte value is separately contradicted by D4 ("the candidate
value is **already 16 bytes** at full `i64`") with no errata recording it, and
§2.2's cost table repeats it ("24-byte value instead of 16").

Narrow the supersession to the layout and tier paragraphs by name, and leave
§1.1's value/symbol/lambda claims to be superseded by RFC-0001 and RFC-0002 —
or state that RFC-0000 supersedes the section's *structure* only.

### 5.3 "Suite" as defined is not the conformance denominator

Terminology: "**Suite** — the programs in `tests/golden/HERMETIC.txt`" (57).
"**Conformance** — goldens passed over goldens captured." Criterion 1: 0/63.

`cargo xtask conform` states the reconciliation the RFC does not: "Denominator
is every captured golden: 57 suite programs plus 6 authored probes (D21)."
On the RFC's own definitions, Suite ≠ conformance denominator, and the RFC
never says probes are in it. Since RFC-0000 exists to fix vocabulary, this is
the one place the vocabulary should be airtight.

### 5.4 ERRATA agreement, where it exists

Where RFC-0000 restates ERRATA it restates it accurately: the three-tier
finding, `take` at 29 and `drop` at 16, `!` as the spelling of `execute`, and
the four-of-eight coverage of `execute`'s arms all match. The RFC did not
inherit an error from ERRATA; it inherited one from D16 (§1.3).

### 5.5 A register row the RFC points at is stale

`open-questions.md` row 16 lists Q16 as Open: "`cargo xtask golden` … never
captures them — all six sit at `0/6`, asserting nothing." Commit `fe25906`
says "Q16 closed", `cargo xtask coverage` reports "6/6 captured, 0 structural
problem(s)", and the six goldens exist. RFC-0000's Registers section presents
these files as the shared state; its Open questions section does not mention
Q16 either way. Not RFC-0000's bug, but its Terminology defines "Probe" by
D21 and a reader checking the register finds a contradiction.

## 6. Assumed but not stated

1. **Where AOT lives.** The Tier model says "AOT is the `cranelift-object`
   build"; the layout table calls `bund2-jit` "Tier 1; optional,
   feature-gated" and gives AOT no crate. The scaffold answers it —
   `crates/bund2-jit/Cargo.toml:23` declares `aot` alongside `jit`, sharing
   `cranelift-codegen`/`-frontend`/`-module` — so AOT and Tier 1 are the same
   crate under different features. Under a literal reading of the RFC's table
   an AOT build pulls in a crate labelled Tier 1, which the RFC says is
   optional. State it.

2. **A third boundary rule exists in the scaffold.**
   `crates/bund2-runtime/Cargo.toml:20-22` — `bund2-runtime` may depend on
   `bund2-jit` behind `aot`/`jit`, with the comment "bund2-async depends on
   bund2-runtime, so it must NOT appear here." That is a real invariant,
   enforced today, and absent from the RFC.

3. **What `bund2-runtime` does while D11 is open.** It is given "the world
   file"; D27 makes that redb; D27 is conditional on D11, which is OPEN. The
   RFC states the gate in Preservation but does not say what the crate's
   dependency set is in the meantime, which is a question criterion 3 would
   otherwise answer.

4. **That the twelve-member list is closed.** "One Cargo workspace, twelve
   members, already scaffolded" — nothing says whether a later RFC may add a
   member, or under what rule. Criterion 4's two boundary checks are named
   crate by crate, so adding a crate silently adds an unchecked boundary.

5. **That the oracle still builds at the pinned SHAs.** Criterion 5 requires a
   full `cargo xtask golden` run, which requires the out-of-tree oracle build.
   Every golden and every citation depends on that build succeeding, and no
   criterion asserts it.

6. **The measurement platform.** 381 MB, 1.4/11.0/15.8 ms and the
   `--no-default-features` dependency graph are all one machine, one OS, one
   linker. Criterion 3's claim is `cfg`-sensitive by construction; nothing
   records what it was measured on.

7. **That "gated" and "out of scope" coincide.** Feature policy names seven
   subsystems; `coverage` reports 120 words "out of scope by decision" from
   D15 + D26 + D28. The mapping from decisions to subsystems to word counts is
   assumed one-to-one and stated nowhere, and criterion 2's denominator is
   exactly that mapping's output.

8. **That feature-gating a subsystem cannot change a *surviving* word.** The
   RFC argues correctly that a gated word is absent, not different. It does
   not address a surviving word whose behaviour differs when a gated crate is
   absent — a `format`/`display` path that falls back, a word that reports
   available subsystems. Given `bund/console` (D15, 31 words, 22 exercised by
   the corpus) sits in presentation, this is the likeliest place "absent, not
   different" breaks, and it is the claim the whole "no golden changes"
   argument rests on.
