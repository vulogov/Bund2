## Blocking

### B1 — §C3 describes a second layer that does not exist, and ERRATA already says so

§C3: "`wrap`/`unwrap` are separate words producing a value that stays on the
stack (D11)… **two layers, not a contradiction**… `wrap`/`unwrap` are
program-visible words a Bund author may use to hold a serialised value *as a
value*." The alternatives list "**`send` accepting an ENVELOPE without
re-serialising**. Not taken, §C3."

The Bund words `wrap` and `unwrap` are OOP words: `wrap` stores a value in an
OBJECT's `.data` member and `unwrap` reads it back
(`reference/Bund/src/stdlib/functions/oop/value_class.rs:95-123`, registered
at `:168-169`). Neither serialises, neither produces an ENVELOPE. The RFC's own
ERRATA entry says this in terms — "the Bund words `wrap`/`unwrap` are OOP
`.data` words rather than a serialisation boundary" (`ERRATA.md:160-165`) — and
the first review's S4 said it on 2026-10-01. §C3 was not revised: it still
asserts the layer, and the alternative it declines is moot because no Bund
program can put an ENVELOPE on the stack to hand to `send`.

The fix is two sentences: there is one layer, the payload is bytes the program
never sees, and the research's "a mechanism the language already has a word
for" is the ERRATA entry. The "(D11)" pointer should go with it — D11 is about
`compile_to_binary`'s object format and carries the same mistaken sentence
(`decisions.md:490-491`, per ERRATA).

### B2 — §C5's definitions contradict the registry and the research, and neither is recorded

Two problems, both from the first review (S6 item 2, S7), both unaddressed.

**The definitions are not the first.** §C5: "Nothing reads `kind` today, so
these definitions are the first." The research defines all three at
`01-extensibility-async.md:302-306` — `Sync` "runs to completion, must not
block"; `Blocking` "must be dispatched to `spawn_blocking`"; `Async` carries a
future — and §2.7 lists the kinds as "required for any async" (`:389`). §C5's
`Blocking` (parks its own VM's thread; nothing is dispatched anywhere) is a
*different* definition from the research's, and §C5's `Async` (undefined)
contradicts the research's (a future). That is a departure, and ERRATA has no
entry for §2.4 or for §2.7's "Await inside a Tier-0 word: yes" (`:388`), which
D84 also overturned. The header's "the review found five places where it
departs" is right; ERRATA records two.

**The `Sync` definition is false for the registry it describes.** §C5: "`Sync`
— returns without yielding the thread. **Every word today.**" `input` and
`password` are registered `Sync` (`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/terminal.rs:886,888`)
and park the thread until a person types; `sleep.seconds` is `Sync`
(`host.rs:1666`) and parks it for the operand. Under §C5's own definition these
are mis-declared the day the RFC is accepted, or the definition is not the one
in use. Either the definition changes ("does not *suspend* the VM"; a `Sync`
word may block the OS thread, which under D84 blocks only its own VM), or the
three words and their kin are re-marked `Blocking` and criterion 7's
withdrawal gets a replacement. The preservation row "`WordKind::Sync` —
Unchanged. 258 declarations, now with a stated meaning" is the place this
lands.

### B3 — The behaviour table disagrees with Bund2's registration of the same words

Current behaviour's table gives `send`/`send.` one row — "Name then object,
**object on top**… `eff(2, 1)`" — and `send.quick`/`send.quick.` one row,
`eff(2, 0)`. `bus.rs` registers `send.` as `eff(1, 1)` and `send.quick.` as
`eff(1, 0)` (`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/bus.rs:290-291`), with the comment "the
workbench forms take one value off the stack — the name — and their object
from the other side". `recv.` is `eff(0, 1)` (`:293`); the table's `recv`/`recv.`
row says "Name". `bus.data.current` pulls nothing (`crossbus.rs:16-22`;
`bus.rs:254-259`, `eff(0, 1)`); the table says "The same" as `bus.data`.

This is the first review's S1, unaddressed in the table while the probe, the
golden and the `bus.rs` tests all exercise the asymmetry correctly
(`tests/probes/bus-words.bund` "send. takes the object from the workbench";
`the_workbench_forms_take_different_operands_from_the_workbench`). The
document's contract and the binary's contract differ on four of eight words,
and RFC-0004's effects are read from documents like this one.

The preservation table has the same gap: no row for the workbench forms'
operand sources, and no row for any error text, although criterion 1 says the
texts are "asserted in `crates/bund2-stdlib/src/bus.rs`". The reference's arms
(`crossbus.rs:27,31,35,45,50,53,63,67,71,75`; `mod.rs:76,98,107,114,126,140,146`)
and two quirks — `send.quick`'s prefix is `SEND` (`crossbus.rs:177`), and a
bad name is reported under `BUS.DATA:` whatever word was called
(`crossbus.rs:35,71,110,142`) — are the contract as much as the bool is.

### B4 — Three criteria name a check weaker than the sentence that claims it

**Criterion 3** says the stamp assertion "asserts the stamp because that is
the part the reference defines". `a_received_value_keeps_its_stamp` asserts
`got[0].timestamp().0 > 0.0` (`bus.rs:533`) — that *a* stamp exists. A decoder
that minted a fresh stamp at `recv` would pass it. The test does not read the
stamp before `send` and compare. D88's decided rule is "the stamp crosses";
the test holds "a stamp is present". Compare the two stamps, or say the test
holds less than the rule.

**Criterion 12** says the test "observes the interleaving and accepts it".
`bus_data_is_advisory_once_there_is_more_than_one_vm` (`host.rs:842-878`)
spawns two racers and asserts exactly one `NODATA`. It cannot see whether both
saw `true`; the outcome it asserts is also the outcome of the two racers
running one after the other with no overlap at all. The claim "both may see
`true`" is a statement about the code, not something the test establishes.
Either say the test asserts the *tolerated outcome* and not the race, or make
the race observable (a barrier between `bus.data` and `recv`).

**Criterion 10** is vacuous without the tier.
`every_vm_thread_declares_its_own_region_and_share` has a `None =>` arm that
only asserts `entries == None` (`host.rs:787-790`), which holds whether or not
`declare_region` ran. The RFC says "four VMs under `--features jit`", which is
honest, and then says "Verified load-bearing" without the qualifier. The
test's own doc comment (`host.rs:738-742`) promises that in a build with no
tier it "asserts the floor is usable at all, by running a body deep enough to
prove the region was declared"; the body is four calls of `w` and nothing
recurses. The comment describes a check that was not written. Note also that
`bund2-async` has no `jit` feature of its own: the invocation that exercises
this is `--features bund2-runtime/jit`.

### B5 — Citations corrected on 2026-10-01 are still wrong, and a decision is described as pending that was ruled

The three research citations in the table above (`:285-291`, `:287-291`,
`:293`) do not resolve to the quoted text. Two of them were listed with the
correct ranges in the first review's citation table and M1. CLAUDE.md's rule is
that a claim about existing behaviour carries a citation that says what the
claim says; a citation known to be wrong for thirteen days is the case the rule
exists for. (`cargo xtask cite` passes: it checks that a file and line exist,
not that the line says what is quoted.)

§C8's last paragraph: "It is now D91, so a work item can be blocked on it rather
than rediscover it. D91 adds a third option… which is why D91 recommends it
*before* the shared module". The register: D91 **RESOLVED — option 1, keep
per-`Interp` modules**, decided by the owner on 2026-10-03, the day before this
document's status line (`decisions.md:8348-8360`). The RFC never says D91 is
resolved, and it omits what D91 says is still owed: "`JITModule` exposes no
size, so how much one compiled body costs is unmeasured… until
`one_compiled_bodys_code_memory` has run this entry's cost column is a multiple
without a unit." That harness is `#[ignore]`d (`host.rs:518-520`) and the
figure has not been recorded. A reader of §C8 learns neither.

### S2 — The identity row omits JSON, which D20 and the first review both except

§C2: "The reference preserves identity and stamp across the bus." D20:
"**Both of those citations are in the non-JSON branches**… For a JSON value
the round trip is not identity-preserving" (`decisions.md:972-975`); the first
review's B2 measured it ("JSON is the one exception, and the RFC does not
mention it"). The preservation row "The payload's identity" states the oracle's
behaviour without the exception. If Bund2's wire codec treats JSON the same
way, the row is simply incomplete; if it does not, there is a second deviation
on this row. Either way the row should say which.

### S3 — "`recv` has no fairness" gives the wrong mechanism

§C8: "`unbounded` with several receivers delivers to whichever wakes first."
No receiver ever waits on the channel. The reference tests `is_empty()` and
calls `recv()` under the one `PIPES` lock (`mod.rs:123-132`), and Bund2 does
the same (`bus.rs:88-100`), so the only wait is for the map mutex and the only
ordering is the mutex's. That is also *why* `recv` cannot block with several
consumers — the invariant the first review's S10 item 2 asked the RFC to state
with its mechanism. The RFC still does not state it; `bus.rs` has it right.
Say "the map lock is held across the dequeue, so `recv` never waits on the
channel and consumers are ordered by the lock", which is both the non-blocking
guarantee and the fairness statement.

### S4 — Three sentences in criterion 1 and 8 overstate what the code holds

- "the `BUS.DATA:` name-casting prefix **every one of the eight** shares".
  `bus.data.current` casts nothing (`crossbus.rs:16-22`); seven share it.
- "the six depth guards". The reference has more than six guard sites
  (`crossbus.rs:45,50,53,84,89,92,123,128` plus `:27`); the test asserts six
  strings (`bus.rs:463-470`). `send.`'s stack guard, checked before its
  workbench guard (`crossbus.rs:49-54`), is not among them.
- Criterion 8: "checked against the oracle's own `--noio` run for each of the
  three shapes". `noio_replaces_every_one_of_the_eight` (`bus.rs:499-514`)
  checks Bund2 alone; no oracle run is in the test or cited. The first review
  reported an oracle `--noio` run for `send` and `bus.data`. Cite that, or the
  claim is unsupported.

### S5 — Accepted documents the RFC consumes or feeds still say the opposite

- **RFC-0000** (Accepted): "`bus`… feature-gated and off"
  (`RFC-0000-architecture.md:255-260`). `crossbeam-channel` is an unconditional
  dependency of `bund2-stdlib` (`Cargo.toml:233`) and the eight words register
  in every build. D28 was amended for D87 (`decisions.md`, "Narrowed on
  2026-10-01 by D87"); RFC-0000's feature list was not, and RFC-0000 does not
  mention D87. The first review's consistency section asked for this.
- **RFC-0001** (Accepted) hands this RFC a question — "RFC-0007 inherits the
  question rather than an answer. Making `BundValue` shareable…"
  (`RFC-0001-value.md:825-826`). The RFC answers it (bytes cross, values do
  not) without saying it is discharging RFC-0001's hand-off.
- **RFC-0002** (Accepted): "per-stack dispatch, when RFC-0007 needs it, is the
  single order with the stack as a parameter" (`RFC-0002:68-71`). The RFC
  does not say whether it needs it. It does not; one sentence closes the
  forward constraint.
- **The roadmap** gives this RFC "tier pinning" (`05-rfc-roadmap.md:96`). D84
  removed the mechanism that needed pinning; the RFC never says so, and
  ERRATA has no entry (B2).

- `crates/bund2-stdlib/src/bus.rs:18-21`: "Whether a received value keeps the

## Preservation analysis — rows the table lacks

The table has eleven rows. Behaviours the design touches and the table does
not name:

1. **The workbench forms' operand sources and effects** — `send.`/`send.quick.`
   take the object from the workbench (`crossbus.rs:57-60`), `recv.` the name
   (`:132-135`); `bus.data.current` consumes nothing (B3).
2. **Every error text**, the `SEND` prefix on `send.quick`, the `BUS.DATA:`
   prefix on `send`/`recv` name errors, and that a failed cast has consumed
   both operands (`crossbus.rs:57-72`) (B3).
3. **Identity across the bus for JSON** (S2).
4. **`Can not determine current stack name`** (`crossbus.rs:19`) — removed
   structurally in Bund2 (`bus.rs:250-253`, `current_name` answers `main`).
   D37's first way out, and correct; it is a reference arm that no longer
   exists and belongs in the table as such.
5. **A poisoned channel map.** The reference unwraps (`mod.rs:45,53,59,73,90,123`);
   Bund2 reports "the bus's channel map is poisoned, so … cannot proceed"
   (`bus.rs:56-66`). New text with no reference counterpart.
6. **A LAMBDA crosses the bus and runs on receipt** (first review, item 8).
   Code crosses; under a second VM that is remote code in the sense the
   `--noeval` flag is about. Nothing says whether a spawned VM's `HostOptions`
   are the embedder's.
7. **Process-global state the design now shares between VMs**: `MT`, `CHACHA`,
   `GENERATORS` (`random.rs:41,45,173`) and `ARGS` (`host.rs:82`). A seeded
   sequence in one VM is advanced by another. In the reference every node is a
   process with its own. First review S10 item 4, still unlisted.
8. **The interner is per thread** (`bund2-value/src/lib.rs:447,469`), so a
   symbol's index differs between VMs and a value re-interns on decode. Not
   program-visible; worth one row because it is why the interner needs no lock.
9. **`WordKind::Async` in a stable API with no meaning** (first review S7). An
   embedder can register one today and it runs as `Sync`. "Deliberately left
   undefined" needs to say what the runtime does with one.
10. **A spawned VM and the debugger words** — acceptance review S3,
    unaddressed: `debug.step` in a `Host` VM has no console and is refused in
    words; two documents produce this and neither states it.
11. **A spawned VM and `bund.exit`** — `request_exit` is per `Interp`; whether
    `exit` in a VM thread ends the VM or the process is not said. (`Host` returns
    the closure's value, so it ends the VM; say so.)
12. **`Vm<T>` dropped before it finishes detaches the thread** (`host.rs:131-133`).
    A reasonable choice, stated in a doc comment and nowhere in the RFC.

## What the RFC assumes but does not state

1. **The map lock is held across the dequeue**, which is the whole of "`recv`
   does not block" under several consumers (S3). The code does it; the text
   never says it.
2. **A host VM's `HostOptions`** — `--noio`, `--noeval`, the reporter — are
   whatever `Runtime::with_threshold` defaults to. Tens of VMs reporting
   through tens of `Box<dyn Reporter>` to one stderr is unaddressed (first
   review, assumption 8).
3. **Channels are never removed** (`mod.rs` has no removal path; neither has
   `bus.rs`), so a long-lived process with computed names grows the map without
   bound — a second unbounded quantity beside the one §C2 names.
4. **Channel names and stack names share one flat namespace**
   (`bus.data.current`), so a stack called `in` is the default channel.
5. **`send.quick`'s observable arity is `eff(2,0)` and its error prefix is
   `SEND`**: the former is in the table, the latter only in a test.
6. **Criterion 10's property is a `jit` property** and the default build does
   not check it (B4).
7. **D85's "tens" is a 64-bit statement** — stated in §C8, good — but the
   host's hard ceiling of 1024 (`DEFAULT_LIMIT * 16`) is 8.4 GiB of reserved
   address space under `jit` and is accepted on any target. On 32-bit that is
   not a limit the host can honour; `with_limit` does not know the target.
8. **The façade's `Vm<T>` is `Send` only if `T` is** (`host.rs:296-300`), so an
   embedder's executor must accept the handle on the thread that spawned it or
   require `T: Send`, which every example here does. Unstated.
9. **A received value's `stack` tag** is rewritten by the receiving push — in
   the golden every value shows `"stack": "main"` after crossing. With one VM
   that is invisible; with two it is where ownership changes (first review,
   item 9).

## Disposition

**Every criterion's test exists and passes today, in both builds.** None of
the findings above is a Bund2 defect; three are tests that hold less than the
criterion's sentence claims (B4), and the rest are the document.

What would make this document say what the binary does:

- B1 and B2 are design text that contradicts the reference, ERRATA and the
  registry; each is a paragraph, plus three ERRATA entries.
- B3 is the behaviour table and two preservation rows, settled by
  `crossbus.rs` and `bus.rs`, which already agree with each other.
- B4 is one `assert_eq!` on two stamps, one barrier, and either a check in the
  no-tier arm of criterion 10's test or a sentence saying there is none.
- B5 is three line numbers and D91's status.
- S1 is dating six passages, as RFC-0006 does.

The first review found that two measurements had been written as though taken.
This review finds nothing of that kind: what is measured is measured. What it
finds is that corrections made to the code and the registers did not all
reach the document, and that a document whose status says "the implementation
of everything in it" is read as describing that implementation.
