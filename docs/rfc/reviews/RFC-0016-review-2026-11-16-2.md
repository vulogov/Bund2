## Should fix

### S1. Criterion 6's worked case is degenerate, and sits 0.0015 from a different answer

**Location.** Criterion 6 (lines 507-511); S2 "Mann–Whitney [A9]" (lines 251-258).

**Evidence.** Computed with mpmath (scratch `hl.py`). For a = [1.1 2.2 3.3 4.4] and b = [0.5 1.5 1.5 2.5 3.0]:
- **The sorted differences D₍₁₎…D₍₂₀₎** are −1.9, −1.4, −0.8, −0.4, −0.4, −0.3, 0.3, 0.6, 0.7, 0.7, 0.8, 1.4, 1.7, 1.8, 1.8, 1.9, 2.8, 2.9, 2.9, 3.9.
- **The median** is (D₍₁₀₎ + D₍₁₁₎)/2 = 0.75.
- **k at 95%:**
  - √(nm(n + m + 1)/12) = √(200/12) = 4.08248;
  - nm/2 − 1.959964·4.08248 = **1.99848**;
  - so k = ⌊1.99848⌋ = **1**.

  k ≥ 1, so the case is an interval and not a refusal. It is [D₍₁₎, D₍₂₀₎] = [−1.9, 3.9], the whole range of the differences.
- **The exact distribution-free interval at 95%**, from the null distribution of U for n = 4, m = 5:
  - P(U ≤ 1) = 2/126 = 0.0159 ≤ 0.025 < P(U ≤ 2) = 4/126 = 0.0317;
  - so the exact interval is [D₍₂₎, D₍₁₉₎] = [−1.4, 2.9].

  Rounding the approximate C_α to the nearest integer, rather than flooring it, also gives k = 2.
- **The tie in b** does not enter the interval: the formula has no tie correction.

**Problem.**
- The case does not exercise order-statistic selection. Any implementation that returns min and max passes it.
- The case sits 0.0015 from the boundary where a "nearest integer" reading of Hollander and Wolfe gives a different k. So it is the one case least able to tell the RFC's ⌊·⌋ from the alternative.
- The RFC attributes the formula to "Hollander and Wolfe's distribution-free interval". That attribution is to a source not in `reference/` and is unmarked. Whether H&W floor or round is exactly what the case is sensitive to.
- The criterion leaves the values to "the test's comment". The first review's S10, as fixed, asked for criteria to state their values.

**Suggested fix.**
- Use conf = 0.90, or another case with k ≥ 2 well away from an integer. At 0.90 here, nm/2 − 1.644854·4.08248 = 3.285, so k = 3 and the ends are [D₍₃₎, D₍₁₈₎] = [−0.8, 2.9].
- State the estimate (0.75), k and both ends in the criterion.
- Say that ⌊·⌋ is Bund2's choice, and that it is conservative against the exact interval at small n.
- Mark the H&W attribution **[UNGROUNDED]**, or drop it, since the RFC's formula stands on its own.
- Say that "median" for even nm is the mean of the two middle differences.

### S2. Fisher's work bound counts one root; `.opts` finds three

**Location.** S2 "Work (D39)" (lines 267-269); §Alternatives (line 444).

**Evidence.** S2 defines three roots for `fisher.opts`, each found by up to 64 doublings and 200 bisection steps (lines 244-248):
- ψ̂, from E[X; ψ] = x;
- the lower end;
- the upper end.

Each step sums the support, which `SUPPORT_CAP` allows up to 10⁸ tables (`infer.rs:47`, `:329-335`).

**Problem.** "At most 264 times the plain word's" is the bound for one root. A two-sided call costs up to 3 × 264 = 792 support sums. At the cap that is about 8·10¹⁰ terms: bounded, so D39 holds, but not the bound the RFC states.

**Suggested fix.** Say "at most 792 times (264 per root, three roots)". Consider whether `.opts` should cap the support lower than the plain word does; if so, that is a new E111 on `.opts` only, which the amended D251 permits.

### S3. Hochberg, Hommel and BY are never defined, so criterion 8's "the definitions" has no referent

**Location.** S4 (lines 305-310); criterion 8 (lines 516-518).

**Evidence.**
- S4 says only "as R's `p.adjust` does with those methods [UNGROUNDED; Q62]".
- The RFC's own rule (§Open questions, lines 578-579) is that "the definitions this RFC states … stand whether or not R agrees". S4 states none.
- Hommel's adjusted p in particular has more than one published algorithm. S4's own O(n²) cap presupposes one.

**Suggested fix.** State each as a formula, in sorted order, then mapped back to input order:
- **Hochberg:** p̃₍ᵢ₎ = min_{j ≥ i} min(1, (n − j + 1)·p₍ⱼ₎).
- **BY:** BH's adjusted values × Σ_{k=1}^{n} 1/k, capped at 1.
- **Hommel:** the closed-testing adjusted p, with the algorithm named step by step.

Point criterion 8 at those formulas. The first review's table rated criterion 8 "checkable" without noticing this; that was a miss.

### S4. Kendall's exact two-sided p and its tie-corrected variance are not defined

**Location.** S3 "Kendall" (lines 291-297); criterion 7.

**Problem.**
- "The exact p of T … by the recurrence" defines the null distribution, not the two-sided p. The fixture must choose one of:
  - 2·min(P(T ≤ t), P(T ≥ t)), capped at 1;
  - the sum of the outcomes no more probable than t;
  - something else.

  These differ.
- "The normal approximation with the tie-corrected variance" names no variance formula, and does not say whether a continuity correction is applied.

Both are marked [UNGROUNDED as R's], but as with S3, the RFC's definitions must stand without R.

**Suggested fix.**
- Define the two-sided exact p, for example as 2·min(·) capped at 1.
- Write the variance: (v₀ − v_t − v_u)/18 + v₁/(2n(n − 1)) + v₂/(9n(n − 1)(n − 2)), with z = S/√var and S = n_c − n_d.
- Say "no continuity correction".
- Say whether `statistic` is z or S in the approximate branch. Line 294 says z.

## Notes

**N1. The reason for `math.round.places`'s cap of 17 is wrong.**
- Line 348: "Seventeen significant digits name every f64 exactly, so a larger n changes nothing." That holds for `math.signif` and for `string.sci`'s mantissa, but not for decimal *places*.
- Run: `{:.17}` of 1.5e-18 is `0.00000000000000000` → 0.0, and `{:.18}` is `0.000000000000000001` → 1e-18.
- Keep the cap if wanted, but give it as a choice, not a consequence.

**N2. Pearson's one-sided far end is not covered by "Infinite ends".** Line 260-263 names the t, Mann–Whitney, Clopper–Pearson and Fisher ends. A one-sided Fisher-z interval's far end is ±1, which is finite, so presumably answered. Say so; this is the rest of the first review's N10.

**N3. "At |r| = 1 the t is infinite: `statistic` is left out"** (line 299) is written for Pearson. Spearman's p also uses a t (line 290), but its `statistic` is S = 0, which is finite. Say whether Spearman keeps `statistic` at |ρ| = 1. And at |r| = 1 Fisher's z gives tanh(±∞) = ±1 for both ends: say that it answers [1, 1], or that the interval is left out.

**N4. `rng.choice.weighted`'s search rule is unstated for zero weights.**
- Zero weights are allowed (line 395: "non-negative").
- With a uniform u in [0, Σw) located by binary search on the cumulative weights, "the first i with cumᵢ ≥ u" can return a zero-weight element at u = 0 or at a boundary. "The first i with cumᵢ > u" cannot.
- State the strict form. It also fixes the stream.

**N5. "The algorithms fix the streams" is not quite true.**
- "One uniform integer draw per position" (line 389) does not say:
  - whether Fisher–Yates draws for position 0 (n draws, or n − 1);
  - which integer sampler is used.
- rand 0.8's `gen_range` over `usize` and `SliceRandom::shuffle`'s internal index sampler differ, and the former depends on the pointer width.
- Name the call, e.g. `gen_range(0..=i)` on `u64`, for i from n − 1 down to 1.

**N6. Hedges' J loses precision as n grows.**
- The lgamma-difference formula cancels two values near (df/2)·ln(df/2).
- Measured with f64 lgamma against mpmath, J's relative error is:
  - 8e-14 at n = 200 per group, so criterion 9 passes;
  - 1.1e-12 at n = 20 000;
  - 4.6e-10 at n = 200 000.
- So "1e-12 relative for closed forms" does not hold for g at large n. Either compute ln Γ(x)/Γ(x − ½) by its asymptotic series past some df, or state the tolerance for g.

**N7. The ratio intervals' formula is implied, not stated.** S5 gives `se` and `conf` 0.95 for both ratios, but not that the interval is exp(log ± z₀.₉₇₅·se). One line.

**N8. Mann–Whitney's interval is not tie-corrected, while the test is.** The order-statistic interval uses √(nm(n + m + 1)/12) (line 253). The plain test's σ subtracts the tie term (`infer.rs:444-456`). This is defensible as a definition, but say it. Also say which end a one-sided interval keeps: [D₍ₖ₎, ∞) for "greater", by S2's alternatives table.

**N9. Above the HL cap, `mannwhitney.opts` refuses the one-sided p too.** n·m > 10⁷ is E111 for the whole word (line 266). So a one-sided Mann–Whitney test at n = m = 5000 has no word. ## What the RFC still assumes and does not state

- **That a `.opts` word's two-sided `p` equals the plain word's**, bit for bit, at the defaults. That is presumably meant, but S2's "the plain word's MAP" is not exactly true (S5).
- **That Fisher's noncentral mass is evaluated in log space** with the maximum subtracted, as `stat_test_fisher` does for the central case (`infer.rs:337-348`). Past |log ψ| ≈ 709, ψ itself overflows, and the 64-doubling bracket reaches |log ψ| = 2⁶⁴.
- **That the normal quantile z₁₋α/₂ for k and for Fisher-z comes from `Family::quantile`**, so it can be E110 like any polish. S2 says so for the t and beta quantiles only.
- **That an INTEGER `conf` is refused.** S2 says "a FLOAT in (0, 1)", while B15 converts INTEGERs for the S6 words. Say which.

- **S2:** the Fisher work bound;
- **S6, S7, S8:** an ERRATA entry for research 07 §3.4, A7's narrowed text taken to the owner, and the remaining unmarked R claims.

Only S7 needs the owner, and only as a clarification of A7. The rest is text.
