## Blocking

### B1 — The RFC's open question 1 is already resolved, and the RFC contradicts the ruling

`docs/registers/decisions.md` D185 (raised today by this RFC, **RESOLVED**
today, "C") supersedes D157 for the spelling and takes **`--profile <base>`
with a required operand**: "a bare `--profile` is refused with the operand
message `parse_args` already produces for `--file`". RFC-0008's flag-table
row was amended for it the same day (`RFC-0008:530`), and D185's status line
says "RFC-0011 §S6 [is] amended the same day". It is not. The RFC still:

- says in its header that "the three questions at the end are the owner's
  to rule on before code" and that D157 is "touched", with no mention of
  D185 (`R:1-13`);
- specifies in §S6 that "with no operand, `<base>` is the program's path
  with its extension removed" (`R:206-208`), which is D185's option B,
  rejected;
- recommends B in open question 1 (`R:357-363`) and closes with "none of
  the three is taken here. The register has no entry for any of them yet"
  (`R:372-374`), which is false of the register in the working tree.

D185 also rules on the environment variable: `BUND2_PROFILE` "names the
base path rather than a switch". §S6 reads it as a switch "as `BUND2_STATS`
is for `--stats`" (`R:209-211`).

Either the RFC is a stale draft the owner already ruled past, or D185's
"amended the same day" is premature. Both cannot stand. The RFC needs its
header to list D185 as consumed, §S6 rewritten to the required operand, and
open question 1 closed with the ruling.

### B2 — "A native is called in one place" is contradicted by D49, which the RFC cites

The preservation table's fourth row reads "A native is called in one place,
`Interp::invoke`, under D49's catch — Preserved. The hook brackets the call"
(`R:301`), and §S2's hook 1 is "around `invoke`" (`R:110-112`). The doc
comment on `invoke` does say "the one place Tier 0 calls one"
(`crates/bund2-interp/src/lib.rs:665`). D49 says otherwise in its first
paragraph: the catch "covers `Interp::invoke` for registered natives, **and
the direct calls `bund2-stdlib` makes to method natives (`oop.rs`) and
conditional runners (`conditional.rs`)**". Verified:
`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/oop.rs:153` and `:276` call a method native's
function through `catch_panic` with no `invoke`, and
`crates/bund2-stdlib/src/conditional.rs:473` does the same for a
conditional's runner.

So hook 1 as drawn sees no method call on an object and no conditional
dispatch. An OOP program's time inside its methods is charged to whichever
native dispatched them (`!` on an object, a `.method` word), which is
exactly the shape the RFC says the reference's profiler has and this one
does not (`R:68-70`). §S2 needs a third hook or §S7 needs a stated limit,
and the preservation row needs to say three call sites, not one.

### B3 — The registry does not hold what §S4 says it records

§S4 records per node "the declared effect from the registry (a pair, or
`opaque`)" and "where it was registered when the registry knows (the file
and line for a word a program registered; the word package for a native)"
(`R:173-178`), and the §S5 sample shows `fib | word | (1, 1)` (`R:194`).

- `Slot` is `generation`, `command`, `alias`, `lambda`, `native`
  (`crates/bund2-api/src/lib.rs:1322-1329`). A word a program registered is
  `lambda: Option<BundValue>`. **It has no effect.** Only `Native` carries
  `effect: StackEffect` (`:1252`). A Bund word's effect is *inferred*, by
  RFC-0004 §S3 ("Bund words are inferred by composition", `RFC-0004:282`),
  by `check` in `crates/bund2-stdlib/src/check.rs`, which this RFC never
  names. Either the profiler runs the inference at report time, which is a
  dependency on RFC-0004 and a cost, or the column is natives-only.
- **No slot and no native records where it was registered.** `Native` has
  `f`, `effect`, `kind`, `id: Option<RegistrationId>`; there is no package
  field. `Slot` has no span and no origin. "When the registry knows" is
  never, today. If the author intends to add either field, that is a change
  to `bund2-api`'s ABI (RFC-0002) and belongs in the design, not in a
  hedge.

## Significant

### S1 — "No Bund word is a row" is overstated, and F7 lists the exceptions

`R:68`: "**No Bund word is a row.** A `dup` is time inside whichever
instrumented Rust function dispatched it." The second sentence holds:
`i_direct` and `i` are instrumented
(`reference/rust_multistackvm/src/multistackvm_inline.rs:41,70`), and that
is where `dup` is dispatched. The first does not. Among the 21 instrumented
VM files are the implementing functions of single Bund words:
`stdlib_string_format` and `stdlib_string_format_in_workbench`
(`stdlib/string/format.rs:9,72`), `stdlib_clear_stacks` and
`stdlib_drop_stacks` (`stdlib/stacks.rs:5,12`), and the `if`, `times`,
`loop`, `ifthenelse`, `execute` and compare functions that **F7**
(`docs/registers/defects.md:53-57`) lists by name. The RFC's own motivating
word, `format` (`R:35-37`), is one of them: the reference's table *does*
have a row for it, under its Rust name. The accurate claim is that the
reference's unit is the Rust function, that most natives are invisible
because the dispatcher is instrumented and they are not, and that a handful
of words appear under a Rust spelling. F7 is directly about the subject and
is not cited.

### S2 — `!` on a literal lambda *is* a node; what varies is where its body's callees hang

§S1: "A `!` on a literal lambda is therefore invisible as a node and
visible as its callees" (`R:98-100`). `!` is an alias of `execute`; `dispatch`
resolves it and calls `call_native(target, n)` (`lib.rs:791`), so hook 1
brackets it and it is a node, named `execute`. What has no node is the
lambda. And where the lambda's callees are charged depends on how the native
runs it, which the RFC does not state:

- `{ … } !` at top reach files a tail request (`values.rs:408-412`,
  `vm.tail_lambda`): `!`'s span closes, `take_pending` pushes the body with
  `who = None` (`lib.rs:1072-1081`), and the callees are *siblings* of
  `execute` under the enclosing frame.
- `[ { … } ] !`, `times`, `map`, `?try` and every other native that runs a
  body run it through `eval_lambda` (`lib.rs:1842`; `values.rs:417`), which
  pushes the frame and runs `run_to` *inside* the native's `invoke` span, so
  the callees are *children* of the native and its inclusive time contains
  them.

Two spellings of one program draw two graphs. §S1 should say which it is
per path, and §S5's tree should show `times` with its body's words beneath
it, since that is the common case.

### S3 — The node name is the resolved name, and ERRATA records why that misleads

§S1: "An alias resolves to its target before it is recorded, since that is
what ran" (`R:88-89`). So a program that spells `!` 69 times reads a report
with `execute` in it. `docs/research/ERRATA.md` (the `execute` entry)
records that "a survey keyed on the registered name returns zero and reads
as 'no dynamic dispatch', which is the opposite of the truth", and that no
program spells it `execute`. A report "written for a reader who did not see
the run" (`R:183`) repeats that mistake unless the node carries the
spelling the program used beside the resolved one. The reference's alias
table has no multi-hop chains (RFC-0003 §S8), so one extra column suffices.

### S4 — §S6's bundle paragraph describes a flag path that does not exist

`R:221-225`: "A bundle passes the flag through, as `--stats` is documented
to in `crates/bund2-cli/src/main.rs`: a carried program that swallowed it
would shadow the one flag its user is trying to use." A bundle **owns all of
argv** (RFC-0006 §B3; `main.rs:795-796`, "`--debugger` cannot reach here — a
bundle owns all of argv"). `--stats` reaches a bundle only as
`stats: env_set("BUND2_STATS")` (`main.rs:791`); the flag does not pass
through. So the profiler reaches a bundle through `BUND2_PROFILE` or not at
all, and with D185's required operand there is no "flag" to pass. The
paragraph should say that, and should say where a bundle's two files go
when `carried.name` (the build-time path, `main.rs:786`) names a directory
the deploy host does not have.

The same paragraph's "`BUND2_PROFILE` is the fallback, as `BUND2_STATS` is
for `--stats`" is also wrong for `script`: `BUND2_STATS` is read in
`run_carried` only; `script` never consults it.

### S5 — Hook 2's failure half is in `unwind_to`, not `run_to`

§S2: a Bund word's time runs "from `push_frame` … to the pop in `run_to`, on
success and on failure" (`R:114-116`). The success pop is `run_to`'s
`Some(None)` arm (`lib.rs:1512-1521`). The failure pop is `unwind_to`
(`lib.rs:1188-1199`), a separate function, called from `run_to`'s error arm
(`:1537`), from its internal-error arm (`:1523`), and from two other callers (`:866`, `:1816`) — and it pops
*every* frame down to the floor in one loop, so a failure forty words deep
closes forty nodes at once. `push_frame` also truncates frames after a tier
shadow (`:1153`), unreachable with the tier declined but a third pop site
all the same. The profiler's mirror stack has to follow all of them; the RFC
names one.

### S6 — §S3's claim that "hook 2 would still bracket" a compiled body is backwards

`R:151-153`: "A compiled body still has a frame (D139), so hook 2 would still
bracket it." D139's frame is pushed by the tier through `Vm::tier_frame`, not
by `push_frame`'s push (`lib.rs:1138-1143`, and the tier's own push at `:1928`: "The tier asks for one through
`Vm::tier_frame` … and it is removed here however `enter` answers"). A hook at
`push_frame`'s `self.frames.push` (`:1176`) never sees it. The conclusion
(decline the tier) survives; the argument for it does not, and §S8's first
item inherits the error: hooking `jit_call_native` alone would give compiled
natives and no compiled words.

### S7 — The recursion rule leaves the sample's own figures undefined

§S2: "an activation whose name is already open adds to the call count and to
exclusive time and not to inclusive" (`R:126-128`). Then:

- the §S5 dot sample labels the self-edge `"fib" -> "fib"` with
  `832 039 · 1.280 s` (`R:240`), an inclusive time per edge — but by the rule
  no nested `fib` activation *has* an inclusive time to put on that edge;
- "exclusive is inclusive minus the children's inclusive" (`R:124`) does not
  say whether the outermost `fib` subtracts the nested `fib` it called. If it
  does not, every `+` beneath a nested activation is counted once in `+`'s
  exclusive and again in `fib`'s, and the sample's `0.301 s` self time is
  not computable from its other columns;
- the §S5 tree is rooted at `` `main` `` (`R:213`). There is no `main` word;
  the top level is a body no word names, and §S1 says such a body "has no
  node". The sample contradicts the rule two sections above it.

Criterion 6 tests only "inclusive ≤ wall", which the undefined case passes
whichever way it is implemented. A criterion that the sum of all exclusive
times is ≤ wall, and that `exclusive(fib) + inclusive(+) = inclusive(fib)`
within the clock's cost, would decide it.

### S8 — D16 does not say what the header attributes to it

`R:9`: "D16 (a body no word names has no name to report)". D16 is "dynamic
dispatch by computed name" (`decisions.md:` the D16 entry, read whole); it
forecloses tree-shaking and devirtualisation and fixes the resolution order.
Nothing in it is about unnamed bodies. The nearest source is RFC-0008 §D2's
"A body assembled at run time has no span at all — D16's consequence"
(`RFC-0008:318-320`), which is §D2's inference, not D16's text. Cite §D2.

### S9 — The `Notice` is a decision under D173 the RFC does not take

§S6: one `Notice` through the reporter, "which on a terminal is one line on
`stderr`" (`R:215-217`). D36 says a Notice is one line on stderr, and D173
says `-q` drops `Warning` lines "and nothing else" — so the Notice prints
under `-q` too. But D173 built a second voice for the command line's own
work: `ui::info!` is hidden by default and shown at `-v`
(`main.rs:1815`, "running the included …" is an `info`). "Wrote two files"
is the command line reporting on its own work, which is what `ui::info!` is
for, not a diagnostic the program raised. The RFC lists D173 as "touched but
not consumed" and then picks one of its two channels. Say which and why, and
whether `-q` silences it.

## Preservation analysis — what the table does not enumerate

- **Natives called outside `invoke`** (B2): method natives and conditional
  runners. Not a row.
- **Three frame-pop sites** (S5). The row "the profiler reads `who`; it adds
  no field to the frame" (`R:303`) is true and insufficient: a mirror stack
  that drifts from `frames` after an `unwind_to` charges the rest of the run
  to the wrong word.
- **The resolved name is what is recorded** (S3). A report naming `execute`
  for a program that wrote `!` is observable output and should be a row.
- **Declining the tier changes where deep recursion fails.** §S7: a profiled
  program "fails exactly as it does without the flag, with the tier's absence
  the one observable difference, and that one only in `--stats`" (`R:270-273`).
  D145's "stated consequence" is that under the tier the levels of a compiled
  recursion above the Tier 1 floor sit on the Rust stack; at Tier 0 they sit
  on the heap. A program near either limit can fail under one tier and not
  the other — RFC-0008 §D6 accepts the same caveat for the debugger. Say it.
- **`debug.*` words under `--profile`.** §W5 attaches a console on the first
  arming word (`main.rs:1808`, `lib.rs:2007-2012`) and sets `tier_off`
  (`:2016`). A stop's wall time lands in whichever node is open. The header's
  `tier: declined (profiling)` is then one of two reasons the tier is off.
- **`--jit-threshold` beside `--profile`**: refused, ignored, or printed?
- **Two renames are not one.** F181's `place` stages and renames *one* file.
  Two files renamed in sequence can leave `.profile.md` written and
  `.profile.dot` not, on a failure between them. State which is written
  first and what a reader sees.
- **Exit code after a write failure.** "A failure to write is an `Error`
  reported the same way" (`R:218`). The program succeeded; does the process
  exit 0 (D36's preserved code for a program error) or non-zero?
- **`-q` and the Notice** (S9).

## Consistency with accepted RFCs and ERRATA

- **RFC-0008** flag table and preservation row: amended for D185 (`:530`);
  RFC-0011 is not (B1). §D7's quotations (`R:44-48`) match `RFC-0008:438-449`.
- **RFC-0006 §B3** (a bundle owns argv): contradicted by §S6 (S4).
- **RFC-0005** criterion 45 and the preservation row at `:5090` say what the
  RFC says of the two `--stats` lines and the 145-failure reading. Consistent.
- **RFC-0004 §S3**: Bund words' effects are inferred, not declared (B3). The
  RFC does not list RFC-0004 among its dependencies and should.
- **RFC-0007 §C8**: `Interp` is `!Send` (`:490-493`). Consistent with §S7;
  but it means the *record* must either be `Send` or be rendered on the VM
  thread before `main` joins it — the RFC says "the two renderers live in
  the CLI" without saying which thread renders.
- **RFC-0003 §S5** is "Errors carry position" (`:648`); the "per-value spans"
  §S8 defers to are RFC-0008 §D2's reading of it (`RFC-0008:42,309-315`).
  Cite §D2, as the RFC does elsewhere.
- **ERRATA**: `03 §3.3(h) → RFC-0008 §D7` is recorded and the RFC builds on
  it (`R:17-20`). Consistent. **Not recorded**: `02-native-binaries.md:183-184`,
  the profile-guided `bund2 run --profile prog.prof`. The RFC says the two
  "must not share a spelling without saying so" (`R:23-24`); with D185 taking
  `--profile <base>` for a different meaning, ERRATA is where that is said.
  One line is due.
- **D170** (`decisions.md:4783`) profiled `bund_examples/fibonacci` with
  macOS `sample`, so the project already has a sampling profile of the VM's
  Rust frames and numbers from it. The Alternatives' "sample instead of
  instrument" row (`R:283-285`) is right about *Bund* frames but should cite
  D170 as what sampling is already used for, and D170's figures are the only
  measured evidence the RFC has for what a reading costs on this host.

## What the RFC assumes but does not state

- That the mirror stack can be kept in step with `frames` across
  `unwind_to`, `evaluate_typed` (a console line run at a stop, which sets
  the tail request aside, `lib.rs:1385-1393`), `eval_lambda` re-entry, and
  the tier shadow — i.e. that every push and pop has exactly one hook.
- That wall time is the right clock for `WordKind::Blocking` natives
  (`input`, bus receives, `sleep`): a word that waits reads as hot. `Native`
  already carries `kind` (`api:1253`); the report could print it and does not.
- That `std::time::Instant` is monotonic and that its cost is small against
  the cheapest native; criterion 3 prints the cost and bounds nothing, so a
  40 ns reading around a 10 ns `dup` is accepted silently.
- That the record is bounded: nodes by the registry plus the program's
  words, edges by pairs; and that per-node `min`/`max` and the
  "registered twice" flag are computed on the hot path at a cost criterion
  3 does not separate from the branch.
- That `script` is the only command that takes the flag. `bund2 eval`,
  `shell` or `test`, if and when Bund2 has them, are not mentioned.
- That `dot` exists on the test host (criterion 2).
- That a `Notice` is rendered by every `Reporter`, a TUI's included, in a
  place a user sees after the program's output.
- That percentages are "of wall", not of the root's inclusive, which differ
  by start-up and by the time outside any frame.

## Verdict

Not ready for Proposed. B1 is a register conflict the owner already ruled
and the document does not reflect; B2 and B3 are design claims the cited
code contradicts. S1–S9 are each a paragraph to fix. The idea — one node per
word, both kinds, two renderings, tier declined — survives every finding
here; what does not survive is the statement of what Bund2 has today, which
is the part CLAUDE.md asks to be grounded.
