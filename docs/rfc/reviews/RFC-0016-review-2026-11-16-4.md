## Should fix

### S1. The floor's shortfall outside the exact branch is up to 4.7·10⁻³, not 2.1·10⁻⁴: the sample that measured it omits the sizes the widening hands to the floor

**Location.** S2, the normal branch (lines 313-320): "Where it is used without ties, sampled at n from 67 to 100 and m from n to 5n at 80–99%, it is at most 1 above the exact k, and the coverage falls short of conf by at most 2.1·10⁻⁴ (D251's amendment)." D251's third-review amendment repeats the figure (`decisions.md:17272`).

**Evidence (`rev5_floor.py`; full output in `rev5_floor_all.txt`).**
- **Where the floor is used.** With the widened condition, the floor handles untied data whenever C(n + m, n) ≥ 2¹²⁸. For s = min(n, m) from 7 to 66, that is every l above a threshold:

  | s | l from |
  |---|---|
  | 7 | 1 080 104 |
  | 8 | 246 696 |
  | 10 | 32 292 |
  | 20 | 691 |
  | 30 | 217 |
  | 50 | 91 |
  | 65 | 67 |

  Each runs up to the 10⁷ cap on n·m. The RFC's sample starts at n = 67.
- **Measured** on 114 sizes there, plus the RFC's range: 912 cases at 0.80, 0.90, 0.95 and 0.99, one- and two-sided, with the exact k from exact integer counts:

  | conf, sides | floor above exact | worst shortfall | at |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.80, two-sided | 88 of 114 | **4.68·10⁻³** | n = 7, m = 1 080 104 (floor 2 723 153, exact 2 712 401) |
  | 0.80, one-sided | 88 of 114 | 4.00·10⁻³ | n = 7, m = 1 080 104 |
  | 0.90, one-sided | 88 of 114 | 2.34·10⁻³ | n = 7, m = 1 080 104 |
  | 0.90, two-sided | 69 of 114 | 8.15·10⁻⁴ | n = 7, m = 1 080 104 |
  | 0.95, one-sided | 69 of 114 | 4.07·10⁻⁴ | n = 7, m = 1 080 104 |
  | 0.95 and 0.99, two-sided; 0.99 one-sided | 0 | conservative | — |

- **A small worked case.** At n = 20, m = 691 at 80% two-sided, the exact k is 5746 (P(U ≤ 5745) = 0.0999980) and the floor's is 5749. Its coverage is 0.798833.
- **The pattern.** By s, the worst shortfall is:

  | s | worst shortfall |
  |---|---|
  | 7 | 4.7e-3 |
  | 10 | 3.2e-3 |
  | 20 | 1.6e-3 |
  | 30 | 1.0e-3 |
  | 50 | 5.0e-4 |
  | 66 | 3.8e-4 |
  | 100 | 2.5e-4 |
  | 300 | conservative at 0.95–0.99; 8.2e-5 at 0.80 |

  The cause is that for small s and large l, U/l approaches a sum of s uniforms, which is lighter-tailed than the normal. The floor's k then sits too far in at the moderate levels.

**Problem.**
- The figure is true of its sample, but the sentence presents it as the floor's behaviour "where it is used without ties", and the A9 amendment quotes it to the owner as the residual cost of the widened C.
- The widened condition creates exactly this region: small s against a large l, just past 2¹²⁸. There the floor is anti-conservative by up to 0.47 percentage points at 80% and 0.08 at 90%.
- The RFC gives an `.opts` user no signal of which branch ran.

This is the same kind of finding as the third review's S1. It is the design's own behaviour, not the reference's, so it does not block. But a ruling quotes the number.

**Suggested fix.**
- State the measurement with its true range, for example: "outside the exact branch the floor is conservative at 95% and 99% two-sided; at 80–90% it can fall short of conf by up to 4.7·10⁻³ (n = 7, m ≈ 1.08·10⁶, 80% two-sided), and by about 2·10⁻⁴ for n ≥ 67."
- Take the correction to the owner as a dated note on D251 A9. The options to show:
  - accept;
  - widen the exact branch again with a wider integer. A `u256` or limb pair would cover s ≤ 66 up to the cap; the work is s·nm/2, at most 3.3·10⁸.
  - add the branch taken to the answer, say `method` naming "exact" or "normal" k.

### S2. A one-sided `conf` below ½ needs counts past the truncation, and a low one makes k exceed n·m

**Location.** S2, the exact branch (lines 296-312): "truncated at degree ⌊nm/2⌋". The normal branch (lines 313-314). Line 324: "One-sided, α replaces α/2 … k < 1 is E101". S2's options table: `conf` is "a FLOAT in (0, 1)".

**Evidence (`rev5_k.py`; sizes 1 ≤ n ≤ 15, n ≤ m ≤ 30, 345 pairs).**
- **Truncation.** One-sided, α = 1 − conf is above ½ whenever conf < ½. The largest k with P(U ≤ k − 1) ≤ α then has k − 1 above ⌊nm/2⌋, so counts truncated at ⌊nm/2⌋ cannot select it. The truncated and full k differ in:

  | one-sided conf | pairs that differ |
  |---|---|
  | 0.4 | 314 |
  | 0.3 | 336 |
  | 0.1 | 343 |
  | 0.49 | 1 |

  For example, at n = m = 3, "greater", conf 0.3, the counts are 1 1 2 3 3 3 3 2 1 1 over 20. The definition gives k = 6, but the truncated counts stop at u = 4 and give 5. Two-sided is never affected, since α/2 < ½.
- **k past n·m, exact branch.** α·(1 + 10⁻⁷) ≥ 1 once conf ≤ about 10⁻⁷. Then every count passes and k = nm + 1, for all 345 pairs at one-sided conf 10⁻⁹. D₍ₙₘ₊₁₎ does not exist, and "less" would index D₍₀₎.
- **k past n·m, normal branch.** k = ⌊nm/2 − z₁₋α·σ′⌋ with z₁₋α < 0. With ties at n = m = 3 (σ′ = 2.291), one-sided conf 0.005 gives ⌊4.5 + 2.576 × 2.291⌋ = 10 > 9, and conf 10⁻⁹ gives 18.

**Problem.**
- S2 defines k for every conf in (0, 1), but its counting procedure cannot compute it for one-sided conf < ½.
- An index past the sorted differences is a panic unless it is guarded, and the RFC gives no refusal for it. It refuses only k < 1. Bund2 does not panic (CLAUDE.md; D37).
- No criterion reaches either path.

**Suggested fix.**
- Refuse k > n·m with the same E101 as k < 1 ("too few for the level").
- Either restrict a one-sided `conf` to [½, 1) for `mannwhitney.opts` (E101 below), or say the counts above ⌊nm/2⌋ are read by symmetry, c(u) = c(nm − u), so no longer table is needed.
- Add one case of each to criterion 6, for example n = m = 3, "greater", conf 0.3 → k = 6, and n = m = 3 with a tie at conf 0.005 → E101.

**N6. Kendall's accuracy depends on how the recurrence and the tail are summed** (`rev5_kendall.py`). Summed directly, the recurrence is within 1.6e-15 (above). Two natural shortcuts fail:

- **P(T ≥ t) formed as 1 − P(T ≤ t − 1)** has relative error 3.1e-10 at n = 10 and 9.3·10⁴⁰ at n = 45.

S3's "every term is non-negative" is the argument for the direct sum only. Say: "summed directly, each tail summed on its own side or read by symmetry P(T ≥ t) = P(T ≤ n(n − 1)/2 − t)". Have criterion 7's n = 45 case use a strong correlation, so that the p lies in a far tail.

**N7. "The counts outgrow `u128` from n = 36"** (line 377) is true of the Mahonian counts. The denominator n! outgrows it a step earlier: 34! ≈ 2.95·10³⁸ < 2¹²⁸ < 35! ≈ 1.03·10⁴⁰. The f64 design makes this moot; say "n! from 35" if the sentence stays.
