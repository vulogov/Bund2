# Bund2 0.56.0

Statistics over data. Four RFCs extend the biomathematics family with
words of Bund2's own: RFC-0015's mathematical biology and genetics,
RFC-0016's statistical inference, RFC-0017's frames, factors and
missing values, and RFC-0018's models over frames. No existing word
changes what it answers. The SMYSL features now take SMYSL 1.15.0 from
crates.io.

## SMYSL 1.15.0, from crates.io

The `smysl` features now take SMYSL's published crates, at exactly
1.15.0, in place of a git revision of 1.14.0 (D244 R2, RFC-0014 U6).
Nothing changes for a build without them.

- **Anthropic works.** SMYSL 1.15.0's Anthropic mapper moved to the
  current API and models and no longer sends `temperature`, which every
  current model refused (RFC-0014 U19). A `bund2` built with
  `smysl-anthropic` runs `smysl.ingest`, `smysl.ingest.text` and
  `smysl.answer` against `claude-opus-5-5`; checked live through
  `bund_examples/smysl_model`.
- What else 1.15.0 brings to a model call, from its changelog: a chunk is
  never wider than the provider's `max_output`, an Anthropic system prompt
  is cached across a run's calls, and a policy decline is an error naming
  its category rather than an empty answer.

## Biomathematics and genetics: RFC-0015 accepted, M1 to M13

RFC-0015 specifies a family of words of Bund2's own for mathematical
biology and genetics: special functions, vectors, distributions,
statistics and tests, linear algebra, seeded randomness, sequences and
alignment, population genetics, phylogenetics, ODEs and fitting. D250
rules its sixteen points. M1 lays the groundwork, M2 adds the special
functions, M3 vectors and descriptive statistics, M4 distributions and
seeded randomness, M5 statistical tests and linear models, M6 linear
algebra, M7 sequences, M8 alignment, M9 population genetics, M10
phylogenetics, M11 dynamics, M12 fitting, and M13 the examples.

- **A generator per VM.** `Vm` gains a defaulted `rng` method, and
  `Interp` keeps its own `StdRng`, seeded from the operating system on
  first use. An embedder's `Vm` that does not override it is unaffected.
- **Dependencies.** `nalgebra` 0.33.3 and `rand_distr` 0.4.3 become direct
  dependencies of `bund2-stdlib`, and `statrs` takes its `nalgebra`
  feature. All three were compiled already, so `Cargo.lock` gains no
  package.
- **Special functions (M2).** Sixteen `math.` words, each with a `.` form:
  `math.log2`, `math.log1p`, `math.expm1`, `math.gamma`, `math.lgamma`,
  `math.digamma`, `math.beta`, `math.lbeta`, `math.betainc`,
  `math.gammainc`, `math.erf`, `math.erfc`, `math.erfinv`, `math.choose`,
  `math.lchoose` and `math.lfactorial`. The deepest operand is the
  formula's first, so `10 3 math.choose` is 120. An INTEGER operand
  converts to FLOAT. Out of its domain a word refuses with E101 rather
  than answering NaN; an answer that overflows is E114 (`172 math.gamma`),
  and an incomplete beta or gamma that does not converge is E110.
  `math.erf` and `math.erfc` are `libm`'s, made direct at the version
  already compiled, since `statrs`'s are accurate only to about 1e-10.
- **Vectors and descriptive statistics (M3).** `vec.add`, `vec.sub`,
  `vec.mul`, `vec.div`, `vec.scale`, `vec.dot`, `vec.sum`, `vec.prod`,
  `vec.cumsum`, `vec.cumprod`, `vec.diff`, `vec.logsumexp` and
  `vec.normalize.sum`; `stat.var`, `stat.sd`, `stat.se`, `stat.median`,
  `stat.mode`, `stat.skewness`, `stat.kurtosis`, `stat.zscore`,
  `stat.quantile` (R's type 7), `stat.cov`, `stat.cor.pearson`,
  `stat.cor.spearman` and `stat.cor.kendall` (τ-b). A word of one series
  reads it as `stat.mean` does, a LIST, a METRICS or the stack down to
  NODATA, in the same four forms; a word of two series takes two LISTs or
  METRICS, and refuses a drain with E115. `+ - * /` keep their LIST
  meanings.
- **Distributions (M4).** A distribution is a MAP, `{ dist=normal ::
  mean=1.0 :: sd=2.0 }`, made by one of seventeen constructors
  (`dist.normal`, `dist.lognormal`, `dist.uniform`, `dist.exponential`,
  `dist.gamma`, `dist.beta`, `dist.chisq`, `dist.t`, `dist.f`,
  `dist.binomial`, `dist.poisson`, `dist.negbinomial`,
  `dist.hypergeometric`, `dist.geometric`, `dist.bernoulli`,
  `dist.multinomial`, `dist.dirichlet`) or by hand, with R's
  parameterisations and R's argument names as its keys. `dist.pdf`,
  `dist.lpdf`, `dist.cdf`, `dist.sf`, `dist.quantile`, `dist.mean`,
  `dist.var`, `dist.sample` and `dist.sample*` take it on top. Densities,
  tails, quantiles and moments are Bund2's own, over the M2 functions, and
  agree with mpmath to within 2e-13 at every committed fixture point;
  `statrs` checks the parameters and draws. Each word has a `.` form.
- **Seeded randomness (M4).** `rng.seed` reseeds the VM's own generator,
  after which every drawing word repeats exactly within this version;
  `rng.uniform` draws from [0, 1). `generator.sample`, `math.random.int`
  and `math.securerandom.int` keep their own unseeded sources. A child VM
  from `vm.spawn` has its own generator. A later change to `rand`,
  `rand_distr` or `statrs` may change every seeded stream, and its release
  notes will say so.
- **Tests, corrections and linear models (M5).** `stat.test.t1`,
  `stat.test.t2`, `stat.test.welch`, `stat.test.paired`,
  `stat.test.chisq`, `stat.test.chisq.table`, `stat.test.fisher`,
  `stat.test.binom`, `stat.test.mannwhitney` and `stat.test.ks2`, each
  two-sided and answering a MAP of `statistic`, `p`, `df` where it applies
  and `method`; `stat.padjust.bonferroni`, `stat.padjust.holm` and
  `stat.padjust.bh`; and `stat.lm`, ordinary least squares by QR with an
  intercept, answering `coef`, `se`, `t`, `p`, `r2` and `df`. The two-
  sample and paired t tests, both χ² tests, Fisher's, the binomial and
  Mann–Whitney agree with R's printed answers on R's help examples and
  `sleep` data.
- **Linear algebra (M6).** `linalg.mul`, `linalg.t`, `linalg.identity`,
  `linalg.det`, `linalg.inv`, `linalg.solve`, `linalg.eigen.sym`,
  `linalg.eigenvalues`, `linalg.eigenvalues.complex`, `linalg.dominant`,
  `linalg.power` and `linalg.mulv`, each with a `.` form, on MATRIXes of
  numbers. `linalg.dominant` answers a projection matrix's growth rate and
  stable stage distribution. `*` keeps its cell-by-cell MATRIX meaning.
- **Sequences (M7).** `bio.valid?`, `bio.complement`, `bio.revcomp`,
  `bio.transcribe`, `bio.backtranscribe`, `bio.translate`, `bio.orfs`,
  `bio.gc`, `bio.kmers`, `bio.count`, `bio.hamming`, `bio.consensus`,
  `bio.pwm` and `bio.pwm.scan`, each with a `.` form, on sequences held as
  STRINGs. `bio.translate` knows all 27 of NCBI's genetic codes, from
  `gc.prt` Version 4.6, which is committed beside the tests.
- **Alignment (M8).** `bio.align` and `bio.align.score`, each with a `.`
  form: global, semiglobal and local alignment with affine gaps, under
  BLOSUM62, a DNA matrix or a program's own. An alignment over 10⁸ cells is
  refused before anything is allocated.
- **Population genetics (M9).** `popgen.freq`, `popgen.hwe` (χ² and
  Wigginton's exact test), `popgen.het`, `popgen.fst` (Hudson's, as Bhatia
  et al. estimate it), `popgen.ld`, `popgen.pi`, `popgen.theta.w`,
  `popgen.tajima`, and Wright–Fisher trajectories with `popgen.wf` and
  `popgen.wf.selection`, drawn from the VM's seeded generator.
- **Phylogenetics (M10).** `phylo.dist` (p, JC69, K2P), `phylo.upgma`,
  `phylo.nj`, `phylo.newick` and `phylo.newick!`, and `phylo.graph`, over
  trees held as a MAP of three flat LISTs, so no word recurses on a tree's
  depth. Newick names are kept verbatim.
- **Dynamics (M11).** `ode.rk4` and `ode.rk45` (Dormand–Prince, adaptive)
  integrate a model given as a lambda, `t y -- dy`, and `sim.gillespie`
  runs a stochastic simulation by the direct method, drawing from the VM's
  seeded generator. A model runs on its own scratch stack, so it cannot
  disturb the caller's.
- **Fitting (M12).** `fit.nls` fits a model lambda, `x p -- y`, by
  Levenberg–Marquardt, answering the parameters, their standard errors,
  the residual sum of squares and its degrees of freedom; on R's
  `Puromycin` data it gives R's `nls` answer. `fit.logistic` fits a
  logistic regression with an intercept, as R's `glm(family = binomial)`
  does, answering coefficients, standard errors, z, p, deviance and AIC.
  Data a covariate separates perfectly are refused rather than answered
  with coefficients that grow without bound.
- **Examples (M13).** `bund_examples/biomath/` runs the RFC's five worked
  examples and a rule base over them: Hardy–Weinberg and an FDR
  correction, a primer's reverse complement and protein, Wright–Fisher
  replicates each in its own seeded VM, an SIR epidemic, a Leslie matrix,
  and rules that call `popgen.hwe` through bridges. Every draw is seeded,
  so a test compares what it prints with `expected.txt`.

## Statistical inference: RFC-0016, all five milestones

RFC-0016 adds intervals, one-sided tests, correlation tests, effect sizes,
rounding and resampling to the biomathematics family, as new words beside
it; no existing word changes. M1 lands two of its sections:

- **Corrections.** `stat.padjust.hochberg`, `stat.padjust.hommel` and
  `stat.padjust.by` (Benjamini–Yekutieli), in the four forms of the
  existing corrections, answering in input order. Hommel's is the
  closed-testing procedure over Simes' tests, computed in O(n²) steps and
  capped at 10⁴ p-values.
- **Rounding and formatting.** `x n math.round.places` and
  `x n math.signif` round to decimal places and significant digits;
  `x n string.fixed` and `x n string.sci` format with n decimals. x may be
  a LIST, answered element by element. Ties are decided on the exact binary
  value, ties to even, so `2.675 2 math.round.places` is 2.67 and
  `2.5 0 math.round.places` is 2.0; the existing `math.round` still answers
  3.0 for 2.5.

A snapshot of the existing tests' answers, recorded before any change,
checks that they answer the same to the bit.

### RFC-0016 M2: intervals and one-sided tests

Five new words take a test's operands with an options MAP above them:
`stat.test.t1.opts`, `stat.test.t2.opts`, `stat.test.welch.opts`,
`stat.test.paired.opts` and `stat.test.binom.opts`, each with a `.` form
that takes the options from the workbench. The options are
`alternative` (`"two.sided"`, `"less"` or `"greater"`), `conf` (default
0.95) and, for the two-sample t tests, `mu`. The answer adds `estimate`,
the interval's `lower` and `upper`, `conf` and `alternative` to the plain
test's keys:

    [ 5.1 4.9 6.2 5.8 ] [ 4.0 4.4 5.1 4.2 ] dict "conf" 0.99 set stat.test.welch.opts

The t tests' intervals are t intervals on the test's df; the binomial's
is Clopper–Pearson. A one-sided interval has only the end that bounds the
alternative, so a program checks `"upper" ?key` before it gets the end.
With empty options each word answers exactly what the plain test does.

### RFC-0016 M3: Fisher, Mann–Whitney and Kolmogorov–Smirnov

- `stat.test.fisher.opts` answers the conditional maximum likelihood
  estimate of the odds ratio and its exact interval. Where an end is
  infinite it is left out.
- `stat.test.mannwhitney.opts` answers the Hodges–Lehmann shift, the
  median of the differences between a and b, with an interval read from
  the sorted differences. The interval is exact for small samples without
  ties, and from the normal approximation otherwise. `mu` tests a shift.
  Up to 10⁷ differences.
- `stat.test.ks2.opts` answers the one-sided statistics D⁺ ("greater")
  and D⁻ ("less") with their p.

### RFC-0016 M4: correlation tests

`a b stat.cor.test.pearson`, `stat.cor.test.spearman` and
`stat.cor.test.kendall` test a correlation and answer a MAP with
`estimate` (the same r, ρ or τ the `stat.cor.` words give), `statistic`
and `p`. Pearson's also answers Fisher's z interval from four pairs up.
Kendall's p is exact for fewer than 50 pairs without ties. Each has a
`.opts` variant taking `alternative` ("greater" is positive association)
and, for Pearson, `conf`.

### RFC-0016 M5: effect sizes, shuffling and sampling

- `a b stat.effect.cohend` answers Cohen's `d` and Hedges' `g`.
- `stat.effect.oddsratio` and `stat.effect.riskratio` take a 2×2 table
  and answer the ratio with its 95% interval. A zero cell that makes the
  ratio 0 or infinite is refused; add 0.5 to each cell to correct for it.
- `stat.effect.cramerv` answers Cramér's V for any table.
- `list rng.shuffle`, `list k rng.choice` (without replacement),
  `list k rng.choice.replace` and `list weights k rng.choice.weighted`
  draw from the generator `rng.seed` seeds, so a seeded program repeats
  its draws. The list may hold any values.

## Frames, factors and missing values: RFC-0017, M1 to M4

RFC-0017 adds tables of data to the biomathematics family, as words of
Bund2's own; no existing word changes what it answers. D252 rules the
two choices underneath, and D253–D259 the rest.

- **A frame is a plain MAP** with three keys: `columns`, the names in
  order; `data`, each name to its LIST of cells; and `levels`, each
  factor column's levels in order. There is no new value type, so a
  frame prints, stores and travels through `encode.base64` as any MAP
  does. Every `frame.` word checks the shape on entry and refuses a
  malformed frame naming what is wrong.
- **NONE means missing.** `na` pushes it, `na.is` tests for it (`==`
  still refuses NONE), and `na.count`, `na.omit` and `na.fill` count,
  drop and replace it in a LIST. JSON's `null` was already NONE. Inside
  a LIST literal `na` stays a word, as `true` and `false` do, so
  `[ 1 na ]` does not hold NONE; `[ 1 ] na +` does.

### RFC-0017 M1: the frame and its missing values

`names columns frame.new` builds a frame, and `frame.check` checks one.
`frame.nrow`, `frame.ncol`, `frame.names`, `frame.column` and
`frame.rows` read it, the last as one MAP per row for a program's own
loop.

### RFC-0017 M2: building, choosing and reshaping

- `frame.with` replaces or appends a column, and `frame.select` picks
  columns.
- `frame.take` takes rows by **1-based** index and `frame.head` the
  first n.
- `frame.filter` keeps the rows its lambda answers `true` for, given
  each row as a MAP.
- `frame.complete` keeps the rows with no NONE in the named columns.
  Use it, not two `na.omit`s, when two columns must stay aligned.
- `frame.sort` sorts stably, NONE last, with `descending` naming the
  columns to sort downwards.
- `frame.factor` and `frame.factor.levels` make a string column a
  factor.

### RFC-0017 M3: CSV and text

    "/data/trial.csv" file dict [ "site" ] "factors" swap set frame.csv.parse

- `frame.csv.parse` reads CSV text with a reader of its own. It takes
  options `header`, `na` (the strings read as NONE, `""` and `"NA"` by
  default), `factors` and `sep`. A missing cell becomes NONE, so rows stay
  aligned where the `csv` conditional drops the cell. A quoted field is
  always text, a non-finite or oversized number is refused, and so is a
  row of the wrong width, naming its row.
- `frame.csv.text` writes CSV back, with NONE as an empty field and
  every STRING quoted.
- `frame.text` prints an aligned table for reading, at most 20 rows.
- `csv` and its conditional are unchanged.
- **A frame does not round-trip through JSON.** `json.from_value`
  refuses a frame's STRINGs, as the reference's converter does. The
  wire format's base64 carries a frame unchanged.

### RFC-0017 M4: aggregate, join and matrix

- `frame by stats opts frame.aggregate` answers one row per group of
  the `by` columns, in `frame.sort`'s order, and one column per
  `[ column statistic ]` pair. The statistics are `count`, `sum`,
  `mean`, `var`, `sd`, `median`, `min` and `max`, each computed by the
  same code as its named word, so the answer is that word's to the bit.
- `left right by opts frame.join` joins on the `by` columns, `"inner"`
  by default or `"left"` with `how`. An unmatched row gets NONE. The
  output's size is checked before it is built.
- `frame names opts frame.matrix` answers the named numeric columns as
  a MATRIX for `stat.lm` and the `linalg.` words.
- `frame.aggregate` and `frame.matrix` take the `na` option. `"refuse"`,
  the default, stops at the first NONE and names its column and row.
  `"omit"` drops incomplete rows and reports how many: a `.dropped`
  column per group, or `{ matrix, dropped }`.
- **Refactors, same answers.** `vec.sum`'s sum and `stat.median`'s median
  are now crate-internal functions their words call, and
  `stat.mean.arithmetic` now calls `describe`'s `mean`, whose arithmetic
  it already had. The existing tests answer the same.

`bund_examples/frames/` runs all of it over a small dose trial, and a
test compares its output with `expected.txt`.

## Models: RFC-0018, M1 to M6

RFC-0018 adds statistical models over frames to the biomathematics
family: a design coded from a frame, linear and generalised linear
models, their residuals and diagnostics, prediction, and the comparison
of nested models. All are words of Bund2's own. `stat.lm` and
`fit.logistic` answer exactly as before. D260 rules research 07's
questions C3–C8, D261 the RFC's sixteen, and D262–D265 its other
choices.

- **A design and a fit are plain MAPs**, as a frame is. A design holds
  the model MATRIX with its column names, `y`, the weights and offset,
  and `spec`, a record of how the frame was coded. A fit keeps that
  `spec`, so `model.predict` codes new rows the same way. Every word
  checks the MAP it reads and refuses a malformed one, naming the key.
- **Where R warns, these words refuse**: a fit that does not converge,
  separation, Poisson rates numerically 0, a perfect fit, a leverage of
  1, and an F test under a fixed dispersion.

### RFC-0018 M1: the design

    frame "response" [ "dose" "site" [ "dose" "site" ] ] dict model.matrix

`frame response terms opts model.matrix` codes the response on the
terms. A term is a column's name, or a LIST of names for an
interaction. A factor gets one column per level after its first, and a
logical column gets one column. `opts` takes `intercept`, `weights`,
`offset` and `na`, `"refuse"` or `"omit"`. An interaction's columns
follow R's variable order (D265), so a design's column names are those
R gives.

### RFC-0018 M2: `fit.lm`

`design opts fit.lm` fits by weighted least squares, with the offset
subtracted. It answers the coefficients with their standard errors, t
and p values, `vcov`, σ, r² and adjusted r², the F test against the
intercept alone, the deviance and null deviance, AIC and BIC, the fitted
values and the leverages. Its computation is `stat.lm`'s, now shared as
a crate-internal function. With unit weights, no offset and an
intercept, the two answer the same to the bit, and a snapshot recorded
before the change checks that `stat.lm` and `fit.logistic` still do.

**One new refusal text.** A singular design is E106 naming the first
column that depends on those before it. `stat.lm`'s and
`fit.logistic`'s texts are unchanged.

### RFC-0018 M3: `fit.glm`

`design dict "family" "poisson" set fit.glm` fits a generalised linear
model by iteratively reweighted least squares, as R's `glm.fit` does.
It supports five families: `gaussian`, `binomial`, `poisson`, `gamma`
and `inverse.gaussian`. Each takes R's links for it, and the canonical
link is the default. It halves a step that leaves the valid range, as R
does. It answers what `fit.lm` does where it applies, with z in place
of t when the dispersion is fixed, and also the linear predictor, the
working weights, the iterations and the halvings. On the binomial logit
path with 0/1 responses, unit weights and no offset, it uses
`fit.logistic`'s arithmetic, and the two agree to 10⁻¹².

### RFC-0018 M4: residuals, influence and prediction

- `fit opts model.residuals` answers one residual per row:
  `"deviance"` by default, or `"response"`, `"pearson"`, `"working"`
  or `"standardized"`.
- `fit model.influence` answers each row's leverage, standardized
  residual and Cook's distance.
- `fit frame opts model.predict` predicts at a new frame's rows. The
  `type` option sets the scale, `"link"` by default or `"response"`.
  `interval` gives `"confidence"` intervals, or `"prediction"` intervals
  for a Gaussian identity fit, at `conf`, 0.95 by default. A new row
  must use only the levels the fit was coded with. An interval end that
  is infinite on the response scale leaves its key out.

### RFC-0018 M5: comparing models

- `small big opts model.compare` tests whether `big` improves on
  `small`. The two must be nested: the same family, link, response,
  weights and offset, and every column of `small` among `big`'s.
  Without a `test` option, it uses F when the dispersion is estimated
  and a likelihood-ratio test when it is fixed, as R 4.4 does.
- `design opts model.anova` answers R's sequential analysis-of-deviance
  table as a frame. Its first row is the null model, and each later row
  adds one term. Each row's test uses the full model's dispersion and
  residual df. A refusal while a row is fitted names that row's term.

### RFC-0018 M6: the example

`bund_examples/models/` codes the frames example's dose trial as a
design, fits it with `fit.lm`, checks it, and predicts new rows with
intervals. It then fits Dobson's trial, R's own `glm` example, as a
Poisson model and compares it with and without treatment.
Its numbers match R's for that example, and a test compares its output
with `expected.txt`.
