# Bund2 0.57.0

Resampling, optimisation, power and further tests. RFC-0019 finishes the
statistics family that 0.56.0 began. It adds noncentral distributions,
root finding and quadrature, power and sample size, ten further tests,
one-way ANOVA with Tukey's HSD, the bootstrap, the jackknife, permutation
tests, minimisers, and maximum likelihood. Every word is Bund2's own and
new; no existing word changes what it answers, except `dist.t`, whose
tails are corrected at a large df.

## RFC-0019: accepted, M1 to M10

RFC-0019 covers research 07's Phases D and E in one RFC. D266 rules its
thirteen questions and D267 its author's choices. Three reviews preceded
its acceptance on 2026-11-22. Each word follows R 4.4.2's algorithm,
ported from R's sources and named where it is, and is checked against
fixtures computed by mpmath at 50 digits or more, or against R 4.6.2.

### M1: noncentral t, χ² and F

`dist.nct`, `dist.ncchisq` and `dist.ncf` are distribution constructors.
Each MAP they answer works with every `dist.` word: density, tails,
quantile, moments and draws. The series are summed outward from the
largest weight, with a rigorous stop at 10⁻¹⁵ and E111 at 10⁶ terms.

### M2: roots, quadrature and the studentized range

- `optim.root` is R's `zeroin` (Brent's method), with `uniroot`'s
  `extendInt` modes `"no"`, `"yes"`, `"downX"` and `"upX"`.
- `optim.integrate` is QUADPACK's QAGS and QAGI, as R's `integrate`. The
  ends may be `"inf"` and `"-inf"`, and any QUADPACK error is E110, where
  R's `integrate` stops.
- `dist.tukey` is the studentized range distribution, its cdf a double
  integral by QUADPACK.

Each takes its function as a lambda.

### M3: power and sample size

`power.t`, `power.prop`, `power.anova` and `power.chisq` each take one
options MAP and solve for the one parameter left out, as R's
`power.t.test`, `power.prop.test` and `power.anova.test` do.
`power.chisq` is Bund2's own, for Cohen's w. Each answers every
parameter and the method. Like R, they answer `n` as a FLOAT; a program
rounds it up with `math.ceil`.

### M4 and M5: further tests

- **Rank tests:** `stat.test.wilcoxon` and `stat.test.wilcoxon.paired`,
  each with an `.opts` form for intervals and the Hodges–Lehmann
  estimate, plus `stat.test.kruskal` and `stat.test.friedman`.
- **Count tests:** `stat.test.mcnemar` and `stat.test.cmh`, the
  Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test over 2×2 strata (D271).
- **Normality tests:** `stat.test.shapiro` (Royston's Shapiro–Wilk,
  `swilk.c` line for line) and `stat.test.ks1`, the one-sample KS test
  against any distribution MAP, exact below 100 values.
- **Equal variances:** `stat.test.bartlett`, and `stat.test.levene`, with
  its `.opts` form for centring on the mean as well as the median.

Where R only warns, as when a confidence level cannot be reached, Bund2
refuses with E101. An F test answers `df1` and `df2` (D272).

### M6: one-way ANOVA, Tukey's HSD and an interval for d

- `stat.test.anova1` is `oneway.test` with equal variances and answers η²
  and ω² beside F (D272, D273); its `.opts` form gives Welch's F.
- `stat.test.tukey` is `TukeyHSD`'s Tukey–Kramer, answered as a frame of
  `pair`, `diff`, `lower`, `upper` and `p` in R's pair order.
- `stat.effect.cohend.opts` adds an interval for d by inverting the
  noncentral t.

### M7 and M8: the bootstrap, the jackknife and permutation tests

- **`rng.derive`** answers the VM generator's next 64-bit draw. It gives
  a seed for a derived generator, so a study can hand reproducible
  streams to the parts it starts.
- **`boot.sample`** resamples a LIST by element or a frame by row. The
  statistic is a lambda or one of `"mean"`, `"median"`, `"sd"` and
  `"var"`. It answers `t0`, the replicates, `bias`, `se`, and any of the
  normal, basic, percentile and BCa intervals, as `boot` 1.3 computes
  them. BCa's acceleration comes from the jackknife (N4).
- **`boot.jackknife`** answers the leave-one-out values, the bias and the
  standard error.
- **`stat.test.permutation`** tests two samples with a lambda statistic,
  or with `"mean.diff"`, `"median.diff"` or Welch's `"t"`. It is exact
  over every split up to 10⁶ of them, and Monte Carlo by `rng.shuffle`
  above that or on request (D274).

After the same `rng.seed`, each of these repeats exactly.

### M9: minimisers, the Hessian and maximum likelihood

- **`optim.nm` and `optim.bfgs`** are R's `nmmin` and `vmmin`, ported line
  for line, with R's defaults and R's key names (D275). On Rosenbrock's
  function, Nelder–Mead matches R 4.6.2's answer and evaluation counts
  exactly. BFGS matches R's iterations and agrees with its answer to
  10⁻¹⁴.
- **`optim.hessian`** is R's `optimHess`.
- **`fit.mle`** maximises a log-likelihood lambda by either method. It
  answers the estimates, `se` and `vcov` from the inverted Hessian, the
  log-likelihood, AIC, and BIC when given n. With `profile` true it also
  answers likelihood-ratio intervals.
- **Refusals:** a Hessian that is not positive definite is E106, naming
  the pivot. Past 10⁷ calls of the lambda, each of these words refuses
  with E111.

### M10: the example

`bund_examples/inference/` takes a three-diet trial from planning to a
likelihood:

1. It plans the sample size by power.
2. It checks each diet for normality, compares the diets by the one-way
   F and Tukey's HSD, and measures an effect by Cohen's d.
3. It bootstraps a mean, jackknifes it, and permutes two diets exactly
   and by Monte Carlo.
4. It fits a normal model beside its closed form, and an exponential
   rate with its profile interval.

Its tests and plans match R 4.6.2's, and a test compares its output with
`expected.txt`.

## Fixes

- **`dist.t` at a large df and near p = ½.** The tails rounded to ½ for a
  small x at any df, and ln B(ν/2, ½) lost every digit as ν grew large.
  The tails now follow R's `pt`, with R's `lbeta`, and with `bgrat` from
  df 1000. Six existing t p-values move, five by an ulp or two. The sixth
  moves by 6.7·10⁻¹⁴: `stat.lm`'s p for a t of 0.002 on 5 df. Each is now
  within 3·10⁻¹⁷ of mpmath's.
- **`url` 2.5.9.** RUSTSEC-2026-0592, a stack overflow in `Url::origin()`
  on nested `blob:` URLs, affects `url` 2.5.8, which the SMYSL provider
  crates pull in.
- **A Linux snapshot (D276).** One t p-value in a unit test's bit-for-bit
  snapshot takes its last bits from the platform's maths library and
  differs by 3 ulp between glibc and macOS. Linux reads its own snapshot,
  as D150 rules for goldens. The arithmetic is unchanged.
